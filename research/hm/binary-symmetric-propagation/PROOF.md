# Exact binary-parameter modules and primitive defect propagation

## Setup and scope

Let S be the established five-variable polynomial ring over a characteristic-zero field, and let H have its established minimal presentation

S(-2) tensor U -> S tensor W -> H -> 0,

where dim U=10 and dim W=2. Put B=Sym(W), with its own grading a; this is the symmetric-power grading, not the S grading. Let n=B_+ and k=B/n. Define

M_d = direct sum over a>=0 of (Sym^a_S H)_d.

Multiplication in Sym_S(H) makes M_d a graded B-module. In particular the a=0 term is S_d, not zero or a copy of H_d. Write V^vee for the graded k-dual and N(s)_a=N_(a+s).

The conclusions below are ordinary graded B-module isomorphisms. No equivariant splitting, selected HM/source evaluation, or claim about the remaining determinantal connecting parameter is made. They concern the full variation in the binary generator parameter, not selected source coefficients.

## 1. Linear B presentations

The symmetric algebra is the quotient of S tensor B by the relations obtained from the presentation of H. These relations have bidegree (2,1). Thus, for d>=2,

S_(d-2) tensor U tensor B(-1) -> S_d tensor B -> M_d -> 0

is a presentation. In particular every minimal B relation has degree one. A constant kernel among the displayed relation generators can be removed by a vector-space change of basis; the number of minimal relations is 2 dim(M_d)_0 - dim(M_d)_1.

The pinned symmetric-power Hilbert functions give:

M_3: (35,20,15,10,5) in B degrees 0 through 4;
M_4: (70,10,5) in B degrees 0 through 2;
M_5: (126,2,1) in B degrees 0 through 2.

All later components vanish. The a=1 entries are the Hilbert function of H; the a=2 entries use Sym^2 H=R^vee(-5); a>=3 uses the higher-symmetric-power theorem.

## 2. Minimal B resolutions and complete decompositions

Each M_d above has finite length over the two-variable ring B. Its minimal resolution has length two. Since the first relations are linear, the Hilbert numerator determines the free second syzygy module without cancellation. Multiplication of the three displayed Hilbert polynomials by (1-t)^2 gives respectively

35-50t+10t^2+5t^6,
70-130t+55t^2+5t^4,
126-250t+123t^2+t^4.

Therefore the minimal graded B resolutions are

0 -> 10B(-2) direct sum 5B(-6) -> 50B(-1) -> 35B -> M_3 -> 0;
0 -> 55B(-2) direct sum 5B(-4) -> 130B(-1) -> 70B -> M_4 -> 0;
0 -> 123B(-2) direct sum B(-4) -> 250B(-1) -> 126B -> M_5 -> 0.

For a finite-length module over B, the last Betti generators in degree j correspond to socle in degree j-2. This follows directly from the top Koszul homology: its cycles are Soc(M) tensor Lambda^2 W. Consequently the socles are:

M_3: dimension 10 in degree 0 and dimension 5 in degree 4;
M_4: dimension 55 in degree 0 and dimension 5 in degree 2;
M_5: dimension 123 in degree 0 and dimension 1 in degree 2.

A degree-zero socle of a module generated in degree zero splits off. Indeed, choose a complement to it in M_0. The B-submodule generated by that complement contains every positive-degree component and meets the degree-zero socle trivially. Remove this direct summand and call the remainder N.

For M_3, N has Hilbert function (25,20,15,10,5) and socle dimension 5 only in degree 4. Hence N^vee(-4) is generated by five degree-zero elements, has Hilbert function (5,10,15,20,25), and vanishes from degree 5 onward. A surjection 5B -> N^vee(-4) is an isomorphism in every degree below 5 by these maximal dimension counts; its kernel is exactly 5n^5. The identical argument applies to the other two modules. Thus

M_3 is isomorphic to 10k direct sum 5[(B/n^5)^vee(-4)];
M_4 is isomorphic to 55k direct sum 5[(B/n^3)^vee(-2)];
M_5 is isomorphic to 123k direct sum [(B/n^3)^vee(-2)].

These are complete B-module identifications, not just Hilbert-function coincidences. The splittings are not claimed canonical. No representation-theoretic multiplicity assumption is required.

There is also a concrete identification of the degree-zero socles: an element f in S_d is killed by both generators W precisely when it annihilates H, because W generates H over S. Therefore (Soc_B M_d)_0=I_d, where I=Ann_S H. This recovers dimensions 10,55,123 and identifies the scalar-annihilator summands intrinsically, although choosing their complements is noncanonical.

## 3. Exact cubic first-defect propagation

For fixed S degree 3, the left Koszul strand of the presentation has only its first two terms:

50B(-1) -> 35B.

The next term has S degree at least 4. The map has no constant linear-relation redundancy, so its kernel is exactly

Z_3 = 10B(-2) direct sum 5B(-6).

For each 1<=a<=8, the degree-a component of Z_3 is H_1(C^a)_3 for the generalized Eagon–Northcott complex normalized in PR507. This also matches a=0, where both components are zero. Thus the entire family of cubic first-defect classes is freely generated in symmetric degrees 2 and 6: ten primitive classes in degree 2 and five additional primitive classes in degree 6, with no B relations. In particular the jump from dimension 40 at a=5 to dimension 55 at a=6 reflects five genuinely new generators, not merely multiplication of previous classes.

## 4. A further quartic propagation consequence

At fixed S degree 4 the left Koszul strand is

45B(-2) -> 150B(-1) -> 70B.

PR508 proves that the left map is injective in every B degree, by its universal binary-column argument. Let Z_4 be the kernel of the right map. The raw 150 linear relations contain 20 independent constant redundancies, because their rank in B degree one is 140-10=130. Splitting these redundancies and using the minimal resolution of M_4 gives

Z_4 is isomorphic to 20B(-1) direct sum 55B(-2) direct sum 5B(-4).

Let N_4 be the first homology of this B strand. Then

0 -> 45B(-2) -> Z_4 -> N_4 -> 0.

A homogeneous degree-zero map from B(-2) to B(-4) is zero. Hence the boundary map has no component in the 5B(-4) summand, and N_4 has a direct free B summand 5B(-4). For 1<=a<=8, (N_4)_a=H_1(C^a)_4. Thus there are exactly five primitive quartic first-defect generators in symmetric degree 4; all other primitive generators of N_4 occur in symmetric degrees 1 or 2. There are twenty in degree 1 and at least ten in degree 2, since the constant projection of the 45-dimensional boundary space to the 55-dimensional quadratic-generator space has rank at most 45. Its exact rank is not determined here.

This is not a claim about the ordinary C^0 quartic defect: C^0 uses the determinantal joining map rather than this left strand in that position. Nor does the free summand specify an equivariant canonical splitting.

## 5. Uniform nonzero binary specialization excludes cubic column defects

Let lambda:W -> K be any nonzero covector over any field extension K of k. Let J_lambda be the ideal of S_K generated by the ten quadratic entries of phi contracted by lambda. Specializing B at this nonzero affine point identifies

Sym_S(H) tensor_B K_lambda = S_K/J_lambda.

Every M_d for d>=3 is supported at the binary origin: for d=3,4,5 this was proved above; for d>=6, the pinned Hilbert functions give (M_d)_a=0 for every a>=1, so M_d is concentrated in B degree zero. Tensoring these modules with K_lambda therefore gives zero. There are no defining relations in S degrees zero or one. In S degree two the contracted map U_K -> (S_K)_2 is injective by the universal binary-column identity of PR508, leaving dimension 15-10=5. Hence, uniformly for every nonzero lambda,

Hilbert function of S_K/J_lambda = (1,5,5),
(S_K)_3 is contained in J_lambda.

In particular the ten quadratic column entries generate all cubics, and their linear syzygy space has dimension 10*5-35=15. This is a uniform specialization theorem derived from the full binary-parameter module, not an evaluation of a selected HM/source instance. It does not assert that a fixed pair of scalar forms acts injectively on H or K, and it does not determine the remaining determinantal connecting channels.
