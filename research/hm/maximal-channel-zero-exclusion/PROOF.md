# A unique linear syzygy excludes the maximal fixed-channel zero scheme

## Interface and new conclusion

Work over C. Let G=A5 act on its rotation module A=Sym²W, and set X=P(A), S=Sym(A*). We use the canonical endpoint interface and results of PR664, pinned at 90d9aa88b3cea99e8dd1acea0acda9095021185e, research/hm/exceptional-channel-loci/PROOF.md. In particular, for every nonzero equivariant channel q, its zero scheme Z=Z_q on X is finite and G-invariant, its ideal sheaf is generated by the twenty quintic minors described there, and length(Z)≤21. Its support is contained in the unique axis orbits of sizes 6,10,15. The square conic is disjoint from that support. PR664 also proves (S_5)^G=0 and recalls that G has no nontrivial characters or two-dimensional irreducible representations.

We first exclude length21 and prove length(Z_q)≤20. A further local symmetry exclusion then gives the stronger final bound

                         length(Z_q) ≤ 18.

Consequently, if the C2 axis orbit occurs in the exceptional locus, its kernel channel differs from BOTH the C3 and C5 kernel channels. Its fixed-channel zero scheme is exactly the fifteen reduced C2 axes. This does not decide whether the C2 axes occur, whether the C3 and C5 channels agree, or the scheme structure of the total rank-drop locus.

The argument is structural. No selected actual HM endpoint, source coefficient, matrix or rank is evaluated. Its only representation calculation is a universal five-class character identity. In particular we do not infer an actual map's rank from the dimensions of its source and target: an explicit polarization splitting proves the needed surjectivity.

## 1. Residual length four must be collinear

Suppose length(Z)=21. Because its quintic ideal-sheaf generators have finite common zero scheme, choose two quintics F,H in I_Z with no common component. Let T=V(F,H), a complete intersection of length25, and let R be residual to Z in T. Complete-intersection annihilator duality gives length(R)=4 and the exact identity, already established in PR664,

                   h⁰(I_Z(5)) = 2 + h¹(I_R(2)).             (1)

No reducedness or local-complete-intersection assumption on Z or R is needed. The chosen T and its residual R are NOT assumed G-invariant.

If h¹(I_R(2))=0, the two-dimensional space H⁰(I_Z(5)) would be a G-stable subspace of S_5. Since G has no nontrivial two-dimensional irreducible representation and (S_5)^G=0, such a subspace is impossible. Therefore R does not impose independent conditions on quadrics.

Here is a scheme-theoretic elementary criterion covering all length-four schemes. Choose a line missing the support of R, dehomogenize, and let B be its coordinate algebra, dim B=4. Write V_d for the images of polynomials of total degree at most d in B. If R is not contained in a projective line, dim V_1=3. If V_2=V_1, multiplication by both affine coordinates preserves V_1. Since V_1 contains 1, it then contains the entire generated algebra B, contradicting dim B=4. Thus dim V_2=4. This is precisely surjectivity of restriction of quadrics to R. Contrapositively, R is contained in a line.

Every length-four subscheme of a projective line is an effective divisor of degree four on that smooth line. Its saturated ideal in S consequently has the form

                            I_R=(l,g),                     (2)

with deg(l)=1, deg(g)=4, and g not divisible by l. In particular R is a complete intersection of type (1,4), even when nonreduced. Restriction of quadrics to the line has rank3 on R, so h¹(I_R(2))=1 and (1) gives h⁰(I_Z(5))=3.

## 2. Linkage creates one and only one linear syzygy

Since F,H vanish on R, write

                 F=l a+g b,       H=l c+g d,

where a,c have degree4 and b,d have degree1. Set D=ad-bc, a quintic. Then

                      lD=dF-bH,       gD=aH-cF.

Thus J=(F,H,D) is contained in the residual ideal (F,H):(l,g)=I_Z. To see both the equality and its syzygies without any equivariance assertion about l,g,F,H, consider the matrix

                         [ -d  -c ]
                    M =  [  b   a ] .
                         [  l  -g ]

Its maximal minors, up to signs, are F,H,D. The ideal of those minors has height2 because it contains the regular sequence F,H and is contained in I_Z, a height2 ideal. The Hilbert–Burch theorem therefore gives the exact minimal graded resolution

      0 → S(-6) ⊕ S(-9) → S(-5)^3 → J → 0.                (3)

All entries have positive degree, so the resolution is minimal. The one-dimensional graded ring S/J is Cohen–Macaulay; hence it has no irrelevant torsion and J is saturated. Its Hilbert-series numerator is 1−3t^5+t^6+t^9. The resulting zero-scheme length is

                [−3·5·4 + 6·5 + 9·8]/2 = 21.

Since J⊂I_Z and both are saturated zero-dimensional ideals of length21, J=I_Z. This also directly verifies the usual CI(1,4)-to-CI(5,5) linkage resolution.

Let U=(I_Z)_5. The degree-six kernel of multiplication

                          U⊗S_1 → S_6                    (4)

is exactly one-dimensional by (3). It is intrinsic to Z, even though the displayed generators and matrix are not. Since Z is G-invariant, this one-dimensional kernel is G-stable. Every character of A5 is trivial, so it contains a nonzero invariant vector. Under the natural inclusion U⊗S_1⊂S_5⊗S_1 this vector is an invariant element in the kernel of ordinary polynomial multiplication.

We now show that no such invariant element exists.

## 3. Universal invariant linear syzygies vanish in degree six

Write phi=(1+sqrt(5))/2 and psi=1−phi. For the five conjugacy classes of A5, take class sizes (1,15,20,12,12), with the two five-cycle classes labeled so that the character of A* is (3,−1,0,phi,psi). Interchanging those two classes changes nothing below.

The characters needed are

                         identity   order2   order3   5a    5b
             A*              3        −1        0    phi   psi
             S_5            21        −3        0     1     1
             S_6            28         4        1    phi   psi.

These entries follow directly from eigenvalues (1,u,u^{-1}). If c=1+u+u^{-1}, then the complete symmetric characters h_n satisfy

    sum h_n t^n = 1/(1−ct+ct²−t³),
    h_0=1, h_1=c, h_2=c²−c,
    h_n=c h_{n−1}−c h_{n−2}+h_{n−3}.

The same recurrence at the identity uses c=3. It yields the table without selected-instance calculations. Averaging gives

       dim (S_5⊗S_1)^G = [63+45+12(phi+psi)]/60 = 2,
       dim (S_6)^G     = [28+60+20+12(phi+psi)]/60 = 2.

Consider the actual, canonical multiplication map mu:S_5⊗S_1→S_6. It has a canonical GL(A)-equivariant right inverse: on a sextic f, in any linear coordinate basis x_i, set

                     p(f)=(1/6) sum_i (∂f/∂x_i)⊗x_i.

This is basis-independent polarization, and Euler's identity gives mu(p(f))=f. It restricts to a right inverse on G-invariants. Thus the invariant multiplication map is surjective; its equal two-dimensional source and target now imply it is an isomorphism. In particular

                      (ker mu)^G=0.                       (5)

The nonzero invariant syzygy forced by (4) contradicts (5). This excludes length21. Combined with PR664's bound, length(Z_q)≤20 follows.

Conceptually, the extremal residual forces a unique low-degree syzygy; the symmetry forces that line to be invariant; and polarization shows the ambient invariant syzygy space is zero. There is no inference that a chosen residual line is invariant.

## 4. Next consequence: the C2 channel is isolated from both known channels

Keep PR664's notation m_5,m_3,m_2 for the orbitwise local lengths of a fixed-channel zero scheme at the axis orbits. The new bound gives

                       6m_5+10m_3+15m_2 ≤ 20.

If m_2>0, then m_2=1 and m_5=m_3=0. Therefore the C2 kernel, when present, differs from both other axis kernels, and its zero scheme consists exactly of the fifteen reduced C2 axes. This excludes the formerly possible reduced union of the six C5 axes and fifteen C2 axes.

At this intermediate stage the possible nonzero lengths under these arithmetic constraints are 6,10,12,15,16,18,20. If C3 and C5 channels coincide, their fixed-channel zero scheme remains reduced of length16. Their coincidence is not decided here. The next local argument removes lengths12 and20. The rank-drop scheme Delta need not have the same infinitesimal structure as a fixed-channel zero scheme.

## 5. Local stabilizer symmetry excludes length two and sharpens the bound to eighteen

Let p be a C_n axis with n=3 or5. Its full projective stabilizer is H=N_G(C_n), dihedral of order2n. Let L⊂A be its axis line. The projective tangent representation is

                         T_p X = L*⊗(A/L).

The cyclic rotation subgroup acts trivially on L and with the two distinct nontrivial inverse characters on A/L. An element of H outside that cyclic subgroup conjugates the rotation to its inverse and therefore exchanges these two eigenspaces. Consequently T_p X, and hence its dual cotangent representation, is irreducible as an H-module. This argument is unchanged by the sign action of the noncyclic element on L.

If a G-invariant finite subscheme has local length exactly2 at p, its local algebra B has one-dimensional maximal ideal n, with n²=0. The natural equivariant surjection from the surface cotangent space m_p/m_p² onto n/n² would then be a one-dimensional quotient of that irreducible two-dimensional representation. This is impossible. Thus the local length at either the C3 or C5 axis orbit is never2.

Combining this with the established twenty-bound gives

                 m_5∈{0,1,3},  m_3∈{0,1},  m_2∈{0,1},
                 6m_5+10m_3+15m_2≤20.

The complete surviving nonzero lengths are therefore

                              6,10,15,16,18,

and length(Z_q)≤18 follows. These are necessary possibilities, not assertions that every one occurs.

Moreover a local length-three quotient at a C5 axis must be the full first infinitesimal neighborhood. Indeed the nonzero quotient n/n² of the irreducible two-dimensional cotangent space must have dimension2. Since dim B=3, it follows that dim n=2 and n²=0. The defining ideal therefore contains m_p², and equal lengths force equality. Thus the only nonreduced surviving fixed-channel scheme is, if it occurs, the union of the six first infinitesimal neighborhoods of the C5 axes, of total length18. The length6,10,15,16 cases are reduced unions of the corresponding permitted axis orbits.

In particular the C3 fixed-channel zero scheme is reduced, either the ten C3 axes alone or their reduced union with the six C5 axes. The C2 channel, if present, remains isolated from both other channels and has exactly its fifteen reduced axes. The C5 channel has either six reduced axes, the reduced union with the C3 axes, or six first-order fat axes. No statement of reducedness of the total rank-drop scheme is inferred.
