# Independent review: exclusion of a length-21 fixed-channel zero scheme

## Verdict and scope

**PASS.** The argument in `PROOF.md` is valid under the stated PR664 interface. Its liaison argument proves `length(Z_q) <= 20`, without assuming that the auxiliary complete intersection, residual scheme, residual line, or chosen Hilbert–Burch generators are invariant. It consequently separates the C2 channel, when present, from both the C3 and C5 channels and makes its fixed-channel zero scheme the reduced C2 orbit of length fifteen. The separately checked local stabilizer argument in Section 5 further proves `length(Z_q) <= 18` and the claimed complete scheme alternatives.

This review independently checked the liaison reduction, the arbitrary length-four scheme argument, the explicit Hilbert–Burch matrix, saturation and equality of ideals, the intrinsic equivariant syzygy, the character/polarization exclusion, and the additional local tangent-representation argument. Only universal representation identities were computed. No selected HM endpoint, source coefficient, matrix or rank was tested.

The review accepts the specified earlier endpoint interface as a dependency. It does not independently re-establish the endpoint map, the finite degeneracy result, or the earlier full axis-orbit classification.

## 1. The liaison reduction is legitimate

A finite plane scheme has a saturated height-two homogeneous ideal and is locally Cohen–Macaulay. The two selected quintics form a regular sequence because they have no common component. Complete-intersection residual duality therefore applies to any such scheme, not just a reduced or local-complete-intersection scheme. Residual involutivity recovers Z from R.

For a (5,5) complete intersection T, its dualizing sheaf is O_T(7). Residual duality gives I_Z/I_T = omega_R(-7); after twisting by five, the exact sequence is

    0 -> I_T(5) -> I_Z(5) -> omega_R(-2) -> 0.

The transpose of the connecting map is restriction of quadrics to R. Together with h0(I_T(5))=2, this yields exactly

    h0(I_Z(5)) = 2 + h1(I_R(2)).

The choice of the pencil or of the residual is not used equivariantly. Only the intrinsic space H0(I_Z(5)) is used as a G-module. In characteristic zero, complete reducibility, the absence of two-dimensional irreducibles, and the absence of invariant quintics exclude a stable two-dimensional subspace.

## 2. Failure of quadratic independence forces a residual line

Choose a projective line avoiding the entire finite support of R. On its affine complement the homogeneous quadratic restriction map identifies with the map from affine polynomials of total degree at most two to the length-four algebra B.

If R is not contained scheme-theoretically in a projective line, the images of 1,x,y are independent, so dim V1=3. If V2=V1, both coordinate generators preserve V1. Since 1 belongs to V1, the entire algebra generated by x,y lies in V1. This contradicts dim B=4. Thus V2=B and quadrics impose independent conditions, proving the stated contrapositive for nonreduced as well as reduced schemes.

A length-four subscheme of P1 is an effective Cartier divisor of degree four. Consequently its plane saturated ideal is (l,g), with degrees one and four and g nonzero modulo l. The rank of quadratic restriction is three: a nonzero section of O_P1(2) cannot vanish on a degree-four effective divisor. Therefore h1(I_R(2))=1 and h0(I_Z(5))=3.

## 3. The explicit matrix, ideal equality, and minimal resolution check

With F=la+gb, H=lc+gd and D=ad-bc, direct expansion verifies

    lD=dF-bH,     gD=aH-cF.

For the displayed matrix

    M = [ -d  -c ]
        [  b   a ]
        [  l  -g ],

the row-deletion minors in row order are (-F,H,-D). Its signed minors are therefore (-F,-H,-D). Both columns annihilate the ordered generator row (F,H,D):

    -dF+bH+lD=0,     -cF+aH-gD=0.

The proof's statement that its maximal minors are F,H,D up to signs is exactly correct.

The identities put J=(F,H,D) inside (F,H):(l,g)=I_Z. Because J contains the regular sequence F,H and is contained in the proper height-two ideal I_Z, its height is exactly two. Hilbert–Burch applies and gives

    0 -> S(-6) + S(-9) -> S(-5)^3 -> J -> 0.

No entry of either column can be a nonzero constant: their degrees are respectively one and four. Thus the resolution is minimal, including in degenerate cases where individual entries vanish.

The quotient S/J is Cohen–Macaulay of dimension one, so H_m^0(S/J)=0 and J is saturated. The Hilbert numerator of S/J is

    N(t)=1-3t^5+t^6+t^9.

Its multiplicity is N''(1)/2=(-60+30+72)/2=21. The containment J in I_Z gives an inclusion of the finite scheme Z into the scheme of J. Both have length twenty-one, so the inclusion is equality; saturation then gives equality of the homogeneous ideals. This verifies the resolution without relying on an unstated mapping-cone cancellation.

## 4. The invariant syzygy is intrinsic, not a symmetry of the residual

The minimal resolution gives precisely one degree-six relation among the three degree-five generators. Equivalently,

    ker((I_Z)_5 tensor S_1 -> S_6)

is one-dimensional. This description depends only on Z and polynomial multiplication, not on any chosen basis or residual construction. Since Z is G-invariant, the kernel is a G-subrepresentation. Every one-dimensional representation of A5 is trivial, so its nonzero elements are invariant.

The inclusion (I_Z)_5 into S_5 is equivariant and injective. The resulting invariant syzygy remains nonzero in S_5 tensor S_1. There is no need for an invariant Hilbert–Burch matrix, invariant line l, or invariant pencil (F,H).

## 5. Independent universal character and multiplication check

The rank-three determinant-one eigenvalues are (1,u,u^{-1}), hence the symmetric-power character recurrence is

    h_n = c h_{n-1} - c h_{n-2} + h_{n-3},
    h_0=1, h_1=c, h_2=c^2-c,

with c the rotation character. An independent exact polynomial calculation modulo phi^2-phi-1 gives

    chi(S_5) = (21,-3,0,1,1),
    chi(S_6) = (28,4,1,phi,1-phi).

For class sizes (1,15,20,12,12), the resulting invariant dimensions are

    dim(S_5)^G = 0,
    dim(S_5 tensor S_1)^G = 2,
    dim(S_6)^G = 2.

These arithmetic identities by themselves do not imply the needed injectivity. The proof correctly supplies the missing map-level fact: polarization

    p(f) = (1/6) sum_i (partial f / partial x_i) tensor x_i

is basis-independent and GL(A)-equivariant; multiplication composed with p is the identity by Euler's formula. Thus multiplication remains surjective on G-invariants. Since those invariant spaces both have dimension two, it is injective there as well. This contradicts the invariant linear syzygy from the previous step.

## 6. The added tangent-representation lemma also passes

At a C_n axis, n=3 or 5, the projective stabilizer is the order-2n normalizer D_n. Write L for the axis line. The identity

    T_p P(A) = L* tensor (A/L)

is intrinsic and equivariant. A generator of C_n acts trivially on L and has distinct primitive inverse eigencharacters on A/L. Therefore any invariant one-dimensional subspace would be one of those two cyclic eigenspaces. Every normalizer element outside C_n conjugates the generator to its inverse and interchanges the eigenspaces. No line is invariant under the full normalizer. Twisting by L* changes only the scalar on the exchanging element, not this argument. Thus the tangent and cotangent representations are irreducible.

For any invariant local subscheme at p, its ideal is preserved by the point stabilizer. The residue-field quotient and the natural map from ambient cotangent to local cotangent are consequently equivariant. A local algebra B of length two has maximal ideal n of dimension one; nilpotence forces n^2=0. It would produce a one-dimensional quotient of the irreducible two-dimensional ambient cotangent representation, which cannot exist.

The same reasoning shows that an invariant local quotient of length three has cotangent dimension two. Its cotangent space is nonzero by Nakayama, so irreducibility excludes dimension one. Since dim n=2, this forces n^2=0. Hence the defining ideal contains the square of the maximal ideal of the smooth surface. That square has colength three, so equality follows. The unique resulting local scheme is the full first infinitesimal neighborhood, with ideal m_p^2. This reasoning is valid for arbitrary nonreduced local subschemes and needs no local complete-intersection assumption.

Together with the twenty-bound and the earlier orbit classification, it gives precisely

    m5 in {0,1,3}, m3 in {0,1}, m2 in {0,1},
    6m5+10m3+15m2 <= 20.

The only nonzero totals are 6,10,15,16,18. Therefore eighteen is the proved universal upper bound. Total eighteen has m5=3 and the other multiplicities zero, so it is exactly the union of the six first infinitesimal neighborhoods of the C5 axes. Every other listed nonzero case is reduced. The arithmetic listing is exhaustive as a list of necessary possibilities; the proof correctly does not claim that each case occurs.

## 7. Final consequences and limitations

Both parts of the strengthened proof pass. The C3 fixed-channel scheme is reduced and consists either of its ten axes alone or their reduced union with the six C5 axes. If a C2 channel exists, its scheme is reduced of length fifteen, and its channel differs from both other channels. The C5 channel has exactly the three stated alternatives: six reduced axes, the reduced sixteen-point C3/C5 union, or six first infinitesimal neighborhoods of total length eighteen.

The result still does not decide whether C2 occurs, whether C3 and C5 channels coincide, whether the C5 infinitesimal case occurs, the infinitesimal structure of the full rank-drop scheme, the fourth Bockstein, prescribed-source membership, higher lifting, or original repair. No unsupported conclusion about any of these questions is used in the proof.
