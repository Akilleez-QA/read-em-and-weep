# Structural exclusion of primary equations through degree four

Status: independently mathematically reviewed (PASS); see REVIEW.md. Not formally checked. Work over C. No selected HM/source coefficients, evaluation matrix, rank test, or large computation is used.

## 1. Statement and dependencies

Let S be the actual primary surface in P4, and let R=C[x0,...,x4], I=I(S) its saturated homogeneous ideal. Then

H0(P4,I_S(d))=I_d=0 for 0<=d<=4.

The required inputs are:

- PR573, 6c2dbd48edea0577acf063a60e1edbbbe92ced5c: equality of the actual primary scheme with the reduced integral BHM surface S of degree 15, including ideal sheaves.
- PR590, eb236dac3af8adcbc7f2f660b55e9d07904858c6: actual normalizer invariance of S. Only its finite Heisenberg subgroup H5 is needed here. The center acts on ambient degree-one forms by a primitive fifth-root character epsilon (either dual convention is allowed).
- The standard presentation of H5 gives generators sigma,tau,z, with z central of order five and sigma tau=z tau sigma, up to replacing z by its inverse.

The local non-Cohen–Macaulay node input from PR574, 3c92fdc469f6870520e7a9d905b5ab9199a5bf37, gives an additional independent contradiction in the quartic step, but is not needed for the representation-theoretic contradiction at its end. We verify that version too. In particular we never assume S is locally Cohen–Macaulay to initiate a link.

The proof has two parts: a degree bound excludes equations of degrees at most three; the residual degree-one case for two quartics has an explicit Hilbert–Burch resolution incompatible with the Heisenberg action (and independently with the actual nodes).

## 2. Primitive central weight forces dimensions divisible by five

For 1<=d<=4, I_d is an H5-subrepresentation of R_d, and z acts by epsilon^d, still primitive. Here is a direct dimension argument requiring no character table. Decompose I_d into tau-eigenspaces, possible because tau has finite order over C. The relation sigma tau=z tau sigma makes sigma carry a tau-eigenspace isomorphically to the eigenspace whose eigenvalue differs by multiplication by epsilon^d or its inverse. Every resulting eigenvalue cycle has length five, and its five eigenspaces have equal dimension. Therefore

5 divides dim I_d.                                           (1)

In particular a nonzero I_d has at least two linearly independent forms.

## 3. Minimal degree and the cubic exclusion

Suppose some I_d with 1<=d<=4 is nonzero, and let d be the least such degree. Every nonzero homogeneous f in I_d is irreducible. Indeed, a factorization into positive-degree homogeneous factors, together with primality of I, would put a smaller-degree factor in I. Polynomial factorizations of a homogeneous polynomial can be taken homogeneous.

Choose independent f,g in I_d by (1). They are nonassociate irreducibles in the UFD R, hence coprime. Thus K=(f,g) has height two and is a regular sequence: R is a domain and g is a nonzerodivisor modulo the prime ideal (f). Consequently R/K is Cohen–Macaulay of dimension three, is unmixed, and has degree d^2.

The height-two prime I contains K and is therefore a minimal prime of K. The associativity formula for multiplicity gives

d^2 = sum over P in Min(R/K) of length((R/K)_P) deg(R/P).

The summand at I is at least deg(R/I)=15. Thus d^2>=15. This excludes d=1,2,3. The only remaining case is d=4.

## 4. Two quartics have exactly a reduced residual plane

Assume d=4 and retain K=(f,g). Its degree is 16. The multiplicity contribution of I equals 15m for the positive integer m=length((R/K)_I). Hence m=1. The remaining multiplicity is exactly one, so there is exactly one other minimal prime P, with

length((R/K)_P)=1,     deg(R/P)=1.                         (2)

There cannot be any embedded associated primes: R/K is the quotient of the regular ring R by a regular sequence, hence all its associated primes have height two. Each primary component is therefore isolated. A P-primary ideal Q with Q_P=P_P equals P, since a primary ideal is the contraction of its localization at its radical. Applying this both at I and P, using their local lengths one, proves the scheme-theoretic equality

K=I intersect P.                                           (3)

This argument does not merely identify a degree-one cycle: it rules out nonreduced components, embedded components, and any irrelevant-ideal component before using residual geometry.

For completeness a homogeneous prime P of dimension-three quotient and degree one is the ideal of a linear plane. Choose linear Noether-normalization parameters y0,y1,y2 in A=R/P. Over C, the resulting inclusion B=C[y0,y1,y2] into A is finite, and its fraction-field degree is deg(A)=1. The domain A is thus integral over B inside Frac(B); since the polynomial ring B is integrally closed, A=B. Hence A_1 has dimension three and P is generated by two independent linear forms. Write

P=(x,y),

where x,y are independent linear forms in R.

In particular the precise colon-link statements hold without any unverified liaison hypothesis:

K:I=P,      K:P=I.                                         (4)

Indeed distinct height-two primes I,P are incomparable, so P:I=P and I:P=I; now apply colon to (3). Equations (3)–(4) prove both residual reducedness and link symmetry directly. Also neither x nor y belongs to I, since there are no linear equations for S.

## 5. Explicit Hilbert–Burch link and its grade verification

Since f,g belong to P=(x,y), write

f=ax+by,       g=cx+dy,

with a,b,c,d homogeneous cubics. Put Delta=ad-bc, a sextic, and L=(f,g,Delta). The identities

x Delta=d f-b g,       y Delta=a g-c f

show Delta belongs to I: for example x Delta belongs to I and x does not, while I is prime. Thus

K subset L subset I.

These inclusions force ht(L)=2: the left inclusion gives ht(L)>=2, and the right inclusion gives ht(L)<=2. Since R is Cohen–Macaulay, grade(L)=2. Consider the matrix

             [ a   b ]
M =          [ c   d ] .
             [ -y  x ]

Its signed maximal minors are (g,-f,Delta). The Hilbert–Burch theorem, in its converse form for maximal minors of grade at least two, gives the exact complex

0 -> R(-7)^2 -> R(-4)^2 direct-sum R(-6) -> R -> R/L -> 0.    (5)

The first map is M and the next is the signed-minor row (with the displayed order). All entries have the required degrees: cubics in the first two rows and linear forms in the last row. No genericity of a,b,c,d is required; the verified grade-two condition is exactly the needed hypothesis. Thus R/L is Cohen–Macaulay, dimension three, and unmixed.

Its Hilbert series is

(1-2t^4-t^6+2t^7)/(1-t)^5.

The numerator has a zero of order two at t=1 and second derivative 30 there, so deg(R/L)=15. Since L subset I and deg(R/I)=15, multiplicity associativity now implies I is the only minimal prime of L and length((R/L)_I)=1. Unmixedness and contraction from this generic localization give

L=I.                                                       (6)

This is a proof of the relevant liaison-perfection assertion, rather than an application that might inadvertently assume local Cohen–Macaulayness of S.

There are now two separate contradictions:

1. Equations (5)–(6) show dim I_4=2, because its only generators of degree four are the independent f,g and the third generator has degree six. This contradicts divisibility by five in (1).
2. Equations (5)–(6) make R/I Cohen–Macaulay and therefore S locally Cohen–Macaulay. At each of the actual thirty nodes, PR574 identifies the completed local ring as

   C[[u1,u2,v1,v2]]/(u_i v_j:1<=i,j<=2).

   Its exact normalization sequence 0 -> A -> C[[u1,u2]] direct-sum C[[v1,v2]] -> C -> 0 gives depth A=1 and dimension A=2. Completion preserves depth and dimension of the local ring, contradicting local Cohen–Macaulayness.

Either contradiction excludes the remaining quartic case. Degree zero is immediate because S is nonempty. This proves the theorem.

## 6. Consequence for ambient restriction, and the degree-five boundary

The independently reviewed lower-conductor theorem at immutable commit 80f302ddd7e0dd19f1c54b725f56e5f81bb47c69, research/hm/lower-conductor-ranks/PROOF.md, gives

h0(O_S(d))=(1,5,15,35,70,126) for d=0,1,2,3,4,5.

Combine that calculation with the injectivity proved here, rather than inferring injectivity from those dimensions. For every 0<=d<=4 the actual ambient restriction

H0(P4,O(d)) -> H0(S,O_S(d))

is an isomorphism. Equivalently H1(P4,I_S(d))=0 in those degrees, by the ideal-sheaf sequence and H1(P4,O(d))=0.

No degree-five conclusion follows: central weight is then trivial, so the primitive-weight divisibility argument fails, and equality of the two degree-five section dimensions alone still does not prove injectivity or surjectivity. No higher ideal syzygies, full projective normality, selected source membership, primitive-twist-four full-adjoint vanishing, lifting, or algebraicity conclusion is claimed.
