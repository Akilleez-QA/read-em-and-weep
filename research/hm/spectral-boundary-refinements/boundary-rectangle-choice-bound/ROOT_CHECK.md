# Independent mathematical verification

If the descended vector-valued quadratic beta has a nonzero diagonal zero e, its lift v together with h yields an L-valued binary quadratic f(w,u,w). Algebraic closure forces a nonzero zero w. Since u is transverse to H, this contradicts repeated-input injection. Thus beta has no projective diagonal zero.

The elementary maximal-rank replacement for Bertini is valid: for a maximal-rank scalar member q0 of rank r<3, take a nondegenerate r-plane W and any nonzero radical vector k. The (r+1)-minor of q0+t q1 restricted to W+ Ck is identically zero for every q1. Its coefficient of t is det(q0|W) q1(k,k). Hence every scalar member vanishes on (k,k), contradicting the diagonal-zero exclusion. This includes r=0 with empty determinant equal to one. A nondegenerate scalar q therefore exists without a generic smoothness theorem.

The inverse-image construction identifies rectangles exactly with beta-orthogonal line/plane decompositions. These correspond exactly to common eigenlines of the q-selfadjoint family. Any common isotropic eigenline would be a beta diagonal zero. Two distinct lines with identical eigenvalue functionals create a two-dimensional common eigenspace containing an isotropic vector, another contradiction. Distinct tuples force orthogonality, giving at most three independent lines. Two nonisotropic orthogonal lines have a one-dimensional invariant orthogonal complement, so exactly two cannot occur. In the three-line case, a linear dependence among the three beta-diagonal values gives a nonzero diagonal zero using complex square roots. The three target values must therefore be independent.

The count is 0, 1, or 3 at the level of points; neither realizability of every count by a global f nor scheme reducedness follows.
