# Independent audit: a universal three-candidate spectral model

## Scope

This supplements PROOF.md. All statements below are abstract tensor geometry over C and its local Artin algebras. No assertion identifies a physical instance or assigns numerical obstruction ideals. The construction works with zero, one, or three actual boundary branches; its three spectral candidates need not be actual branches.

## 1. A simple-spectrum scalar member always exists

Let E have dimension three, let beta:Sym^2 E -> K satisfy beta(v,v) != 0 for every v != 0, and let q=lambda_0 beta be a nondegenerate scalar member, as supplied by the existing maximal-rank lemma. Write W for the scalar system of beta. Its restriction to the smooth conic Q={q=0} is basepoint-free: at every point some scalar coordinate of beta(v,v) is nonzero.

Identify Q with P^1. The restriction of a plane quadric is a section of O_{P^1}(4). Choose a nonzero restricted section s_0. Its zero set is finite and nonempty. Basepoint-freeness and the fact that a finite union of proper linear subspaces does not cover a complex vector space provide a restricted section s_1 nonzero at every zero of s_0. Thus s_0,s_1 have no common zero. They determine a degree-four nonconstant morphism P^1 -> P^1. In characteristic zero this morphism is separable, and its ramification points and branch values are finite. Hence a general section s_0-t s_1 has four distinct zeros. Lift this section to a scalar member q_1 in W. The plane conics q=0 and q_1=0 meet transversely at those four points.

Set T=q^{-1}q_1, so q(Tv,w)=q_1(v,w). This operator is q-selfadjoint. If an eigenvalue mu were repeated, then:

- If its eigenspace has dimension at least two, that eigenspace contains a nonzero q-isotropic vector over C.
- Otherwise there is a Jordan chain (T-mu)w=v, (T-mu)v=0, with v != 0. Selfadjointness gives q(v,v)=q((T-mu)w,v)=q(w,(T-mu)v)=0.

In either case an isotropic eigenvector v satisfies q(v,v)=q_1(v,v)=0 and q_1(v,-)=mu q(v,-). The gradients of the two quadratic equations are proportional there, contradicting transversality. Thus T has three distinct eigenvalues.

This proof uses only elementary separability of a morphism of smooth curves, rather than a general Bertini theorem. It does not require any boundary branch to exist.

## 2. Lifting over a local Artin base

Let A be a local Artin C-algebra with residue C, E_A a free rank-three module, K_A a finite free module, and beta_A:Sym^2 E_A -> K_A reducing to beta. Lift lambda_0 and lambda_1 to dual linear forms and set q_A=lambda_0 beta_A, q_{1,A}=lambda_1 beta_A. The form q_A is perfect because its residue is. Define T_A=q_A^{-1}q_{1,A}.

The characteristic polynomial of T_A has three distinct roots modulo the maximal ideal. Hensel lifting gives roots rho_1,rho_2,rho_3 in A with every rho_i-rho_j a unit. The Lagrange projectors

P_i = product_{j != i} (T_A-rho_j)/(rho_i-rho_j)

give a direct sum E_A=L_1 direct-sum L_2 direct-sum L_3 into free rank-one eigenspaces. Distinct eigenspaces are q_A-orthogonal because their eigenvalue differences are units. Each q_A|L_i is perfect: in an eigenbasis the Gram matrix is diagonal and its determinant is a unit. Choose generators e_i of L_i.

The relative eigenline scheme of T_A is exactly the disjoint union of these three sections, including nilpotent test schemes. One direct proof is local on a test scheme: a stable line has a generator with some eigenbasis coordinate a unit; stability then gives its eigenvalue rho_i, and (rho_j-rho_i)x_j=0 forces all other coordinates zero. This also shows that no extra infinitesimal eigenlines occur.

## 3. Exact cross-term ideals

For each i define I_i in A to be the ideal generated by all coordinates of beta_A(e_i,e_j) in K_A for j != i. This ideal does not depend on rescaling generators or changing the basis of K_A. Inserting each spectral section into the equations beta_A(a,a^{perp_q})=0 gives exactly I_i, so the full orthogonal-splitting scheme is

coproduct_{i=1}^3 Spec(A/I_i).

This is an equality of schemes, not just equality of supports or tangent equations. Indeed every splitting must be q_A-orthogonal, and its line must be stable for T_A because q_{1,A} is a scalar member of beta_A. Conversely an eigensection satisfying these cross-term equations has complementary plane L_j direct-sum L_k and satisfies all splitting equations. Both implications are identities over arbitrary A-algebras.

If the residue eigenline is not an actual common eigenline for all scalar operators, some beta_A(e_i,e_j) coordinate is nonzero modulo the maximal ideal, hence is a unit, and I_i=A. Therefore a one-branch fiber has exactly one proper candidate ideal and two unit candidate ideals. A zero-branch fiber has three unit ideals. In the three-branch case all three ideals are proper. No claim that the surviving ideals vanish follows.

## 4. Application to the polar Artin ring

For A=O_{D,p}, all bundles and tensors used above exist intrinsically after choosing local trivializations. The polar matrix has a unit 2x2 minor at p; its defining 3x3 minors put it into rank-two normal form over A itself. The line L generated by f(h,u,h) is primitive in the polar image and J_A. Set H=P^{-1}(L), E_A=H/Ah, and K_A=J_A/L. These are locally free, H has rank four, E_A rank three, and beta_A is induced by f(-,u,-) modulo L. The same inverse-image argument as PROOF.md, Section 3, now over A rather than C, identifies B_A with the splitting functor in Section 3 above. Thus the cross-term presentation applies to the actual determinant-defined boundary, not a replacement scheme.

To justify the primitive line and ranks: f(h,u,h) and f(h,u,u) remain independent modulo the maximal ideal, so they form a basis of the rank-two polar image over A. The inverse image of the first summand has rank four. The tautological h is primitive in H and the tautological u is complementary to H. Descent of beta_A follows from P(H) subset L. The residue anisotropy is sufficient for the spectral construction; no unsupported notion of anisotropy over a nonreduced ring is needed.

## 5. Exact lifting obstruction for a chosen candidate

Fix a proper I=I_i. Let R' -> R be a surjection of local Artin C-algebras with square-zero kernel M. Suppose a polar lift alpha':A -> R' reduces to alpha:A -> R, and alpha(I)=0, so the given point in R lies on this boundary branch. There is a canonical A/I-linear map

ob(alpha'): I/I^2 -> M, [g] |-> alpha'(g),

where A/I acts on M through alpha and the induced R-action. It is well-defined because alpha'(I) is contained in M and M^2=0. A lift of the boundary point over this prescribed polar lift exists if and only if this map is zero. When it exists it is unique, since A -> A/I is surjective. Equivalently, every coordinate alpha'(beta_A(e_i,e_j)) must vanish in M. Those coordinate values satisfy all relations among the generators; they are not automatically independent obstruction coordinates.

For a small extension satisfying m_{R'}M=0, this map factors through I/m_A I and is C-linear. For a first-order polar tangent vector d:m_A/m_A^2 -> C, the obstruction is restriction to I, with values in C epsilon. In particular equations in m_A^2 are invisible to first-order tangent tests but may obstruct higher-order lifts. No surjectivity of a tangent-to-obstruction map, flatness, or vanishing of the full ideal follows from tangent rigidity.

For a fixed Artin target R the branch lifts exactly when alpha(I)=0, equivalently alpha factors through A/I. This is the complete obstruction statement, with no linear approximation.

## Audit verdict

The simple-spectrum proposal is valid and yields a universal, exact three-candidate model. Its use eliminates the need for an implicit-function construction merely to isolate the unique surviving branch, while an independent implicit construction remains a useful check. Cross-term ideals are presentation-dependent in generators but intrinsic as the kernels of the three candidate branch quotient maps, up to the labeling of the spectral sections.
