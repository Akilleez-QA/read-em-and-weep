# Scheme-theoretic rigidity of boundary rectangles over polar pairs

## Theorem and hypotheses

Work over C. Let V have dimension five, J be finite-dimensional, and f:V^3→J be trilinear, outer symmetric, cyclic zero, and globally repeated-input injective: for every nonzero v the map A_v:V/Cv→J, z↦f(v,v,z), is injective. Give

R ⊂ P(V)×Gr(2,V)×Gr(3,V)

the scheme structure defined by the 2×2 minors of the rectangular map X⊗u⊗Y→J. Let B⊂R be the closed subscheme cut out by the determinant of addition X⊕Y→V. Give

D ⊂ P(V)×P(V)

the scheme structure defined by all 3×3 minors of the polar map f(h,u,-). Line factors in these bundle maps are retained implicitly; all calculations below can be made after local trivializations and are invariant under changing them.

Then:

1. The intersection line defines an honest morphism π:B→D, on the full schemes including nilpotents.
2. For every complex point p=(Ch,Cu) of D, the scheme-theoretic fiber B_p is reduced, with length 0, 1, or 3.
3. The morphism π is unramified. It is finite under the established finiteness theorem for R (or, alternatively, proper plus the fiber result).
4. Under the established finite-support theorem for D, write A_p=O_{D,p}. The part of B above p is a disjoint union of n_p closed subschemes Spec(A_p/I_{p,j}), where n_p∈{0,1,3}. Consequently

length_C B ≤ 3 length_C D.

This does not assert that B, D, or R is reduced; that π is flat or étale; or that the full rectangular scheme R is unramified over any chosen parameter space.

## 1. Constant rank on the actual determinant boundary

At every closed point of B, the pointwise exclusions show that X∩Y is one-dimensional, u∉X+Y, and f(h,u,h)≠0 for a generator h of the intersection. Thus addition has rank four. The open sets on which a 4×4 minor is a unit cover B: their complement is a closed finite-type C-scheme with no closed points and hence is empty.

On such an open set elementary invertible row and column operations put the 5×5 addition matrix into block form diag(I_4,s). Its determinant is a unit times s. The defining equation of B therefore gives s=0 exactly, even in a nonreduced ring. It follows that addition has a line-bundle kernel and a rank-four image subbundle H_B⊂V⊗O_B, with line-bundle quotient.

Projection of the kernel to X is fiberwise nonzero and identifies it with a line subbundle h_B of X and V. Its projection into Y has opposite sign. In fact this is the scheme-theoretic intersection: an element of X∩Y gives the kernel element (x,−x). All these constructions commute with arbitrary base change because the matrix is locally diag(I_4,0). Thus h_B gives a morphism B→P(V), not merely a map on the reduction.

## 2. The rectangular line and polar minors vanish identically

The rectangular map has rank exactly one at every closed boundary point, since its image contains f(h,u,h)≠0. A unit 1×1 minor and the defining 2×2 minors put it locally in rank-one normal form. Its image is therefore a line subbundle M⊂J⊗O_B. The section f(h,u,h), after trivializing h_B and u, generates M: it is nonzero modulo every residue field and hence its coordinate in this line is locally a unit.

Outer symmetry and h_B⊂X,Y imply, as identities of bundle maps,

f(h_B,u,H_B) ⊂ M.

Indeed H_B is the image of X⊕Y, and either summand can be paired with h_B in the opposite outer slot. This is an exact module inclusion, not vanishing only on closed points.

Locally split V=H_B⊕O·t. The polar image is then generated by M and f(h,u,t). All 3×3 polar minors vanish identically. Hence the morphism (h_B,u):B→P(V)×P(V) factors through the specified determinantal scheme D. This proves claim 1.

The values f(h,u,h) and f(h,u,u) are independent at every closed point. Thus a polar 2×2 minor is locally a unit on B, and the polar map actually has constant rank two there. This observation is compatible with, but not a substitute for, the preceding verification of the 3×3 equations.

## 3. The fixed fiber, over arbitrary test schemes

Fix p=(Ch,Cu)∈D(C). It is an independent pair: on the diagonal f(h,h,-)=A_h has rank four. For an independent pair the established two-polar-value independence shows that rank f(h,u,-)≥2, so its rank is exactly two. Put

P=f(h,u,-), L=C f(h,u,h), H=P^{-1}(L), E=H/Ch, K=J/L.

Then dim H=4, dim E=3, and u∉H. The symmetric map f(-,u,-) descends modulo L to β:E×E→K. The earlier proof gives β(v,v)≠0 for every nonzero v, and a nondegenerate scalar member q=λ_0β.

We claim that B_p represents exactly the following functor on C-schemes S:

pairs of subbundles a,C⊂E⊗O_S, of ranks one and two, such that E⊗O_S=a⊕C and β(a,C)=0.

First let S→B_p. The intersection line is exactly the constant subbundle Ch⊗O_S, since its Grassmannian map is constant as a scheme map. The rectangular image line is exactly L⊗O_S because it is generated by the constant nonzero vector f(h,u,h). Section 2 implies P(H_B)⊂L⊗O_S, hence H_B⊂H⊗O_S. This inclusion of rank-four subbundles is an isomorphism: modulo every residue field it is an isomorphism, and its determinant is thus a unit locally. Therefore H_B=H⊗O_S, including its infinitesimal structure.

The quotients a=X/(Ch⊗O_S), C=Y/(Ch⊗O_S) are subbundles of E⊗O_S and their addition is an isomorphism. Quotienting the exact image condition f(X,u,Y)⊂L yields precisely β(a,C)=0.

Conversely, from such a splitting take X,Y to be the inverse images of a,C under H⊗O_S→E⊗O_S. They are subbundles of V⊗O_S of ranks two and three, with intersection Ch⊗O_S and sum H⊗O_S. Hence the addition determinant vanishes. Since P(H)⊂L, all mixed terms involving h are in L; the remaining condition β(a,C)=0 makes the whole rectangular map factor through L⊗O_S. Its 2×2 minors vanish, and its image equals L because f(h,u,h) generates it. This defines S→B whose image under π is the constant scheme map p. These constructions are inverse and compatible with all base changes. This proves the claimed scheme identification, not only a point bijection.

## 4. Exact identification with the common-eigenline zero scheme

Define q-selfadjoint operators T_λ by q(T_λv,w)=λβ(v,w), for λ∈K*. A basis of K* gives finitely many equations and suffices throughout. Let Z⊂P(E) be the common-eigenline scheme: the zero scheme of every composite

a → E⊗O_{P(E)} → (E⊗O_{P(E)})/a,

where the first arrow is T_λ restricted to the tautological line a. No commutativity of the operators is assumed.

Every closed point of Z is nonisotropic for q. Indeed T_λv=μ_λv implies λβ(v,v)=μ_λq(v,v); if q(v,v)=0 then β(v,v)=0, impossible. Consequently Z lies scheme-theoretically in the open set U of q-nonisotropic lines. To be explicit, Z∩(P(E)\U) has empty support, so it is the empty scheme; any map to Z factors through U even from a nonreduced test scheme.

For a splitting in Section 3, q(a,C)=0. Since q is nondegenerate, a^⊥ is a rank-two subbundle, and C→a^⊥ is an isomorphism by the fiberwise rank test. Thus C=a^⊥ exactly. The direct sum E=a⊕a^⊥ says that q restricted to a is invertible, so a lies in U. Conversely a line in U has this canonical complementary plane.

On U, the condition β(a,a^⊥)=0 is scheme-theoretically equivalent to T_λa⊂a for every λ. Indeed q induces a perfect pairing on a^⊥ and identifies the quotient E/a with a^⊥; under this identification the components of T_λa modulo a are precisely the linear functionals q(T_λa,-)|_{a^⊥}=λβ(a,a^⊥). This is equality of zero-scheme conditions over arbitrary rings. Therefore the splitting functor is represented by Z, and

B_p ≅ Z

as schemes.

## 5. Tangent rigidity, reducedness, and lengths

At a point a=Cv of Z, choose the representative of a projective tangent vector as w∈v^⊥. This is possible and unique modulo the normalization because q(v,v)≠0. A first-order eigenline deformation over C[ε]/(ε²) satisfies

T_λ(v+εw)=(μ_λ+εδμ_λ)(v+εw).

Its linearized equations are

(T_λ−μ_λ)w=δμ_λv.

Pairing with v using selfadjointness makes the left side zero, and q(v,v)≠0 forces δμ_λ=0 for every λ. If w≠0, v,w are independent simultaneous eigenvectors with the same joint eigenvalue tuple. On their two-dimensional span every vector z therefore obeys

λβ(z,z)=μ_λq(z,z).

A homogeneous quadratic over C on a two-dimensional vector space has a nonzero isotropic vector z. For that z all scalar coordinates of β(z,z) vanish, contradicting the established anisotropy of the vector-valued quadratic. Thus w=0 and T_aZ=0.

The closed points of Z are at most three: distinct common eigenlines cannot have the same eigenvalue tuple by the preceding two-dimensional argument; selfadjointness then makes distinct lines q-orthogonal. They are nonisotropic and hence linearly independent. If two exist, their sum is nondegenerate and its one-dimensional orthogonal complement is invariant under all operators, giving a third. Thus there are exactly 0,1,or3 points, as in the earlier set-theoretic proof.

Since Z is projective of finite type with finite support, it is finite. At each local Artin ring its maximal ideal m satisfies m/m²=0 by tangent vanishing. Nakayama gives m=0. Hence Z is reduced and its scheme length is 0,1,or3, proving claim 2. This is a relative assertion; it does not kill directions moving the polar pair.

## 6. Unramifiedness of the boundary-to-polar morphism

For any closed b∈B with p=π(b), the relative tangent space is the tangent space of B_p at b and is zero. Equivalently, Ω_{B/D}⊗κ(b)=0. The sheaf Ω_{B/D} is coherent. Nakayama and the fact that any nonempty closed support on a finite-type C-scheme has a closed point imply Ω_{B/D}=0. Since π is locally of finite type and residue extensions at geometric points are separable in characteristic zero, π is unramified. One may also use the usual finite-type criterion of zero relative tangent spaces with separable residue extensions.

The earlier theorem makes R, hence B, finite over C. Any morphism between finite C-schemes is finite. Alternatively, B is proper over C, D is separated over C, and π has finite fibers; therefore it is proper and quasi-finite, hence finite.

## 7. Local Artin structure and scheme-length bound

Use now the established finiteness of D. Fix p and set A=O_{D,p}, a local Artin C-algebra. Since π is finite, its preimage over Spec A is Spec C_p for a finite A-algebra C_p. If it is empty there is no factor. Otherwise the Artin algebra C_p decomposes as a finite product of local Artin C-algebras C_j, one for each closed point above p.

Claim 2 gives C_p/m_A C_p ≅ C^{n_p}, with n_p∈{1,3}. Consequently each local factor obeys C_j/m_A C_j≅C, and the residue class of 1 generates this quotient as an A/m_A-module. Apply Nakayama to the finite A-module C_j/A·1: it is zero. Thus the natural algebra map A→C_j is surjective, and C_j≅A/I_j for an ideal I_j⊂A.

It follows that

length_C C_p = Σ_j length_C(A/I_j) ≤ n_p length_C A ≤ 3 length_C A.

Summing over p proves length_C B≤3 length_C D. This gives a precise interpretation of relative rigidity: each local boundary branch is a closed subscheme of the corresponding local polar scheme. It may retain nonzero nilpotents from A or impose additional equations on A. Nothing here says I_j=0, that all branches have the same length, or that the map is flat. In particular reduced geometric fibers alone must not be upgraded to étaleness.

## Scope and provenance

This is abstract universal tensor geometry. No numerical instance is evaluated. The conclusions concern the determinant-defined boundary B, not an unproved reduced replacement or a different boundary scheme structure.
