# Independent mathematical verification

The vertical derivative has rank two by the previously proved fixed-polar tangent rigidity. Choosing two rows with invertible minor gives a polynomial Newton process over the Artin ring. Every inverse exists because its residue determinant is nonzero; errors double ideal order and nilpotence terminates. Uniqueness is proved by an invertible divided-difference matrix. Substitution into the remaining equations therefore gives the exact quotient ideal, independently of presentation, on all local Artin test rings.

The linear obstruction is the coefficient derivative modulo the image of the vertical Jacobian. Row elimination identifies it with the derivative of the exact residual vector. This establishes the tangent criterion only; residuals in m squared can obstruct later orders.

The one-branch elementary spectral lemma checks: a common nonisotropic line splits off a selfadjoint two-plane. If the cubic discriminant vanished identically, one of the quadratic factors would vanish identically. Polarization bounds its linear null image by one dimension, yielding either a common isotropic eigenline or a two-dimensional common eigenspace. Both contradict beta's absence of diagonal zeros.

The independent universal lemma also checks. Restrict the basepoint-free scalar system to the smooth conic, obtaining sections of O(4) on P1. Two sections without common zeros give a degree-four separable map; a nonbranch fiber has four simple points. A repeated selfadjoint eigenvalue yields an isotropic eigenvector, either in a multidimensional eigenspace or from a Jordan chain, contradicting that transverse intersection. This works even when no common eigenline exists.

Simple roots lift uniquely and polynomial projectors produce three exact spectral sections over the Artin base. Their relative eigenline scheme has no extra nilpotents because root differences are units on each chart. In the one-branch case only I1 is proper, and J12,J13 lie in m while J23 is the unit ideal. Thus I1 is generated exactly by the two cross vectors. Those vectors lie in the free codimension-two kernel of the metric and separating scalar functionals. The latter are independent because the separator has distinct eigenvalues.

For a prescribed polar lift across a square-zero extension, evaluating I gives the canonical map I/I squared to the square-zero kernel. Its vanishing is necessary and sufficient and the lift is unique. A small extension kills m times I, giving the stated smaller source. This proves criteria rather than actual source values, flatness, or existence over the full base.
