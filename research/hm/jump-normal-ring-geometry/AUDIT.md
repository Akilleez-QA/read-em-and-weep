T=\mathbf C[[a,b,c,t_4,\ldots,t_{24}]],\qquad d=ac-b^2,

and let

\[
R_1=T[[z]]/(z^2,az),

R_2=T[[z_1,z_2]]/

Write \(J_i\) for the ideal generated by the images of the \(z\)-variables.

## Verdict and necessary qualifications

All the proposed universal-algebra claims are correct with the following conventions made explicit:

1. The Fitting ideals \(\operatorname{Fitt}_0(J_2)=(d)\), \(\operatorname{Fitt}_1(J_2)=(a,b,c)\), and \(\operatorname{Fitt}_2(J_2)=T\) are computed **over \(T\)**. Over \(A=T/(d)\), they are \(0\), \((a,b,c)A\), and \(A\), respectively.
2. The isomorphism \(J_2\cong(a,b)\subset A\) is an **\(A\)-module isomorphism**. Multiplication of any two elements of \(J_2\) in \(R_2\) is zero; this is not the usual multiplication on the ideal \((a,b)\) inside the domain \(A\).
3. Rank one, reflexivity, and maximal Cohen–Macaulayness of \(J_2\) are assertions **over \(A\)**. Over \(T\), it is a torsion module of depth 23.
4. Dual numbers occur in the **fiber over the generic point of the divisor**. The localization of \(R_2\) at its embedded associated prime is a **one-dimensional** local ring, not that zero-dimensional fiber.
5. “Vertex” means the whole locus \(V(a,b,c)\), with the 21 passive formal coordinates retained. It has dimension 21, codimension 2 in \(\operatorname{Spec}A\), and codimension 3 in \(\operatorname{Spec}T\).

## 1. Split square-zero structure and reductions

Every formal series reduces uniquely to a constant term plus a term linear in the \(z\)-variables, modulo precisely the stated linear relations. Consequently

\[
R_i=T\ltimes J_i
\]

as rings, where the trivial-extension product is

\[
(t,m)(t',m')=(tt',tm'+t'm).
\]

In particular,

\[
J_1\cong T/(a),\qquad
J_2\cong\operatorname{coker}_T
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&c\end{pmatrix},\qquad J_i^2=0.
\]

The structural inclusion \(T\hookrightarrow R_i\) and the quotient \(R_i\twoheadrightarrow T\) split. Since \(T\) is reduced, the nilradical of \(R_i\) is exactly \(J_i\), and

\[
(R_i)_{\mathrm{red}}=T.
\]

Thus both spectra have the same underlying topological space as \(\operatorname{Spec}T\), and both rings have dimension 24.

## 2. The module \(J_2\) and its explicit ideal model

Let \(A=T/(d)\). The element \(d\) is irreducible: its degree-two initial form \(ac-b^2\) is an irreducible quadratic form, so a nontrivial factorization into nonunits in the formal power-series ring is impossible. Since \(T\) is a regular local UFD, \((d)\) is prime and \(A\) is a domain of dimension 23.

Define

\[
\phi:T^2\longrightarrow(a,b)A,\qquad (x,y)\longmapsto bx-ay.
\]

It is surjective, and the two columns \((a,b)\) and \((b,c)\) of the presentation matrix map to zero. To prove that they generate the full kernel, suppose

\[
bx-ay=dh.
\]

Modulo \(a\), this gives \(b(x+bh)=0\). As \(T/(a)\) is a domain, \(x=-bh+aq\) for some \(q\in T\). Substitution and cancellation of the nonzero element \(a\) give \(y=bq-ch\). Hence

\[
(x,y)=q(a,b)-h(b,c).
\]

Therefore

\[
J_2\xrightarrow{\sim}(a,b)A,
\qquad z_1\longmapsto b,\quad z_2\longmapsto-a.
\]

Because this is a nonzero torsion-free module over the domain \(A\),

\[
\operatorname{Ann}_T(J_2)=(d),\qquad
\operatorname{Supp}_T(J_2)=V(d).
\]

Likewise, \(\operatorname{Ann}_T(J_1)=(a)\) and \(\operatorname{Supp}_T(J_1)=V(a)\).

The same annihilation is visible directly from the matrix factorization

\[
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&c\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}c&-b\\-b&a\end{pmatrix}
=dI_2.
\]

## 3. Fitting ideals, fiber ranks, and the jump locus

The displayed two-generator, two-relation presentation gives

\[
\operatorname{Fitt}^T_0(J_2)=(d),\qquad
\operatorname{Fitt}^T_1(J_2)=(a,b,c),\qquad
\operatorname{Fitt}^T_2(J_2)=T.
\]

For \(J_1\), the corresponding ideals are \(\operatorname{Fitt}^T_0(J_1)=(a)\) and \(\operatorname{Fitt}^T_1(J_1)=T\).

For a prime \(\mathfrak p\subset T\), base change preserves the split square-zero description:

\[
R_2\otimes_T\kappa(\mathfrak p)
=\kappa(\mathfrak p)\ltimes
(J_2\otimes_T\kappa(\mathfrak p)).
\]

The rank of the presentation matrix over \(\kappa(\mathfrak p)\) is 2 off \(V(d)\), 1 on \(V(d)\setminus V(a,b,c)\), and 0 on \(V(a,b,c)\). Consequently:

| Location in \(\operatorname{Spec}T\) | Nilradical dimension in the fiber | Fiber algebra |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Outside \(V(d)\) | 0 | \(\kappa(\mathfrak p)\) |
| \(V(d)\setminus V(a,b,c)\) | 1 | \(\kappa(\mathfrak p)[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\) |
| \(V(a,b,c)\) | 2 | \(\kappa(\mathfrak p)[\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2]/(\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2)^2\) |

These fiber algebras have total vector-space dimensions 1, 2, and 3, respectively. The dimension 1 or 2 in the claim refers to the **nilradical**, not the whole algebra.

For \(R_1\), the fibers are reduced fields outside \(V(a)\) and dual numbers on all of \(V(a)\).

## 4. Rank one, MCM, reflexive, and precisely where nonfree

The matrix over \(T\) has nonzero determinant, and hence defines an injective map \(T^2\to T^2\). Thus

\[
0\longrightarrow T^2\longrightarrow T^2\longrightarrow J_2\longrightarrow0
\]

is a free resolution. Its length is exactly one because \(J_2\ne0\) is annihilated by the nonzero element \(d\), and so cannot be a free \(T\)-module. Auslander–Buchsbaum gives

\[
\operatorname{depth}_T(J_2)=24-1=23.
\]

As \(d\) annihilates \(J_2\), this is also its depth over \(A\). Hence \(J_2\) is maximal Cohen–Macaulay over \(A\). The ideal model shows that its rank over \(A\) is one.

The hypersurface \(A\) is Cohen–Macaulay, and its singular locus is

\[
V(\partial_a d,\partial_b d,\partial_c d)
=V(c,-2b,a)=V(a,b,c),
\]

which has codimension 2 in \(\operatorname{Spec}A\). Thus \(A\) is regular in codimension one and satisfies \(S_2\), so it is normal. The module \(J_2\) is torsion-free and satisfies \(S_2\); equivalently here, its localized free resolution proves MCM at every point of its support. It follows that \(J_2\) is reflexive over \(A\).

For completeness, the nonfree locus can be checked without invoking reflexivity:

- Where \(a\) or \(b\) is invertible, \((a,b)A\) is the unit ideal.
- Where \(c\) is invertible, the relation \(a=b^2/c\) gives \((a,b)A=(b)A\), a free rank-one module because \(A\) is a domain.
- At any prime containing \((a,b,c)\), all entries of the presentation matrix vanish in the residue field. The module therefore requires two generators there, whereas a free rank-one module requires one.

Thus \(J_2\) is nonfree **exactly** on \(V(a,b,c)\).

## 5. Depth and Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity of the rings

The finite local extension \(T\to R_i\) has the same residue field, and the extension of the maximal ideal of \(T\) has radical equal to the maximal ideal of \(R_i\). Consequently depth can be computed over \(T\). The direct-sum decompositions give

\[
\operatorname{depth}(R_i)
=\min\{\operatorname{depth}(T),\operatorname{depth}_T(J_i)\}
=\min\{24,23\}=23.
\]

Therefore both rings have dimension 24 and depth 23; neither is Cohen–Macaulay.

Let \(\mathfrak m\) be the maximal ideal of \(T\), and let \(\mathfrak n_i=\mathfrak m\ltimes J_i\). For \(k\ge1\),

\[
\mathfrak n_i^k=\mathfrak m^k\oplus\mathfrak m^{k-1}J_i.
\]

It follows that

\[
\ell(R_i/\mathfrak n_i^{k+1})
=\ell(T/\mathfrak m^{k+1})+\ell(J_i/\mathfrak m^kJ_i).
\]

The first summand has Hilbert polynomial degree 24 and leading coefficient \(1/24!\). The second has degree 23, since \(\dim_T J_i=23\). Thus the Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity at the closed point is

\[
e(R_i)=1.
\]

This does not imply regularity: the usual multiplicity-one regularity conclusions require additional hypotheses such as unmixedness. Here there is an embedded associated prime. Indeed, the embedding dimensions are 25 for \(R_1\) and 26 for \(R_2\), since all displayed defining relations are quadratic.

More generally, at a prime lying over \(\mathfrak p\subset T\), the local ring is regular when \(\mathfrak p\) is outside the nilradical support. On the support its dimension is \(\operatorname{ht}_T\mathfrak p\) and its depth is \(\operatorname{ht}_T\mathfrak p-1\). Thus the non-Cohen–Macaulay locus is exactly the divisor supporting the nilradical; the two-generator jump at the vertex does not create a further depth drop.

## 6. Associated primes and primary decompositions

As \(T\)-modules,

\[
\operatorname{Ass}_T(T\oplus J_1)=\{(0),(a)\},\qquad
\operatorname{Ass}_T(T\oplus J_2)=\{(0),(d)\}.
\]

For \(J_2\), the second assertion follows because it is nonzero and torsion-free over the domain \(A\): every nonzero element has annihilator \((d)\) over \(T\). Under the bijection of spectra induced by the nilpotent quotient, this yields

\[
\operatorname{Ass}_{R_1}(R_1)=\{J_1,(a,J_1)\},
\]

\[
\operatorname{Ass}_{R_2}(R_2)=\{J_2,(d,J_2)\}.
\]

The first prime in each set is the unique minimal prime; the second is embedded and has height one. There is **no additional associated prime at the vertex** of the quadratic divisor.

Here is an independent primary-decomposition proof. In the ambient ring \(S_i=T[[z\text{-variables}]]\), write \(I_i\) for the displayed defining ideal, \(P_i=(z\text{-variables})\), and set \(f_1=a\), \(f_2=d\). Then

\[
I_i=P_i\cap Q_i,\qquad Q_i=I_i+(f_i).
\]

Indeed, in \(R_i=T\oplus J_i\), annihilation \(f_iJ_i=0\) gives

\[
f_iR_i=f_iT\oplus0,
\]

whose intersection with \(J_i=0\oplus J_i\) is zero. This is exactly the asserted intersection identity upstairs.

The ideal \(P_i\) is prime. Moreover,

\[
S_i/Q_i\cong(T/(f_i))\ltimes J_i.
\]

In each case \(T/(f_i)\) is a domain and \(J_i\) is torsion-free over it. In a trivial extension \(B\ltimes M\) with these properties, an element \((u,m)\) with \(u\ne0\) is a nonzerodivisor: if \((u,m)(v,n)=0\), then \(uv=0\) forces \(v=0\), and then \(un=0\) forces \(n=0\). Every element with \(u=0\) is square-zero. Hence every zero divisor is nilpotent, proving that zero is primary in \(B\ltimes M\). Therefore \(Q_i\) is primary with radical

\[
\sqrt{Q_i}=(f_i,z\text{-variables}).
\]

Both decompositions are irredundant. Explicitly,

\[
(z^2,az)=(z)\cap(a,z^2),
\]

and

\[
I_2=(z_1,z_2)\cap
(d,z_1^2,z_1z_2,z_2^2,az_1+bz_2,bz_1+cz_2).
\]

Embedded primary components need not be unique; these are valid specific choices.

## 7. Generic divisor fiber versus embedded-prime localization

This distinction is essential. Put \(f=a\) for \(R_1\) or \(f=d\) for \(R_2\), let

\[
D=T_{(f)},\qquad K=\operatorname{Frac}(T/(f)),
\]

and let \(\mathfrak q=(f,J_i)\subset R_i\). Since \(T\) is regular and \((f)\) is a height-one prime, \(D\) is a DVR with residue field \(K\). In both examples \((J_i)_{(f)}\cong K\). Consequently

\[
(R_i)_{\mathfrak q}\cong D\ltimes K
\cong D[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2,f\epsilon).
\]

This ring has dimension 1, maximal ideal \((f,\epsilon)\), and depth 0: its nonzero element \(\epsilon\) is annihilated by the whole maximal ideal. Its reduction is the DVR \(D\), not the field \(K\).

By contrast, taking the fiber of the finite morphism \(\operatorname{Spec}R_i\to\operatorname{Spec}T\) over the generic point of \(V(f)\), equivalently quotienting this localization by \(f\), gives

\[
R_i\otimes_T K
\cong K[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2).
\]

These are zero-dimensional dual numbers of length 2. Thus “generically dual numbers along the divisor” is correct only when explicitly understood as a statement about this fiber.

If a completed one-dimensional transverse local model is desired, a choice of coefficient field identifies the completion noncanonically with

\[
K[[u,\epsilon]]/(\epsilon^2,u\epsilon).
\]

At the unique **minimal** prime \(J_i\), on the other hand, the torsion nilradical disappears entirely and

\[
(R_i)_{J_i}\cong\operatorname{Frac}(T).
\]

Thus both total spaces are generically reduced even though they have nonzero nilradical supported on a divisor.

## Scope of the conclusion

The computations above prove the stated properties **if** the proposed square-zero presentations are the relevant actual rings. They do not prove an all-orders identification from a first-order deformation calculation, a tangent-space computation, or a schematic nilpotent-support heuristic. Such an identification remains a separate geometric/formal-algebraic obligation.


## Additional checks against the frozen proof

### Smooth-divisor compatibility

Let \(\mathfrak p\in V(d)\setminus V(a,b,c)\). At least one of \(a,c\) is a unit in \(T_{\mathfrak p}\): if the entry known to be invertible is \(b\), then \(ac=b^2\) in the residue field forces both \(a\) and \(c\) to be invertible. If \(a\) is a unit, the first relation eliminates \(z_1=-(b/a)z_2\), and the second becomes \((d/a)z_2=0\). The square-zero relations then reduce to \(z_2^2=0\). Thus

\[
(R_2)_{\mathfrak p\oplus J_2}
\cong T_{\mathfrak p}[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2,d\epsilon).
\]

The quotient \(A_{\mathfrak p/(d)}\) is regular on this locus. Since \(T_{\mathfrak p}\) is regular, \(d\) belongs to a regular system of parameters there. The analogous elimination works when \(c\) is a unit. The claimed rank-one local pattern and its noncanonical completed version are therefore correct. This does not assert a globally identified family of external deformation hulls.

### Localized depth and loci

At every \(\mathfrak p\supset(f_i)\), the localized module \((J_i)_{\mathfrak p}\) is nonzero (its support is the whole divisor), torsion, and has a free resolution of length one over the regular local domain \(T_{\mathfrak p}\). It is not free because it is nonzero torsion. Auslander–Buchsbaum therefore gives depth \(\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak p-1\). The idealization has that same depth and dimension \(\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak p\). Hence the non-Cohen–Macaulay locus is exactly \(V(f_i)\). The nonregular locus is also exactly that divisor: on it a nonzero nilradical precludes regularity, whereas off it the ring equals a localization of \(T\).

The embedded height-one associated prime has a one-dimensional local ring of depth zero, so each ring fails \(S_1\), even though its depth at the closed point is 23. Neither is a complete intersection because every local complete intersection is Cohen–Macaulay.

### Hilbert series and module multiplicities

The defining ideals are homogeneous quadratically generated ideals, completed at the standard homogeneous maximal ideal. Their associated graded rings are exactly the corresponding homogeneous polynomial quotients. The nilradical graded modules have presentations

\[
J_1\cong(\mathbf C[t_1,\ldots,t_{24}]/(a))(-1)
\]

and

\[
0\longrightarrow\mathbf C[t](-2)^2
\xrightarrow{M}\mathbf C[t](-1)^2
\longrightarrow J_2\longrightarrow0.
\]

It follows that

\[
H_{R_1}(q)=\frac{1+q-q^2}{(1-q)^{24}},\qquad
H_{R_2}(q)=\frac{1+2q-2q^2}{(1-q)^{24}}.
\]

The multiplicities of the dimension-23 nilradical modules are respectively 1 and 2; the total-ring multiplicities are both 1. These calculations agree with the independent Hilbert–Samuel argument above.
