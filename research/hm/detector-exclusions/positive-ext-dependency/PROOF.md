# Positive Ext dependency: a compatible secondary-map countermodel

## Result and scope

The positive-twist groups H²(End₀(HM)(1)) and H²(End₀(HM)(2)) remain unproved here. In particular, neither the verified negative-H¹ table nor Riemann–Roch is used to assert their vanishing.

A distinct dependency is resolved rigorously: even granting both positive-H² vanishings, all of the relevant numerical cohomology data, the surface Euler characteristic, and both primary cohomology maps are compatible with different normalized boundary projection ranks. We construct two exact four-term sequences of bounded complexes whose normalized projected ranks are respectively zero and six. Both have the known sixteen-dimensional boundary group, its fixed ten-plus-six projection, the same primary maps, and the same cohomology dimensions for all four terms and the intermediate image object.

These are cochain/derived countermodels to an inference from that specified data. They are NOT examples of HM bundles or normalization sheaves, do not vary the actual HM geometry, and prove no actual normalized restriction rank. Geometry or additional compatibility may select a unique secondary map. The point is to identify data which cannot logically supply that selection by themselves.

## 1. The actual criterion and a sharper rank formula

Use the notation of normalization-module-resolution/PROOF.md:

    E=B(-1)⊕3B, L=9B(1), P=5B(2), D=f_*(f*B⊗O(10,2)),
    0→E→L→P→D→0,
    K=im(L→P),
    V=ker[H²(E)→H²(L)], J=coker[H¹(L)→H¹(P)].

There is a well-defined secondary map σ:V→J, and the composite connecting map Δ:H⁰(D)→H²(E) has image ker σ. The actual dual restriction is pr_U Δ, where H²(E)=U⊕W with dim U=10 and dim W=6, and pr_U is its intrinsic first-summand projection.

For arbitrary V⊂U⊕W set A=V∩W and write

    v=dim V, a=dim A, d=v-a=dim pr_U(V),
    s=rank σ, b=rank(σ|A).

Then the exact rank identity is

    rank(pr_U|ker σ)=v-s-(a-b)=d-s+b.                 (1)

Indeed ker σ has dimension v-s, and its intersection with the projection kernel is ker(σ|A), of dimension a-b. The permitted range of b is

    max(0,s-d) ≤ b ≤ min(a,s),

because σ(V)/σ(A) is generated by V/A of dimension d. Thus

    max(0,d-s) ≤ rank(pr_U ker σ) ≤ min(d,v-s).       (2)

These bounds are sharp as pure linear algebra: split V=A⊕C with dim C=d; choose b independent image directions for A and s-b new independent image directions for C, with remaining vectors in the kernel. This construction works for every integer b in the stated interval whenever dim J≥s.

When V=U⊕W and s=10, (2) allows every rank from zero to six. More generally, for full V=C¹⁶ the sharp interval is max(0,10−s)≤rank(pr_U ker σ)≤min(10,16−s). Numerical knowledge of rank σ alone therefore does not remove the positional dependence on the six-dimensional auxiliary subspace.

## 2. Numerical compatibility with the HM data

For this countermodel only, grant the stronger hypothetical positive table

    h⁰(B(1))=h⁰(B(2))=h²(B(1))=h²(B(2))=0.

The known negative-H¹ vanishing and Serre duality give h³=h⁴=0 at these positive twists. The previously derived polynomial

    χ(B(t))=3 binom(t+4,4)−(15/2)t²−(75/2)t−25

gives χ(B(1))=−55 and χ(B(2))=−85, hence h¹(B(1))=55 and h¹(B(2))=85 under precisely the displayed hypothetical vanishings. They are not asserted for the actual bundle.

The verified groups at twists −1 and zero give

    h¹(E)=5+3·24=77, h²(E)=10+3·2=16,
    h¹(L)=9·55=495, h¹(P)=5·85=425.

All other E,L,P cohomology is zero under this provisional table; h⁰(E)=0 follows from stability and tracefreeness, and its higher groups from duality. The implied Euler characteristic of D is

    χ(D)=χ(P)−χ(L)+χ(E)=−425+495+(−77+16)=9.

This agrees with the independently computed surface Euler characteristic. Consequently the countermodel is compatible with the known numerical constraints even in a favorable hypothetical positive-cohomology situation.

## 3. Fixed primary maps and varying secondary data

Work over C with cohomological complexes. Let E have zero differential, E¹=C⁷⁷ and E²=V=U⊕W=C¹⁰⊕C⁶. Let L have only L¹=C⁴⁹⁵, and P only P¹=C⁴²⁵.

Choose and fix an injection f¹:E¹→L¹, and let f²=0. Set Q=L¹/f¹(E¹), dim Q=418. Choose and fix a linear map

    T:Q→P¹, rank T=415,

so dim ker T=3 and J=coker T has dimension ten. Fix a splitting P¹=im T⊕J. The primary map L→P is T composed with L¹→Q; it is fixed throughout and kills f(E¹).

Set K=Cone(f), using Cone(f)^n=L^n⊕E^(n+1) and differential (l,e)↦(d_L l+f(e),−d_E e). Then

    K⁰=E¹, K¹=L¹⊕V, d_K⁰(e)=(f¹(e),0),
    H¹(K)=Q⊕V, dim H¹(K)=434,

and all other H*(K) vanish. Its first triangle supplies the connecting projection H¹(K)=Q⊕V→H²(E)=V.

For any surjection σ:V→J, define a chain map g_σ:K→P by

    g_σ¹(l,v)=T([l])+σ(v),

where the two summands lie in the fixed direct summands im T and J. This is a chain map because T kills f(E¹). Its composition with L→K is exactly the fixed primary map L→P, independently of σ.

Let D_σ=Cone(g_σ). Explicitly its only terms are D_σ^(-1)=C⁷⁷, D_σ⁰=C⁴⁹⁵⊕V=C⁵¹¹, and D_σ¹=C⁴²⁵, with differentials −(f¹,0) and g_σ¹. The first is injective, the second is surjective, and their composite is zero by construction. The induced cohomology map H¹(K)→H¹(P) is (q,v)↦T(q)+σ(v), and it is surjective. Hence

    H⁰(D_σ)=ker T⊕ker σ, dim H⁰(D_σ)=3+6=9,
    Hⁱ(D_σ)=0 for i≠0.

The two connecting maps compose as

    H⁰(D_σ)=ker T⊕ker σ → H¹(K)=Q⊕V → V,
    (q,v)↦v,

up to harmless convention-dependent signs. Thus im Δ_σ=ker σ, exactly as in the normalization criterion. In particular χ(D_σ)=9 for every surjective σ.

## 4. Two indistinguishable numerical models with different projected ranks

Choose σ₀:U⊕W→J to be any isomorphism U→J and zero on W. Its kernel is W, so

    rank(pr_U Δ_σ₀)=0.

Choose U=U₆⊕U₄. Define σ₁ to kill U₆ and identify U₄⊕W with J. Its kernel is U₆, so

    rank(pr_U Δ_σ₁)=6.

In fact every integer k from zero through six occurs with all these same dimensions and primary maps. Choose a k-dimensional U_k⊂U and a (6−k)-dimensional W_(6−k)⊂W, let Z_k=U_k⊕W_(6−k), and let σ_k be the quotient V→V/Z_k followed by an isomorphism V/Z_k≅J. Then dim ker σ_k=6 and rank(pr_U ker σ_k)=k. This is a symbolic choice of abstract subspaces, not a geometric matrix rank experiment.

Every cohomology group dimension of E,L,P,K,D is the same in the two endpoint models and the entire interpolating family. The projection V→U is the same. The primary maps E→L and L→P are identical, not merely of identical ranks. The secondary maps both have rank ten. The ranks differ solely because their kernels sit differently relative to W.

The variation is the homotopy datum associated to the zero primary composite E→L→P. On the zero-differential E,L,P representatives, a degree−1 homotopy includes a map E²→P¹. This is precisely the slot in which σ appears. Forgetting this datum while retaining the primary maps erases the distinction.

## 5. Compatibility with genuine four-term exact sequences of complexes

The cone triangles above can be realized simultaneously by two degreewise short exact sequences, so this is not an arbitrary collection of long-exact-sequence dimension lists.

For a chain map a:A→B, take the standard mapping-cylinder short exact sequence

    0→A→Cyl(a)→Cone(a)→0,

with Cyl(a) quasi-isomorphic to B and the first map representing a. Apply this first to f:E→L and then to g_σ:K→P. Splicing the quotient map Cyl(f)→K with the injection K→Cyl(g_σ) produces the degreewise exact sequence

    0→E→Cyl(f)→Cyl(g_σ)→D_σ→0.

Its second and third terms have cohomology L and P respectively, and its intermediate image is K. The contractible enlargements are necessary: the original zero-differential f is not degreewise injective at degree two. The resulting strict middle differential need not be identical as σ varies; what remains fixed is the displayed E,L,P model and both primary maps in the homotopy/derived category, hence their cohomology maps. No claim of identical strict four-term complexes is made. Naturality of the connecting maps identifies its double boundary with the cone calculation in §3. An explicit cylinder and independent check are supplied in AUDIT.md.

This realization is in bounded complexes of vector spaces. It asserts neither existence of corresponding coherent sheaves on P⁴ nor compatibility with the fixed geometric normalization resolution or HM multiplication structure. Those are precisely stronger data not captured by the numerical/primary-map premise being excluded.

## 6. What remains useful to prove next

A positive-H² vanishing proof would simplify the actual criterion to V=C¹⁶, but cannot alone settle the actual secondary map. Under this full-V assumption, an actual injectivity proof (projected rank ten) additionally needs enough information to show σ(V)=σ(W), equivalently s=b; an actual zero-rank proof needs ker σ⊂W. More generally formula (1) quantifies exactly what remains after numerical ranks are known.

The actual values of H²(B(1)), H²(B(2)), the actual σ, normalized restriction, the cusp correction, and all thirty physical obstruction values remain unevaluated. The auxiliary finite-dimensional models here are symbolic universal countermodels, not numerical evaluations of the prohibited geometric instance.
