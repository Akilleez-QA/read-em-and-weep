## 2. Ambient intersection and nondegeneracy

The intersection computation has the correct sign: H^4=1, E_z^4=integral over P3 of (-h)^3=-1, all mixed H/exceptional terms vanish because H restricts trivially to E_z, and different exceptional divisors are disjoint. Hence (3H-E)^4=81-30=51.

Global generation follows from pulling back the actual surjection O_P4^10 onto I_Z(3) and taking the image ideal on the blowup. Positive fourth self-intersection establishes image dimension four and generic finiteness before the inverse argument, so there is no circularity. The projection formula then gives deg(Phi) deg(V)=51. The analogous product on Y is justified by its positive square 75.

Linear independence of the c_i gives nondegeneracy of V. The actual resolution gives (I_S)_3=0; therefore no nonzero linear combination of the cubic sections vanishes on S, proving nondegeneracy of T. Passing to Y does not introduce a linear relation, since Y is birational to the integral S and agrees with it on a dense open set.

## 3. Ambient inverse and scheme-theoretic caution

For x outside S, rank L(x)=9 and its kernel is generated by c(x). This proves the exact equivalence in equation (7), not merely one inclusion. A general y in V can be chosen outside the closed images of Y and E, of dimensions at most two and three. Its nonempty fiber lies over P4 minus S. Generic finiteness then forces P(ker B(y)) to be a point.

A constant-rank four matrix gives a kernel line bundle locally, hence a morphism to P4. Four independent rows have a 4 by 5 matrix whose five signed maximal minors give its kernel. These quartics produce an actual rational inverse, so the argument does not rely on counting points in a potentially nonreduced fiber. It proves degree one and therefore degree(V)=51.

All 5 by 5 minors vanish on reduced V because they vanish on the dense original cubic image. The incidence equations extend to the entire graph by density, giving q composed with Phi equal to pi wherever q is defined. The distinction between the inverse to P4 and its rational lift to M is correctly stated. The wording “suitable charts” on T correctly avoids assuming that an arbitrary row choice valid on V remains nonzero on T.

## 4. Finite-trisecant lemma

The polynomial identity argument is valid: if a positive-dimensional linear space Lambda meets P4 minus S, proportionality there implies c_i restricted to Lambda equals y_i h identically for one nonzero cubic h. This uses that Lambda is integral and that the open complement of S is dense.

The restricted ideal is correctly the image ideal I_Z O_Lambda, not necessarily the tensor product module. It defines Z times_P4 Lambda. This scheme is a closed subscheme of the finite reduced C-scheme Z, so it is itself reduced. Thus its equality with the cubic hypersurface (h) forces Lambda to have dimension one and h to have three simple zeros. Dimension at least two would give a positive-dimensional zero hypersurface. The conclusion is exactly three distinct nodes, not a length-three divisor with unproved reducedness.

There are finitely many possible trisecant lines because every one is determined by two nodes. The argument excluding four collinear nodes is also correct: every cubic would vanish on that whole line, contradicting generation of the finite base scheme. No existence or exact count of trisecants is used.

## 5. Finiteness of the lines on S

A positive-dimensional reduced component of the projective Fano scheme contains an integral projective curve. Its normalization is smooth over C, and the pulled-back universal family is the projectivization of a rank-two vector bundle, hence a smooth projective P1-bundle U.

The image of U in S is closed and cannot be one-dimensional while containing infinitely many distinct lines. It is therefore all of the integral surface S. The dominant morphism U to S factors through X by normality of U and the universal property of normalization, and is generically finite. In characteristic zero, the resulting separable function-field extension makes pullback of global regular two-forms injective.

The target space of this injection is zero: K_U restricts to O_P1(-2) on each fiber, so every global section vanishes on every fiber. This contradicts p_g(X)=4. Thus the reduced Fano scheme is zero-dimensional and has finitely many closed points. Possible nonreduced structure has no effect on the set of actual lines. This fully supplies the extra surface argument not furnished by ambient birationality.

## 6. Surface birationality and its degree

A general x in S can avoid both the finitely many S-lines and all node-trisecant lines. If P(ker B(c(x))) were positive-dimensional, either it meets the complement of S and is a forbidden trisecant, or it lies in S and contains a forbidden line through x. Therefore the matrix has rank four at general image points of S.

The same kernel morphism then recovers x. Since Y to S is birational, this supplies a rational inverse to Phi restricted to Y and proves degree one. The degree product gives degree(T)=75. There is no illicit inference that birationality of the ambient map alone forces birationality on its special surface.

## 7. Contracted curves

D restricted to E_z is O_P3(1). A basepoint-free linear subspace of H0(P3,O(1)) must be the full four-dimensional space, since any proper subspace of linear forms has a common projective zero. Hence each exceptional divisor is mapped by a linear embedding and contains no contracted curve.

Any contracted integral curve C consequently has a curve image Gamma under pi. Incidence puts Gamma in Lambda_y. If Lambda_y meets the complement of S, the trisecant lemma applies. If Lambda_y lies in S, its dimension cannot be at least two, since an integral surface containing a plane must equal that plane, contrary to degree 15. Thus Gamma is a line in either case.

In the second case, the proof correctly uses actual constancy of Phi on C, rather than merely the linear incidence relation, to obtain proportional restricted cubics and exactly three reduced node zeros. C is the strict transform because pi is an isomorphism away from Z. Conversely a three-node line has strict transform P1 and D-degree zero, so global generation contracts it. Containment in Y is equivalent to containment of the original line in S. All statements in Theorem (d) follow.

The argument deliberately does not classify finite nonsingleton fibers on the finitely many lines in S. It neither proves normality of V or T, nor an embedding, nor existence or absence of trisecants. These limits and the independent original deformation boundaries are stated accurately in Section 8.
