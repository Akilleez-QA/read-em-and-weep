# No thirty-point base orbit in the universal pure-V nonic pencil

## Theorem and boundary

For every nonzero member of the two-dimensional universal pure-V nonic
pencil, its finite base scheme has no orbit of thirty points.

More precisely, on each of the fifteen involution mirrors, every basepoint
belongs to one of the prescribed six order-five axes, the prescribed ten
order-three axes, or the unique fifteen-point orbit of order-two axes.
There is no basepoint with stabilizer exactly cyclic of order two.

This is a whole-pencil theorem, with both coefficient variables retained
symbolically. It does not select an actual source coefficient. It does not
assert actual-source occurrence or determine local reducedness of the
fifteen-axis orbit; those are separate questions. No global nonic Groebner computation is used.

## 2. An actual finite-group mirror, and all its conjugates

The first two pinned binary-group generators are

    h1=diag(i,−i),       h2=[[0,1],[−1,0]].

They are elements of the given binary icosahedral group, not arbitrary
rotations chosen from SL2. In the quadratic coordinates

    a=p x^2+q xy+t y^2

their actions, and the product action, are

    A1=diag(−1,1,−1),
    A2=[[0,0,1],[0,−1,0],[1,0,0]],
    A3=[[0,0,−1],[0,−1,0],[−1,0,0]].

The checker reconstructs these symmetric-square matrices directly and
checks their Klein four relations and Pi-intertwining identities.
The minus eigenspace of A1 is the full plane q=0. Its projectivization
is therefore an actual involution mirror H. Both p and t remain free
homogeneous coordinates on H: no axis representative is substituted.

All fifteen involutions in A5 are conjugate. Thus their mirrors are
conjugate to H. Each pencil member is G-equivariant, so its base scheme
is G-stable. It suffices to classify base support on this entire mirror.
This calculation consequently applies to the whole union of fifteen
mirrors, not just to one chosen direction.

The plus axes of A2 and A3 are respectively defined by q=0,p−t=0 and
q=0,p+t=0. These are order-two axes in the unique fifteen-point orbit
of PR1035. In particular, the binary divisor p^2−t^2 on H consists of
order-two axes, not points with stabilizer exactly C2. The Klein four
stabilizes each of these projective axes.

## 3. Exact restriction of the whole coefficient pencil

Set z=sqrt(5), and define the homogeneous quadratics

    U=p^2−(2z/5)pt+t^2,
    V=p^2+(2z/3)pt+t^2.

Direct homogeneous gcd computation gives U as the restriction of the
six-axis ideal generated by Pi(a^3), and V as the restriction of the
ten-axis ideal generated by the maximal minors of L. Their roots are
therefore exactly the two order-five, respectively two order-three,
axes on H. Both are squarefree and disjoint, and disjoint from p^2−t^2.

Let alpha,beta be independent coefficient indeterminates. The checker
reconstructs B=Pi times multiplication by a^2, its signed maximal-minor
row N, and F1=NLR1,F2=NLR2. On the whole mirror q=0 it proves

    alpha F1+beta F2
       =(20/3) U^2 V (z f, 0, 0, g),              (1)

where the residual binary cubics are

    f=alpha p^3+(z alpha/3+2z beta/15)p^2t
                      −alpha pt^2+(z alpha/5+2z beta/5)t^3,

    g=(alpha+2beta)p^3−z alpha p^2t
                      +(5alpha/3+2beta/3)pt^2+z alpha t^3.

Every entry of (1) is an exact polynomial identity in alpha,beta,p,t.
The fixed factor U^2V is exactly the prescribed minimum degree-six
axis divisor on H. Any additional base support on H must be a common
projective root of f and g.

## 4. Homogeneous resultant and projective boundaries

The homogeneous binary cubic resultant is

    Res(f,g)=−(65536z/2025)
        (alpha−beta/2)^2 (alpha+beta/2)^2 (alpha+beta)^2.       (2)

It is computed as the degree-three Sylvester determinant after t=1,
with symbolic coefficients; equivalently it is the binary homogeneous
resultant. No specialization of leading coefficients is used to infer
its projective behavior. The following direct boundary check additionally
rules out loss of roots or exceptional cases:

    at t=0: (f,g)=p^3(alpha, alpha+2beta);
    at p=0: (f,g)=z t^3((alpha+2beta)/5, alpha).

For either coordinate endpoint, simultaneous vanishing forces
alpha=beta=0. Thus no nonzero pencil member has a residual common root
at either endpoint. In particular, possible leading-coefficient drops
cause no missing common root at infinity.

The coefficient point alpha=0,beta nonzero is also covered: the
resultant becomes −(4096z/2025)beta^6, which is nonzero. Formula (2)
therefore leaves exactly three projective coefficient lines. On each,
alpha is nonzero as a homogeneous scaling coordinate, and the complete
homogeneous gcd, after removing that common scalar, is

    beta= 2alpha: gcd(f/alpha,g/alpha)=U;
    beta=−2alpha: gcd(f/alpha,g/alpha)=V;
    beta=−alpha:  gcd(f/alpha,g/alpha)=p^2−t^2.                (3)

All gcds in (3) are monic in p and squarefree. These are exact binary
polynomial gcd calculations, not just divisibility witnesses. Hence no
other common roots occur on the exceptional lines. For every other
projective coefficient ratio, (2) is nonzero and there is no common
projective root at all.

## 5. Exclusion of the thirty-point orbit

By (1)–(3), every basepoint on H lies in U=0, V=0, or p^2−t^2=0. These
are order-five, order-three, and order-two axes respectively. None has
stabilizer exactly cyclic of order two. Equivariance gives the same
statement on every involution mirror.

PR1035 proves that a thirty-point orbit consists of points with stabilizer
exactly C2 and meets each involution mirror in two points. Such an orbit
would therefore contradict the complete mirror classification above.
This proves the theorem for every nonzero pencil member.

As a consequence, the remaining possible additional support consists only
of the unique fifteen-axis orbit and the twelve-point conic orbit, subject
to the existing coefficient restrictions. This proof does not determine
their ambient local lengths or claim which nonzero member is the actual
source gate.
