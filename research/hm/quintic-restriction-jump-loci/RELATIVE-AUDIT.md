## 1. Universal equation and direct images

On P⁴×Ω the universal equation has bidegree (5,1). Therefore its ideal sheaf is O_P⁴(−5)⊠O_Ω(−1), and the left term of the universal B restriction sequence has exactly the asserted negative tautological twist. The nonzero equations give a Cartier divisor, so tensoring with the locally free pullback of B preserves the short exact sequence.

Product cohomology yields the stated H^iB and H^iB(−5) tensor factors. The direct-image long exact sequence has precisely the six nonzero terms displayed in the proof. In particular both outer relative cohomology sheaves vanish, and taking kernel and cokernel of f gives the two canonical short exact sequences with no rank-constancy requirement. Multiplication by the universal equation is the bundle-valued form of the pointwise multiplication; under the fixed Serre identification its source is U⊗O(−1), not U⊗O.

## 2. Sheaf injectivity versus fiber kernels

When c≠0, the determinant of f is a nonzero section proportional to Q. The smooth rank-two fiber furnished by the previous theorem ensures that its restriction to Ω is nonzero. Ω is integral, so local representatives of Q are nonzero divisors. The adjugate identity then proves that a local kernel section of f must vanish. This remains valid at the vertex: the germ of Q is a nonzero element of the local domain even when its residue at that point is zero.

It follows that ker f is the zero sheaf and R¹π_*𝔅 is canonically A⊗O. Tensoring the resulting resolution of the cokernel is not left exact. Consequently the presence of pointwise kernel spaces does not contradict injectivity of f as a sheaf map.

## 3. Quadric cokernel, support, and depth

The cokernel 𝒞 is annihilated by Q by the second adjugate identity. Its zeroth Fitting ideal is (Q); independently, its generic rank one on the integral quadric implies that its annihilator as an O_Δ-module is zero. Indeed an annihilating element would annihilate a nonzero one-dimensional vector space after passage to the fraction field, and must be zero. Hence its annihilator over Ω is exactly (Q), so the scheme-theoretic support assertion is justified, not merely a set-theoretic inference from a Fitting ideal.

At each y in Δ the stalk is nonzero, is Q-torsion over the regular local domain R=O_Ω,y, and has a free resolution of length one. It cannot be projective over R, since a nonzero free module over a domain is not torsion. Thus its projective dimension is one. Auslander–Buchsbaum yields depth_R(𝒞_y)=dim R−1. Passing to the quotient ring R/(Q) preserves the module depth, while the ring dimension falls by one. This is exactly the maximal Cohen–Macaulay condition over Δ.

Off the vertex an entry of the presentation matrix is invertible in R, permitting invertible row and column operations reducing the presentation to diag(1,uQ). Hence 𝒞 is locally a free rank-one O_Δ-module there. At a vertex point the matrix reduces to zero over the residue field and its cokernel fiber has dimension two. Local freeness would force rank one because Δ is integral and the generic rank is one; it is therefore excluded at every such point. This includes nonclosed points of the vertex if the residue field is interpreted accordingly.

The first Fitting ideal is generated by the entries (a,b,d), as claimed. The matrix analysis uses only formal linear coordinates and invertible operations, with no evaluation of a selected quintic or HM map.

The quotient D⊗O(−1) in sequence (2) is locally free, so that sequence splits locally over Ω. It follows that R²π_*𝔅 has projective dimension at most one and contains the nonzero torsion summand locally at every point of Δ. A locally free module on the integral Ω is torsion-free, so R² is not locally free there. No global splitting follows and none is asserted.

## 4. Derived base change and canonical maps

The universal family over Ω is smooth projective, and 𝔅 is locally free, hence flat over Ω. Thus the proper-flat base-change theorem applies locally on the Noetherian base: the derived direct image is perfect and its derived tensor with a residue field is the cohomology complex of the fiber. The precise statement was checked in [Stacks Project, Lemma 30.22.1](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/07VJ); the broader formulation in [Lemma 36.30.1](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0A1G) also applies. This is a statement about the derived object, not individual underived cohomology sheaves.

Write E¹=R¹π_*𝔅 and E²=R²π_*𝔅. The canonical truncation triangle is

    E¹[−1] → Rπ_*𝔅 → E²[−2] → E¹.

For c≠0, E¹ is locally free and E² has projective dimension at most one. After derived tensoring with k(x), the first term has cohomology only in degree one, and the third term has cohomology Tor₁(E²,k(x)) in degree one and E²⊗k(x) in degree two. There is no degree-zero cohomology in that third term, since Tor₂ vanishes. The resulting long exact sequence therefore has exactly the injection, cokernel, and degree-two isomorphism asserted in (4)–(5). These maps come from canonical truncation and are the natural cohomology base-change maps; no choice of local splitting is needed.

Since the quotient in (2) is locally free, its higher Tor groups vanish. The Tor long exact sequence canonically identifies Tor₁(𝒞,k(x)) with Tor₁(E²,k(x)). Tensoring the length-one resolution of 𝒞 canonically identifies this group with ker f_x. The source of f_x is U⊗ℓ_x, which preserves the correct dependence on projective parameterization. A chosen equation p trivializes ℓ_x, but is not needed for the canonical sequence.

Tensoring (2) remains exact at its left because the quotient is locally free. Combining with the degree-two base-change isomorphism recovers the displayed cokernel-to-H² exact sequence. Consequently degree-one base change fails precisely on Δ, by one dimension away from the vertex and by two dimensions at the vertex; degree-two base change is an isomorphism everywhere. There is no general theorem forcing local freeness of R² from this isolated degree-two base-change statement, so the torsion behavior is consistent.

## 5. The zero-scalar case

For c=0, f vanishes. Its kernel and cokernel are respectively U⊗O(−1) and T⊗O. Both relative middle cohomology sheaves are extensions of locally free sheaves, hence locally free of rank 26. The truncation argument now has no higher Tor groups and gives base change in both degrees. The general statement of (4) remains valid in this case because Tor₁(𝒞,k(x))=0.

No global splitting of these rank-26 bundles is implied or required. Neither this case nor the nonzero-scalar case is selected by the argument.

## 6. Relative self-Ext and top twist

Local freeness of 𝔈 identifies the relative self-Ext cohomology sheaves with R^iπ_*End(𝔈). The trace splitting separates the scalar and trace-free summands. Applying the universal scalar restriction sequence gives

    R⁰π_*O_𝒳 = O,
    R¹π_*O_𝒳 = R²π_*O_𝒳 = 0,
    R³π_*O_𝒳 = H⁴(P⁴,O(−5))⊗O(−1) ≅ O(−1).

The one-dimensional Serre-trace factor is fixed, so the top negative tautological twist is correct. Equivalently, relative adjunction gives ω_𝒳/Ω≅π*O(1), consistent with relative Serre duality. Pointwise one-dimensional top Ext spaces do not imply a trivial relative line bundle.
