## 2. Recovering the stabilizer character from one fixed node

For a direction L in G let Z_L be its five-node orbit and let E_L=H0(T(5)|Z_L)^G. PR590 proves that this line is one-dimensional and that evaluation E_L -> T(5)_p is an isomorphism for each p in Z_L. In particular, to compute an element's character on E_L it suffices to choose a representative that fixes p and compute the action on that fiber. This avoids assuming a simultaneous global trivialization of branch differences.

Choose universal Schrodinger coordinates indexed by F5 with L the diagonal direction and p=[e0]. This is a choice of group-representation convention, not a selected source or evaluation of the primary map. The stabilizer B of L in K is a Borel subgroup of order twenty, generated by a unipotent u of order five and a diagonal element t of order four. Their standard linear normalizer lifts can be taken to be

u(e_i)=zeta^(i^2)e_i,
m(e_i)=-e_(2i),

with inverses/relabeling allowed by the function-versus-vector convention. Both fix p. The matrix m is unimodular: its unsigned index permutation is one four-cycle and a fixed point, of determinant -1, and multiplication by -I in dimension five corrects its determinant. It satisfies m^2(e_i)=e_(-i). Its quotient image is a generator of the order-four torus. The lift uhat is unimodular since sum_i i^2=0 in F5 and is unipotent in K. These elements and their quotient images are explicitly given in Horrocks–Mumford (1973), Section 1, equations (1.4)–(1.5), printed pp66–67:
https://www.dam.brown.edu/people/mumford/alg_geom/papers/1973a--Rk2BdleP%5E4-NC.pdf

For uhat, the odd order excludes branch interchange, and its eigenvalue on e0 is one. Hence uhat acts trivially on T(5)_p and therefore on E_L.

For m, the O(1) fiber scalar at p is -1 in either the vector or dual convention, so its O(5) scalar is -1. It remains essential to compute the independent branch sign.

## 3. The order-four lift swaps the two branches

On the modular base the action factors through A5=K/{+I,-I}. The direction stabilizer becomes D5, and the image of m is one of its involutions, outside its order-five normal subgroup. The associated cusp pair consists precisely of the two fixed points of this order-five subgroup: this follows from compatibility of the cusp-pair indexing with directions and from the fact that an order-five group cannot permute a two-element set.

An involution in D5 outside C5 interchanges these fixed points. For a direct verification, put the two fixed points at 0 and infinity. A projectivity preserving each is diagonal and commutes with the nontrivial order-five projectivity, whereas the indicated involution conjugates it to its inverse. It cannot preserve both individually, so must interchange them.

The two normalization preimages of p lie over these distinct cusps. Since m fixes p, its lift to X permutes its two preimages, and since it interchanges the cusps, it must exchange the preimages. On the local quotient C^2/C_diag its action is therefore -1. Multiplying this branch sign by the O(5) fiber scalar gives

m|T(5)_p = (-1)(-1)=+1.

There is also a direct geometric cross-check that does not need a labeling of the cusp pair. BHM Proposition 10, printed p64, gives the two branch tangent planes at e0 as v2=v3=0 and v1=v4=0. The index permutation i -> 2i exchanges these two planes. The actual coordinate-copy equality of PR570 transfers these tangent spaces to S; m preserves S by actual normalizer equivariance. An automorphism fixing p takes a branch tangent plane to the tangent plane of the image branch, so the distinct displayed planes force m to exchange the branches. This recovers exactly the same sign. This is use of a cited geometric description and a universal permutation, not an evaluation of any selected HM/source matrix.

The central quotient involution is represented by m^2, so it acts positively as well. Equivalently, it fixes the base, cannot exchange these preimages over distinct cusps, and its fiber scalar at e0 is one. The lift ambiguity by Z does not affect degree five; changing a representative by H5 does not affect its action on G-invariants. Thus this calculation is intrinsic to the actual N-linearization.

Since u and t generate B, the full stabilizer character on E_L is trivial. The six E_L are permuted transitively by K. Therefore the actual target is Ind_B^K(1), the unsigned six-direction permutation representation. This unsigned conclusion is a result of cancellation of two nontrivial signs, not an initial assumption. In particular the untwisted branch module would have the nontrivial D5 character instead.

The action on six directions is doubly transitive: after fixing infinity, the Borel's order-five subgroup acts transitively on the five remaining affine directions. Thus the permutation character has inner product two with itself (there are two orbits on ordered pairs). The constant line occurs once, so its five-dimensional complement is irreducible. This is the unique five-dimensional quotient representation J5. Hence

H0(T(5))^G = 1 + J5.                                      (1)

## 4. The seven-dimensional source has no trivial summand

For precision the required symmetric-cube identity is not inferred from a seven-dimensional count. Horrocks–Mumford, Lemma 3.3(vi), printed p70, gives

Sym^3(V0) = V3 tensor (J5 + W'),                            (2)

where V0,V1,V2,V3 are its four Galois-conjugate five-dimensional N-representations, with successive nontrivial Heisenberg central characters; W' has irreducible character chi2', and J5 has irreducible character chi5. The Appendix distinguishes these characters. Galois conjugation gives the same identity for every Vi, changing the two-dimensional label if necessary but preserving J5. Dual conventions are included because Vi*=V_(i+2). Thus (2) applies to the actual ambient linear-forms representation A*, without identifying inequivalent five-dimensional central characters.

We must also identify the actual Q2 as an N-extension, not just as an H5-module. Its nontrivial central character and dimension five make it irreducible on H5. There is a standard Vi with that same character. Choose an H5-intertwining isomorphism Q2≅Vi. For every n in N the two transported n-actions implement the same conjugation action on H5, so their ratio commutes with H5 and is scalar by Schur's lemma. These scalars form a character of N trivial on H5, hence a character of K. Since K=SL2(F5) is perfect, that character is trivial. Therefore Q2 is isomorphic to the standard Vi as an N-module, with no possible hidden twist. The same reasoning identifies Q2* with the five-dimensional factor of Sym^3(A*) in (2), because their central characters are inverse to that of Q2.

Consequently the actual source decomposes as

P3 = Q2* tensor (J5 + Wspin)

as an N-module. Taking invariants in P3 tensor Q2 gives

(P3 tensor Q2)^G = J5 + Wspin,                             (3)

because (Q2* tensor Q2)^H5 is the identity line and N acts trivially on it by conjugation. The central scalar Z already acts trivially on this product, so its G-invariants and H5-invariants coincide. Formula (3) in particular identifies the source's even five-dimensional part as irreducible; it is not merely a parity count.

## 5. Exact rank by equivariance and the established nonzero image

The actual multiplication map mu^G is K-equivariant by PR590. Equations (1) and (3) imply that every such map vanishes on Wspin and has image contained in J5. Schur's lemma makes its restriction J5->J5 either zero or an isomorphism. The independently established bound a>=1 excludes the zero map. Hence a=5.

Using the actual relation r5=24+a from PR590 gives r5=29. Its actual cohomological cokernel has dimension one and is the trivial summand in (1). Thus

H1(S,O_S(5))≅C as an N-representation.

This also identifies the unique invariant obstruction abstractly: after choosing an equivariant identification of the six residue lines with the permutation module, it is the sum-of-coordinates functional.
