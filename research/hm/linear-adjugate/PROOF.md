# Adjugate reconstruction and the determinantal node filtration of the primary linear syzygy map

Status: structural mathematical proof; independently reviewed in REVIEW.md. Not formally checked. Work over C. No selected actual HM/source coefficients, matrix, point evaluation, numerical rank test, numerical search, or large computation is used. The only displayed local differential is the universal tensor-Koszul map for two transverse formal planes.

## 1. Inputs and notation

Let P=C[x0,...,x4], m=P_+, S⊂P4 the actual integral primary surface, Z⊂S its thirty reduced transverse double points, I=I_S, J=I_Z, A=P/I, and R=P/J. Thus I is prime of height two, J has height four, and I⊂J.

The first dependency gives the existing exact minimal graded resolution

0 → P(-10) --c--> P(-7)^10 --L--> P(-6)^10 --f--> P → A → 0.       (1)

Here c is a column of cubics, L is a square 10 by 10 linear map, and f is a row of sextics. These symbols denote its actual differentials, not a new chosen coefficient instance. In particular fL=0, Lc=0, and the entries of f generate I.

Combining the first two dependencies gives

Ext³_P(A,P(-5)) ≅ R(5).                                      (2)

At every z∈Z the completed ambient local ring and surface ideal are

Q=C[[u1,u2,v1,v2]], n=(u1,u2,v1,v2),
K=(u_i v_j : 1≤i,j≤2)=(u1,u2)(v1,v2).                        (3)

The surface is smooth elsewhere. Its Hilbert polynomial is

P_S(t)=(15/2)t(t-1)-25.                                      (4)

Write I_r(L) for the homogeneous ideal generated by the r by r minors. For an ideal H, H^sat means saturation by m, whereas H:J denotes ordinary ideal quotient. Script I and script J below denote the ideal sheaves of S and Z in P4.

## 2. The final primary syzygy is the actual node generator column

Apply Hom_P(-,P(-5)) to (1). Its last differential is

P(2)^10 --c^t--> P(5).

Consequently

Ext³_P(A,P(-5)) = (P/(c1,...,c10))(5).

An isomorphism of P-modules preserves its annihilator. Comparing with (2), whose annihilator is J, proves the equality of actual homogeneous ideals

(c1,...,c10)=J.                                               (5)

This does not follow merely from equal Hilbert functions. In particular c is primitive: no nonunit irreducible polynomial divides all its entries, since otherwise J would lie in a height-one principal prime. The same argument applies to f because its entries generate the height-two prime I. Thus both vectors have entry gcd equal to a nonzero constant.

## 3. The adjugate factors with no nonconstant multiplier

Let F=Frac(P). Tensor (1) with F. The module A⊗F is zero, f has rank one, and c has rank one. Exactness therefore gives rank_F L=9, ker_F L=F c, and the left kernel of L is F f. This is a consequence of the exact resolution, not a numerical rank test.

The determinant of L is zero. Its classical adjugate is nonzero because rank_F L=9, and

L adj(L)=adj(L)L=0.

Every column of adj(L) therefore lies in F c, and every row lies in F f. There is a nonzero λ∈F with

adj(L)=λ c f.                                                (6)

It remains essential to show that λ is a constant rather than a rational function. Write λ=a/b with coprime polynomials a,b. Since every entry of λcf is a polynomial, b divides every c_i f_j. If an irreducible q divided b, choose i with q not dividing c_i and j with q not dividing f_j; these choices are possible by the primitivity of the two vectors. Then q cannot divide c_i f_j, a contradiction. Hence b is a unit and λ∈P.

Every nonzero adjugate entry is homogeneous of degree nine, while each nonzero c_i f_j has degree 3+6=9. Equation (6) forces the polynomial λ to have degree zero. Thus

adj(L)=λ c f for one λ∈C*.                                  (7)

The signed entries of adj(L) are exactly the 9 by 9 minors. It follows that

I_9(L)=J I                                                   (8)

as homogeneous ideals, before any saturation. This is the precise multiplicative defect in treating the submaximal minors of L as the reduced primary ideal.

## 4. Exact recovery by one ideal quotient

Because I⊂J and I is prime,

sqrt(I_9(L))=sqrt(IJ)=I.                                     (9)

One ideal quotient already recovers I:

(I_9(L):J)=I, and consequently (I_9(L):J^∞)=I.                 (10)

Indeed I⊂(IJ:J) is immediate. Since ht J=4 and ht I=2, there is j∈J\I. If hJ⊂IJ, then hj∈IJ⊂I, so primeness of I forces h∈I. The same reasoning proves (IJ:J^n)=I for every n≥1. This is not a general cancellation rule for arbitrary ideals; the prime I and J⊄I are used explicitly.

There is also the reciprocal ideal quotient

(I_9(L):I)=J.                                                (10a)

To prove the nontrivial inclusion, let hI⊂JI and fix a node-line prime q_z, a minimal prime of the radical ideal J. If h∉q_z, localization makes h a unit and gives I_(q_z)=J_(q_z)I_(q_z)=q_zP_(q_z)I_(q_z). Nakayama's lemma would force I_(q_z)=0, impossible for a nonzero ideal in the domain P_(q_z). Thus h lies in every q_z, and hence in their intersection J. The opposite inclusion is immediate.

Consequently L alone reconstructs both actual ideals by the intrinsic operations I=sqrt(I_9(L)) and J=(I_9(L):I), after which (10) gives the reduced primary ideal by one colon without taking a radical. Alternatively, the cubic kernel in §2 supplies J directly. No source-specific coefficient selection is needed to establish these constructions.

## 5. The determinantal scheme has a square-zero length-120 node defect

Let D=Proj(P/I_9(L)). Sheafifying (8) gives an exact sequence

0 → script I/(script J script I) → O_D → O_S → 0.             (11)

The left term E is the nilradical of O_D because the reduction of D is S by (9). It is annihilated by script J, and it is square-zero: I²⊂JI because I⊂J. Outside Z, script J is the unit ideal, so E=0 and D=S scheme-theoretically.

At z∈Z, apply the pinned completed local model (3). The completed stalk of E is K/nK. Its four generator classes u_i v_j are linearly independent: they are distinct degree-two monomials, whereas every element of nK has order at least three. Thus

E_z ≅ k(z)^4,
length(E)=30·4=120.                                         (12)

Faithful flatness of completion transfers the generator count and module length to the algebraic stalks. Therefore D is nonreduced at every node, with precisely a four-dimensional square-zero nilpotent module there.

This module is exactly the full local socle of O_D at a node. The reduced quotient Q/K=C[[u1,u2]]×_C C[[v1,v2]] has no n-socle: multiplication by u1 on the first branch and by v1 on the second shows an element killed by n is zero. Hence any socle element of Q/nK maps to zero in Q/K and belongs to K/nK; conversely n kills K/nK. In particular the node is an embedded associated point of D. Away from Z the scheme equals the integral S, so there are no other projective embedded associated points.

Additivity of Euler characteristic in (11) yields

P_D(t)=P_S(t)+120=(15/2)t(t-1)+95.                            (13)

The degree and sectional genus are unchanged. The number 120 is the length of a projective zero-dimensional sheaf; the corresponding graded module I/JI has support on the affine node lines and has dimension one, not finite length. No assertion that the homogeneous product JI is m-saturated is needed or made.

## 6. The next step: all projective rank strata and the lower minor ideals

The local model determines more than (8). It recovers the reduced nodes and their double structure from the lower minors of the same linear map:

sheaf(I_7(L))=script J,
sheaf(I_8(L))=script J²,
sheaf(I_9(L))=script J script I.                             (14)

We give the local argument, including its scheme-theoretic content.

### 6.1. Rank strata from the number of local ideal generators

At a point p∈S, tensor the stalk of (1), with twists trivialized, by k(p). The map f becomes zero. Therefore

Tor_1^{O_P4,p}(O_S,p,k(p)) ≅ k(p)^10 / im(L(p)).

The left side is I_p/m_p I_p, so

rank L(p)=10-μ(I_p).                                        (15)

At a smooth point of S, the codimension-two smooth embedding has μ(I_p)=2. At a node, (3) gives μ(I_p)=4. Thus rank L is eight on S\Z and six on Z.

Outside S the localized complex resolves zero and is split exact, so its residue complex is exact. Since f and c both have rank one there, L has rank nine. Consequently the projective rank strata are exactly

rank 9 on P4\S, rank 8 on S\Z, rank 6 on Z.                  (16)

In particular there is no rank-seven stratum and no point of projective rank at most five. These are abstract local consequences of exactness and the pinned local singularity, not evaluations of an actual selected matrix.

### 6.2. The universal formal node differential

The tensor product of the two Koszul resolutions of the ideals (u1,u2) and (v1,v2) gives the minimal free resolution

0 → Q → Q^4 --B--> Q^4 → K → 0,

or, after adjoining Q→Q/K, local Betti numbers (1,4,4,1). Exactness can also be checked by resolving the four monomial generators of K; the two parameter sets are disjoint. For the ordered generators (u1v1,u1v2,u2v1,u2v2), the universal first-syzygy map has columns

b1=(v2,-v1,0,0), b2=(0,0,v2,-v1),
b3=(u2,0,-u1,0), b4=(0,u2,0,-u1).                          (17)

This describes every transverse formal node after a local coordinate isomorphism; it is not a selected HM/source matrix.

Over a local ring, every finite free resolution is the direct sum of its minimal resolution and contractible free pairs. Comparing (1) at the node with the local minimal Betti numbers shows that exactly six such pairs occur between the two middle terms. There are none at either end, since their ranks are already one. Accordingly, after invertible row and column operations over Q,

L is equivalent to diag(I_6,B).                              (18)

Invertible row/column operations preserve determinantal ideals. For this block form,

I_7(L)Q=I_1(B), I_8(L)Q=I_2(B), I_9(L)Q=I_3(B).              (19)

The first ideal is visibly n. Every 2 by 2 minor is in n². Conversely the minors include all ten degree-two monomials in u1,u2,v1,v2, up to signs: the minors within columns b3,b4 give u1²,u1u2,u2²; those within b1,b2 give v1²,v1v2,v2²; mixed columns give the four u_i v_j. Hence

I_1(B)=n, I_2(B)=n².                                        (20)

Equations (18)–(20), followed by faithful-flat descent from completion, prove the first two identities of (14) at every node. Away from Z the rank is at least eight, so both lower minor ideals are units locally; this proves (14) globally. Its third identity was already proved homogeneously in (8).

### 6.3. Correct homogeneous formulations

Let q_z be the homogeneous ideal of the projective point z. Equality of coherent ideal sheaves is equivalent to equality of their saturated homogeneous ideals. Thus (14) gives

I_7(L)^sat = J,
I_8(L)^sat = (J²)^sat = ⋂_(z∈Z) q_z² = J^(2).                (21)

Here J^(2) denotes the symbolic square of the reduced point ideal. Each q_z is generated by four independent linear forms; its square is q_z-primary and saturated, and the intersection defines the disjoint union of double points. This justifies the displayed symbolic-square identification, rather than confusing it with the unsaturated ordinary square.

The raw homogeneous ideals I_7(L) and J are not equal: their generators start in degrees seven and three respectively. Similarly (21) does not assert I_8(L)=J². The homogeneous identity (8), in contrast, really holds before saturation.

Since rank L is everywhere at least six on P4, one also has I_6(L)^sat=P. Therefore (8), (14), and (21) identify the full nonempty projective determinantal filtration below generic rank: the node scheme, its double-point thickening, and the primary surface with its square-zero embedded-node defect.

## 7. Contribution and boundary

The resolution's previously undetermined cubic map is identified with the actual node ideal, and the linear middle map reconstructs both actual ideals by radical and reciprocal ideal quotients; once the node ideal is known, the primary ideal requires a single colon. Its submaximal minors do not define the reduced primary surface: their exact extra structure is the square-zero length-120 embedded node module. Continuing the local analysis identifies the lower minors scheme-theoretically with the reduced and doubled nodes after saturation.

The argument depends on the actual exact resolution, actual canonical-duality module, prime surface ideal, and actual transverse-node local type. A linear map with only the same dimensions or a matching Hilbert numerator is not enough. These are structural results about the primary surface and its own resolution; they do not settle the distinct full-adjoint/primitive-four boundary, prescribed-source membership, any actual middle-connecting determinant nonvanishing, higher lifting, or algebraicity.
