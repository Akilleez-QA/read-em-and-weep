# A conditional classification of quartic-generator jumps

## 1. Inputs and statement

Work over C and write P=P(W), where W=H_0 is the two-dimensional parameter space of the original module. Keep the actual annihilator family J from [PR519, commit cf328c6b0d05551a52911a3a4ba420c68f6fcc6f](https://github.com/Akilleez-QA/read-em-and-weep/blob/cf328c6b0d05551a52911a3a4ba420c68f6fcc6f/research/hm/gorenstein-flat-family/PROOF.md). Its canonical quartic multiplication morphism is

f:T tensor (J_3/I_3 O_P) -> J_4/((T I_3) O_P).

After ordinary, noncanonical bundle splittings, its source and target are respectively 50O_P(-3) and 15O_P direct sum 10O_P(-1). Its fiber cokernel has dimension a(w), the number of quartic minimal generators of the compressed Gorenstein quotient G_w. [PR516, commit 89b66cbede7513c4f196fdbda0ee3bb77c53d2e0](https://github.com/Akilleez-QA/read-em-and-weep/blob/89b66cbede7513c4f196fdbda0ee3bb77c53d2e0/research/hm/gorenstein-resolution/PROOF.md) gives 0<=a(w)<=15.

Use the established level-five Heisenberg group Gamma and its normalizer: Gamma fixes W, and the induced projective action of the binary icosahedral quotient on P(W) is A_5. Its action on the actual annihilator family and f is functorial. These are the original-module symmetries, not symmetries inferred from a Hilbert function. The representation setup is the one in [Horrocks--Mumford (1973), Sections 1, 2.2 and the appendix](https://www.dam.brown.edu/people/mumford/alg_geom/papers/1973a--Rk2BdleP%5E4-NC.pdf).

The projective action is faithful: the binary two-dimensional irreducible W has scalar central involution, so its projectivization factors through A_5. It is nontrivial, since a scalar action on W would contradict irreducibility. Simplicity of A_5 therefore makes this projective action faithful. The two binary dimension-two characters in the cited appendix both have central-involution character -2.

**Theorem.** Exactly one of the following types of behavior is possible:

1. a(w) is constant on P, with its constant value in {0,5,10,15}.
2. a(w)=0 away from the unique A_5 orbit of size 12, and a(w)=5 at every point of that orbit.

This is an exhaustive list of possible patterns, not a claim that all patterns occur. In particular, neither the generic value nor the existence of the exceptional pattern is determined here.

## 2. Removing the Heisenberg multiplicity

Let z be the central generator of Gamma. Its character on degree-four polynomials is nontrivial, since the degree-one character is faithful of order five. The Heisenberg representation theorem says that for each nontrivial central character there is a unique irreducible Gamma representation of dimension five. Fix the representation V_4 for this degree-four character.

Both bundles in f are entirely V_4-isotypic. For the target this follows since it is a subquotient of S_4 O_P. For the source, the cubic quotient is entirely of central degree three and tensoring by T adds degree one. Because Gamma acts trivially on P and its order is invertible in C, the usual isotypic idempotents work on bundles and commute with restriction to fibers. The evaluation map therefore gives canonical isomorphisms

V_4 tensor Hom_Gamma(V_4,F) -> F

for either bundle F in f. Thus f is Id_(V_4) tensor phi for a morphism of multiplicity bundles.

The ordinary splitting of the target can be made Gamma-equivariant: average an ordinary splitting of its equivariant short exact sequence over Gamma. The cubic quotient description in PR519 is already Gamma-equivariant (the determinant choice in the binary calculation only concerns W, on which Gamma acts trivially). Consequently, after choices of bases in multiplicity spaces,

phi:10O_P(-3) -> 3O_P direct sum 2O_P(-1).

In particular,

a(w)=5(5-rank(phi_w)).

The bound a(w)<=15 shows that rank(phi_w)>=2 everywhere. Let r be its generic rank. Then r belongs to {2,3,4,5}.

No normalizer-equivariant splitting, or separate linearization of the multiplicity spaces under A_5, is needed or asserted. Normalizer elements transport the chosen Gamma irreducible factor to an isomorphic factor; the intertwiner is unique only up to scalar. This potential projective ambiguity does not change determinantal ideals. Section 4 also gives an intrinsic verification which avoids making any such intertwiner choices.

## 3. The intrinsic determinantal divisor and its degree

Let I_r(phi) be the ideal sheaf locally generated by all r by r minors. This is independent of bundle frames. Since r is the generic rank, I_r(phi) is a nonzero ideal sheaf. On the smooth curve P every nonzero ideal is invertible, so

I_r(phi)=O_P(-D)

for an effective divisor D. Its support is precisely the set where the fiber rank is less than r.

For clarity, locally at a point p choose a uniformizer t. Smith normal form over the discrete valuation ring gives nonzero diagonal entries t^(e_1),...,t^(e_r), up to units, with e_i>=0, and all other entries zero. Then

mult_p(D)=e_1+...+e_r,

and the fiber rank deficiency r-rank(phi_p) is the number of positive e_i. In particular, a coefficient one in D means exactly one lost rank and a single elementary factor t. Equivalently D measures the determinant index of the image in its saturation; this formulation does not assume that the image itself is a subbundle with locally free quotient.

A chosen r-row minor selecting k of the two O_P(-1) target rows is a global section of O_P(3r-k). Indeed the source determinant is O_P(-3r), while those target rows have determinant O_P(-k). A nonzero such section has zero divisor of degree 3r-k, and D is bounded by that zero divisor, coefficient by coefficient. Although different minors can have different degrees, this argument is valid separately for each one; a nonexistent common degree is not required.

At least one r-minor is nonzero. Because there are only three O_P target rows, every r-minor uses at least max(0,r-3) lower rows. Therefore

deg(D)<=3r-max(0,r-3).

For r=2,3,4,5 the resulting bounds are respectively 6,9,11,13. The r=4 bound is 11, not merely 12. If r=5, every maximal minor has degree exactly 13.

## 4. Normalizer invariance without an equivariant splitting

The original map f is canonical multiplication in the actual annihilator family, hence is equivariant under the normalizer acting on the parameter line. Its generic rank is 5r. Let D_f be the divisor of its ideal of 5r-minors. This ideal, and therefore D_f, is preserved by that action.

Locally f=Id_(V_4) tensor phi. In Smith form every exponent e_i for phi occurs five times for f. Hence

D_f=5D.

It follows coefficientwise that D is normalizer-invariant. Its action on P factors through A_5, so D is A_5-invariant. This reasoning remains valid after any ordinary choices of bundle splitting and bases, and does not silently promote such choices to equivariant ones.

## 5. The orbit obstruction

A point stabilizer in any finite subgroup of PGL_2(C) is cyclic. For example, after moving the fixed point to infinity it is a finite group of affine transformations, and averaging an orbit supplies a second common fixed point; it then embeds in the multiplicative group. Since cyclic subgroups of A_5 have orders 1,2,3,5, its orbit sizes on P are 60,30,20,12.

There is exactly one orbit of size 12. There are six subgroups of order five, each fixing two points. Distinct such subgroups cannot share a fixed point, because the stabilizer is cyclic. These twelve points are invariant as a set and every one has stabilizer of order five, so together they form one orbit. Denote its reduced divisor by Omega_12.

Every effective invariant divisor is a sum of reduced orbit divisors with nonnegative integral coefficients. Consequently, an invariant effective divisor of degree less than 12 is zero, and one of degree at most 13 is either zero or Omega_12.

If r<=4, Section 3 gives deg(D)<=11; thus D=0 and the rank is constant. This gives the constant values a=15,10,5 for r=2,3,4 respectively.

If r=5, then D=0 or D=Omega_12. In the first case a=0 everywhere. In the second case each coefficient of D is one, so Section 3 gives rank(phi)=4 at each exceptional point, and rank(phi)=5 elsewhere. Thus a=5 exactly on Omega_12 and a=0 elsewhere. This proves the theorem.

## 6. Additional precise consequences and remaining dependency

If a is positive at a generic point, it is constant everywhere. A jump to a>=10 cannot occur in a generically quartic-generator-free family. No orbit of size 20,30,60 can support a jump. If jumps occur, their multiplicity-space determinantal scheme is reduced of length 12; for the unreduced original map, its top-minor divisor has multiplicity five at each of those points and total degree 60.

In the jumping case the multiplicity cokernel is a torsion sheaf of length 12 with one-dimensional fibers at the twelve points. The cokernel of f is then its tensor product with V_4, so it has length 60 and five-dimensional exceptional fibers. In the constant-rank cases the cokernel is a vector bundle of rank a.

The unresolved dependency is to distinguish the generic rank r, and, if r=5, decide between D=0 and D=Omega_12. Nothing above computes a selected actual source-instance matrix or claims that any particular case occurs. These conclusions concern quartic minimal generators of the actual compressed Gorenstein family; they do not settle the original fixed-pair first-order residual, higher-order lifting, or algebraicity problem.
