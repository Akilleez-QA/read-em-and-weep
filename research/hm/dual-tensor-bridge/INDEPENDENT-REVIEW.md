## Checks

1. **Exterior presentation and collapse.** For the quotient of the free module E=S tensor W by L, the exterior square is the quotient of the rank-one free module exterior-square E by wedges of L with E. There are no degree-zero or degree-one relations. A nonzero quadratic relation a tensor x+b tensor y has wedges with x and y equal to minus b and a, times x wedge y. Thus the quadratic wedge image is nonzero. It is an invariant subspace of the irreducible S_2 tensor det W and is therefore the entire target. Since S is generated in degree one, the ideal generated by S_2 contains every degree at least two. The exterior square is exactly (S/m^2) tensor det W, and the stated trivial determinant identifies it with S/m^2.

2. **Balanced pairing and surjectivity.** An element of I annihilates H, so the pairing formula is independent of the representative in R. Compatibility with polynomial multiplication gives balancing and the dual S-action; symmetry gives descent to the ordinary symmetric square. In degrees above five the target is zero. In degree zero the map is the dual of the symmetric degree-five action isomorphism from PR488. For the reversed dual N, dualizing N/mN identifies it with the socle of R in complementary degree. Levelness therefore gives generation of N in degree zero. The image of the graded S-linear map contains this generating space, so the map is surjective in every degree. Absence of linear relations gives source dimension 5 times 3=15 in degree one, equal to the target dimension, hence an isomorphism there.

3. **Actual quadratic embedding, not a substitute map.** The inclusion L_2 into S_2 tensor W is a nonzero equivariant inclusion of A* tensor W. Under the stated normalizer irreducible conventions, A* tensor W occurs exactly once in A* tensor (Sym^2 W tensor W). The canonical map r supplies a nonzero inclusion of that summand. Schur's lemma consequently identifies the actual inclusion with a nonzero scalar multiple of id_(A*) tensor r. This is precisely the step needed to transfer the universal calculation to the actual relation space.

4. **Universal injectivity.** Multiplication of the final W factor in r by an additional W produces the four displayed tensors. The displayed ordering takes the multiplying W factor first and the argument of r second; reversing this domain convention merely permutes the middle two inputs. The first and last tensors have monomial positions not occurring in the other images. The two middle tensors have, respectively, the unique positions x^2 tensor y^2 and y^2 tensor x^2. Thus every coefficient in a vanishing linear combination is zero. Tensoring this injection with A* remains injective. Its image has dimension 5 times 4=20, not merely an upper bound of 20. Since L has no lower-degree relations, there is no additional relation contribution in degree two. The symmetric-square component consequently has dimension 15 times 3 minus 20=25. Surjectivity onto N_2, also of dimension 25, proves the degree-two isomorphism. The stated A* tensor J_5 quotient follows from the supplied multiplicity-free decomposition.

5. **Tensor splitting and dimensions.** In characteristic zero, the two idempotents (1+swap)/2 and (1-swap)/2 split the balanced tensor square into its symmetric and exterior parts, without any freeness assumption on H. The pairing kills the exterior part and restricts to Phi on the symmetric part. Its kernel is therefore the direct sum of the exterior square and ker Phi. The low-degree dimensions are (3,15,25)+(1,5,0)=(4,20,25), with kernel dimensions (1,5,0). The determinant identification is subject only to the chosen trivialization already used in the artifact.

6. **Presentation bound.** The symmetric-square relation submodule is the image of L multiplied by the degree-zero generator space W. If L is generated in degrees two and three, as in PR482, that image is generated in those same possible degrees. This does not determine the additional kernel of the map onto N.

## Limits

The remaining symmetric kernel is shown to vanish through degree two only. This review neither proves nor assumes that it vanishes in higher degrees. The cubic and middle Betti ambiguities of H, the original fixed-pair quotient, higher-order lifting, and algebraicity are not resolved here.
