# Symmetric evaluation for the Horrocks–Mumford bundle

## Result and scope

Let E be the positive Horrocks–Mumford bundle on P^4 over C, with det E = O(5), and let V = H^0(E), dim V = 4. Then the natural multiplication map

    Sym^2 V → H^0(P^4, Sym^2 E)

is an isomorphism. In particular h^0(Sym^2 E) = 10.

This proof uses the geometric evaluation-degeneracy theorem, not actual HM monad matrices, a numerical rank experiment, a cohomology table, or generic-surjectivity inference. All local matrices below are the universal normal form of a transverse rank-one degeneracy locus.

## Verified public geometry source

G. Horrocks and D. Mumford, “A rank 2 vector bundle on P^4 with 15,000 symmetries,” Topology 12 (1973), 63–81, §5, proof of Theorem 5.1, printed page 75 (PDF page 13, zero-based index 12):
https://www.dam.brown.edu/people/mumford/alg_geom/papers/1973a--Rk2BdleP%5E4-NC.pdf

Precisely the needed facts are established together there. Evaluation is onto off 25 skew lines C. Rank zero on C is excluded because all wedge products would then vanish to second order, whereas they generate the ideal of C. A section nonzero at the given point becomes the first local basis vector. The remaining three sections have second coordinates u,u',u'', which generate the local ideal of C. The paper explicitly concludes that “their differentials are independent at x.” This is the transverse, reduced codimension-three degeneracy assertion, not merely a set-theoretic description.

Thus, after local changes of bases, evaluation has the form

    ev : R^4 → R^2,    (c0,c1,c2,c3) ↦ (c0, x c1 + y c2 + z c3),

where (x,y,z) is a regular sequence defining C. Evaluation never has rank zero.

Independent modern reference: S. Nollet and P. Rao, “Smoothing surfaces on fourfolds,” Canadian Mathematical Bulletin 68 (2025), 1389–1410, Example 4.9, repeats this local description and identifies the evaluation cokernel as a line bundle on C; Example 4.10 treats the reflexive evaluation kernel:
https://doi.org/10.4153/S0008439525100751

## 1. The evaluation cokernel has degree -1 on each line

Write A = im(ev), K = ker(ev), and T = coker(ev). The local normal form shows T = i_*L for a line bundle L on C. On C, let M denote the rank-one image of V⊗O_C → E|C, and let K0 denote the kernel of V⊗O_C → M. Then

    0 → K0 → V⊗O_C → M → 0,
    0 → M → E|C → L → 0.

The first normal derivative of ev induces a canonical isomorphism

    K0 ≅ L ⊗ (I_C/I_C^2).

Indeed, the three kernel vectors in the local normal form map to x,y,z modulo I_C^2, tensored with the cokernel basis. On a component ell ≅ P^1, N*_{ell/P^4} ≅ O_ell(-1)^3. Put d = deg L|ell and m = deg M|ell. The two exact sequences give

    3(d-1) + m = 0,    m + d = 5.

Hence d=-1 and m=6. Therefore

    T ≅ ⊕_{ell ⊂ C} i_{ell*} O_ell(-1).

In particular H^0(T)=H^1(T)=0. This derivation does not assume a precomputed splitting type of E on a jumping line.

## 2. The defect of symmetric evaluation

Let

    J = im(Sym^2 V⊗O → Sym^2 E),    Q = Sym^2 E/J.

There is a canonical exact sequence of sheaves on P^4

    0 → V⊗T → Q → Sym^2 T → 0.                 (1)

For its first map, send v⊗t to ev(v) times a lift of t to E, modulo J. Changing the lift by a section of A changes the product by a product of two evaluation images, hence by J. This also proves O-linearity, including annihilation by I_C.

To check injectivity and exactness, use the universal local form, writing a,b for the target basis. Then

    J = R a^2 ⊕ I ab ⊕ I^2 b^2,    I=(x,y,z),
    Q = (R/I) ab ⊕ (R/I^2) b^2.

The four basis vectors of V⊗T map respectively to

    ab, x b^2, y b^2, z b^2.

These are independent over R/I, because x,y,z form a basis of I/I^2. They generate exactly the kernel of Q → Sym^2 T, which sends b^2 to its class modulo I. This proves (1).

Now Sym^2 T = i_*L^2, and each L^2|ell = O_ell(-2). Consequently

    H^0(Q)=0,    h^1(Q)=25,    H^j(Q)=0 for j≥2.

Important: the locally visible cross term O_ell(5) is NOT a global subbundle of Q. Changes of lifts mix it with the first normal neighborhood of b^2. Sequence (1) accounts canonically for the cancellation and avoids a false 150-section lower bound.

## 3. Exact symmetric-kernel complex

There is an exact complex

    0 → (det V)⊗O(-5) → K⊗V → Sym^2 V⊗O → J → 0.       (2)

The middle map is multiplication after K ↪ V⊗O. The first map is obtained by taking the complementary two-by-two minors of ev: the resulting skew bivector has its columns in K. Globally its source is (det V)⊗(det E)^(-1), as stated. Equivalently, off C this is the usual inclusion det K → K⊗V, and the complementary-minor formula extends it across C.

Here is a local exactness check. In the universal form V_R = R e0 ⊕ R^3, K is the module of syzygies of (x,y,z) in the final three coordinates. Since I is generated by a regular sequence, it is of linear type: its symmetric algebra maps isomorphically to its Rees algebra. In degree two this says precisely that the kernel of Sym^2(V_R) → R a^2⊕I ab⊕I^2 b^2 is generated by the products K·V_R. One can also see this from the Koszul generators x e2−y e1, x e3−z e1, y e3−z e2 of K.

The kernel of K⊗V_R → Sym^2(V_R) consists of skew tensors whose columns are in K. All entries involving e0 vanish. A three-by-three skew matrix has three coefficients; the condition that (x,y,z) annihilates its columns says its coefficient vector is proportional to (x,y,z). Regularity of the sequence implies the proportionality scalar lies in R (or use exactness of its Koszul complex). Hence this kernel is the free rank-one module generated by the complementary-minor skew matrix. This proves the left part of (2), including at C. Away from C, (2) is the standard symmetric-power sequence for a quotient of a trivial rank-four bundle by a rank-two subbundle.

The constant line det V does not affect dimensions or ordinary sheaf cohomology.

## 4. Global sections

From 0→A→E→T→0 and H^0(T)=0 we have H^0(A)=H^0(E)=V. The global-sections map in 0→K→V⊗O→A→0 is the identity evaluation map V→V. Therefore

    H^0(K)=H^1(K)=0.

Let D = im(K⊗V→Sym^2 V⊗O). Split (2) into

    0→(det V)⊗O(-5)→K⊗V→D→0,
    0→D→Sym^2 V⊗O→J→0.

The intermediate cohomology groups H^1(O(-5)) and H^2(O(-5)) vanish on P^4. Thus H^0(D)=H^1(D)=0. The second sequence gives canonically

    H^0(J) = Sym^2 V.

Finally 0→J→Sym^2 E→Q→0 and H^0(Q)=0 give

    H^0(Sym^2 E) = H^0(J) = Sym^2 V,
    h^0(Sym^2 E)=binomial(5,2)=10.

This completes the geometric proof.

## 5. The actual cup-product map is injective

The proof yields more than a dimension count. The natural multiplication map

    V⊗H¹(E) → H¹(Sym²E)

is injective. In particular, since h¹(E)=2, its image has dimension 8.

Use D as in §4. The exact sequence 0→(det V)⊗O(-5)→K⊗V→D→0 yields an isomorphism

    H²(K⊗V) → H²(D),

because both H²(O(-5)) and H³(O(-5)) vanish. The natural multiplication diagram has top row

    0→V⊗K→V⊗V⊗O→V⊗A→0

and bottom row

    0→D→Sym²V⊗O→J→0.

The vertical maps are induced by multiplication, and the diagram commutes. Its connecting homomorphisms identify H¹(V⊗A)→H¹(J) with the preceding isomorphism H²(K⊗V)→H²(D), because the middle terms in both rows have no H¹ or H². Thus H¹(V⊗A)→H¹(J) is an isomorphism.

Since H⁰(T)=H¹(T)=0, inclusion A→E identifies H¹(A) with H¹(E). Since H⁰(Q)=0, inclusion J→Sym²E induces an injection H¹(J)→H¹(Sym²E). Composing gives exactly the cup-product map, not merely some abstract linear embedding. This proves injectivity.

## 6. Downstream cohomology and representation consequences

The following uses the explicitly pinned mathematical dependencies below.

Write F for the normalized HM bundle, E=F(3), and B=Sym²(F)(1). Then B(5)=Sym²(F)(6)=Sym²E.

    h¹(B(5))=h⁰(B(5))−χ(B(5))=10+22=32.

For an independent check of the Euler characteristic, c1(E)=5H, c2(E)=10H² imply

    ch(Sym²E)=3+15H+(45/2)H²−(25/2)H³−(725/8)H⁴.

Multiplication by td(P⁴)=1+(5/2)H+(35/12)H²+(25/12)H³+H⁴ gives degree-four coefficient -22.

PR453 at commit 56516a96a7eacfde0c8e272ca38db415cc7f294b gives the following dichotomy in §3 equation (7) and §5 equation (11):
https://github.com/Akilleez-QA/read-em-and-weep/blob/56516a96a7eacfde0c8e272ca38db415cc7f294b/research/hm/cubic-invariant-target/PROOF.md

- The invariant part of H¹(B(5)) is W⊕R, dimension 8, or W⊕2R, dimension 14.
- Each of the 24 nontrivial characters of the specified Heisenberg quotient has the same multiplicity m, with 1≤m≤6.
- Thus the total dimension is either 8+24m or 14+24m.

Since the total dimension is 32, only the first case is possible, and m=1. Thus the invariant summand is W⊕R and every nontrivial character occurs once. No stronger character identification for a separately named kernel is asserted.

The dimension argument alone proves only that the map r↦(ar,br) of the prior proof has (a,b)≠(0,0). Section 5 now supplies the additional, stronger fact: the actual cup-product source V⊗H¹(E), identified in PR453 as W⊕R, injects into H¹(B(5)). Consequently its image L is the full eight-dimensional invariant part W⊕R. In PR453 the first R is the degree-four-generator summand and the second R is the primary cup-product summand. If a=0, the already nonzero pair (a,b) would map onto the second R and kill that primary summand, contradicting injectivity. Hence a≠0. The previously unexcluded possibility L=W (a=0,b≠0) is therefore excluded by the injection argument, not by the dimension count alone.

Combining the exact sequence in §2 with the symmetric-kernel complex and the prior higher-cohomology vanishing gives an exact sequence of vector spaces

    0→H¹(J)→H¹(B(5))→H¹(Sym²T)→H²(J)→0,

of dimensions 8,32,25,1. Here h²(J)=1 follows from h²(E)=h³(E)=0 and H⁴(O(-5))=C via (2). These additional E vanishings are standard prior cohomological inputs, not needed for §5's injection. No augmentation-representation identification is asserted without tracking the equivariant line twists.

PR455 is not needed for the proof or these corollaries. The fixed-pair order-five quotient, actual-source membership, six pure conditions, module lifting and algebraicity remain open.
