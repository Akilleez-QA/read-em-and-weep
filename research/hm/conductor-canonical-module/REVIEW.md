## 1. Maps and canonical-module shift

For the saturated coordinate ring R of the reduced finite nonempty scheme Z, a linear form avoiding every point is a nonzerodivisor. Thus dim R = depth R = 1. The standard graded comparison sequence is exactly

0 → R → Γ_*(O_Z) → H¹_m(R) → 0,

including negative degrees. In particular the use of the full module U, rather than its nonnegative truncation, is essential and correct.

With the conventions in the proof, [U^vee(-5)]_n = Hom_C(U_(5-n), C). The map α_n(t)(a) = λ(at) therefore has precisely the stated degree. It is invertible because λ is nonzero on every one-dimensional node fiber, and its multiplication compatibility is checked in the correct degrees.

The pinned construction of the deficiency isomorphism identifies βq with restriction of this functional to R_(5-n), not merely with some abstract quotient having the same dimensions. Hence βq = i^vee(-5)α. Dualizing the comparison sequence degreewise is exact, since every graded piece is finite-dimensional, and identifies its kernel with (H¹_m(R))^vee(-5). Graded local duality consequently gives the asserted actual module identification

M ≅ ω_R(-5).

The treatment of all integer degrees, including n > 5 where the quotient target vanishes, is correct. The common-scalar ambiguity and the separate determinant-line warning for equivariance are also accurate. The ordinary graded assertion does not depend on an equivariant trivialization of the ambient canonical line.

## 2. Levelness, Hilbert series, regularity, and last syzygies

Since M is generated by its five-dimensional degree-two piece and vanishes below degree two, its minimal generator space has dimension five, entirely in degree two. Therefore ω_R = M(5) has five minimal generators in degree -3. This proves levelness and type five; it is stronger than what follows from the h-vector alone.

Independently applying canonical Hilbert-series duality in dimension one gives

Hilb_(ω_R)(t) = -Hilb_R(t^(-1))
             = t^(-3)(5 + 10t + 10t² + 4t³ + t⁴)/(1-t).

Multiplication by t⁵ for the shift (-5) yields exactly the displayed Hilbert series of M and the sequence 0, 0, 5, 15, 25, 29, 30, 30, ... . The h-vector gives reg R = 4 and a(R) = 3. The reversed numerator gives reg M = 6, consistent with the local-duality computation below.

Because R is Cohen–Macaulay of codimension four, dualizing its minimal free resolution into P(-5) produces a minimal presentation of ω_R from the dual of its last term. A summand P(-b) in F₄ becomes P(b-5), whose generator degree is 5-b. Thus the five generators in degree -3 force F₄ = P(-8)^5. No additional assumption about cancellation or the middle Betti numbers is being made.

The further last-syzygy consequence for M is also valid. The reversed dual resolution of R is an exact minimal resolution of ω_R because R has no Ext outside codimension four. Its final term is Hom_P(F₀(R),P(-5)) = P(-5), since F₀(R) = P. Shifting by (-5) yields final term P(-10) in the minimal resolution of M. Thus β_(4,10)(M) = 1 and β_(4,j)(M) = 0 for j ≠ 10. This does not require determining any middle Betti numbers.

## 3. Complete section ring and local cohomology

The degreewise normalization sequence identifies the kernel of N_sec → T_full with B = Γ_*(O_S), so the exact sequence with quotient M is valid as a sequence of P-modules. It does not make M a quotient ring. Finiteness of B follows because it is a submodule of the finite P-module N_sec, and its sheaf support gives dimension three.

For 0 → B → N_sec → M → 0, Cohen–Macaulayness of N_sec in dimension three and of M in dimension one gives exactly

H⁰_m(B) = H¹_m(B) = 0,
H²_m(B) ≅ H¹_m(M),
H³_m(B) ≅ H³_m(N_sec).

Canonical biduality and the contravariant shift rule give

Ext⁴_P(M,P(-5)) ≅ Ext⁴_P(ω_R,P(-5))(5) ≅ R(5).

Consequently H¹_m(M) ≅ R^vee(-5), with top nonzero degree five. It is nonzero, so depth B is exactly two, not merely at least two. Since N_sec is Cohen–Macaulay with regularity three, H³_m(N_sec) has top nonzero degree zero. The local-cohomology definition of regularity therefore gives reg B = max(5+2,0+3) = 7.

The nonnormality argument is valid. Finiteness over A makes N_sec integral over A and hence over the intermediate ring B. To make explicit why the fraction fields agree: choose a nonzero ambient linear section ℓ. For a homogeneous s ∈ N_sec of degree n, the quotient s/ℓ^n lies in K(X) = K(S), the degree-zero fraction field of A. Thus s lies in Frac(A), and A ⊂ B ⊂ N_sec have the same fraction field. The nonzero degree-two quotient makes the latter inclusion strict. This explanation supports the existing proof; it is not a required correction.

## 4. Ext and the ambient gap

The Ext long exact sequence has the stated direction. N_sec has its only nonzero Ext into P(-5) in degree two, while M has its only nonzero Ext in degree four. It follows that

Ext²_P(B,P(-5)) ≅ ω_(N_sec),
Ext³_P(B,P(-5)) ≅ R(5),

and every other Ext group vanishes. These conclusions also agree with depth B = 2 and projective dimension three over the five-variable polynomial ring.

Finally, for saturated A the section comparison gives 0 → A → B → H¹_m(A) → 0. Its cokernel is finite-length: both rings have no negative graded pieces here, and Serre vanishing gives equality in all sufficiently large degrees. This finite-length difference preserves local cohomology in indices at least two and hence the displayed Ext³ module, but it need not vanish. The proof correctly distinguishes A from B throughout and does not use depth or regularity of B to claim the corresponding statements for A.

The new map-level canonical-module identification, level/type assertion, last syzygy degrees, exact section-module extension, and depth/regularity/Ext conclusions are justified. The unresolved finite-length ambient-section gap and the separate full-adjoint/source-lifting questions remain outside the result, as stated.
