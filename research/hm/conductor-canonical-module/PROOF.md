# The actual conductor image is the shifted canonical module of the node ring

Status: independently mathematically reviewed (PASS); see REVIEW.md. Not formally checked. Work over C. No selected actual HM/source coefficients, evaluation matrix, numerical rank test, large computation, or public write is used.

## 1. Inputs and conventions

Let P=C[x0,...,x4], m=P_+, S the actual primary surface, nu:X→S its finite normalization, H=nu*O_S(1), and T=nu_*O_X/O_S. The support Z consists of thirty reduced nodes and T has one-dimensional fibers there. Put

R=P/I_Z, U=⊕_(d∈Z) H0(Z,O_Z(d)), T_full=⊕_(n∈Z) H0(T(n)),
N_sec=⊕_(n∈Z) H0(X,nH), B=⊕_(n∈Z) H0(S,O_S(n)),
M=im(N_sec→T_full), E=⊕_(n∈Z) H1(S,O_S(n)).

Here B is the complete section ring, not an assumed homogeneous coordinate ring. Write A=P/I_S for the latter. To avoid collision with N_sec, write G for the normalizer group. Use V(a)_n=V_(n+a) and (V^vee)_n=Hom_C(V_(-n),C). All spaces dualized degreewise below have finite-dimensional graded pieces.

## 2. The local-cohomology exact sequence for the node ring

Because Z is a nonempty finite reduced projective scheme, R has dimension one. Choose a linear form ell nonzero at every node. Multiplication by ell is injective on R: evaluation of ell f=0 at each node implies f=0 at each node. Thus depth_m R=1 and R is Cohen–Macaulay. Its saturated ideal also gives H0_m(R)=0.

The graded sheaf/local-cohomology sequence, including all negative degrees, is therefore

0→R --i--> U → H1_m(R)→0.                                      (1)

The arrow i is actual restriction, with R_d=0 for d<0. There is no truncation of U: every U_d has dimension thirty, also for d<0. Define the graded canonical module

omega_R=Ext4_P(R,P(-5)).

Graded local duality gives omega_R≅(H1_m(R))^vee. In particular omega_R is a finite CM module of dimension one.

## 3. Exact comparison of actual maps

The residue functional gives the isomorphism

alpha:T_full → U^vee(-5),
alpha_n(t)(a)=lambda(at),  a∈U_(5-n).                            (2)

It is an isomorphism because each node summand is one-dimensional and lambda is nonzero there. It is P-linear: for x∈P_1 and a∈U_(4-n),

alpha_(n+1)(xt)(a)=lambda(axt)=alpha_n(t)(xa).

All negative and positive twists are included. No independent trivializations of the thirty fibers are chosen.

The actual cohomology sequence and the vanishing H1(X,nH)=0 give

0→M→T_full --q--> E→0.                                         (3)

Let beta:E→R^vee(-5) be PR594's isomorphism. Its construction, not just its isomorphism class, shows

beta q = i^vee(-5) alpha.                                       (4)

Indeed in degree n the right side takes t to the functional p↦lambda(pt) on R_(5-n), which is exactly the defining map for beta q. When 5-n<0 the target is zero, so the same assertion remains valid. This checks the quotient map on the nose.

Dualizing (1) degreewise is exact and gives

0→(H1_m(R))^vee(-5)→U^vee(-5) --i^vee(-5)--> R^vee(-5)→0.        (5)

Equations (2)–(5) identify kernels, yielding the actual graded P-module isomorphism

                         M ≅ omega_R(-5).                       (6)

This is the theorem. It is canonical up to the one common scalar in lambda and the fixed standard local-duality identification. More intrinsically M identifies with (H1_m(R))^vee(-5) through the actual residue map; its embedded image inside U^vee(-5) is fixed by that construction. This is not an inference from equal Hilbert functions.

The construction through local cohomology is G-equivariant because lambda, multiplication, restriction, and all connecting maps are equivariant. If omega_R is defined equivariantly as Ext4 into the naturally linearized ambient canonical module, local duality respects this identification. Writing that ambient canonical module simply as P(-5) equivariantly requires retaining its determinant line, or fixing the determinant-one normalizer convention and a compatible trivialization. The unconditional assertion (6) is an ordinary graded P-module assertion; no suppressed determinant character is needed for it.

## 4. Levelness and the last syzygies of the node ring

From PR588 and (6), omega_R=M(5) has exactly five minimal generators, all in degree -3. The number is exactly five because M_n=0 for n<2 and dim M_2=5; no lower-degree products can account for an element of M_2. Hence R is level of type five, not Gorenstein.

The already known h-vector gives

Hilb_R(t)=(1+4t+10t²+10t³+5t⁴)/(1-t).

Since R is CM of dimension one, reg(R)=4 and a(R)=3. Canonical duality reverses this h-vector and yields

Hilb_M(t)=t²(5+10t+10t²+4t³+t⁴)/(1-t).                           (7)

Thus dim M_n is 0,0,5,15,25,29,30,30,... for n=0,1,..., consistently with the actual conductor ranks. This calculation is a consequence/check of (6), not its proof. It also gives reg(M)=6 and depth_m M=1.

There is a new resolution consequence without constructing a matrix. Since pd_P R=4, write its minimal resolution with last term F4. Dualizing into P(-5) gives a minimal presentation of omega_R whose generator summands are F4^*⊗P(-5). A summand P(-b) of F4 contributes a canonical generator in degree 5-b. All five generator degrees are -3, so

                              F4=P(-8)^5.                       (8)

Equivalently, beta_(4,8)(R)=5 and beta_(4,j)(R)=0 for j≠8. No middle Betti numbers, ghost cancellation, or complete resolution are inferred merely from the Hilbert numerator.

Conversely, dualizing the minimal resolution of R into P(-5) reverses it into a minimal resolution of omega_R, since R is CM of codimension four. Its last term is Hom_P(P,P(-5))=P(-5), coming from F0(R)=P. Shifting by -5 gives the last term P(-10) in the minimal resolution of the actual M. Thus beta_(4,10)(M)=1 and beta_(4,j)(M)=0 for j≠10. This is another actual module consequence, not a choice of a Betti table fitting (7).

## 5. Exact section-ring extension and depth

Taking the kernel of the actual section map to T_full gives

                  0→B→N_sec→omega_R(-5)→0.                     (9)

This is an exact sequence of graded P-modules, not a quotient of rings: M has no asserted quotient-ring multiplication. B is a graded subring of N_sec containing the image A of the unit map P→N_sec. Because N_sec is finite over P, its submodule B is finite as well. Its dimension is three: its associated sheaf is O_S, supported on the surface. The degree-two conductor rank is nonzero, so B is strictly smaller than N_sec.

From depth N_sec=3 and depth M=1, the depth lemma gives depth B=2. One can see the equality, rather than just a lower bound, directly from local cohomology: (9) gives

H0_m(B)=H1_m(B)=0,
H2_m(B)≅H1_m(M)≠0,
H3_m(B)≅H3_m(N_sec).                                            (10)

Since M=omega_R(-5) and canonical biduality for the CM ring R gives Ext4_P(omega_R,P(-5))≅R, local duality identifies

(H1_m(M))^vee≅R(5).

Consequently H2_m(B)≅R^vee(-5), and its largest nonzero degree is 5. The largest nonzero degree of H3_m(N_sec) is 0, since N_sec is CM of regularity 3. Thus

                       reg(B)=max(5+2,0+3)=7.                  (11)

These are actual complete-section-module assertions. In particular B is not Cohen–Macaulay. It is not normal either: N_sec is integral over A and hence over B, lies in the same fraction field, and is strictly larger than B. For the fraction-field assertion, finite birational normalization identifies the degree-zero function fields of S and X; a nonzero common ambient linear section then identifies their graded fraction fields, and hence the fraction fields of A, B, and N_sec. Equivalently the associated surface S has its known nonnormal nodes.

Dualizing (9), the only nonzero Ext of N_sec into P(-5) is Ext2, while that of M is Ext4. Hence the exact Ext sequence gives

Ext2_P(B,P(-5))≅Ext2_P(N_sec,P(-5))=omega_(N_sec),
Ext3_P(B,P(-5))≅Ext4_P(M,P(-5))≅R(5),                           (12)

with all other Ext groups zero. This is compatible with PR594's Ext3_P(A,P(-5))≅R(5), but does not identify A and B.

## 6. The precise ambient gap and contribution

The natural injection A→B has finite-length cokernel C: it is the standard graded section comparison for the saturated homogeneous coordinate ring A, and Serre vanishing makes it an isomorphism in all sufficiently large degrees. More precisely C≅H1_m(A), with H0_m(A)=0. No argument above proves C=0. Thus depth_m A may differ from depth_m B, although their local cohomology in degrees at least two agrees and their Ext3 modules agree. In particular (9) must not be rewritten with A in place of B without a proof of ambient-section surjectivity.

The new step is a map-level identification of the actual conductor image with the node canonical module, transferring actual quadratic generation into levelness/type and the last syzygy degrees of the thirty-node ring. It also gives the exact canonical-module extension of the complete section ring and fixes its depth and regularity. It neither proves ambient projective normality nor computes the primary ideal, the full-adjoint K4 boundary, prescribed-source membership, higher lifting, or algebraicity. The useful next dependency remains the finite-length ambient gap A→B, not a dimension-based substitution of these rings.
