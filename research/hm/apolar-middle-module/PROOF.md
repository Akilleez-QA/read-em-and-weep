# Apolar reconstruction and rigidity of the entire HM middle module

## Result and inputs

Let S=Sym(S₁) over C, dim S₁=5. Reindex the actual HM middle-cohomology module as Q_k=M_(k−5), so Q is supported in degrees 0,…,5 and has Hilbert function

    (2,10,20,20,10,2).

Put U=Q₀. The audited results in ../quintic-scalar-propagation/PROOF.md and AUDIT.md establish full generation by U and nonzero scalar c in Φ=cΨ. The prior ../quintic-pairing-representation/PROOF.md identifies Ψ:S₅→Sym²U* as a nonzero, surjective, normalizer-equivariant map unique up to scalar. The trace/Serre pairing gives perfect, symmetric, multiplication-self-adjoint pairings Q_k×Q_(5−k)→C. These are the only geometric inputs below.

We prove that Ψ reconstructs Q as an explicit quotient of S⊗U, and consequently reconstructs every multiplication map, not only the bottom-to-top quintic product. New consequences include an exact scalar annihilator formula, a dual quotient description of quartic cofactor kernels, and the stronger rigidity statement

    End_S^gr(Q)=C, and Q is indecomposable even as an ungraded S-module.

No selected polynomial, cofactor, HM, or source instance is evaluated.

## 1. The relation module is a catalecticant kernel

For 0≤k≤5 define the linear contraction map

    C_k:S_k⊗U → (S_(5−k)⊗U)*,
    C_k(Σ_i p_i⊗u_i)(q⊗v)=Σ_i Ψ(p_i q)(u_i,v).

Set R_k=ker C_k for 0≤k≤5, and R_k=S_k⊗U for k≥6. Set R=⊕_(k≥0)R_k. This is an S-submodule of S⊗U: for ℓ∈S₁ and k<5,

    C_(k+1)(ℓr)(q⊗v)=C_k(r)(ℓq⊗v),

while degree 6 and higher are all relations. Thus the canonical reconstruction is

    Q(Ψ)=(S⊗U)/R.                                      (1)

To identify (1) with actual Q, let π_k:S_k⊗U→Q_k be multiplication. It is surjective by the imported full-generation theorem. Self-adjointness and Φ=cΨ give

    ⟨π_k(r),π_(5−k)(s)⟩=c C_k(r)(s).                  (2)

Since c≠0, π_(5−k) is surjective, and the pairing on Q is perfect, (2) implies

    ker π_k=ker C_k=R_k.                               (3)

Above degree 5 both kernels are the whole source. Hence the map induced by multiplication is a graded N-equivariant isomorphism Q(Ψ)≅Q, identity on U. Scaling Ψ by any nonzero scalar leaves R unchanged.

In particular, the six ranks of C_k are exactly (2,10,20,20,10,2). This is a deduction from actual generation and duality, not an independent evaluation of a catalecticant instance. As dim S_k=binom(k+4,4), the relation-space dimensions are

    dim(R₀,…,R₅)=(0,0,10,50,130,250).

For k≥6 the dimension is 2 binom(k+4,4). These are dimensions of all relations in each degree, not claims about numbers of minimal generators.

Every multiplication map has the explicit rule

    S_d⊗Q(Ψ)_k → Q(Ψ)_(k+d),   a⊗[r]↦[ar].           (4)

Thus Ψ together with the fixed coordinate action determines all intermediate maps, compatibly and simultaneously. No independent scalar choices at the intermediate edges remain. If another generated, perfectly self-dual module has the same pairing up to a nonzero scalar and the same identified U, (3) gives its unique compatible isomorphism to (1) which is identity on U. This is uniqueness in this specified class, not a claim about every module sharing the Hilbert function.

## 2. A literal inverse-system realization

Write D_d=S_d* with contraction action of S: for a∈S_e and f∈D_d,

    (a⌟f)(b)=f(ab),   b∈S_(d−e),

with zero result if e>d. This convention avoids factorial ambiguity; identifying D with polynomials in dual coordinates would require the corresponding divided-power convention.

Each u∈U determines a vector-valued quintic

    F_u∈D₅⊗U*,   F_u(p)(v)=Ψ(p)(u,v).

Let E⊂(⊕_(d≥0)D_d)⊗U* be the S-submodule generated by all F_u. Give its degree-(5−k) contraction part the reindexed degree k. The map

    S_k⊗U → D_(5−k)⊗U*,   p⊗u↦p⌟F_u

is precisely C_k, after the natural tensor–Hom identification. Therefore its kernel is R_k and its images realize (1) as the full derivative/contraction module E. The two generators F_u for a basis of U are vector-valued quintics. In a basis their entries form a symmetric 2×2 matrix of quintic functionals. One should not replace Q by the scalar apolar algebra of the three entries: that scalar algebra is its faithful acting algebra, a different object.

The induced pairing on Q(Ψ), obtained from C_k, is perfect because C_(5−k) is the transpose of C_k. Under (1) it is the actual trace/Serre pairing scaled by c⁻¹.

## 3. Exact scalar annihilator and faithful acting algebra

Let I=Ann_S Q(Ψ). Generation by U gives, for 0≤k≤5,

    I_k={a∈S_k : a⊗U⊂R_k}
       ={a∈S_k : Ψ(ab)=0 for every b∈S_(5−k)}.        (5)

Indeed a annihilates the module iff it annihilates U; the second equality is the definition of R_k tested against every v∈U. For k≥6, I_k=S_k.

Equivalently, let T=im[Ψ*:Sym²U→S₅*], a three-dimensional space of scalar quintic functionals. Then

    I=Ann_S(T)                                       (6)

under the contraction action. Thus S/I is the scalar apolar algebra of this net, and Q is a faithful module over it. Formula (6) does not identify Q with S/I: their degree-zero dimensions already differ.

In particular I₀=I₁=0, because R₀=R₁=0, and I₅=ker Ψ has dimension 126−3=123. The ideal contains S_≥6, does not contain S₅, has radical S_+, and Q has Loewy length six. There are also no nonzero quadratic scalar annihilators. Indeed I₂ is N-stable in S₂=Sym²V*, where S₁=V*. The HM representation identity Sym²V=V₁⊗χ₃′ (Lemma 3.3(v), already recorded and independently checked in ../quintic-scalar-propagation/PROOF.md §6 and AUDIT.md §6) dualizes to

    S₂=V₃⊗χ₃′.

Here χ₃′ is self-dual and irreducible, V₃ is irreducible under H₅, and χ₃′ is inflated from N/H₅. The H₅-isotypic evaluation equivalence identifies N-submodules of this tensor product with N/H₅-submodules of χ₃′. Thus S₂ is irreducible. Consequently I₂ is either zero or all of S₂. The latter would give S₂U=Q₂=0, contrary to dim Q₂=20. Therefore

    I₂=0.

The degree-four dimension of the faithful acting algebra is also fixed without evaluating a matrix. The N-equivariant contraction map

    S₁⊗T → S₄*,   ℓ⊗f↦ℓ⌟f

has irreducible fifteen-dimensional source: S₁ is H₅-irreducible and T≅χ₃′ is quotient-irreducible, so the same isotypic argument applies. It is nonzero, since a nonzero quintic functional cannot vanish on every product S₁S₄=S₅. Hence it is injective. Its image is the annihilator-orthogonal complement I₄^⊥ by (5)–(6); therefore dim(S/I)₄=15. Together with the previous conclusions, the scalar apolar algebra has Hilbert entries

    dim(S/I)_(0,…,5)=(1,5,15,?,15,3),

and zero above degree 5. The degree-three entry is not determined here.

The Hilbert function happens to give H_Q(t)=2(1+t)^5, the same as two copies of an Artinian complete intersection of five quadrics in five variables. Nevertheless Q cannot be that rank-two free module: a quadratic complete-intersection ideal would annihilate it, whereas I₂=0. Section 5 independently excludes any direct-sum model. The ten-dimensional space R₂ therefore consists of genuine vector-valued quadratic relations and contains no subspace a⊗U for any nonzero a∈S₂. These statements do not determine the remaining Hilbert function or minimal generators of I.

## 4. Quartic cofactor kernels are duals of a degree-four quotient

Let j₁,…,j_r∈S₄ be arbitrary quartics, with J their linear span. This is a universal statement; no particular conductor cofactors are selected. Define

    α:Q₁→Q₅^⊕r,   m↦(j₁m,…,j_rm),
    β:U^⊕r→Q₄,    (u₁,…,u_r)↦Σ_a j_a u_a.

Perfect pairings identify Q₁ with Q₄* and Q₅ with U*. Multiplication-self-adjointness makes α the transpose of β. Therefore

    ker α ≅ (Q₄/JU)*,   rank α=dim(JU).              (7)

There is no rank hypothesis on the quartics. In reconstructed terms,

    Q₄/JU ≅ (S₄⊗U)/(R₄+J⊗U).                      (8)

Writing d=dim J, restriction of π₄ gives

    dim ker α = 10−2d+dim[(J⊗U)∩R₄].               (9)

In the actual five-cofactor situation, α is a square map between ten-dimensional spaces. It is invertible iff JU=Q₄. Equivalently, the five quartics must be linearly independent and their ten-dimensional subspace J⊗U must meet R₄ trivially. A linear dependency among the quartics already forces singularity. No assertion is made that the actual cofactors have such a dependency or transversality.

If (J) denotes the ideal generated by J, then (Q/(J)Q)₄=Q₄/JU, so (7) also interprets the cofactor kernel as the dual degree-four piece of a standard module quotient. More generally the same argument applies to any family in S_d acting Q_k→Q_(k+d): its common kernel is dual to Q_(5−k) modulo the sum of the family’s products with Q_(5−k−d), whenever 0≤k≤k+d≤5.

## 5. New rigidity: scalar graded endomorphisms and ungraded indecomposability

Let f be any degree-zero S-endomorphism of Q. Denote its actions on U=Q₀ and Q₅=U* by g and h. The quintic multiplication maps B_p:U→U* satisfy B_p=cΨ(p), viewed as symmetric matrices. S-linearity gives

    h B_p=B_p g   for every p∈S₅.                    (10)

Surjectivity Ψ:S₅→Sym²U* implies (10) for every symmetric bilinear form B on U. Choose a basis of the two-dimensional U. Taking B equal to the identity matrix gives h=g as matrices in the corresponding dual bases. Taking B=diag(1,0) forces g to be diagonal; taking B with off-diagonal entries 1 and diagonal entries 0 forces its two diagonal entries to agree. Hence g=h=λ id. Since Q is generated by U, f=λ id on all Q. This proves

    End_S^gr(Q)=C.                                  (11)

The conclusion is stronger than equivariant Schur rigidity: no N-equivariance of f is assumed.

Now every ungraded S-endomorphism splits into homogeneous-degree components, because Q is finite graded. Each component is S-linear. Negative-degree components vanish: they kill Q₀, and Q₀ generates Q. By (11), the degree-zero component is scalar. Thus

    End_S(Q)=C·id ⊕ E_+,

where E_+ consists of strictly degree-raising endomorphisms and E_+⁶=0. This is a local algebra: λ id+n is invertible exactly when λ≠0, by the finite geometric series. Its only idempotents are 0 and 1, so Q has no nontrivial direct-sum decomposition even after forgetting its grading. In particular every graded automorphism is a single scalar, and any two graded perfect self-duality isomorphisms Q≅D(Q), with D(Q)_k=Q_(5−k)* and its usual S-action, differ by a scalar.

This rigidity is not merely full generation restated. It depends essentially on the surjectivity of the quintic pairing onto all symmetric forms on the two-dimensional generator space.

## Scope and dependency boundary

The reconstruction is canonical relative to S, U, and Ψ; changing the chosen basis of U gives a compatible isomorphic module. It gives universal relation, inverse-system, annihilator, and kernel formulas without selecting coordinates for an actual HM/cofactor/source matrix. The nonzero scalar and all actual Hilbert ranks are imported from the audited geometric propagation theorem. No cofactor invertibility, source nonvanishing, Massey product, or obstruction value is inferred. Only local proof-file writes and abstract finite-dimensional algebra are used.
