# Independent audit: the unique component containing the twenty-five lines

## Verdict and scope

The structural argument passes, conditional on the pinned normalizer action, linear presentation, global dimension bound, and PR566 smooth-neighborhood theorem. The conclusion is uniqueness of the surface component meeting the distinguished lines, not irreducibility or reducedness of the whole primary degeneracy scheme. No selected HM/source evaluation or numerical experiment is used. This is mathematical review, not proof-assistant verification.

## 1. Degree at least seven without global normality

Let S be any integral projective surface in P4, smooth on a neighborhood of a line L, with N_(L/S)=O_L(-5). The line is an effective Cartier divisor on ALL of S: its ideal is invertible near L by smoothness and codimension one, and is the unit ideal outside L. Consequently its ideal is O_S(-L).

The three ambient linear equations of L generate its ideal after restriction to S, because L is already a closed subscheme of S. Thus O_S(H-L) is globally generated by three sections. Its self-intersection is nonnegative on an integral projective surface: the associated morphism has image of dimension at most two, with square zero if the image has dimension below two and positive generic degree times image degree otherwise. This reasoning uses Cartier intersection theory and does not require S to be normal or Cohen–Macaulay away from L.

Now H^2=deg S, H.L=1, and L^2=deg N_(L/S)=-5. Hence

(H-L)^2=deg S-7 >=0.

This proves deg S>=7 and avoids every potential embedded-component difficulty in a residual hyperplane-section argument.

## 2. Partition of the lines and the global degree budget

Smoothness of the actual primary scheme at every point of each distinguished line gives a unique surface component through that line. Distinct line-bearing surface components cannot meet the line anywhere. Assigning each of the twenty-five lines to its unique component therefore partitions the lines.

The normalizer preserves this partition. Under the pinned identification of the line set with the affine torsor F5^2, its action contains translations together with SL2(F5). The latter is transitive on nonzero vectors: extend any two nonzero vectors to determinant-one ordered bases and map one basis to the other. Therefore the affine action is two-transitive. An invariant equivalence relation for a two-transitive action has either singleton classes or a single class.

The linear 15-by-10 presentation gives degree-ten maximal minors whose common zero scheme is the primary rank-drop scheme. The absence of a divisorial component implies that their greatest common divisor is one. Choose one nonzero linear combination f. For each of its finitely many irreducible factors, at least one minor is not divisible by that factor. Over C a second linear combination g can avoid all those proper linear subspaces simultaneously. Thus f and g are relatively prime, and their common zero scheme Z is a complete intersection of type (10,10), of pure dimension two and degree100.

Every surface component of the primary rank-drop scheme is a component of Z. The sum of their reduced degrees is therefore at most100. Twenty-five different line-bearing surfaces would each have degree at least seven, contradicting 25*7=175>100. Hence one and only one surface component S contains all twenty-five distinguished lines. It is invariant under the normalizer. Other surface or curve components disjoint from all distinguished lines are not excluded.

## 3. Degree divisibility and the projections

The Heisenberg action on the invariant surface S linearizes O_S(n). Its center acts on H0(O_S(n)) by the ambient scalar character to the power n, up to the harmless dual convention. For 5 not dividing n this central character is nontrivial; all complex irreducible Heisenberg representations with that character have dimension five. Therefore h0(O_S(n)) is divisible by five.

Take sufficiently large n congruent to1 modulo5. Serre vanishing identifies the three dimensions for n,n+1,n+2 with the Hilbert polynomial. Their second difference is deg S and is divisible by five. Thus deg S is a multiple of five, and in particular at least ten.

For every distinguished line L_i, the same three generating ambient linear forms define a morphism S to P2 with pullback O(1)=O_S(H-L_i). Its square deg S-7 is strictly positive, so the image is two-dimensional, hence all P2, and the map is generically finite of degree deg S-7. No assertion of finiteness everywhere is justified or needed.

## 4. Exclusion of the degree100 endpoint

If deg S=100, its containment in the complete intersection Z above forces the fundamental cycle of Z to equal [S], with generic multiplicity one and no other components. A complete intersection is Cohen–Macaulay and has no embedded associated primes. Generic reducedness therefore implies reducedness, so Z=S scheme-theoretically.

Complete-intersection adjunction would then give omega_S=O_S(10+10-5)=O_S(15). Its degree on each distinguished line is15. However PR566 supplies a smooth neighborhood of the line, normal degree-5, and ordinary adjunction gives K_S.L=3. These disagree. Thus deg S cannot be100.

Combining the bounds gives

10 <= deg S <=95,  5 divides deg S,

deg(S -> P2 by projection from L_i)=deg S-7 in {3,8,13,...,88}.

This does not determine the actual surface degree, identify its global singularities, establish convergence of a formal neighborhood, or resolve the original Q4/algebraicity problem.
