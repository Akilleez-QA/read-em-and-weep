# An actual spinor filtration and nilpotent on the invariant quadric cone

## New results and limit

Let Q0=(q0=0) be the rank-three invariant positive quadric, with negative vertex line L. Put E=F_Q0(2), B=End_0(F), and let S be its standard rank-one reflexive spinor sheaf. There is an actual saturated equivariant injection

    0 -> S -> E -> J -> 0,
    J torsion-free of rank one, J**=S(2).

Writing T=S(2)/J, the exact defect has Hilbert polynomial

    chi(T(t))=9t+27.

In particular it has one-dimensional degree nine, retaining all possible zero-dimensional defect. The injection gives a nonzero invariant nilpotent

    N in H0(Q0,B_Q0(2)).

Its polynomial multiples give exactly the forced positive A+D5 subspace of H0(B_Q0(4)), and an odd T6 subspace. The positive sections commuting with N are precisely those positive polynomial multiples, of dimension eight. Thus positive invariant-vertex excess, if present, consists of sections not commuting with this canonical nilpotent. This does NOT prove that excess is absent or that the positive map has rank33.

## 1. Weighted-cone model and the spinor sheaf

Take a binary pair (a,b) transforming as W-prime, and negative coordinates (u,v) transforming as W. Give a,b weight1 and u,v weight2. The second Veronese of the weighted polynomial ring C[a,b,u,v] is

    C[a²,ab,b²,u,v], with relation (a²)(b²)-(ab)²=0.

Its Proj is Q0, so Q0=P(1,1,2,2), with ordinary hyperplane O_Q0(1)=O_wt(2). The class group is Z generated by the ruling-plane class Pi=O_wt(1), and 2Pi is the ordinary hyperplane class. One geometric verification resolves Q0 by the projective bundle over C0=P1 with fibers the planes joining a conic point to L. Its Picard group has generators H and f; the exceptional divisor has class H-2f. Removing that divisor gives Cl(Q0)=Zf, with H=2f.

The odd Veronese module defines S=O_wt(1). It is a rank-one reflexive sheaf, locally free away from L, with S^[2]=O_Q0(1). Its standard ambient resolution is

    0 -> W-prime O_P4(-1) --M--> W-prime O_P4
      -> i_*S -> 0,                               (1)

where M is the equivariant tracefree 2-by-2 positive-coordinate matrix with determinant a nonzero multiple of q0. In coordinates A=a²,C=b²,D=ab one may use the relations D a-A b and C a-D b. Thus the source representation in (1) is indeed W-prime, not W. Equivalently M is the unique contraction V tensor W-prime -> W-prime.

Consequently

    H0(S)=W-prime, h0(S)=2,
    H0(O_Q0(1))=V direct-sum W, h0=5,
    h0(S(1))=2*5-2=8,
    chi(S(t))=2 binomial(t+3,3).                  (2)

The weighted model also gives H0(O_wt(r)) directly as its weight-r polynomial piece. Multiplication by a nonzero weight-one polynomial injects consecutive pieces, so h0(O_wt(r))>=8 for every r>=3.

## 2. The actual primary sections factor through S

The actual ambient primary groups, from the normalized monad, are

    H1(F)=A plus W-prime,
    H1(F(1))=P4 plus W plus S4,
    H1(F(2))=1 plus A plus T6,
    H1(F(3))=W,
    H0(F(2))=0, H0(F(3))=P4.

Here S4 is the center-odd four-space, and the A and P4 terms are even. Since q0 is invariant, the map W-prime -> T6 is zero. The quadric restriction sequence therefore gives

    H0(E)_-=W-prime,
    H0(E) is W-prime or W-prime direct-sum A.      (3)

The even choice depends on a scalar that is not selected here. Similarly H0(E(1)) has no W-prime constituent: it is an extension of P4 by a subspace of P4 plus W plus S4.

Evaluate the canonical two-space (3) to get W-prime O_Q0 -> E. Composing with the relation matrix in (1) is a W-prime-valued linear relation, hence an equivariant map W-prime -> H0(E(1)). It is zero. Thus evaluation factors through S. The resulting nonzero map S->E is injective, since S is torsion-free of rank one and E is locally free on the integral normal Q0.

## 3. Saturation is forced by the actual small section space

Let K be the saturation of S in E. A saturated rank-one subsheaf of a vector bundle on a normal variety is reflexive: it is the intersection of the ambient bundle with its generic rank-one subspace, and extension across codimension-two subsets preserves this description. Thus K=O_wt(r) for an integer r. The inclusion S->K gives an effective divisorial difference, so r>=1.

If r>=3, equation (2) gives h0(K)>=8, contradicting h0(E)<=5 from (3). If r=2, K=O_Q0(1), whose global representation V plus W cannot inject into W-prime plus A. Its equivariant linearization cannot acquire a correcting character, because G is perfect. Thus r=1. The inclusion S->K is then an isomorphism: there is no effective divisorial difference, and both sheaves are reflexive.

This proves saturation. The quotient J is torsion-free of rank one. Since det(E)=O_Q0(3)=O_wt(6), its reflexive hull is O_wt(5)=S(2). Hence

    0 -> J -> S(2) -> T -> 0,                     (4)

with T supported in codimension at least two. A notation such as J=I_Z tensor S(2) is NOT asserted globally: S(2) is noninvertible along L. Only off L is the quotient described by an ordinary ideal times a line bundle.

## 4. Exact degree and Euler characteristic of the defect

The normalized monad has terms 5O(-1), 2 Omega²(2), 5O. The exterior Euler sequence gives

    chi(F(t))=15 chi(O(t))-10 chi(O(t+1))
              +2 chi(O(t+2))-5 chi(O(t-1)).

Since E=F_Q0(2), its restriction sequence gives

    chi(E(t))=chi(F(t+2))-chi(F(t))
             =(2/3)t³+6t²+(31/3)t-5.

Combining (2), (4), and 0->S->E->J->0 yields

    chi(T(t))=chi(S(t))+chi(S(t+2))-chi(E(t))
             =9t+27.

This proves the one-dimensional defect has degree9. It does not identify its support or discard embedded or isolated zero-dimensional contributions.

There is also exact untwisted cohomology h0(T)=27 and H1(T)=0. The spinor resolution makes S and S(2) acyclic in positive degrees. The primary restriction sequence gives (h0(E),h1(E))=(2,7) or (5,10), and H2(E)=0. Thus h0(J)=h0(E)-2, H1(J)=H1(E), H2(J)=0, and (4) gives h0(T)=20-(h0(E)-2)+h1(E)=27, H1(T)=0. Neither this cohomology nor the Hilbert polynomial identifies T with the structure sheaf of a curve or determines a genus: its reflexive non-Cartier ambient sheaf at the vertex is retained.

## 5. The canonical nilpotent and its forced polynomial multiples

On Q0 minus L, symmetric squaring S->E gives

    O_Q0(1)=S² -> Sym² E=B_Q0(3).

The homomorphism extends uniquely across L by normality and reflexivity of the locally free target Hom(O(1),B(3))=B(2). Thus there is a nonzero invariant N in H0(B_Q0(2)). It is pointwise nilpotent: under Sym² F tensor det(F)^(-1)=End_0(F), the square of a vector spans a nilpotent rank-one endomorphism.

Because S is saturated, N has no divisorial zero: away from codimension-two defects, S is a line subbundle of E and its square is nonzero. Multiplication by N therefore injects H0(O_Q0(2)) into H0(B_Q0(4)). The decomposition of the ordinary quadratic functions is

    H0(O_Q0(2))_+=D5 plus A,
    H0(O_Q0(2))_-=V tensor W=T6.

The invariant positive equation q0 removes exactly the trivial summand from Sym²V. Thus the N-multiples geometrically realize the forced eight-dimensional positive kernel A+D5, as well as a six-dimensional odd subspace at this singular vertex.

## 6. An exact centralizer criterion for the remaining positive excess

At the generic point, the centralizer of a nonzero nilpotent in the three-dimensional tracefree endomorphism space is its own one-dimensional span. If a section beta in H0(B_Q0(4)) commutes with N, it is therefore f N for a rational section f of O_Q0(2).

As N has no divisorial zeros and beta is regular, f has no pole at any codimension-one point. Normality extends f across codimension-two subsets. Hence f is a global section of O_Q0(2). Conversely every such multiple commutes with N. Therefore

    ker[ [N,-]:H0(B_Q0(4)) -> H0(B_Q0(6)) ]
      = N H0(O_Q0(2)).

Since N is even, the positive kernel of this commutator is exactly A+D5, dimension8. In particular rank33 of the positive q0 multiplication map is equivalent to absence of a positive section of B_Q0(4) having nonzero commutator with N. This criterion is exact but its vanishing is not proved here.
