# All secant-detection exceptions lie on the thirty-one icosahedral axes

## Interface and scope

Work over C with the binary icosahedral group G, its binary module W, A=Sym²W, D=Sym⁴W and the irreducible four-dimensional descended module P. Write X=P(A). We use precisely the canonical endpoint interface of PR661–663 in Akilleez-QA/read-em-and-weep:

- L:D O_X → A O_X(1) is surjective everywhere, with rank-two kernel K.
- Q=Hom_G(D,P⊗Sym²A*) has dimension two. Its two parity lines are Q_+ and Q_-.
- Restricted evaluation E:Q O_X → K*⊗P O_X(2) has rank at least one everywhere, rank two on the square conic C, and finite degeneracy support disjoint from C.
- Both parity restrictions are nowhere zero. The six C5 axes and ten C3 axes are degeneracy points, with exact rank one and an orbitwise constant, mixed kernel line in Q.

These are pinned respectively at PR661 commit 1325647fd916bf2daa71b11efeae6d52cfefa2cd, PR662 commit f75a18fd411350adda35dedf18751eee002a39bc, and PR663 commit ad3ab59f6fbbbb3e5bde1da58b3b92a1f49c6889. This argument does not evaluate any selected actual HM endpoint, source coefficient, matrix or rank.

## 1. A scheme-theoretic degree bound for each fixed channel

For 0≠q∈Q let Z_q be the zero scheme of the bundle map q|K:K→P O_X(2). Then

                         length(Z_q) ≤ 21.                  (1)

Empty schemes are allowed. To prove this, choose fixed vector-space bases and represent L by a 3×5 matrix of linear forms. Represent q by a 4×5 matrix of quadrics. For each row r of q, form every 4×4 minor of the matrix obtained by stacking the three rows of L above r. These twenty homogeneous quintics generate the ideal sheaf of Z_q.

This last claim is scheme-theoretic, not merely set-theoretic. On an open set where one 3×3 minor of L is invertible, invertible local row/column operations put L into [I_3 0]. Subtracting a combination of those three rows from r leaves precisely its two entries on K. The minors using the three pivot columns and either remaining column are these entries, up to units; every other minor belongs to their ideal. Invertible changes of columns preserve the ideal of the full set of such minors. Taking all four rows therefore gives exactly the local ideal of q|K.

The common zero scheme is finite: a zero of q|K belongs to the finite degeneracy support of E. Consequently the quintic linear system has no fixed curve. Choose a nonzero member F. For each of its finitely many irreducible factors, the members divisible by that factor form a proper linear subspace. Over C choose a member H outside the union of these subspaces. Then F,H have no common component. Their complete intersection has length 5·5=25 by plane Bézout, and contains Z_q as a closed subscheme. This proves the preliminary bound twenty-five, with embedded/nonreduced zero structure included. We now sharpen it structurally.

First, Sym⁵(A*) has no A5-invariant vectors. Indeed the restriction of an invariant quintic to the square conic is an invariant section of O_P¹(10). If nonzero, its zero divisor has degree ten and is A5-invariant, impossible because every A5 orbit on P¹ has at least twelve points. The restriction is therefore zero, and the quintic is divisible by the invariant equation of the conic. Its quotient is an invariant cubic. Repeating with degrees six and two on the conic reduces to zero. Also A5 has no nontrivial complex representations of dimension two (its irreducible dimensions are 1,3,3,4,5) and has only the trivial character. Consequently Sym⁵(A*) contains no A5-stable two-dimensional subspace.

Suppose now length(Z_q)≥22. Let T=V(F,H) be the chosen complete intersection, and let R be the residual scheme, I_R=I_T:I_Zq. The Artinian Gorenstein coordinate rings of T give residual annihilator duality for every finite subscheme Z_q, without any lci assumption on Z_q or R. Zero-dimensional complete-intersection liaison consequently gives length(R)=25−length(Z_q)≤3 and

             h⁰(I_Zq(5)) = 2 + h¹(I_R(2)).

For clarity the exact sequence producing this identity is

     0 → I_T(5) → I_Zq(5) → ω_R(−2) → 0.

Here ω_T=O_T(7), so residual duality identifies I_Zq/I_T with ω_R(−7). The connecting map from H⁰(ω_R(−2)) to H¹(I_T(5)) is dual to restriction H⁰(O_X(2))→H⁰(O_R(2)); H¹(I_T(5)) is dual to H⁰(O_X(2)) by the complete-intersection Koszul sequence. This proves the formula, including the empty residual case. Every plane scheme of length at most three imposes independent conditions on quadrics. One elementary proof is to choose a line avoiding its support, dehomogenize, and consider the length-r quotient algebra generated by the two affine coordinates: its subspaces spanned by polynomials of degree ≤d strictly increase until they equal the whole algebra (equality at one step makes the subspace stable under both generators). Starting with dimension one, degree r−1 suffices; for r≤3 this is degree at most two. Hence h¹(I_R(2))=0.

Thus H⁰(I_Zq(5)) has dimension two. Since Z_q is A5-invariant it is an A5-stable two-dimensional subspace of Sym⁵(A*), a contradiction. This proves (1).

Every Z_q is A5-invariant, because q is an equivariant family. In particular every orbit in its support has at most twenty-one points.

## 2. Complete orbit classification off the square conic

A nonsquare point [a] of P(Sym²W) is an unordered pair {p,r} of distinct points of P(W). Its stabilizer H in A5 is the subgroup preserving that pair. Let H_0 be the subgroup fixing both points. Then H_0 is cyclic: in coordinates p=0,r=∞ it lies in the multiplicative torus of PGL2. Also [H:H_0]≤2. A5 has cyclic subgroup orders only 1,2,3,5.

If |H_0|=n>1, the pair is exactly the two fixed points of that cyclic subgroup C_n. Every element normalizing C_n preserves this pair. Conversely every element preserving the pair normalizes C_n, since the subgroup fixing both points is H_0. Thus H=N_A5(C_n). The normalizers have orders 4,6,10 for n=2,3,5 respectively:

- for C2 the normalizer is its centralizer V4, of order four;
- for C3 the normalizer has order six;
- for C5 the normalizer has order ten.

For example these orders follow from the fifteen involutions, ten C3 subgroups and six C5 subgroups of A5, by orbit–stabilizer for conjugation. Their pair-orbits therefore have sizes fifteen, ten and six. They are precisely the respective rotation-axis directions. Distinct cyclic subgroups of these maximal orders have distinct fixed pairs, since the pointwise pair stabilizer is cyclic and A5 has no cyclic group of larger compatible order.

If H_0 is trivial, then |H|≤2, so the orbit has size sixty or thirty. Therefore the complete list of possible orbit sizes off C is

                              6, 10, 15, 30, 60.             (2)

In particular every orbit of size at most twenty-one off C is one of the three unique axis orbits. This argument does not assume a real representative for an arbitrary complex point.

## 3. Exhaustion up to a single binary alternative

Let Δ denote the rank-drop scheme of E, and a a point of its support. Since E_a has rank one, there is a unique line [q_a] in P(Q) with q_a|K_a=0. Equivariance makes q_a the same projective channel on the entire orbit of a. Thus that orbit lies in Z_(q_a), and (1) bounds its size by twenty-one. Since Δ is disjoint from C, (2) applies.

It follows that

       Supp(Δ) is either the sixteen C5/C3 axes,
       or all thirty-one C5/C3/C2 axes.                      (3)

There are no other secant-detection exceptions, including no thirty-point or sixty-point orbits. If even one C2 axis is exceptional, equivariance makes all fifteen exceptional. Every exceptional point has exact rank one and a mixed kernel line by PR663. Hence there are at most three exceptional projective channels [q]∈P(Q); all other nonzero families are detected on K at every direction of X.

This strengthens finiteness to complete support classification with only one unresolved yes/no orbit. It does not decide whether the C2 orbit occurs, or whether the two already known channel lines agree.

## 4. Further exact constraints on fixed-channel multiplicities

For a fixed q, invariant scheme length is constant along each axis orbit. Let m_5,m_3,m_2 be its local lengths there, with zero if that orbit is absent. Then

                    6m_5 + 10m_3 + 15m_2 ≤ 21.             (4)

All m_i are nonnegative integers. Consequently m_5≤3, m_3≤2 and m_2≤1. In particular any nonempty C2 contribution is reduced. A fixed channel cannot annihilate both the C2 and C3 axis orbits, since 15+10=25>21. Thus, if C2 axes are exceptional, their kernel line is DISTINCT from the C3 kernel line. This is a normalization-independent distinction between exceptional channels, not a numerical parity-ratio computation.

If the C3 and C5 channels coincide, their common fixed-channel zero scheme has (m_5,m_3,m_2)=(1,1,0), and is reduced of length sixteen: every increased multiplicity would raise the length to at least twenty-two. If a C2 channel equals the C5 channel, its fixed-channel zero scheme has (m_5,m_3,m_2)=(1,0,1), and is reduced of length twenty-one. These statements concern Z_q, not the possibly different infinitesimal structure of Δ; the rank-drop scheme's length is not bounded here.
