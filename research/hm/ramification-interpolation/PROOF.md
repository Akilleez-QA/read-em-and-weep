# Ramification interpolation and the saturated complementary-value module

## Statement and scope

Work over the complex numbers with the actual smooth bicubic D in X = P(Wprime) × P(W). The first factor is positive. Let R be the ramification scheme of D → P(W), the negative projection. The pinned actual geometry proves that R is reduced of length twelve and projects injectively to both lines. No coordinates or coefficients of the actual bicubic are evaluated below.

For every integer n ≥ 2, restriction is onto:

    H0(X,O(4,n)) → H0(R,O_R(4,n)).

Its kernel K_n has dimension 5n−7. In particular K_2 has dimension three, K_4 has dimension thirteen, and K_6 has dimension twenty-three. At fixed positive degree four, negative degree two is the smallest nonnegative degree permitting arbitrary interpolation on all twelve ramification pairs. This is a sharp geometric interpolation statement, not a claim that every interpolant is the symbol of an admissible middle operator.

The three-dimensional kernel K_2 has explicit universal Bezout generators. The unsaturated bihomogeneous ideal generated by the positive partial derivatives misses precisely 5−n independent kernel sections for n=2,3,4. It misses none in bidegree (4,n) for n≥5. Thus a search using only polynomial multiples of the two positive derivatives would impose spurious restrictions at the three lowest relevant degrees.

## 1. Scheme-theoretic ramification ideal

Fix determinant-compatible homogeneous coordinates (s,t) on the positive line and (y0,y1) on the negative line, solely to write universal formulas. Write F for any nonzero generator of the actual invariant bicubic line, and put

    G = ∂F/∂s,   H = ∂F/∂t.

Both are sections of O(2,3). Euler's identity 3F=sG+tH implies that their common zero scheme lies on D. On the chart s≠0, write x=t/s and F=s³f(x,y). Then H=s² f_x and G=s²(3f−x f_x), so the ideal (G,H) equals (f,f_x). This is exactly the ramification scheme of the negative projection of the smooth curve D. The same argument applies on t≠0. Therefore the common zero scheme is R, including its scheme structure.

Because R is finite on the smooth surface X, G,H are a regular sequence in each local ring where needed. Their Koszul complex gives

    0 → O(-4,-6) → O(-2,-3)² → I_R → 0.        (1)

The first arrow is (-H,G), and the second is (G,H). The two-dimensional coefficient space in the middle is the positive derivative-generator space. Intrinsically the first term also carries its determinant line. Our fixed determinant trivialization identifies that line with the ground field; for the binary icosahedral group this determinant character is trivial. Rescaling F scales both generators and the Koszul arrows coherently; no relative monad or Atiyah normalization is changed.

## 2. Exact interpolation and its hidden generators

Twist (1) by O(4,n):

    0 → O(0,n−6) → O(2,n−3)² → I_R(4,n) → 0.   (2)

For n≥2, both H1(O(2,n−3)) and H2(O(0,n−6)) vanish by the elementary cohomology of P1 and Kunneth. Consequently H1(I_R(4,n))=0. The sequence of restriction to R proves the asserted surjectivity. Since R is reduced of length twelve, every line bundle on R has twelve-dimensional global sections. Since h0 O(4,n)=5(n+1), the kernel has dimension 5n−7.

More precisely (2) gives the canonical exact sequence

    0 → H0 O(0,n−6) → H0 O(2,n−3)² → K_n
      → H1 O(0,n−6) → 0.                         (3)

For n=2 the middle space is zero and K_2 ≅ H1(P(W),O(-4)). For n=3 and n=4, the ordinary derivative multiples contribute dimensions six and twelve, while the final term contributes two and one. For n≥5 the final term vanishes. For n≥6 the initial term accounts for the ordinary Koszul relation and must not be omitted.

For n=0,1 the domain H0 O(4,n) has dimension five or ten, respectively, so it cannot surject onto the twelve-dimensional target. This proves minimality without computing any actual evaluation matrix or rank.

Under the actual binary icosahedral marking, K_2 is the negative quadratic representation A (with the fixed determinant convention). Since H0 O(4,2)=D5 tensor A=A+V+P4+D5, the evaluated value space is V+P4+D5. This is the fiber-weighted module H0(R,O_R(4,2)), not the ordinary permutation module on twelve points. These are consequences of the exact sequence, not measurements at the twelve points. Choosing scalar fiber trivializations does not remove these transformation weights.

## 3. Universal Bezout generators for K_2

Introduce a second formal negative pair y'=(y0',y1'). The numerator

    G(s,t;y) H(s,t;y') − H(s,t;y) G(s,t;y')

vanishes when y and y' are proportional. Hence it is divisible by the determinant y0 y1'−y1 y0'. The quotient B has positive degree four and negative degrees two in each of y,y'. Expand

    B(s,t;y,y') = B0(s,t;y)(y0')²
               + B1(s,t;y)y0'y1' + B2(s,t;y)(y1')².    (4)

At a common zero y of G,H, the numerator is identically zero in y'. Since the determinant is a nonzero polynomial in y' for every projective y, all three B_i vanish there. The same conclusion scheme-theoretically follows by the polynomial identity modulo the ideal (G,H) on either negative affine chart. Thus B_i belong to K_2.

They are linearly independent over the ground field. Over the function field of the positive line, G and H are coprime binary cubics: a common projective root would give a component of R dominating the positive line, impossible since R is finite. The binary cubic Bezout matrix is therefore nonsingular. For completeness this universal fact follows by dehomogenizing and, on the dense locus where the first cubic has three distinct finite roots α_i, evaluating the Bezout form at (α_i,α_j): the off-diagonal terms are zero, and diagonal terms are nonzero multiples of G'(α_i)H(α_i). Change from coefficients to evaluation is an invertible Vandermonde matrix. The resulting determinant identity with the resultant is polynomial, so extends to all coprime pairs, including repeated roots and a root at infinity. The companion verifier also checks the universal cubic determinant identity with eight free coefficients.

Any constant linear relation among B0,B1,B2 would therefore give a nonzero constant vector in the kernel of an invertible matrix over that function field, a contradiction. Since dim K_2=3, they form a basis. Formula (4) is an explicit saturated-ideal construction, requiring no selected actual coefficients. A change of negative coordinates changes this basis through the negative quadratic representation and its determinant factors; the subspace K_2 is canonical. Rescaling F multiplies all B_i by the common square scalar and preserves that subspace.

## 4. A complete generator recipe in every degree (4,n)

For every n≥2, K_n is generated by the following two finite families:

- G and H multiplied by a monomial basis of biforms of degree (2,n−3), when n≥3;
- B0,B1,B2 multiplied by a monomial basis of negative forms of degree n−2.

For n≥5 the first family already suffices. For n=2,3,4, naturality of the connecting map in (3) identifies the image of the second family in its final term with the multiplication map

    H1(O(-4)) tensor H0(O(n−2)) → H1(O(n−6))

on the negative line. This map is onto. Indeed, under Serre duality, the dual map sends a polynomial q of degree 4−n to the linear map p ↦ p q from H0(O(n−2)) to H0(O(2)). Its kernel is therefore the set of q satisfying p q=0 for every p of degree n−2. The polynomial ring is a domain and there exist nonzero such p, so this kernel is zero and the original map is onto. The first family already spans the kernel of the connecting map, proving the recipe.

In particular the missing one-dimensional class in degree (4,4) is supplied by quadratic negative multiples of the Bezout generators, and the missing two-dimensional space in degree (4,3) by linear multiples. This gives an explicit saturated-kernel construction, not only its dimension. It does not assert that individual generators have preferred scalar normalizations or are admissible middle-operator symbols.

## 5. Relation to the complementary-middle interface

The pinned next-division note proves, at R and for source inputs with p_C(w,z)=Abar(v,u),

    lambda(E_C(z)−E_A(u)−(b_m/a_m)E_B(u))
       = 2k a(w)² h(v) z(w).                       (5)

We do not re-prove or enlarge that statement. On the positive null conic v=r², h(v) has positive degree four; a(w)² has negative degree four. For a fixed quadratic source input z, z(w) has negative degree two. Thus the right side of (5), written in its pinned homogeneous scalar convention, has bidegree (4,6), not (4,2). Before evaluation of z, its scalar coefficient 2k a²h has bidegree (4,4).

The interpolation theorem applies to these two scalar line bundles with kernels K_6 and K_4, respectively. It says that arbitrary twelve scalar values in either of these fibers can be extended to a biform; all choices differ by the explicitly described saturated kernel. It does not identify O_R(4,2) with either physical line bundle, does not divide by a chosen nonvanishing actual polynomial, and does not promote arbitrary independent values to a globally compatible linear map on the source inputs. The degree-two theorem is the sharp lower-degree geometric benchmark, separate from the physical degree-six balance.

This resolves the scalar interpolation dependency: there is no further obstruction from extending the twelve scalar values alone. The unresolved task is whether the required value section is in the image of the restricted symbol map of a genuine, globally compatible middle operator and its coupled source equations. The three missing low-degree saturated generators and the precise affine freedoms K_4 and K_6 prevent a polynomial ansatz from confusing absence of derivative-multiple representatives with a genuine obstruction. No full cochain, actual selected-source evaluation, fourth Bockstein value, higher-order lifting, or algebraicity conclusion is supplied.
