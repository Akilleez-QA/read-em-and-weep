## 2. Homogeneous Koszul identity and denominators

Let \(\varepsilon_i\) denote exterior multiplication by \(e_i\) and \(\iota_i\) contraction by \(e_i^*\). Using
\(\iota_i\varepsilon_j+\varepsilon_j\iota_i=\delta_{ij}\), the product rule gives

\[
\delta d+d\delta
=5+\sum_i x_i\partial_i-\sum_i\varepsilon_i\iota_i.
\]

The Euler operator is multiplication by polynomial degree \(a\), while \(\sum_i\varepsilon_i\iota_i\) is multiplication by exterior degree \(p\). Thus the displayed constant \(5+a-p\) is correct for the proof's **wedge** differential. It is not the constant for the alternative contraction convention.

For a closed quadratic two-form, \((a,p)=(2,2)\), so the primitive is \(\delta r/5\), a linear one-form. In components this proves
\(r_{ij}=x_i b_j-x_j b_i\) with linear \(b_i\).

For a closed sextic one-form, \((a,p)=(6,1)\), so the primitive is \(\delta q/10\), a quintic scalar. This proves \(q_i=x_i h\). Both denominators are nonzero over \(\mathbb C\), and all degrees and alternating signs match the identities used later.
