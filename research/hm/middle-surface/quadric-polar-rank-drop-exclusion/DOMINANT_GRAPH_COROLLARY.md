# Degree-two gcd excludes dominant graph threefolds

Suppose a K7 compatible ordered threefold dominates both factors. The earlier exclusions ensure generic polar rank three and zero common radical. A cubic-minor gcd of degree three gives a constant partner, incompatible with dominance. If its degree is two, the primitive cofactor vector is Ta for a linear map T; second-factor dominance forces T invertible.

The invariant-form classification in PROOF.md applies without assuming gcd irreducibility. Having three independent symmetric forms with T^tB+BT=0 forces T semisimple with opposite eigenvalue multiplicities (3,1) or (2,2). For (3,1), choose a nonzero vector e in the one-dimensional eigenspace; for (2,2), choose either eigenspace. Differentiating dense graph compatibility toward the opposite eigenspace yields at least two independent tangent differences at e and forces v(e²)∈Ce.

Thus a dominant compatible graph can only have maximal-minor gcd degree at most one. This finer restriction was independently audited in AUDIT.md. The subsequent universal diagonal–Hartogs theorem now excludes all K7 compatible threefolds, so this statement is retained as a valid intermediate result, not as the current open K7 frontier.
