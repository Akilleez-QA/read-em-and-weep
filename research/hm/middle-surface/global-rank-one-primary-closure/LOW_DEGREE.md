# Low-degree equations of the universal first-order rank-one closure

## Scope and setup

This is a formal universal-algebra calculation only. It neither evaluates any actual model operation nor identifies the full zero ideal of an independently supplied operation.

Work over C. Let V have dimension n (the application here is n=5), and set

S = C[x_1,...,x_n,y_1,...,y_n,z_1,...,z_n],

B = C[u_1,...,u_n,r_1,r_2,r_3,p_1,...,p_n]/(p_1,...,p_n)^2.

Define phi:S→B by x_i↦r_1(u_i+p_i), y_i↦r_2u_i, z_i↦r_3(u_i−p_i), and Q=ker(phi). Give S its three-block multigrading. Distinct multidegrees have distinct r monomials, so Q is multihomogeneous.

For a multihomogeneous F of degree (a,b,c), factoring out r_1^a r_2^b r_3^c gives the exact formula

phi(F)/(r_1^a r_2^b r_3^c) = F(u,u,u) + (D_xF−D_zF)_(u,u,u)[p].

Thus F belongs to Q exactly when both its diagonal value and the indicated first derivative vanish. This criterion is necessary as well as sufficient, since B is the direct sum of its p-degree-zero and p-degree-one parts.

## The complete answer through total degree three

For n=5:

- Q_0=Q_1=Q_2=0.
- Q_(a,b,c)=0 for a+b+c=3 unless (a,b,c)=(1,1,1).
- Q_(1,1,1) is the direct sum of a 40-dimensional outer-symmetric, total-symmetrization-zero space and the 10-dimensional alternating space.
- As a GL(V)-module, Q_3 ≅ S_(2,1)V* ⊕ Λ³V*.
- In particular, dim Q_3=50, and all 50 cubic directions are minimal homogeneous generator directions.

For general n, the two summands have dimensions n(n²−1)/3 and binomial(n,3), respectively.

This proves that the initial degree of Q is three when n≥2. It does not prove that Q is generated by cubics or describe its higher-degree minimal generators.

## Why there are no lower-degree equations

A polynomial supported in one block maps to the same polynomial in u in p-degree zero; this is injective. This handles linear forms and the single-block cases in degrees two and three.

For two distinct blocks write their inputs as A and B, with weights t_A,t_B drawn from 1,0,−1. All these weights are distinct. In degree two a possible relation is bilinear, and in degree three it has bidegree (2,1) after labeling the repeated block A and the single block B.

In either case F(A,B) is linear in B. If F(u,u)=0, differentiation of this polynomial identity yields

(D_AF+D_BF)_(u,u)[p]=0.

The first-order condition is therefore

(t_AD_AF+t_BD_BF)_(u,u)[p] = (t_B−t_A)(D_BF)_(u,u)[p]=0.

Write F(A,B)=sum_j f_j(A)B_j. Then the last expression is a nonzero scalar times sum_j f_j(u)p_j. Its vanishing forces every f_j=0, hence F=0. This proves injectivity in every two-block case under consideration. Distinct r monomials prohibit cancellation between different multidegrees.

## The trilinear kernel: an elementary proof

Identify a polynomial in S_(1,1,1) with a trilinear form T(A,B,C). Split T uniquely into its symmetric and antisymmetric parts under the outer transposition A↔C:

T=T_+ + T_-.

For T_+, the derivative T_+(p,u,u)−T_+(u,u,p) vanishes identically. Its remaining condition is T_+(u,u,u)=0, equivalently zero total symmetrization. The outer-symmetric space is Sym²(V*)⊗V*, with dimension n²(n+1)/2. Diagonal evaluation onto Sym³(V*) is surjective, so its kernel has dimension

n²(n+1)/2 − binomial(n+2,3) = n(n²−1)/3.

This kernel is the Schur module S_(2,1)V*.

For T_-, diagonal evaluation is automatically zero. Its first derivative equals 2T_-(p,u,u). Vanishing is equivalent, by polarization, to antisymmetry in the last two arguments. Together with antisymmetry in the outer arguments, this is precisely full alternation. Hence the surviving outer-antisymmetric part is Λ³V*.

These conditions separate without cancellation: diagonal evaluation depends only on T_+, and the first derivative depends only on T_-. This establishes the exact direct-sum description, not merely containment of the displayed equations.

## Explicit cubic equations and a 50-element basis for n=5

An outer-symmetric spanning family is

E_ijk = x_i y_j z_k + x_k y_j z_i − x_i y_k z_j − x_j y_k z_i.

After removing the common factor r_1r_2r_3, its universal substitution is

2p_i(u_k p_j−u_j p_k),

which is zero in B. Equivalently, E_ijk is outer-symmetric and has zero diagonal evaluation. This family spans the complete outer-symmetric kernel: the kernel of multiplication Sym²(V*)⊗V*→Sym³(V*) is generated by (ac)⊗b−(ab)⊗c.

The alternating equations are

D_ijk = det[[x_i,y_i,z_i],[x_j,y_j,z_j],[x_k,y_k,z_k]],   i<j<k.

They vanish under substitution because their three column vectors lie in the span of u and p, even before quotienting by (p)^2.

Here is a concrete basis, avoiding redundancy in the spanning family:

1. For every ordered pair i≠j, take

   B_ij = 2x_i y_j z_i − x_i y_i z_j − x_j y_i z_i.

   For n=5 these give 20 equations.

2. For each i<j<k, put

   A_i = x_j y_i z_k + x_k y_i z_j,
   A_j = x_i y_j z_k + x_k y_j z_i,
   A_k = x_i y_k z_j + x_j y_k z_i.

   Take A_i−A_j and A_i−A_k. For n=5 these give 20 equations.

3. Take D_ijk for each i<j<k. For n=5 these give 10 equations.

To verify independence and completeness, decompose the outer-symmetric space by index multisets. A multiset {i,i,i} contributes no kernel. Each {i,i,j}, i≠j, contributes one dimension, represented by B_ij. Each {i,j,k} with distinct indices contributes two dimensions, represented by the two A-differences. These account for the entire outer-symmetric kernel. The determinant equations form the standard basis of Λ³V*, in the complementary outer-antisymmetric space.

Since Q has no elements in degrees zero, one, or two, the degree-three part of S_+Q is zero. Consequently the above 50 equations give a basis of the degree-three part of Q/S_+Q, the space of minimal homogeneous generator directions. In particular every homogeneous generating set of Q requires these 50 independent degree-three directions; higher generators remain undetermined here.
