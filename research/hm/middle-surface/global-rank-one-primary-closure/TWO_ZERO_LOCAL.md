# The rank-one primary closure at the two-zero boundary

## Scope and result

Work over C, on the good common-line open B of the rank-ten hypothesis in `/research/hm/middle-surface/full-boundary-product-blowup/PROOF.md`. This note computes the schematic closure of that specified rank-one thickening from the all-inputs-nonzero locus. It does **not** identify the full original zero scheme X with this primary piece and does not determine the other components or embedded structure of X.

At any nonzero rank-one point with exactly one surviving input, the primary closure has local algebra

    T = A ⋉ I^{⊕4},       I=(a,c)⊂A,

where A is the smooth seven-dimensional reduced rank-one chart and (a,c) cuts out the two-zero center. The symbol ⋉ denotes the split square-zero extension, with multiplication (f,m)(g,n)=(fg,fn+gm). At closed center points this ring has dimension 7, depth 6 and embedding dimension 15. Its nilradical is torsion-free of rank four but is not locally free at the center. It has a single associated prime and is primary, despite failing Cohen–Macaulayness. Its product-input-ideal blowup is the finite modification

    T ⊂ R=A ⋉ A⁴,

which is an isomorphism off the center and has length-five square-zero fiber over every closed center point.

## 1. Explicit coordinates and contraction: middle input survives

Choose a linear functional ell with ell nonzero on the surviving vector and write

    y=tU,  ell(U)=1,  t invertible;
    x=aU+p,  z=cU+q,  p,q∈ker(ell).

Here U contributes four coordinates. On a sufficiently small affine good-line chart, let A=O(B_chart)[t,t^{-1},a,c], with localization as needed. These are actual algebraic coordinates in the ambient open: its ring is S=A[p_1,…,p_4,q_1,…,q_4]. The reduced rank-one locus is p=q=0.

The imported exact normalized projective thickening and its structure map are

    x=r_1(u+v), y=r_2u, z=r_3(u−v), (v)²=0.

On the current chart this becomes U=u, t=r_2, a=r_1, c=r_3, and

    p_i ↦ a v_i,       q_i ↦ −c v_i

in R=A[v_1,…,v_4]/(v)². Its image is exactly A⊕I^{⊕4}. The kernel is

    Q=((p,q)², c p_i+a q_i for i=1,…,4).                 (1)

Indeed, after killing (p,q)² the algebra is a split square-zero extension by the free A-module on the eight p_i,q_i. For each i the map A²→I, (s,t)↦as−ct, has kernel generated by (c,a). This is the usual syzygy of the regular sequence a,c. Thus the four displayed linear-in-nilpotents relations give exactly I^{⊕4}, with no additional relations.

This is contraction from the good all-nonzero primary core, not just an arbitrary image model. On D(ac), the imported core has precisely this map and kernel. Multiplication by ac is injective in R, because its underlying A-module is free. Hence

    ker(S→R)=ker(S→R[(ac)^{-1}])
             = contraction to S of the primary-core ideal on D(ac).

Equivalently, Q is (ac)-saturated and defines the schematic closure of that core in this chart. The same argument works with additional localizations restricting to the good common-line neighborhood. Any global primary closure defined by contraction from this core therefore restricts to (1).

## 2. Both outer-surviving cases, including the factor two

The trilinear equation need not be invariant under all permutations; no such symmetry is assumed here. Instead use the same proven normalized map and change coordinates explicitly.

If the first input survives, set U=u+v_old, so x=tU, and set v=−v_old. Then

    y=bU+q, z=cU+r,     q=bv, r=2cv.

Thus the kernel is

    ((q,r)², 2c q_i−b r_i : i=1,…,4).

After replacing r_i by r_i/2, this is the presentation of A⋉(b,c)^{⊕4}.

If the third input survives, set U=u−v_old and v=v_old. Then

    z=tU, x=aU+p, y=bU+q,     p=2av, q=bv.

The kernel is

    ((p,q)², b p_i−2a q_i : i=1,…,4).

After replacing p_i by p_i/2, this is A⋉(a,b)^{⊕4}. In both cases U has ell(U)=1 because ell(v_old)=0. The changes u↔U are invertible square-zero coordinate changes, and the good-line open remains valid because its reduced point is unchanged. Contraction follows just as in Section 1, by inverting the product of the two vanishing-slot scalar coordinates. These formulas cover all three two-zero strata without invoking unproved permutation invariance.

## 3. Primary structure and nilmodule

The nilradical N of T is I^{⊕4}, and T/N=A is a domain on the chosen chart. If f∈A is nonzero, multiplication by (f,m) on T is injective: its scalar component forces the other scalar component to vanish, and torsion-freeness of N then forces its nil component to vanish. Conversely every element of N is square-zero. Hence all zero divisors are nilpotent, Q is primary, and Ass(T) consists of its unique minimal prime N. This assertion concerns the primary closure alone.

The ideal I has rank one and is free away from V(a,c). At a center local ring, its minimal number of generators is two and its rank is one, so it is not free. Consequently N has rank four and minimal number of generators eight at the center. Although the generic multiplicity remains five, this primary closure is not a locally free degree-five algebra over its smooth reduction at the center. Indeed the closed fiber of T over A at a center point is C⋉C⁸, of length nine.

There is an exact sequence of A-modules

    0 → A⁴ → A⁸ → N → 0,

with the first map the four separate syzygies (c,a), up to the harmless signs and factors two above. It is minimal at a center point.

## 4. Depth, tangent space, and singularity properties

At a closed center point A is regular local of dimension 7. The displayed minimal resolution gives pd_A(N)=1 and depth_A(N)=6. Therefore

    depth(T)=depth_A(A⊕N)=6,       dim(T)=7.

One can compute depth over A because A→T is finite local and its maximal ideal extends to an ideal with radical the maximal ideal of T. Equivalently, depth is the local cohomological depth for that extended ideal. The embedding dimension is

    dim_C(m_T/m_T²)
      =dim_C(m_A/m_A²)+dim_C(N/m_A N)
      =7+8=15.

All generators in (1) are at least quadratic at the center. In fact (1) has 40 minimal quadratic generators there: 36 square monomials in the eight nil coordinates and four independent scalar–nil relations. Their quadratic classes are linearly independent. The primary piece therefore has the full ambient fifteen-dimensional tangent space, despite dimension seven.

It is not Cohen–Macaulay at a center point and hence is neither Gorenstein nor a local complete intersection there. No Cohen–Macaulay type is assigned to this non-CM ring. Away from the center I is principal and the presentation becomes A[v_1,…,v_4]/(v)². At closed points there it has dimension/depth 7, tangent dimension 11, and Cohen–Macaulay type four; it remains non-Gorenstein and non-lci.

More generally at a possibly nonclosed center point with d=dim(A_local), depth(T_local)=d−1 and embedding dimension d+8 over its residue field. In particular at the generic point of the center the primary closure has dimension 2 and depth 1. It satisfies S₁, since it has no embedded associated primes, but fails S₂ there. The local generic component multiplicity is five throughout; this is distinct from the length-nine reduction fiber or the length-five blowup fiber.

## 5. Exact product-ideal blowup and comparison

Use the middle-surviving coordinates; the rescaled outer cases are identical. The surviving input ideal is the unit ideal. The two other input ideals in T are generated respectively by a,p_i and c,q_i. Under T⊂R their product J is

    J = ac A ⊕ (ac A)⁴ = ac R ⊂ T.

The nil generators of J can be taken as ac v_i, and (ac) itself is regular. In the intrinsic Rees blowup, each chart corresponding to a nilpotent generator ac v_i is empty: its degree-one homogeneous element is nilpotent. The single remaining chart is

    T[J/(ac)] = T[v_1,…,v_4] = R.

It covers the blowup, proving Bl_J(Spec T)=Spec R. This recovers exactly the good-diagonal product-blowup chart in the imported proof, now as the blowup of this primary closure. This does not identify the entire blowup of X with this chart, nor exclude other directions of its projective fiber.

The map Spec R→Spec T is finite: R is generated over A, hence over T, by 1,v_1,…,v_4. It is an isomorphism when either a or c is invertible. There is an exact sequence

    0 → T → R → (A/I)⁴ → 0,

and the conductor is I⊕I⁴=IR⊂T. Over a closed center point all target nil generators av_i,cv_i vanish after a=c=0, so its scheme fiber is C[v]/(v)², of length five. This finite modification is not flat at the center. For example, finite flatness near a center point would have rank one because it is an isomorphism generically, contradicting that fiber length.

The blowup algebra R is locally free of rank five over A and has dimension/depth 7, tangent dimension 11, Cohen–Macaulay type four, and is neither Gorenstein nor lci. Thus this explicit boundary extraction repairs the nilmodule's nonfreeness and the depth defect, while retaining nonreduced rank-four square-zero structure. It is not a resolution to a smooth scheme and is not a normalization claim for nonreduced schemes.

## 6. Intrinsic Hartogs extension and the local S₂ repair

Let j denote the inclusion of the complement of the two-zero center in Spec T. Its underlying topological space is the corresponding open in Spec A. Since A is regular and the center has codimension two, Hartogs extension gives j_*O_A=O_A. Moreover I restricts to O_A on that complement. It follows, including multiplication, that

    j_*O_(Spec T minus center) = O_A ⋉ O_A⁴ = R.

This identifies the finite enlargement intrinsically from T and its open complement; it is not dependent on choosing the displayed nil basis. In the displayed split A-module presentation it is also the reflexive hull:

    T^{**}_A = A ⊕ (I^{**})⁴ = A⊕A⁴ = R,

because I^{**}=A for this codimension-two ideal in a regular domain. The algebra structure on that hull is the one uniquely extending the multiplication on the open complement. Consequently the product-ideal blowup here is the finite Hartogs/S₂ extension of the primary piece, and it is Cohen–Macaulay. This usage of S₂ extension concerns the nonreduced algebra itself and should not be confused with normalization of its reduction. Its canonicity can be stated through j_* without claiming a globally chosen splitting or identifying the complete original scheme X.
