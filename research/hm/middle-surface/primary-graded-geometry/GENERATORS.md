# Global homogeneous generation of the primary kernel

## Result

Use the notation and universal square-zero substitution of `../global-rank-one-primary-closure/PROOF.md`, and the fifty-dimensional cubic space calculated in `../global-rank-one-primary-closure/LOW_DEGREE.md`. Over C, for the five-by-three generic matrix,

    Q = (Q_3) + P^2.

Consequently the fifty displayed cubic basis elements, together with all products of pairs of the thirty two-by-two minors, generate Q. There are no minimal generators in degrees greater than four.

Cubics alone do **not** generate Q. They all have column multidegree (1,1,1), whereas, for example,

    (x_1 y_2 - x_2 y_1)^2

is a nonzero element of Q of multidegree (2,2,0). The same applies to the other two column pairs. Each of these three multidegrees contributes fifty independent minimal quartic generator directions, hence at least 150 minimal quartic directions altogether. We do not assert here that this lower bound is the exact total number of minimal quartics.

The argument below explicitly retains and computes the vertex torsion of the conormal module. It does not infer graded exactness merely from a sheaf calculation.

## 1. Cauchy decomposition and a multiplication fact

Let U be the five-dimensional space of row coordinate forms and W the three-dimensional space of column coordinate forms. Thus

    R = Sym(U tensor W),
    R_d = direct sum over lambda partitioning d of
          S_lambda U tensor S_lambda W,

with at most three rows in lambda. Denote the summand by E_lambda. The rank-one coordinate ring A=R/P has A_d=E_(d).

We use the elementary Cauchy–Pieri multiplication rule: the ideal generated by E_lambda is the sum of E_mu for partitions mu containing lambda. One way to see the rule is to multiply successively by R_1. Pieri decomposes U tensor S_lambda U and W tensor S_lambda W by adding one box; in the polynomial-ring multiplication, precisely the matching shapes survive the Cauchy decomposition, each with nonzero coefficient. Nonvanishing follows from the Young symmetrizer realization of the Cauchy summands (equivalently, the one-box straightening rule for bideterminants). Iteration gives every containing diagram; no smaller or noncontaining diagram can occur by Pieri. Characteristic zero is used here.

In particular this is a representation-theoretic calculation in a generic polynomial ring, not an evaluation of any model matrix or source operation.

## 2. The square of the minor ideal, including its small-degree exception

Put Delta_i equal to the determinant of the upper-left i-by-i generic submatrix. The highest weight vectors for E_(2,2) and E_(2,1,1) are respectively

    Delta_2^2,             Delta_1 Delta_3.

Both belong to P^2. For the second assertion the exact polynomial identity is

    X_11 det(X_[123],[123])
      = det(X_[12],[12]) det(X_[13],[13])
        - det(X_[12],[13]) det(X_[13],[12]).

These statements use formal variables X_ij only.

Conversely, the degree-four Cauchy summands are E_(4), E_(3,1), E_(2,2), E_(2,1,1). The first is excluded from P. The summand E_(3,1) is excluded from P^2: the first normal derivative of its highest weight vector Delta_1^2 Delta_2 on the rank-one cone is nonzero, while every product of two elements of P has zero first normal derivative. Since the summands are irreducible and P^2 is GL(U) times GL(W)-stable, this proves

    (P^2)_4 = E_(2,2) direct sum E_(2,1,1).

P^2 is generated in degree four. The preceding multiplication rule now gives the exact formula

    P^2 = sum of E_lambda with lambda containing (2,2) or (2,1,1).

Thus, in degrees d at least four, the only summands of R/P^2 are E_(d) and E_(d-1,1). In degree three there is the additional E_(1,1,1). In degree two the conormal part is E_(1,1).

This proves in particular that the ten three-by-three determinants survive in P/P^2 in degree three, but every positive-degree coordinate multiple of them lies in P^2. They form an actual finite-length vertex module, not a missing saturated equation.

## 3. The exact graded conormal comparison

Let X=P(U dual) times P(W dual), with the Segre polarization L=O(1,1). Its conormal bundle in the ambient projective space is

    Omega_U external tensor Omega_W.

This identification can also be read directly from the first normal derivative of a minor: on the rank-one parametrization it is the external product of the corresponding row and column Euler syzygies. Set

    Csec = direct sum over d>=0 of
           H^0(P^4,Omega_U(d)) tensor H^0(P^2,Omega_W(d)).

The normal-derivative homomorphism induces a graded A-module map

    P/P^2 --> Csec.

For d>=2 its target is E_(d-1,1). It is nonzero, hence surjective, on that irreducible summand: multiply a two-by-two minor by a power of a coordinate. Equivalently, multiplication of degree-two Euler syzygies by degree-(d-2) polynomials spans both factors. The decomposition in Section 2 therefore proves the exact graded sequence

    0 --> T --> P/P^2 --> Csec --> 0,

where

    T_3 = exterior^3 U tensor exterior^3 W,
    T_d = 0 for d != 3,       A_+ T = 0.

Here dim(T_3)=10. This calculation proves graded exactness at the vertex as well as away from it. No assumption of torsion-freeness or saturation of P/P^2 has entered.

## 4. Contraction by the column vector field

On P^2 let v be the projective vector field induced by diag(1,0,-1). Its zero scheme Z consists of the three reduced coordinate points. For example, near [1:0:0], using affine coordinates b=r_2/r_1 and c=r_3/r_1, its components are -b and -2c. Thus its zeros form a regular codimension-two zero scheme.

Contraction with v has the exact Koszul sequence

    0 --> O_P2(-3) --> Omega_W --> I_Z --> 0.

The left term is det(Omega_W)=O(-3). Twisting by d and taking global sections is exact also on the right, since H^1(P^2,O(d-3))=0 for every integer d. In particular its graded kernel is exactly H^0(O(d-3)), zero for d<3.

The derivative used in defining Q is precisely this contraction on the column factor of the normal derivative. For a minor involving columns j,k, contraction sends its column Euler syzygy to

    (t_k-t_j) r_j r_k,       (t_1,t_2,t_3)=(1,0,-1),

which is the minor image in the defining substitution, up to the consistent row-sign convention. These identities on the minor generators prove compatibility as A-module maps in every degree.

The image is exactly N=P/Q, as can either be seen from its definition or from the all-degree computation in `HILBERT.md`. Thus, if K is the kernel of Csec --> N, then

    K_d = H^0(P^4,Omega_U(d)) tensor H^0(P^2,O(d-3))
        = S_(d-1,1) U tensor Sym^(d-3) W       for d>=3,
    K_d = 0                                              for d<3.

Together with Section 3 this gives the exact graded sequence

    0 --> T --> Q/P^2 --> K --> 0.

This is the complete graded conormal kernel, with its finite-length vertex term included.

## 5. Generation in degree three modulo P squared

The module K is generated in degree three. Indeed, for k>=0 the multiplication map

    H^0(O_P4(k)) tensor H^0(Omega_U(3))
      --> H^0(Omega_U(k+3))

is surjective: it is the nonzero Cartan multiplication onto the irreducible S_(k+2,1) U. The analogous map

    H^0(O_P2(k)) tensor H^0(O_P2(0))
      --> H^0(O_P2(k))

is an isomorphism. Since A_k=Sym^k U tensor Sym^k W, taking their tensor product is exactly the A-module multiplication K_3 --> K_(k+3), and is surjective.

In degree three, P^2 has no elements, so the exact sequence above reads

    0 --> T_3 --> Q_3 --> K_3 --> 0.

Its dimensions are 10,50,40, as independently calculated in LOW_DEGREE.md. Hence Q_3 contains generators for T and lifts generators for K. For any homogeneous class in Q/P^2, first subtract an A-linear combination of these lifts to kill its image in K. The residual class lies in T, which is itself generated in degree three. This proves Q/P^2 is generated by Q_3. Lifting coefficients from A to R proves

    Q=(Q_3)+P^2.

## 6. Necessary quartics in the two-column multidegrees

The all-multigraded formula of HILBERT.md gives, for alpha=(2,2,0),

    dim R_alpha = 15^2 = 225,
    dim (R/Q)_alpha = dim Sym^4 U + dim S_(3,1)U
                    = 70 + 105 = 175,
    dim Q_alpha = 50.

Every lower-degree equation has degree three and involves all three columns, so (R_+ Q)_alpha=0. Thus this entire fifty-dimensional space is minimal in degree four. The same holds for (2,0,2) and (0,2,2). It is the S_(2,2)U space generated by products of minors in the corresponding column pair.

The displayed redundant cubic-plus-quartic generating set is therefore globally complete, and the need for quartics is genuine. Establishing the exact minimal quartic count beyond this lower bound would require an additional calculation of the multiplication R_1 tensor Q_3 --> Q_4; it is not needed for the generation theorem proved here.
