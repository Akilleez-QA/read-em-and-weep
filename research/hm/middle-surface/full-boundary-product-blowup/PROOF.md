# Product-ideal blowup: primary thickening through the full zero-input boundary

## Theorem

Work over C. Let V have dimension five, let f:V³→J be the canonical trilinear map with outer symmetry and the cyclic-zero identity, and let X=V(f)⊂M=V³. Assume the repeated-input injection and the rank-ten residual quadratic hypothesis at each common line under consideration, exactly as in the imported open-thickening theorem. Let I_i⊂O_X be the coordinate ideal of the origin in input i and put K=I_1I_2I_3. Write

- P=P(V)³, with tautological lines L_1,L_2,L_3;
- Z⊂P for the scheme cut out by the components of f, viewed as sections of O_P(1,1,1);
- Δ⊂P for the diagonal;
- B⊂P(V) for the open set of common lines where the residual quadratic map has rank ten.

Then the following statements hold.

1. On the entire scheme X, without any rank restriction,

       Bl_K X ≅ Tot_Z(L_1⊕L_2⊕L_3).

   This is canonically the scheme-theoretic strict transform of X in the ambient blowup Bl_(I_1^M I_2^M I_3^M) M. The ambient blowup is the product of the three origin blowups and is Tot_P(L_1⊕L_2⊕L_3). Here strict transform means the scheme-theoretic closure of the inverse image of the open where all three inputs are nonzero. It is not the full inverse image of X.
2. There is an open Z°⊂Z whose reduction is precisely Δ_B and which contains every point of Δ_B. It is a square-zero thickening with locally free nilradical of rank four. The second projective projection gives a canonical finite locally free retraction Z°→B of degree five. The first and third projections also give retractions, although they need not agree as scheme morphisms.
3. Put W°=Tot_(Z°)(L_1⊕L_2⊕L_3), an open of Bl_K X. Its reduction is

       D̃_B=Tot_B(O_B(-1)⊕O_B(-1)⊕O_B(-1)).

   Its nilradical is square-zero and locally free of rank four on D̃_B. Locally it has algebra O_(D̃_B)[p_1,…,p_4]/(p)², so it retains generic multiplicity five everywhere along the resolved rank-one component, including two-zero and all-zero scalar strata. Local retractions are finite locally free of degree five. No global retraction of W° to D̃_B is asserted.
4. The reduction map D̃_B→X sends (ℓ,x,y,z), with x,y,z∈ℓ, to (x,y,z). It resolves the choice of common line at the vertex. Over a nonzero rank-one point of X with common line in B, the scheme fiber of W°→X is a reduced point when at least two inputs are nonzero, and is the length-five square-zero algebra C⊕C⁴ when precisely one input is nonzero. Over the all-zero vertex, the fiber is Z° itself. For the full blowup the vertex fiber is the full projective scheme Z.

The last statement is important: preservation of degree-five local transverse structure does not mean that every fiber of the blowup has length five, or that the blowup is finite over X. Its vertex fiber is positive dimensional. No classification is made of the other projective directions in Z, or of embedded structure in the original two-zero or vertex germs.

## 1. Rees charts, including zero divisors

We use the Rees algebra in its image definition:

    Rees_A(K)=⊕_(n≥0) K^n t^n ⊂ A[t].

If g is one of a finite set of ideal generators, the chart D_+(gt) has coordinate ring

    A[K/g] := image(A[T_h : h a generator of K] → A[1/g]),
    T_h ↦ h/g.

This formula remains valid when g is a zero divisor or nilpotent. Indeed a degree-zero Rees fraction has the form a/g^n with a∈K^n; such a fraction is zero precisely when some power of g kills a. This is exactly the kernel arising on mapping to A[1/g]. If g is nilpotent, this chart is empty. No cancellation inside A is assumed.

For a closed subscheme X=Spec(A_0/F) in an ambient affine scheme and an ambient blowup chart C⊂A_0[1/g], its induced map to A[1/g] has kernel

    ker(C→A[1/g]) = (F C : g^∞),

provided C[1/g]=A_0[1/g]. Consequently the intrinsic blowup chart is the schematic closure, in the ambient chart, of X over D(g). This is an image/subalgebra argument, not a claim that Rees formation commutes with closed base change. We verify the needed equalities explicitly below.

## 2. The ambient product blowup

Choose coordinates x_a,y_b,z_c on the three copies of V. The ambient product ideal is generated by all 125 monomials x_a y_b z_c. Fix g=x_a y_b z_c. Its blowup chart contains each ratio

    x_d/x_a = (x_d y_b z_c)/g,
    y_e/y_b = (x_a y_e z_c)/g,
    z_h/z_c = (x_a y_b z_h)/g.

Conversely every product-generator ratio is a product of these three types. Thus its coordinate ring is the polynomial ring

    C[v_1,v_2,v_3,r_1,r_2,r_3],

where the selected coordinate of each v_i equals one, each v_i therefore contributes four free coordinates, and x=r_1v_1, y=r_2v_2, z=r_3v_3. Here r_1=x_a, r_2=y_b, r_3=z_c and g=r_1r_2r_3. The ring has fifteen free coordinates. These are exactly the charts and transitions of the product of the three origin blowups, equivalently Tot_P(L_1⊕L_2⊕L_3). The charts cover because all degree-one Rees generators have been used.

This proves the product-blowup assertion without requiring a general theorem about products of ideals on arbitrary schemes. The separated coordinate blocks are essential to this particularly simple ambient description.

## 3. Intrinsic blowup equals saturated strict transform

In the ambient chart just constructed put s=r_1r_2r_3 and let F_j(v) be the components of f(v_1,v_2,v_3). Trilinearity gives the total-transform ideal

    (s F_j(v) : j).

Its saturation by g=s is exactly (F_j(v):j). To see this without any reducedness assumption, set C_0=C[v_1,v_2,v_3] and H=(F_j)⊂C_0. Multiplication by each r_i, hence by s, is injective on (C_0/H)[r_1,r_2,r_3]. Polynomial indeterminates are regular even when the coefficient ring is nonreduced or has zero divisors. Therefore H extended to the polynomial ring is s-saturated. On the other hand sH⊂H and H⊂(sH:s), proving

    (sH:s^∞)=H.

Moreover, after inverting s the ambient chart is exactly the original ambient open D(g). Thus Section 1 identifies the intrinsic blowup chart with

    (C_0/H)[r_1,r_2,r_3].

This is simultaneously the strict-transform chart and the three-line-bundle chart over Z. The identifications commute with ambient blowup transition functions and with the morphisms to X, so they glue canonically.

The preimage where all three input vectors are nonzero is s≠0 in every such chart. It is schematically dense in the resulting strict transform, since s is regular there. It follows both that the stated strict transform is indeed the closure of this open and that no additional component supported solely on the scalar boundary has silently been retained.

By contrast the ordinary closed base change of the ambient blowup has chart ideal sH, not H. It generally contains additional scalar-boundary structure. Replacing an intrinsic Rees algebra by that base change would lose the exact torsion removal established above.

## 4. Exact normalized projective calculation near the diagonal

We import the exact elimination and coherent Nakayama argument from /research/hm/middle-surface/rank-one-open-thickening/PROOF.md, Sections 1–2. The following coordinate calculation checks that scalar normalization really preserves the argument.

Choose a linear functional ell nonzero on a given common line. In the projective affine chart where all three directions have ell≠0, choose their unique representatives with ell=1 and write

    (v_1,v_2,v_3)=(u+p,u,u-p+w),
    ell(u)=1,   p,w∈H_ell:=ker(ell).

These are independent algebraic coordinates: u contributes four coordinates and p,w four each. The diagonal is p=w=0. Put

    A_u(w)=f(u,u,w),
    B_(u,p)(w)=f(p,u,w),
    F_u(p)=f(p,u,p).

The outer symmetry and cyclic identity give the exact equation

    f(v_1,v_2,v_3)=(A_u+B_(u,p))w-F_u(p).

Choose local algebraic target projections L,pi with LA=1 and pi A=0. This is possible by the repeated-input injection. The first four equations are

    (1+LB)w=LF.

Their determinant equals one along the diagonal, so they eliminate w algebraically after an open localization. The remaining ideal J in the smooth eight-dimensional (u,p)-space satisfies J⊂P_p², where P_p=(p_1,…,p_4), and its quadratic classes are the residual quadratic map Q. Rank ten makes those classes span P_p²/P_p³. Coherent Nakayama gives J=P_p² on a Zariski neighborhood of the diagonal point, exactly as in the imported theorem. This is equality of ideals, not just a leading-term calculation.

Because F is quadratic, the eliminated expression w=(1+LB)^(-1)LF is zero modulo P_p². Thus the actual projective thickening has local presentation

    O_B[p_1,…,p_4]/(p)²,
    (v_1,v_2,v_3)=(u+p,u,u-p).

The last formula is an equality on this nonreduced scheme. In particular the second projection is precisely the displayed local retraction to u. No assertion about a transverse slice of an arbitrary abstract local normal form was needed.

Taking the union of neighborhoods obtained at every good diagonal point, and shrinking their support away from the complement of Δ_B, gives Z° with reduction Δ_B. Its square-zero nilradical N is locally free of rank four and is intrinsic. The second projection maps Z° to B: its underlying image lies in B, so the morphism factors through this open. The local normal forms show it is finite locally free of degree five. They also show that its inverse image of a sufficiently small base open has exactly the displayed ring: a square-zero thickening has the same topological space as its reduction, and localizing a function differs from localizing its reduction only by a unit of the form 1+nilpotent. Hence the local finite-flat claims glue to the asserted global finite-flat projective retraction.

The first and third projections have the same underlying identity on B and so also retract the diagonal. Since the nilradical is locally free rank four, these splittings also make the algebra locally free rank five. This does not identify the three splittings.

## 5. Pullback to the three line bundles

The total-space map W°→Z° is smooth and flat. Locally the three tautological lines are trivial, giving

    O_(W°)=O_B[r_1,r_2,r_3,p_1,…,p_4]/(p)².

Its reduction is the threefold sum of O_B(-1), since all three tautological lines restrict to that same line on the diagonal. Its nilradical M is square-zero, and as a module on the reduction it is canonically p^*N, where p:D̃_B→B is the bundle projection. This follows by flat pullback of the nilradical exact sequence. The quotient by this ideal is reduced, so it is indeed the entire nilradical.

The normalized local structure map to X is particularly useful:

    (x,y,z)=(r_1(u+p), r_2u, r_3(u-p)),      (p)²=0.       (*)

Here each r_i is independently free and is a nonzerodivisor. Each scalar-zero locus is an effective Cartier divisor on W°. Any subset of the scalar parameters is a regular sequence; their common zero loci retain the same square-zero residual algebra. On the reduction these divisors have ordinary coordinate normal crossings. This does not say W° itself is smooth or reduced.

At every closed point W° has local dimension seven, tangent dimension eleven, is Cohen–Macaulay of type four, and is neither Gorenstein nor a local complete intersection. These follow from the displayed free rank-five algebra over a smooth seven-dimensional base, exactly as for the all-nonzero normal form. Its local component cycle is five times its smooth reduction.

The local base projections to (u,r_1,r_2,r_3) are retractions, but their gluing has not been proved and is not needed. The projective retraction Z°→B alone does not identify the three tautological line bundles on Z° with pullbacks of their reductions. One must not infer a global retraction W°→D̃_B from it.

## 6. Scheme fibers and precise boundary coverage

Take a closed nonzero rank-one triple with common line ℓ∈B, choose ell with ell(ℓ)≠0, and write its normalized direction as u_0 and scalar values as c_i. Formula (*) implies r_i=c_i in the scheme fiber, because applying ell to input i recovers r_i exactly. For each nonzero c_i, the remaining vector constraint is respectively

    u+p=u_0,   u=u_0,   u-p=u_0.

If at least two c_i are nonzero, these equations imply u=u_0 and p=0; when the two surviving inputs are the outer pair, this uses invertibility of 2 in C. The scheme fiber is therefore one reduced point.

If precisely one c_i is nonzero, there is only one of these three vector constraints. It eliminates u in terms of p, leaving precisely C[p_1,…,p_4]/(p)². Thus the fiber over a two-zero-input point is length five, not a reduced point. This is compatible with a unique underlying projective diagonal direction.

Over (0,0,0), each r_i is zero and there is no condition left on projective directions. The full fiber is Z with its scheme structure, and the fiber of W° is Z°. Its good-diagonal portion is therefore a four-dimensional square-zero thickening with rank-four nilradical and local degree five over B, rather than a finite fiber. In particular the blowup is not a closed immersion near the two-zero or vertex locus, unlike the previously established exactly-one-zero extraction.

Every rank-one triple with good common line and at least one nonzero input has a unique point of D̃_B above it. At the vertex the common line is extra resolved data: each line in B supplies a point. The construction covers all three possible two-zero strata and the all-zero scalar stratum with good diagonal directions. It makes no assertion that all points above an original two-zero point lie in W°: the zero inputs can have other limiting projective directions in the full scheme Z. Nor does it classify points over the vertex outside Δ_B.

## 7. Compatibility, canonicity, and limits

On the all-inputs-nonzero locus K is the unit ideal, so the blowup is the identity and recovers the imported rank-ten open thickening. On the exactly-one-zero neighborhoods of /research/hm/middle-surface/boundary-primary-extraction/PROOF.md, K is locally the principal zero-input ideal (a). The same intrinsic blowup is therefore its canonical closed primary extraction S/P² from S/(aP²). There is no new choice in this comparison: both constructions are the blowup of the same ideal on the same open.

The blowup is defined globally and intrinsically by K, while its canonical total-bundle description uses only the three ordered input factors and trilinearity. Coordinate choices in Sections 2–6 prove statements about it and do not enter its definition. The good-diagonal open can be chosen in more than one way; the resulting neighborhoods have the same germ along Δ_B. Thus the claimed boundary geometry is canonical as a germ, not a claim of a distinguished maximal ambient open.

The earlier two-zero blowup along the entire two-zero stratum has a projective P¹ of rank-one directions and retains endpoint coordinate components. That is a different center and a different blowup. The present product ideal instead records three individual projective input directions; its good-diagonal neighborhood has removed those coordinate-only components and retains the square-zero primary geometry. No identification of the two full blowups, or of all their exceptional directions, is claimed.

Finally, blowup discards ideal-power torsion, and the passage from total transform sH to H visibly discards structure supported on the scalar boundary. Its clean primary geometry cannot be pushed down to deduce absence of embedded components or a complete primary decomposition in the original two-zero or vertex germs. This is a canonical resolved extraction near specified projective directions, not a resolution to a smooth or reduced scheme and not a classification of the complete original boundary.
