## 1. Minimal hypotheses for the new bridge

The new implication requires the following precise inputs over C:

1. V is the five-dimensional Schrödinger representation, and the projective action is the standard full group N=A semidirect SL2(F5), with A=F5^2 and its standard quotient action. The canonical complement constructed by exact Weyl intertwiners is the relevant complement.
2. The operation f:V^3 -> J is trilinear, outer symmetric, cyclic zero, and equivariant with a consistent natural homogeneous linearization. Any scalar or target twist for a chosen input lift is common to all evaluations of this same operation.
3. Every repeated-input map A_u:V/Cu -> J is injective for every nonzero u. In particular its image R_u has dimension four.
4. The residual middle map Q_u:Sym^2(V/Cu) -> J/R_u is the quotient of b_u(xz)=f(x,u,z). The actual family b has linear entries, as a morphism O(-1)^15 -> J tensor O, and rank b_u=4+rank Q_u.
5. Z2={rank Q<=2} has finite support (the empty case is allowed).

The full source, generic-rank, bundle, defect-degree, and invariant-curve hypotheses in the cited upstream proofs are necessary when item 5 is obtained from those proofs. They are not consequences of trilinearity and repeated-input injectivity alone. Once item 5 is taken as an input, those geometric hypotheses are not used again in the new bridge. Neither irreducibility of J nor its dimension twenty plays an additional role in this bridge beyond the given matrix setup.

## 2. Classification of all point orbits of size at most 150

### Nontrivial A-stabilizer

An A-stabilizer is an F5-subspace. It cannot have dimension two, because a common projective fixed line would be a Heisenberg-invariant vector line, contradicting irreducibility (equivalently the scalar commutator obstruction). Every nonzero Weyl operator has order five and trace zero in its first four nontrivial powers, so Fourier inversion gives five distinct eigenvalues. Each of the six lines in A therefore fixes exactly five points. The fixed sets of different lines are disjoint by the nontrivial scalar commutator. A translates transitively among the five eigenlines, and SL2(F5) is transitive on the six labels. Thus these points constitute one orbit E of size thirty, and account for every point with nontrivial A-stabilizer.

### Trivial A-stabilizer and possible indices

If A_p=1, its stabilizer injects into G=SL2(F5) with image S, giving |N.p|=25[G:S]. For an orbit of size at most 150, the index is at most six. Perfectness of G excludes indices two, three and four because their coset permutation groups are solvable. The only remaining indices are one, five and six; none is omitted.

Whenever z=-I belongs to S, comparing the cocycle on zg and gz gives 2c(g)=(1-g)c(z). Hence its graph is conjugate by A to the standard S, with conjugator c(z)/2. This works even though 5 divides the order of some subgroups: no invalid coprime-complement theorem is being used here.

For index one, the exact operators D and T satisfy TDT^-1=D^-1 and D^5=1. A common invariant line must have D eigenvalue one, hence be Ce0. The Fourier intertwiner does not preserve this line. For index six, the order-twenty subgroup has a unique Sylow-five subgroup and equals its Borel normalizer. It contains z and is conjugate to the subgroup generated by the projective D and T. Its sole fixed line is Ce0, which has nontrivial A-stabilizer. Both indices are excluded.

### Index five: subgroup structure and the central extension

An order-twenty-four subgroup S must contain z: otherwise it would inject into A5, contradicting Lagrange's theorem. Its image of order twelve in A5 fixes a letter and is A4. Indeed an intransitive action with no fixed letter must have orbit sizes three and two and would embed in (S3 x S2) intersect A5, of order six.

The preimage Q of the normal V4 has order eight, quotient V4 by z, and a unique involution. The unique involution assertion follows from diagonalizing any order-two determinant-one matrix over F5. Thus Q is quaternion, not cyclic. The order-three quotient S/Q cycles the three nonzero elements of Q/<z>.

The inverse image of this projective S in SL(V) is a finite central extension by μ5, of order 120. The stated averaging proof splits it: if f(s,t) is its additive F5-valued factor set, summing the cocycle identity over the last argument gives f(s,t)=b(s)+b(t)-b(st), with b(s)=24^-1 sum_t f(s,t). Thus a genuine linear representation of S exists. This step does not assume that the full projective G-action has already been globally linearized.

The canonical reflection R has dimensions (3,2), determinant one, and order two. Every SL(V) lift of [R] is ξR for ξ in μ5, and only R has order two. Hence the splitting sends z to R exactly, not to a scalar-altered reflection.

On V_minus, any invariant line would give a quaternion character on which z acts as -1, impossible since z is a commutator. On V_plus, actual commutation with R follows by taking traces in R_s R R_s^-1=cR: trace R=1 forces c=1. The induced projective G-action factors through A5, is nontrivial by D, and is therefore faithful by simplicity. In particular its V4 restriction is projectively faithful.

The determinant character of V_plus is trivial on Q because Q is contained in [S,S]: commutators with the order-three action span Q/<z>, and z is already a commutator in Q. Three V4 characters with product one and a projectively faithful projectivization must be exactly the three distinct nontrivial characters. Repetition would leave a nontrivial projective kernel; three distinct characters including the trivial one would have nontrivial product. These three character lines are cyclically permuted by S/Q, so no invariant line exists. Finally every S-invariant line must lie in one of the two reflection eigenspaces. This excludes index five.

All possible cases have therefore been covered. The sole orbit of size at most 150 is E; any nonempty invariant finite set of that size is E itself.

## 3. The degree-seven complete intersection and its local bound

Because rank b=4+rank Q, the seven-minor base locus has support exactly Z2. Finiteness of that support is sufficient; no reducedness or prescribed scheme length is needed. The minors are homogeneous of degree seven because b is genuinely linear.

Over the infinite field C, four general linear combinations of these minors have zero-dimensional intersection: at each step choose a combination avoiding every positive-dimensional irreducible component of the preceding intersection. None of those components is contained in the finite base locus. The resulting four hypersurfaces form a regular sequence in projective four-space, so their complete intersection C has length 7^4=2401. This is the length of C, not the length of Z1 or Z2.

At a point p of Z1, rank b_p<=5. After constant invertible row and column operations, the constant part of the matrix has rank at most five. In the determinant expansion of any seven-minor, every nonzero summand requires at least two factors vanishing at p. Thus every seven-minor lies in m_p^2, and so do the four chosen local equations.

In the regular local ring O_(P4,p) of dimension four, those equations generate an m-primary parameter ideal I contained in m^2. Cohen-Macaulayness gives length(O/I)=e(I), and monotonicity of multiplicity gives e(I)>=e(m^2)=16. This argument does not presume that their initial quadratic forms form a regular sequence or have an isolated common zero.

Summing local lengths over the distinct points of Z1 yields

    16 |Z1_red| <= length(C)=2401,
    |Z1_red| <=150.

The bound includes rank-zero points. Invariance of ranks makes Z1_red an N-invariant finite set. Section 2 now implies it is empty or exactly E.

## 4. Canonical-point residual rank and the partial-kernel correction

At u=e0 use the B-stable complement E0=span(e1,e2,e3,e4) for V/Cu. The exact lifts Z,D,T fix u as a vector. B=<Z,D> is an honest C5^2 on E0, and T acts by i ->2i. The B-character of e_i e_j is (i+j,i^2+j^2). These ten characters are distinct because their two coordinates determine the unordered pair of roots, and none is trivial.

Consequently a B-invariant residual kernel K is the sum of a subset of these character lines. T-invariance requires complete monomial orbits, of dimensions four (squares), four (adjacent pairs), and two (opposite pairs). The resulting residual ranks before using injectivity are 0,2,4,6,8,10. Kernel invariance requires no honest target representation: any common scalar in equivariance preserves the zero condition modulo R_u.

The stronger bound is valid on the actual partial kernel K; it does not incorrectly extend a correction to all of Sym^2(E0). For q in K, b_u(q) lies in R_u. Since A_u:E0 -> R_u is an isomorphism, define v(q)=A_u^-1 b_u(q), only for q in K. If a chosen g in B satisfies equivariance with target operator L_g (including any scalar or target twist), then

    b_u(gq)=L_g b_u(q),
    A_u(gw)=L_g A_u(w).

Both formulas use the same L_g because they come from the same trilinear operation and g fixes u exactly. Therefore injectivity of A_u gives v(gq)=g v(q). No character from a tautological line survives, since g fixes the chosen representative u; no target scalar survives, since it cancels between the two maps.

The B-characters of E0 are (j,j^2). None equals a quadratic character: equality would imply 2ik=0 for nonzero i,k. Thus Hom_B(K,E0)=0 as well as Hom_B(Sym^2 E0,E0)=0, and v vanishes. It follows that the unreduced b_u vanishes on K.

If a square e_i^2 belonged to K, then f(e_i,u,e_i)=0. Outer symmetry and cyclic zero give f(e_i,u,e_i)=-2f(e_i,e_i,u). The latter is nonzero by repeated-input injectivity at e_i, since u and e_i are independent. Hence no square belongs to K. This step needs repeated-input injectivity at all nonzero repeated inputs, not just at e0.

The entire four-dimensional square module survives, so K can contain only a subset of the remaining modules of dimensions four and two. The residual rank is therefore in {4,6,8,10}. Equivariance and the verified transitivity of E transport this to all thirty canonical points. This is an abstract representation-theoretic necessity, not evaluation of any actual operation or an existence assertion for these ranks.

## 5. Final implication and limitations

The finite-support and multiplicity argument forces Z1_red to be empty or E. The canonical-point rank bound gives E intersect Z1 empty. Thus Z1_red is empty. A closed determinantal subscheme of the finite-type projective space with empty support is itself empty, so it is also legitimate to call the rank-at-most-one determinantal locus empty scheme-theoretically. Every projective middle input consequently has residual rank at least two, or equivalently rank b at least six.

There is no circular use of the desired Z1 emptiness in this new implication. Z2 finiteness is imported from the separate surface/curve argument; the canonical-point proof uses only symmetry, equivariance, and repeated-input injectivity. This new theorem does not show Z2 is empty, rule out rank two or three elsewhere, improve every fiber bound automatically, or settle existence of independent zeros. No claim of those kinds should be appended without further proof.

**Gaps found in the new bridge: none.** The substantive remaining dependency is the validity and applicability of the upstream finite-Z2 theorem under the full standing source and geometric hypotheses. This audit verifies that dependence is explicit rather than silently deduced from the tensor identities.

### Cardinality of the remaining finite rank-two locus

The canonical rank bound gives E disjoint from Z3, hence from Z2. Since the orbit theorem proves that E is exactly the set of points with nontrivial A-stabilizer, A acts freely on every point of Z2. Thus its finite reduced support has cardinality divisible by 25.

The same complete intersection of four degree-seven minors contains every point of Z2. Its length is 2401, and each distinct support point contributes at least one to that length. Therefore |Z2_red|<=2401; divisibility by 25 improves this to <=2400. No rank-one multiplicity factor sixteen is being incorrectly imposed on rank-two points.

For any N-orbit in Z2, its stabilizer injects into G with subgroup image S, and the orbit cardinality is 25m where m=[G:S]. The small-orbit theorem excludes m<=6 because these points are outside E. Indices seven and nine are impossible by Lagrange's theorem. If m=8, then |S|=15. Its odd order makes S disjoint from the central subgroup of order two, so S injects into A5. A group of order fifteen has a unique Sylow-five subgroup because its number divides three and is congruent to one modulo five. It would consequently lie in the normalizer of that subgroup in A5. That normalizer has order ten: there are twenty-four 5-cycles in A5 and four nonidentity elements per subgroup, giving six Sylow-five subgroups and normalizer order 60/6=10. This contradicts the order fifteen. Thus m>=10 and each N-orbit has at least 250 points.

It follows that Z2 is empty or its reduced support has between 250 and 2400 points, inclusive, with cardinality divisible by 25. Because Z1 is empty, every point counted here has residual rank exactly two. This conclusion does not require that index ten actually occur as a point stabilizer, and does not assert realization of any orbit size or support cardinality.
