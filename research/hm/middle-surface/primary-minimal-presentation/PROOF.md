# Exact minimal homogeneous presentation of the primary ideal

## Result and scope

Over C, let R=C[x_i,y_i,z_i : 1<=i<=5], with the three column multigrading, and let Q be the kernel of the universal square-zero substitution

    x_i -> r_1(u_i+p_i), y_i -> r_2 u_i, z_i -> r_3(u_i-p_i), (p)^2=0.

Then Q has exactly 50 minimal cubic generators and 150 minimal quartic generators, and no generators of other degrees. The mixed quartic pieces Q_(2,1,1), Q_(1,2,1), Q_(1,1,2) are all generated by linear multiples of Q_3. For the quotient B=R/Q,

    beta_(1,3)(B)=50, beta_(1,4)(B)=150, beta_(2,4)(B)=150.

The degree-four linear syzygies split by repeated column, with each of the three pieces isomorphic as a row-GL(V) representation to S_(2,1,1)V direct sum exterior^4 V (dimensions 45+5). Here V denotes the five-dimensional space of row coordinate forms; this is the dual convention to texts that call the underlying row vector space V.

This is a universal polynomial calculation. It makes no actual-model, source-operation, or physical evaluation claim.

The proof below supplies the remaining multiplication calculation, by three explicit highest-weight vectors rather than an assumed generic rank.

## 1. Target representations in a mixed quartic multidegree

For alpha=(2,1,1), or either permutation, the row representation in R_alpha is

    Sym^2 V tensor V tensor V
      = S_(4)V + 2 S_(3,1)V + S_(2,2)V + S_(2,1,1)V.

The quotient B_alpha is S_(4)V + S_(3,1)V. Complete reducibility in characteristic zero therefore gives

    Q_alpha = S_(3,1)V + S_(2,2)V + S_(2,1,1)V.

Each occurs once in Q_alpha, and their respective dimensions for dim V=5 are 105,50,45, summing to 200.

For a column a equal to x, y, or z, define the GL(V)-equivariant multiplication map

    m_a: V tensor Q_(1,1,1) -> Q_((1,1,1)+e_a),
         a_i tensor F -> a_i F.

To prove m_a surjective, it suffices to exhibit a nonzero highest-weight vector of each of the three target shapes in its image. We do so separately and uniformly for all a.

## 2. Explicit highest-weight images

Use the cubics

    B_ij = 2 x_i y_j z_i - x_i y_i z_j - x_j y_i z_i  (i!=j),
    D_123 = det [[x_1,y_1,z_1],[x_2,y_2,z_2],[x_3,y_3,z_3]].

They are in Q_3. Let E_ij (i<j) act as the derivation replacing row-j variables by their corresponding row-i variables. These are the raising operators for the convention that row 1 is highest.

### Shape (3,1)

For each a, a_1 B_12 is a nonzero highest-weight vector of row weight (3,1). Indeed B_12 has weight (2,1), E_12 B_12=0, and all other raising operators kill it as well; a_1 is highest. Polynomial multiplication by a_1 cannot annihilate a nonzero polynomial. Thus im(m_a) contains the S_(3,1)V summand.

### Shape (2,2)

The elementary derivative identities are

    E_12 B_12=0, E_12 B_21=-B_12.

Hence

    H_a = a_2 B_12 + a_1 B_21

has weight (2,2) and is killed by E_12 and by every other raising operator. Write M_ab;12=a_1 b_2-a_2 b_1 for a pair of columns. Exact expansions give

    H_x = -M_xy;12 M_xz;12,
    H_y = -2 M_xy;12 M_yz;12,
    H_z = -M_zy;12 M_zx;12.

Every right-hand side is a nonzero product in the polynomial ring. In particular this argument works for the middle column y as well as for the outer columns. Thus im(m_a) contains the S_(2,2)V summand for all three a.

### Shape (2,1,1)

For each a, a_1 D_123 is a nonzero highest-weight vector of row weight (2,1,1). All raising operators kill D_123: those affecting its rows make two determinant rows equal, and the others act trivially. As before multiplication by a_1 is injective in R. Thus im(m_a) contains the S_(2,1,1)V summand.

The three shapes are distinct. Their irreducible submodules therefore have zero pairwise intersection, and fill the 200-dimensional target. Consequently

    im(m_a)=Q_((1,1,1)+e_a), rank(m_a)=200

for each a. This proves the desired exact multiplication rank structurally.

## 3. Explicit 200-generator minimal generating set

The following 50 cubics are those of LOW_DEGREE.md, repeated here to make the presentation self-contained:

1. B_ij for all ordered pairs i!=j (20 cubics).
2. For each i<j<k, define

       A_i=x_j y_i z_k+x_k y_i z_j,
       A_j=x_i y_j z_k+x_k y_j z_i,
       A_k=x_i y_k z_j+x_j y_k z_i.

   Take A_i-A_j and A_i-A_k (20 cubics).
3. D_ijk, the 3-by-3 determinant in rows i<j<k and columns x,y,z (10 cubics).

For each column pair (a,b) in {(x,y),(x,z),(y,z)}, put

    M_ab;ij=a_i b_j-a_j b_i, i<j.

Take the 50 standard products

    M_ab;ij M_ab;kl,
    i<j, k<l, i<=k, j<=l.

The inequalities choose the two-column semistandard tableaux of shape (2,2), with each product occurring once. Equivalently, start with all 55 unordered products (repetition allowed) of the ten minors and omit precisely

    M_AB;il M_AB;jk for 1<=i<j<k<l<=5,

where (A,B) is the fixed column pair.

There are five omissions. The Pluecker relation

    M_AB;ij M_AB;kl - M_AB;ik M_AB;jl + M_AB;il M_AB;jk=0,
    i<j<k<l,

expresses each omitted product in terms of retained products. These 50 products span the entire two-column quartic piece, which has dimension 50 by HILBERT.md; hence they are a basis. Equivalently, this is the standard monomial basis of S_(2,2)V.

Use all three pairs, yielding 150 quartics of multidegrees (2,2,0), (2,0,2), and (0,2,2). Their directions are minimal because every cubic generator has multidegree (1,1,1), so no linear multiple of a cubic lies in these multidegrees.

All remaining possible degree-four products of two minors have multidegree (2,1,1), (1,2,1), or (1,1,2). Section 2 puts their entire Q pieces in R_1 Q_3. Therefore Q=(Q_3)+P^2 shows that these 50 cubics and 150 quartics generate Q globally. All are minimal, and no additional minimal generators exist.

## 4. Exact degree-four linear syzygies

Pieri gives

    V tensor Q_3
      = V tensor (S_(2,1)V + exterior^3 V)
      = S_(3,1)V + S_(2,2)V + 2 S_(2,1,1)V + exterior^4 V.

The source has dimension 250. Since each m_a surjects onto S_(3,1)V + S_(2,2)V + S_(2,1,1)V, its kernel is exactly, as a representation,

    ker(m_a) = S_(2,1,1)V + exterior^4 V,
    dim ker(m_a)=45+5=50.

The two source copies of S_(2,1,1)V map onto the unique target copy; the displayed kernel means their remaining single copy, not a claim that one preselected source copy is killed. The exterior^4 V part comes from the familiar determinant identities

    a_i D_jkl - a_j D_ikl + a_k D_ijl - a_l D_ijk = 0,
    i<j<k<l,

for each a=x,y,z. These give five independent syzygies in each repeated-column degree; the other irreducible 45-dimensional summand is determined exactly by ker(m_a).

The three repeated-column multidegrees are distinct, so their kernels add directly. Thus

    dim ker(R_1 tensor Q_3 -> Q_4)=3*50=150,
    rank(R_1 tensor Q_3 -> Q_4)=3*200=600.

Also dim Q_4=750: 600 in the three mixed pieces plus 150 in the three two-column pieces. The 150 selected quartics are a basis modulo R_1 Q_3. Consequently no degree-four syzygy can have a nonzero constant coefficient on a selected quartic generator, and all degree-four syzygies are precisely the 150 linear syzygies just computed. There are no smaller-degree syzygies because Q has no equations below degree three and its selected cubics are linearly independent. Therefore these syzygies are minimal and

    beta_(1,3)(R/Q)=50,
    beta_(1,4)(R/Q)=150,
    beta_(2,4)(R/Q)=150.

For Betti numbers of Q itself, shift the homological indices down by one. No claim is made here about higher-degree syzygies or the remaining Betti table.
