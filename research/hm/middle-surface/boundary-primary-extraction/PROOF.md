# Canonical primary extraction at the exactly-one-zero boundary

## Bounded theorem

Import the audited Zariski local normal form of `/research/hm/middle-surface/zero-input-rank-one-boundary/PROOF.md`. At a rank-one point with exactly one zero input and rank-ten common line, write

    R = S/(a P^2),    P=(q1,q2,q3,q4),
    S = O_B[a,q1,q2,q3,q4] locally,

where B is smooth of dimension six. Permitted localizations are understood. Let X=Spec R, let C be the zero-input coordinate component, with ideal I=(a) in R, and let D be the reduced rank-one component, with ideal L=P/(aP²).

Then the blowup of X along I is canonically

    W := Bl_I X = Spec S/P² = V_X(L²).

The structure map W -> X is a closed immersion. This is the scheme-theoretic closure of X minus C in X, retaining the degree-five primary structure on D. Its identification is independent of a chosen generator of I or a chosen elimination chart. On each normal-form chart it has a retraction to D that is finite locally free of degree five, including above a=0. The extracted scheme is canonical. Moreover, on any such neighborhood containing the entire audited all-inputs-nonzero rank-ten locus, there is no global retraction to D at all, by the existing nonsplitting theorem and restriction.

On a suitable union of these boundary neighborhoods with the corresponding all-inputs-nonzero thickening neighborhoods, put I_i equal to the ideal generated by the coordinates of the ith input, for i=1,2,3. A single canonical construction is

    W = Bl_(I_1 I_2 I_3) X.

On this union it is again the closed subscheme V_X(L²). No assertion here describes the blowup at two-zero-input points, the vertex, or rank-exceptional common lines.

## 1. Principal-ideal blowups when the generator is a zero divisor

For any ring R and principal ideal I=(a), the Rees algebra is the image

    Rees_R(I) = image(R[T] -> R[t]),    T |-> a t.

Its degree-n component is a^n R t^n. Hence its homogeneous kernel has degree zero equal to zero and degree n≥1 equal to Ann_R(a^n) T^n. The irrelevant ideal is generated by the image of T. Therefore its Proj has one affine chart, D_+(T), with ring

    (Rees_R(I)[T^(-1)])_0 = R / H,
    H = union_(n≥1) Ann_R(a^n).

For clarity, every degree-zero fraction is represented by r T^n/T^n and therefore comes from R; an element of R becomes zero precisely when some positive power of T kills it, equivalently some a^n kills it. This proves the displayed ring identity without treating a as invertible in R or assuming that it is a nonzerodivisor.

Consequently a blowup of a principal ideal need not be the identity. It is the identity for an invertible ideal generated locally by a nonzerodivisor; a zero-divisor generator instead removes its power torsion. If the ideal is zero on an entire connected component, the blowup has no points above that component away from its intersections with the retained part.

## 2. Exact Rees algebra and saturation in the boundary model

In the smooth ambient ring S, a is a nonzerodivisor on S/P². For every n≥1,

    (aP² : a^n) = P².

Indeed, cancel one a in S and then use (P²:a^(n-1))=P². It follows that

    Ann_R(a^n) = P²/(aP²) =: H,    n≥1.

The Rees algebra and its Proj are thus explicitly

    Rees_R((a)) = R[T]/(H T)
                = S[T]/(aP², T P²),
    Bl_(a) X   = Spec R/H
                = Spec S/P².

Equivalently the homogeneous ideal (aP², T P²) saturates with respect to the irrelevant generator T to P² S[T]. Its Proj is the same as that of (S/P²)[T]. This is the relevant Rees torsion-saturation computation. It is not permissible to replace the Rees algebra by the polynomial algebra R[T] merely because I has one generator.

Likewise the kernel of R -> R[1/a] is H. Thus W is exactly the scheme-theoretic closure of D(a) in X. In ambient terms its ideal is

    (aP² : a^infinity) = P².

This retains the whole P-primary component, including its nilpotents; it does not merely take the reduced component D.

Since L=P/(aP²), its square is precisely H. Therefore

    W = V_X(L²),    H = Ann_R(I).

These formulas are intrinsic in X and D and give an independent description of the same closed immersion. Here “first infinitesimal neighborhood” means V_X(I_(D/X)²) inside X. It does not mean the first infinitesimal neighborhood inside the original fifteen-dimensional ambient vector space, where D has codimension eight and its full ambient conormal has rank eight. After the four equations are eliminated, the remaining normal rank is four; these four directions are the ones retained by W.

## 3. What happens to the center and to the other component

On W, a is a nonzerodivisor and I O_W=(a) is an invertible ideal. The inverse image of the center is the effective Cartier divisor

    E = V_W(a) = Spec S/(a,P²).

Its reduction is C intersection D. It is a square-zero thickening of that intersection, locally free of degree five over B. There is no new positive-dimensional projective exceptional fiber: because W -> X is a closed immersion, the scheme-theoretic fiber over each point in D is its residue field, and the fiber over each point of C minus D is empty. The divisor E's nonreduced structure concerns its scheme structure as a divisor, not a positive-dimensional fiber over a point of X.

The open X minus C is not dense in the underlying topological space of X, because C is a separate irreducible component. Thus this operation should not be advertised as a birational modification retaining every component. It is a canonical primary-component extraction, and it is an isomorphism over X minus C.

## 4. The recovered finite-flat local model

Set A=O_B[a] on a normal-form base chart. Then

    O_W = A[q1,q2,q3,q4]/(q1,q2,q3,q4)²
        = A direct-sum A q1 direct-sum ... direct-sum A q4.

The q-summand has square zero. Hence projection to Spec A=D is finite locally free of degree five and every fiber is the length-five square-zero algebra on four generators. At a=0 its fiber is still length five. If the ambient chart has additional inverted functions, their restrictions to W are a base function plus a nilpotent; after the corresponding base localization the same description holds.

This does not contradict the failure of finite flatness for X -> D in the audited boundary theorem. The blowup has removed the four-dimensional q-fiber carried by the component C. Base changing X itself to a=0 gives S/(a), whereas base changing W gives S/(a,P²).

The nilradical of W is M=P/P², a locally free rank-four O_D-module. The natural map from the old nilradical N=aP/aP² to M is locally multiplication by a under the old identification N ≅ P/P². Thus its image vanishes after restricting to a=0 although M's boundary fiber remains rank four. The construction preserves the primary thickening rather than relying on the image of the old nilradical under nonflat base change.

The exact sequence

    0 -> M -> O_W -> O_D -> 0,    M²=0,

is canonical, while the displayed algebra splitting uses the chart. In particular, the direction of the canonical map is the quotient O_W -> O_D, corresponding to the closed immersion D -> W. A retraction W -> D requires an algebra section O_D -> O_W. The local normal forms provide such sections locally. In fact, the audited theorem `/research/hm/middle-surface/rank-one-global-splitting/PROOF.md` proves that the all-inputs-nonzero rank-ten thickening T of its full reduced locus U_nonzero has no global retraction to U_nonzero. If the present neighborhood contains that entire locus, then W restricted there is T. Any retraction W -> D would restrict to a retraction T -> U_nonzero: its underlying topological map is the identity on D, since W is a nilpotent thickening of D. This contradicts the audited theorem. Thus no global retraction exists on any such neighborhood. Consequently “degree-five finite flat thickening” here means finite locally free retractions on the stated charts; it cannot mean a global finite-flat retraction across the whole boundary neighborhood containing U_nonzero.

## 5. Intrinsic center and canonical gluing

Let the ith input be the one that vanishes at the boundary point. Its coordinate ideal I_i is intrinsic: it is the pullback of the ideal of the origin of that copy of V, generated by the image of V* and independent of any basis.

In the outer-zero elimination chart the input is x=a u+p and p=-a D_q^(-1)q. Thus every input coordinate is a multiple of a on X. Conversely a=ell(x)/ell(u), where ell(u) is a unit. Therefore I_i=(a) on X. In the middle-zero chart the same argument uses y=a u+p and p=(a/2)E_q^(-1)q. The other outer-zero case follows by outer symmetry. This establishes equality of ideals, not just equality of their zero sets.

The two other input coordinate ideals are the unit ideal near this point, since each corresponding vector is nonzero. Thus their product I_1 I_2 I_3 is exactly I_i=(a) there. On the locus where all three inputs are nonzero, all three ideals are units, and their product blowup is the identity. In the previously established rank-ten all-inputs-nonzero thickening model X=S/P², L²=0, so that identity agrees with V_X(L²).

Choose the union U of sufficiently small boundary normal-form neighborhoods and suitable rank-ten all-inputs-nonzero neighborhoods; exclude the two-zero-input locus and the exceptional-line loci. On U let D be the reduced rank-one locus. Then blowup formation commutes with restriction to open subsets, and the local calculations identify

    Bl_(I_1 I_2 I_3)(U) = V_U(I_(D/U)²).

These identifications are canonical over U: each is the closed immersion with the specified ideal sheaf. Thus changes of linear functional, target splitting, local scalar a, or eliminated ambient coordinates do not create a gluing ambiguity. Where two local generators of the same nonzero principal ideal are compared at a local boundary point, they differ by a unit; more generally the construction depends on the ideal itself and needs no selected generator.

For the neighborhood of one specified boundary type, Bl_(I_i) alone suffices. It would not extract the component at a different zero-input boundary where the ith input is nonzero, because there I_i is the unit ideal. The product of the three coordinate ideals is what supplies a uniform extraction over all three boundary types. It is defined on the full original zero scheme, but only its restriction to U is analyzed here.

## 6. Nonreduced blowup versus ambient blowup

In the eliminated smooth ambient scheme Y=Spec S, the principal ideal (a) is an effective Cartier divisor. Its blowup is Y itself. Base changing this identity to X yields X, not W. Hence

    (Bl_(a) Y) times_Y X = X,
    Bl_((a) O_X) X = W.

There is no contradiction: Rees algebras and blowups do not in general commute with a nonflat closed base change. The degree-n Rees piece after tensoring from S is a copy of R; its map to a^n R has the torsion kernel computed above.

The original fifteen-dimensional ambient coordinate ideal of the zero vector has five generators, so blowing it up is yet another construction. Its ambient behavior cannot be inferred by calling it the principal ideal (a); the principal identification occurs only after the four equations have been eliminated and the ideal restricted to the resulting graph. No claim identifying its ambient blowup or its base change with W is needed here. The result is the blowup on the nonreduced zero scheme X, along its intrinsic input coordinate ideal.

## Scope

All calculations use the audited normal form and elementary commutative algebra. They prove the claimed extraction on the rank-ten, exactly-one-zero boundary and its overlap with the established nonzero thickening locus. They do not evaluate an operation, selected inputs, actual ranks, matrices, jets, residues, or detectors. No extension is asserted at two-zero-input points, at the vertex, or on exceptional common lines.
