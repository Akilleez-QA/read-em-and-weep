# Distinct-factor annihilator quadrics admit no support curve

This addendum uses the notation and hypotheses of `PROOF.md`. It does not modify that independently audited file. All arguments are abstract; no source operation or instance is evaluated.

## Result

Suppose Q=ab for two independent covectors a,b∈E*. Then no complete nonconstant curve of support hyperplanes satisfying (1) of `PROOF.md` exists.

Together with that file, this implies that a positive-dimensional complete family of limiting supports at a residual-rank-one point satisfying square avoidance can occur only if its scalar annihilator quadric is a **double plane**, Q=a² up to a nonzero scalar. No exclusion of this remaining double-plane case is asserted.

## 1. Rational division on the normalized curve

Normalize the complete integral parameter curve T. To avoid confusion with the fixed factor a, denote its universal covector by c∈H⁰(T,E*⊗L), where L is the pullback of O(1). Put A_t=ker c_t and choose a constant linear extension V:Sym²E→E of v.

The hyperplanes on which Q vanishes identically are exactly ker a and ker b. The support map is nonconstant, so the preimages of these two points of P(E*) are finite. On their complement, Q|A_t is nonzero. Sections 2–3 of `PROOF.md`, which do not use completeness until the final constancy step, give unique regular sections λ and ℓ on this open set such that

    c∘V−λab=cℓ.                                      (1)

Here λ is a section of L, ℓ is an E*-valued function, and products on the right use the polynomial normalization of the main proof. Equivalently ℓ is a rational section of the trivial E* bundle on T, with possible poles only above [a] and [b]. The section λ is rational in L. Equation (1) is an identity of rational quadratic-polynomial sections.

## 2. Local pole-direction lemma

Fix a point p with support covector [c_p]=[a]. Let R=O_{T,p}, a discrete valuation ring because T is a normalized curve, and trivialize L near p. In this trivialization c has coefficients in R and its residue is a nonzero scalar multiple of a. The polynomial c∘V has coefficients in R.

Reduce (1) modulo the ideal generated by the fixed linear polynomial b. The λab term disappears. Writing bars for this reduction gives

    overline(c∘V)=c̄ ℓ̄

in the degree-two part of Frac(R)⊗Sym•(E*/Cb). The left side is regular. The residue of c̄ is nonzero, since a and b are independent.

If ℓ̄ had a pole of order m>0, choose a uniformizer z. Then z^mℓ̄ is regular with nonzero residue, while z^m overline(c∘V) has zero residue. Reducing the identity multiplied by z^m gives

    0=(nonzero residue of c̄)·(nonzero residue of z^mℓ̄),

impossible in the polynomial ring Sym•(E*/Cb), which is a domain. Therefore ℓ modulo Cb is regular at p. Equivalently every polar coefficient of ℓ at p lies in Cb.

The same argument at a point over [b], reducing modulo a, proves that ℓ modulo Ca is regular there. This argument allows arbitrary contact order of the support curve with either factor point and arbitrary pole order. It assumes neither transversal approach nor a reduced pullback divisor.

## 3. The quotient covector is constant

Let B=span(a,b)⊂E*. Away from the two exceptional support values, ℓ is regular. At a point over [a], its image modulo B is regular because its image modulo Cb is regular; at a point over [b], use regularity modulo Ca. Thus the rational function

    ℓ mod B : T ⇢ E*/B

extends everywhere to a regular section of the trivial (E*/B) bundle. Since T is complete and integral, it is constant. Choose a constant lift ℓ₀∈E* of this value. Then

    ℓ−ℓ₀ ∈ B⊗C(T).                                  (2)

No assertion that ℓ itself is regular or constant is made.

## 4. Restriction to the common factor plane

Put W=ker a∩ker b, a fixed two-dimensional subspace of E. Then Sym²W⊂K. The shear convention is

    S_d(ef)=(d(e)f+d(f)e)/2.

By (2), S_{ℓ−ℓ₀} vanishes on Sym²W over C(T). Also Q vanishes there. Restricting the full identity (1) to Sym²W therefore gives

    c∘(v−S_{ℓ₀})|Sym²W=0

over C(T). The map v−S_{ℓ₀} is constant and c is regular, so this identity extends over every point of T. Hence

    (v−S_{ℓ₀})(Sym²W)⊂I:=⋂_{t∈T} A_t.                (3)

Importantly, the argument does not require W⊂A_t. It restricts the global quadratic identity, rather than asserting an unproved inclusion in the varying five-plane K_t.

If the support image is a projective line, the arbitrary-Q pencil exclusion in Section 5 of `PROOF.md` already supplies a contradiction. Otherwise the covectors c_t span a vector space of dimension at least three, and therefore dim I≤1.

If I=0, (3) vanishes on every square in Sym²W. If dim I=1, the map (v−S_{ℓ₀}):Sym²W→I determines a homogeneous binary quadratic polynomial. Over C it is either identically zero or has a zero in P(W). Thus in either case there is e∈W\{0} with

    (v−S_{ℓ₀})(e²)=0.

Since e²∈K and S_{ℓ₀}(e²)=ℓ₀(e)e, this is a compatible square, contrary to the hypothesis. The theorem follows.

## Scope

The proof depends on the independence of a and b in the local pole-direction lemma. For Q=a², reduction modulo a at the exceptional support value makes the universal covector vanish in the residue, so that lemma does not apply. The double-plane case remains open here.
