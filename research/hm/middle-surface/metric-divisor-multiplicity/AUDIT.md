# Independent audit: rank-one metric divisor multiplicities

## Verdict

Accepted. The final proof explicitly assumes residue characteristic zero, as holds in the antecedent geometry over C. Characteristic zero of the fraction field alone would not make 3/10 a unit; this precision was corrected during review and checked in the final text.

The exact multiplicity formula, sharp intervals, balanced cancellation, and universal realization examples are correct. This audit uses only symbolic vector-bundle and DVR algebra; no source-operation instance, numerical test, or source computation was used.

Reviewed: the accompanying PROOF.md, /research/hm/middle-surface/ternary-metric-boundary/PROOF.md, and /research/conductor-normalization/metric-discriminant-localization/PROOF.md.

## 1. Normal form and descent

For a primitive symmetric form over a DVR with 2 invertible, a minimal-valuation quadratic coefficient can be made a diagonal entry using polarization. Successively splitting that entry gives diagonal entries which are units times powers of the uniformizer. Primitivity makes the first exponent zero; rank one in the residue field makes the others positive. Ordering gives 1 <= a <= b. Unit square factors can be removed after unramified faithfully flat extension, for example by adjoining square roots of the units and taking a local component. One can also use strict henselization and completion. The uniformizer remains a uniformizer, so all coefficient-ideal valuations are preserved. This is a valuation calculation, not a claim that these coordinates provide an actual global atlas.

## 2. Pole computation

In h=diag(1,t^a,t^b), the coefficients of h(W(h^-1),-) are exactly

    W1_11 + t^-b P,
    t^a W2_11 + W2_22 + t^(a-b) Q,
    t^b W3_11 + t^(b-a) W3_22 + W3_33,

where P=W1_33+t^(b-a)W1_22 and Q=W2_33. The third coefficient is regular. In each of the first two coefficients, all omitted terms are regular. The ordinary tensor trace of W is regular and contains no inverse metric. It therefore cannot cancel any negative-valuation term. Since the normalization scalar is a unit, the pole order is exactly

    k=max(0,b-ord(P),b-a-ord(Q)).

The convention ord(0)=infinity correctly handles exact cancellation. Different covector coordinates cannot cancel each other's poles: their minimum valuation is the valuation of the coefficient ideal.

## 3. Choice independence and actual middle section

Any two extensions of v differ by h tensor w because h is a surjection with kernel K. Under this change P increases by 2t^b w1 and Q by t^b w2. These additions do not change the pole orders truncated at zero. More strongly, the trace change and inverse-metric contraction change cancel exactly in tau.

Regular changes of basis preserve the coefficient ideal; changes of line frames multiply coordinates by units. Changing the splitting adds a regular covector to lambda, so its pole order remains unchanged. These facts are also consequences of the intrinsic normalized graph on the nondegenerate locus.

For k>0, alpha=t^k lambda is primitive. The graph closure generated by f1,f2,t^k f3+u is saturated, and its quotient is a line bundle with [u]=-t^k[f3]. Thus the multiplicity concerns the actual middle section, not merely a chosen rational covector. For k=0 the graph is complementary to U and the multiplicity is zero. Inclusion into V/C adds no zeros when V/H is locally free. The leading residue has the stated O(kD) twist, and the primitive determinant-cleared annihilator has common coefficient order a+b-k. Consequently F has that exact order, including all multiplicities.

## 4. Square avoidance and sharpness

If b>a, special-fiber square avoidance at e3 forces W1_33 or Q to be a unit. In the first case P is a unit and k=b. In the second case k>=b-a. Regularity gives k<=b, so the interval is exact. It follows that a<=mult(F)<=2a.

If a=b=m, only P can contribute a pole. Thus 0<=k<=m and m<=mult(F)<=2m. The avoidance condition does not prevent cancellation of P, despite holding at every isotropic direction.

The displayed balanced example W(r^2)=(y^2-z^2,0,y^2) satisfies all-direction avoidance: a proportional output with y nonzero forces its proportionality scalar to vanish and then contradicts the third coordinate; with y zero and z nonzero it similarly contradicts the first coordinate. Its only eigenline is e1, which is never isotropic. Its trace is zero, tau=-e3*/3, and k=0 although the determinant order is 2m.

For a single family realizing all allowed values, take

    W(r^2)=(y^2+c z^2,z^2,0),
    c=t^(b-k)-t^(b-a).

For any c over any field extension, a proportional output with z nonzero forces the proportionality scalar to vanish, contradicting the second coordinate. When z is zero and y nonzero, the second coordinate forces that scalar to vanish, contradicting the first. Again only e1 remains and is never isotropic. Thus avoidance holds for every geometric isotropic direction on both fibers, without a genericity argument. Here P=t^(b-k), Q=1, so the exact order is max(k,b-a). This realizes every k in [b-a,b], including the balanced interval [0,m]. All powers appearing in c are nonnegative for these ranges.

## Scope

These are sharp universal local metric-and-correction statements. The examples do not assert realizability by an original source operation, a prescribed global family, or an actual component of the source problem. The results refine the earlier localization A<=Delta but do not independently establish global existence, a global dimension bound, or a source exclusion.
