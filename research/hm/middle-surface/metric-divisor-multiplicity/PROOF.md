# Exact middle-divisor multiplicities at rank-one ternary metric degeneration

## Scope

This is universal bundle algebra over a DVR with residue field of characteristic zero (in particular over a ℂ-algebra DVR in the antecedent setting). Thus 2, 3, and 5 are units. It refines `/research/conductor-normalization/metric-discriminant-localization/PROOF.md` and uses the normalization and square-avoidance conventions of `/research/hm/middle-surface/ternary-metric-boundary/PROOF.md`.  The examples below concern abstract metric-and-correction tensors only; they make no realization assertion for the original source problem.

Let R be a DVR, t a uniformizer, E a free module of rank three, and U and M line modules. Let h:Sym²E→M be surjective and generically nondegenerate, with rank-one special fiber. Put K=ker(h). Assume a regular correction v:K→E⊗U satisfying geometric square avoidance: for every nonzero isotropic r in each geometric fiber, v(r²) is not proportional to r. Trivialize U and M and extend v to a regular W:Sym²E→E. Such an extension exists since h is surjective.

After an unramified faithfully flat extension and, if needed, completion, orthogonal diagonalization and removal of unit square factors give

    h=diag(1,t^a,t^b),       1≤a≤b.

Indeed symmetric Gram–Schmidt over a DVR with 2 invertible yields unit times powers of t on the diagonal; unit square roots exist over a strictly henselian extension. These extensions preserve the valuation of every original coefficient and of every coefficient ideal, so the resulting multiplicities descend. The letters a,b here denote elementary-divisor exponents, not Chern classes or coefficient-ideal orders used elsewhere.

## 1. Exact formula

Write W^i_jj for the e_i coefficient of W(e_j²), and set

    P=W¹_33+t^(b−a)W¹_22,       Q=W²_33.

Use ord(0)=+∞. The multiplicity k of the canonical middle divisor is exactly

    k=max{0, b−ord(P), b−a−ord(Q)}.                 (1)

In particular:

* If b>a, square avoidance forces

      b−a≤k≤b.

  Every such degeneration contributes positively, although its determinant order is a+b.

* If a=b=m, then

      k=max{0,m−ord(W¹_22+W¹_33)}.                (2)

  Thus k=0 precisely when W¹_22+W¹_33 is divisible by t^m. Square avoidance does not rule this out.

For abstract metric-and-correction data all integers in the displayed intervals occur. This realizability statement is proved in Section 5 and does not assert realizability by any source operation.

## 2. Derivation, extension independence, and normalization

The intrinsic contraction and normalized complement covector are

    τ=tr(W)−(1/3)h(W(h⁻¹),−),       λ=−(3/10)τ.

Here tr(W)_j=Σ_i W^i_ij is regular because W is regular; no inverse metric enters this trace. Direct contraction gives the three coefficients of h(W(h⁻¹),−):

    W¹_11+t^(−b)P,
    t^a W²_11+W²_22+t^(a−b)Q,
    t^b W³_11+t^(b−a)W³_22+W³_33.

The third is regular. The only possible negative-valuation parts of the first two are respectively t^(−b)P and t^(a−b)Q. Subtracting a regular trace cannot cancel a negative-valuation coefficient. The scalar −3/10 is a unit. Consequently the pole order of λ is exactly the right side of (1).

Any two regular extensions differ by h⊗w for a regular vector w. Under this change,

    P ↦ P+2t^b w¹,       Q ↦ Q+t^b w².

The first change has valuation at least b and the second has valuation at least b, hence at least b−a. Neither changes the truncated pole orders in (1). More intrinsically, tr(W) increases by h(w,−), while W(h⁻¹) increases by 3w, so τ itself is unchanged.

Rescaling h by a unit changes neither τ nor the primitive projective metric and its elementary-divisor exponents. The cancellation h·h⁻¹ makes τ invariant even under scalar rescaling over the fraction field. Regular changes of basis preserve its pole-order ideal. A change of splitting H=U⊕E adds a regular covector to λ, since τ(2i(d))=(10/3)d; therefore its pole order is unchanged. Changes of U or M frames are by units and have the same invariance. Formula (1) is thus an invariant multiplicity calculation in an orthogonal frame, not a choice-dependent quantity.

## 3. Pole order equals the saturated-graph middle multiplicity

Let H=U⊕E. If k=0, λ is regular and its graph is a subbundle complementary to U. Hence the middle map U→H/C is nowhere zero and has multiplicity zero.

If k>0, put α=t^kλ, a regular covector with a unit component. Choose f₁,f₂ spanning ker(α) and f₃ with α(f₃)=u, where u frames U. The saturated graph C⊂H is generated by

    f₁, f₂, t^k f₃+u.

The quotient H/C is free on [f₃], and [u]=−t^k[f₃]. Therefore the middle map has exact vanishing ideal (t^k). Passing from H/C to V/C does not change this ideal whenever V/H is a bundle. This proves (1) as a statement about the actual middle divisor, including multiplicities.

For completeness, the leading covector t^kλ modulo t transforms by c^k under a replacement t↦ct. It therefore defines the normal-line-valued residue in E*⊗U⊗O(kD)|D. Its nonvanishing and kernel are intrinsic. No assertion that it is simply valued in O(D) is made when k>1.

Equivalently, the determinant-cleared annihilator has middle coefficient δ=t^(a+b) and E-coefficients (3/10)δτ. Its coefficient ideal has valuation a+b−k. Thus the localization identity A=Δ−F becomes, at this DVR,

    mult(A)=k,       mult(Δ)=a+b,       mult(F)=a+b−k.

In particular at rank one, mult(F)≥a>0, so equality of the middle and determinant multiplicities never holds here. More sharply, when b>a one has a≤mult(F)≤2a; when a=b=m one has m≤mult(F)≤2m. Thus rank-one degeneration always loses at least the smaller positive elementary-divisor exponent from the determinant multiplicity, and unequal exponents cannot cancel completely.

## 4. Consequences of square avoidance

Suppose b>a. On the special fiber the radical is spanned by e₂,e₃. In particular e₃² is in K₀. Square avoidance at e₃ says W₀(e₃²) is not proportional to e₃, so at least one of W¹_33 and W²_33 is a unit. If W¹_33 is a unit, then P is a unit, because b−a>0, and k=b. Otherwise Q is a unit, and (1) gives k≥b−a. Regularity gives the upper bound k≤b. This proves the unbalanced interval without assuming anything about the other radical directions.

When a=b=m, the second potential pole is regular, and (2) follows. Square avoidance at each individual radical square does not control the trace-like sum W¹_22+W¹_33. An explicit cancellation satisfying square avoidance at every isotropic direction follows.

## 5. Balanced cancellation and all attainable multiplicities

First fix R=ℂ[[t]], h=diag(1,t^m,t^m), and define the abstract regular symmetric tensor by

    W((xe₁+ye₂+ze₃)²)=(y²−z²)e₁+y²e₃.

If y≠0, proportionality W(r²)=c r forces c=0 from the e₂ coordinate and then contradicts the nonzero e₃ coordinate. If y=0 and z≠0, the e₃ coordinate forces c=0, contradicting the nonzero e₁ coordinate. The only remaining nonzero directions are multiples of e₁, which are never h-isotropic. This proves square avoidance over every field extension and on both fibers, without restricting to selected radical lines.

Here tr(W)=0 and W(h⁻¹)=t^(−m)e₃, so

    τ=−e₃*/3,       λ=e₃*/10.

The canonical graph is regular and transverse to U. Thus k=0 while ord(det h)=2m. This is a direct universal symbolic example of complete balanced cancellation.

One can also realize every value allowed by (1) with the triangular family

    W_c((xe₁+ye₂+ze₃)²)=(y²+c z²)e₁+z²e₂,

where c is any element of R. If z≠0, proportionality W_c(r²)=λr forces λ=0 from the third coordinate, contradicting its second coordinate. If z=0 and y≠0, the second coordinate forces λ=0, contradicting the first. Thus the only proportional-output direction is e₁, which is never isotropic. This proves square avoidance over every field extension and on both fibers, independently of c.

When b>a, for any integer k in [b−a,b] choose

    c=t^(b−k)−t^(b−a).

Then P=t^(b−k) and Q=1, so (1) gives precisely the chosen k. When a=b=m, for any integer k in [0,m] choose

    c=t^(m−k)−1.

Again P=t^(m−k) and Q=1, and (2) gives precisely k. The exponents are nonnegative in each stated interval. The metric determinant is generically nonzero throughout, and v is simply W_c restricted to ker h, so all the abstract hypotheses are satisfied. This family supplies the full sharp ranges without any genericity argument or restriction to selected radical directions.

## Conclusion

At a primitive rank-one ternary metric degeneration, the elementary-divisor exponents and two explicit correction combinations determine the exact canonical middle multiplicity by (1). Unequal exponents force a positive contribution; equal exponents allow complete cancellation consistent with all square-avoidance conditions. Determinant order alone does not determine this multiplicity. These are universal local statements and carry no source-realization or global existence claim.
