# Remaining K7 surface images have total divisorial degree at most two

Retain E of dimension four, K of dimension seven, and square-avoiding correction v. Suppose a compatible ordered threefold exists.

The low-polar-rank classification and common-radical exclusion show that W=K-perp must have generic polar rank three and zero common radical. Form its 3-by-4 linear polar matrix M. The signed maximal minors Δ_i are cubic polynomials, not all zero, and satisfy MΔ=0. Let g be their homogeneous gcd over the nonzero entries.

If deg g=3, the vector Δ/g is a nonzero constant vector e. The cofactor identity gives M(a)e=0 identically. Equivalently B_i(a,e)=0 for every a and every form B_i in W, so e is a common radical. This is excluded. Consequently deg g≤2.

Every irreducible surface component of the polar rank-at-most-two locus is a hypersurface in P(E), and its equation divides all Δ_i. Thus the entire divisorial part of this locus, with its gcd multiplicities, has degree at most two. Its reduced surface components are therefore planes or integral quadrics. Any two-dimensional factor image of the compatible threefold is one of these components, by the partner-fiber dimension argument.

This is an additional necessary restriction, not an exclusion of the remaining planes or quadrics. If both factor images have dimension three, the earlier transverse-basepoint argument instead requires all net base points to be Jacobian-degenerate; the present divisorial argument does not eliminate that possibility.
