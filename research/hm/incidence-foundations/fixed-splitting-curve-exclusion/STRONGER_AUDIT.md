# Independent audit: fixed splitting and moving splitting

## Verdict

The fixed-splitting lemma and stronger moving-splitting argument are valid. The stronger conclusion admits a standalone proof, without the finite-polar theorem or prior middle-dimension bound. It excludes every projective curve in the rectangular incidence with nonconstant middle map, and hence makes the entire rectangular middle image finite.

## Fixed splitting: weights and rank

Let T be a smooth projective integral curve with nonconstant middle map and tautological line U of degree −d, where d>0. Fix complementary X,Y of dimensions 2,3. The established injection argument gives u outside X and Y. Set a=P_Xu, b=P_Yu. Both are nonzero and independent, with u=a+b.

Outer symmetry and cyclic zero give

    f(a,u,b)=A_a(b)+A_b(a).

These two repeated values are independent. Indeed, if A_a(b)=λA_b(a), trilinearity and cyclic zero give A_(a+λb)(b)=0, contradicting injection since b and a+λb are independent. Thus the displayed cubic never vanishes. In particular the rectangular rank is exactly one, without requiring the finite-polar theorem for this step.

The constant-rank map U⊗(X⊗Y)→J⊗O_T has image a line subbundle M, with locally free cokernel. Twisting its surjection onto M by U* shows that M⊗U* is globally generated, so deg M+d≥0. The cubic above defines a nowhere-zero map U^⊗3→M, an isomorphism of line bundles. Thus deg M=−3d, contradicting the preceding inequality. All three copies of U are essential: both projections a,b are linear in the representative u.

## Moving splitting: global generation

Let X_T,Y_T be the tautological subbundles on the normalization T of a curve component with nonconstant middle map. First consider a curve with generic complementarity. Rank is exactly one everywhere: at complementary points this follows from the nonzero cubic; at noncomplementary points the nonzero f(h,u,h) belongs to the rectangular image. The elementary boundary lemma, independent of the finite-polar theorem, gives u outside X and Y and intersection dimension at most one. Write D for the zero divisor of the determinant of

    X_T⊕Y_T → V⊗O_T.

Put e=deg D=d_X+d_Y≥0. The rank-one image line M remains a subbundle everywhere, including D. Twist the surjection U⊗X_T⊗Y_T→M by U*⊗det(X_T)*⊗det(Y_T)*. Its source becomes

    X_T* ⊗ Λ²Y_T*.

This is globally generated: both dual bundles are quotients of trivial bundles and exterior powers and tensor products preserve global generation. Consequently

    deg M+d+e ≥ 0.                                      (1)

## Boundary transversality and exact pole orders

At any p in D, the boundary lemma says X_p∩Y_p=Ch and H=X_p+Y_p has dimension four. It also gives f(h,u,H)⊂M_p. Necessarily u is outside H. Otherwise both

    f(h,u,h)=−2A_h(u),      f(h,u,u)=A_u(h)

would belong to M_p, contradicting repeated-value independence. Here h and u are independent because u is outside X_p.

Choose a uniformizer t at p and a local nowhere-zero representative u of U. If the determinant vanishes to order m, a Smith form over the local discrete valuation ring is diag(1,1,1,1,t^m): precisely four invariant factors are units because the fiber addition map has rank four. The last transformed coordinate of u is a unit, by transversality to H. Solving the addition equation therefore produces projections a,b with exact pole order m. Their leading terms are opposite and nonzero, since the fiber kernel is generated by (h,−h). Thus, for some λ≠0,

    (t^m a)|_p=λh,      (t^m b)|_p=−λh.

For the rational cubic c=f(a,u,b),

    (t^(2m)c)|_p=−λ²f(h,u,h)≠0.

Hence c has exact pole order 2m, not merely an upper bound. Because M is a subbundle of the trivial J-bundle, this ambient valuation is also its valuation as a section of M. Off D, the fixed-splitting pointwise argument makes c nonzero. Therefore, as a rational section of M⊗U^−3,

    div(c)=−2D,      deg M+3d=−2e.                      (2)

Combining (1) and (2) gives 0≤−2d−e<0. This is impossible.

## Standalone treatment of entirely noncomplementary curves

If a complete curve is everywhere noncomplementary, intersection dimension is exactly one everywhere and H=X_T+Y_T is a rank-four subbundle of the trivial V-bundle. The same repeated-value argument above proves u outside H at every point. Hence U⊕H→V⊗O_T is an isomorphism. Its regular projector onto U has constant matrix entries on the complete integral curve. Its image U is therefore a fixed line, and the middle map is constant. This argument uses no finite-polar statement.

Every complete irreducible curve in R is either generically complementary or everywhere noncomplementary. Both cases exclude a nonconstant middle map. If the proper middle image had positive dimension, one could choose a projective integral curve in an irreducible component of R whose middle map is nonconstant, by general hyperplane sections (equivalently, a complete multisection above a curve in the image). This contradiction proves finite middle image directly. No prior upper bound on its dimension or quasi-finiteness is necessary.

## Consequences and limits

The middle image of R is a finite set. Components supported over a fixed middle point can still have positive dimension; finiteness of the entire incidence is not asserted.

For an independent-zero component whose generic full residual kernel and densely compatible scroll satisfy the explicit type III hypotheses of the prior addendum, its middle closure lies in this finite image. The full projective fixed-middle zero fiber has dimension two, so the projective component has dimension at most two and its affine component has dimension at most five after the three independent scalings. A component dominated by the ordered compatible scrolls has projective fiber dimension exactly two and thus affine dimension exactly five. In particular the previously possible six-dimensional scroll-dominated components are excluded. This does not improve bounds for arbitrary residual kernels or for components not known to induce rectangular triples.

The result supersedes both previously remaining possibilities of constant-splitting curves and varying-splitting curves meeting the exceptional set. Only universal tensor geometry, line bundles on curves, and local algebra were used.
