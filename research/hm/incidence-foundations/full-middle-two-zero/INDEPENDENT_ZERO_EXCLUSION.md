# Localization of non-rank-one zeros to the residual degeneracy locus

Retain the symbolic identities and repeated-input injection of PROOF.md. The target-splitting argument depends only on the middle input u and inverts functions of u alone. It never inverts a function of the outer coordinates a,c,p,q. Consequently its exact ideal holds on the entire outer-input affine fiber above every rank-ten middle input, not only in the germ of (0,u,0).

Over any algebraically closed field extension of C, the equations p_i q_j+p_j q_i=0 imply p=0 or q=0. Indeed if p_i is nonzero, the diagonal equation implies q_i=0; the equations with that i then imply every q_j=0. If p=0, the other equations give a q=0: either x=a u is zero, or q=0 and both outer inputs are multiples of u. If q=0, they give c p=0: either z=c u is zero, or p=0. Thus above a rank-ten middle input the reduced zero set consists exactly of the two coordinate components and the rank-one locus. In particular every zero with all three inputs nonzero and span dimension at least two has middle line in the rank-drop locus of Q_u.

Assume additionally (H), the codimension-at-least-two bound for the projective rank-drop locus. Its application to the canonical equivariant setting is supplied by /research/hm/normalizer-rank-five/quintic-normalizer-exclusion/COROLLARY.md Section 2, with generic rank ten supplied by /research/hm/local-defect/rank-eight-global-exclusion/PROOF.md. It is not a consequence of the three universal tensor hypotheses alone. Under (H), the support inclusion above restricts the middle-input projection of every non-rank-one, all-inputs-nonzero family to a subset of projective dimension at most two (affine cone dimension at most three). This is a bound on its image, not on the total family dimension or existence.

There is a sharper necessary condition valid even at a rank-drop middle line. Write x=a u+p and z=c u+q. Projection modulo im A_u gives Q_u(p⊙q)=0, with the symmetric tensor convention determined by polarization. If x,u,z are linearly independent, then p and q are independent in V/Cu, so p⊙q is a nonzero symmetric tensor of rank exactly two. Hence the kernel of Q_u must contain a rank-two tensor. Mere rank drop is not sufficient. At rank nine its unique projective kernel tensor must have rank two for a linearly independent zero to exist. A rank-one kernel tensor can describe only dependent residual directions; rank at least three cannot be p⊙q.

There is one further exact compatibility condition. If p,q are independent and Q_u(p⊙q)=0, then f(p,u,q) lies in im A_u. Let v=A_u^{-1}f(p,u,q) in V/Cu, represented in the chosen complement H. The full equation is c p+a q+v=0. Thus a zero with these specified residual directions exists if and only if v belongs to span(p,q); in that case its two scalar coefficients a,c are unique. Both outer inputs are automatically nonzero when p,q are independent. The tensor's rank-two condition alone does not supply this compatibility. Changing the complement changes the displayed representatives and scalar coefficients but not existence of the original triple.

This gives an exact structural reduction of the independent-zero problem to a rank-two-kernel incidence and its residual A-component compatibility. It neither evaluates Q_u at a selected line nor decides whether that incidence is nonempty. The all-zero middle input, nonzero-outer-only germs, and full vertex remain outside this statement.
