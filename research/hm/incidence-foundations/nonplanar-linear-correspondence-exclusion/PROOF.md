# Nonplanar fixed linear factor correspondences are excluded

Work over C (equivalently any algebraically closed field of characteristic zero). Let E have dimension four, K⊂Sym²E have dimension at most five, and T:E→E be invertible. Let F⊂P(E) be an integral closed nonplanar surface such that aT(a)∈K for every [a]∈F. Suppose v:K→E is linear, densely compatible on the graph product family, and square-avoiding:

    v(aT(a))∈span(a,T(a)) at general rank-two points;
    v(e²)∉Ce whenever 0≠e²∈K.

**Theorem. These hypotheses are impossible.**

More precisely, the product-span hypothesis alone forces F to be a quadric, T up to scalar to be a balanced involution, and the quadric equation to have no mixed terms for the eigenspace splitting. Compatibility then forces a forbidden square. We assume a fixed projective-linear graph throughout; no assertion that arbitrary nonplanar factor correspondences are linear is made.

## 1. Kernel bound, including nonsemisimple T

Define L_T:Sym²E→Sym²E by L_T(ab)=(aT(b)+bT(a))/2. Thus L_T(a²)=aT(a). We claim

    dim ker L_T≤4,

with equality if and only if T is semisimple with eigenvalues λ,λ,−λ,−λ for some λ≠0.

Write E=⊕E_λ for the generalized eigenspaces of T, with m_λ=dim E_λ and T|E_λ=λI+N_λ. All eigenvalues are nonzero. On Sym²E_λ the operator 2L_T is 2λI plus a nilpotent, hence invertible. On E_λ E_μ≅E_λ⊗E_μ for distinct λ,μ, it is (λ+μ)I+N_λ⊗I+I⊗N_μ. The last two commuting summands have nilpotent sum. Consequently kernels occur only for opposite pairs, and

    dim ker L_T ≤ Σ_{unordered {λ,−λ}} m_λ m_{−λ}.

To bound this sum, choose one sign in each opposite pair and let A and B be the sums of multiplicities in the two chosen groups. The displayed sum is at most AB≤(A+B)²/4≤4. Equality four requires A=B=2, all four dimensions paired, and only one opposite pair: if two pairs had positive multiplicities then AB would contain positive cross-pair terms absent from the displayed sum. Thus the only equality candidate has E=P⊕Q with dim P=dim Q=2 and eigenvalues λ and −λ.

Equality of the kernel dimension with the dimension four of P⊗Q then forces

    N_P⊗I_Q+I_P⊗N_Q=0.

Taking partial trace over Q gives 2N_P+(tr N_Q)I_P=2N_P=0; similarly N_Q=0. This rules out every nontrivial Jordan block, rather than assuming diagonalizability. Conversely, for T=λI_P⊕(−λI_Q), its kernel is exactly PQ≅P⊗Q, of dimension four. The claim follows.

## 2. The degree and exact normal form

An integral surface in P³ is an irreducible hypersurface. Let f be its homogeneous defining polynomial, of degree d≥2 because F is nonplanar. Put H=span{a²:[a]∈F}⊂Sym²E. The annihilator H^⊥ is the vector space of quadrics vanishing on F. Since its homogeneous prime ideal is generated by f, this space is zero for d≥3 and is Cf for d=2.

If d≥3, then H=Sym²E and dim L_T(H)≥10−4=6, contradicting L_T(H)⊂K.

Therefore d=2 and dim H=9. Since

    dim L_T(H)=9−dim(H∩ker L_T)≤5,

we have dim(H∩ker L_T)≥4. Section 1 forces equality throughout: dim ker L_T=4, ker L_T⊂H, dim L_T(H)=5, and K=L_T(H). After rescaling T,

    E=P⊕Q,  dim P=dim Q=2,  T(p+q)=p−q,  ker L_T=PQ.

The inclusion PQ⊂H=ker f, where f is regarded as a linear functional on Sym²E, says precisely that f has no mixed terms:

    f(p+q)=f_P(p)+f_Q(q).

Conversely, these normal forms have a five-dimensional product span for every integral such quadric: H contains the four-dimensional kernel and has dimension nine. This is the complete normal form under the span hypothesis alone.

In particular products are p²−q², and their span K is the hyperplane in Sym²P⊕Sym²Q given, with the natural quadratic/tensor pairing, by

    K={u+w : f_P(u)−f_Q(w)=0}.

No full-support hypothesis was needed.

## 3. Smooth diagonal contact, including a quadric cone

Since F is integral over C, the rank of f is at least three: quadrics of rank at most two factor. Its block-diagonal form gives rank f=rank f_P+rank f_Q. Each block has rank at most two. Therefore the possibilities are (2,2), (2,1), or (1,2). In particular neither block is zero, and one block has rank two.

Exchange P,Q and replace T by −T if necessary so that f_P has rank two. This changes neither the projective graph nor compatibility nor its product span. Over C a nondegenerate binary quadratic has a nonzero isotropic vector e∈P, with f_P(e)=0 and df_P(e)≠0. Thus [e]∈F, Te=e, e²∈K, and [e] is a smooth point of F. Every q∈Q is an affine-cone tangent vector at e, since df_e(q)=0. Its class gives a two-dimensional subspace Q of T_[e]F, disjoint from Ce.

This explicitly avoids the vertex in the rank-three cone case. If the other binary block has rank one, nothing changes: its whole two-dimensional underlying vector space remains tangent at the chosen point. No smoothness claim at a singular isotropic point is used.

## 4. Compatibility differentiation gives the contradiction

On the affine cone over F, the polynomial identity

    v(aT(a))∧a∧T(a)=0

holds identically: it holds densely by compatibility, and the cone is integral. The product aT(a) takes values in K, so this expression and its derivatives are defined on the cone. At a=e, differentiate in a tangent direction ξ. The term differentiating v vanishes because e∧e=0. The remaining terms are

    v(e²)∧ξ∧e + v(e²)∧e∧Tξ
      =v(e²)∧e∧(T−I)ξ=0.

For ξ=q∈Q, (T−I)q=−2q. Hence

    v(e²)∧e∧q=0 for every q∈Q.

For independent q₁,q₂∈Q, this places v(e²) in both planes span(e,q₁) and span(e,q₂), whose intersection is Ce. Therefore v(e²)∈Ce, contradicting square avoidance.

This is the rank-two tangent factor-difference criterion proved directly at a smooth point. Dense compatibility is sufficient; it need not initially be asserted on any particular curve through the square.

## 5. Scope and next bounded step

This excludes every both-nonplanar surface case in which the ordered factor surface is the restriction of ONE FIXED projective-linear isomorphism of P(E). The span condition alone permits only the block-diagonal quadric normal forms above, and compatibility excludes every such normal form.

It does not prove arbitrary both-nonplanar correspondences are graphs, birational, or linear. It does not resolve remaining curve-fiber components or complete the global theorem. A useful next bounded task is to analyze the generic rank of the symmetric bilinear annihilator matrix M(a) along a nonplanar first image F. If rank three is established, its unique kernel line gives a rational correspondence on F; one must then control its degree or diagonal contact without assuming extension to an ambient linear map. Rank at most two along F remains a separate case.
