# Independent audit: quadratic gradient syzygies

## Verdict

**PASS over C, with the scope stated in Section 5.** The argument establishes the symmetric constant-matrix description of quadratic gradient syzygies and eliminates ranks three and four under the stated incidence and compatibility hypotheses. It does **not** eliminate rank five, and it correctly makes no claim to do so.

Audited against `PROOF.md` in this directory and `/research/hm/incidence-foundations/nonplanar-factor-degree-bounds/PROOF.md`, with the rank-four application checked against `/research/hm/incidence-foundations/nonplanar-linear-correspondence-exclusion/PROOF.md`.

## 1. Polarization and symmetry

Both coefficient extractions are correct. The first is the coefficient of t² in the identity with first argument x+ty and final argument x; the second is the coefficient of t with final argument y. Their identical mixed terms cancel. Thus the biform sum r_i(x)q_i(y) is symmetric.

The tensor argument is valid: if S=W direct-sum U, then (S tensor W) intersect (W tensor S)=W tensor W by the four-block decomposition of S tensor S. Independence of the q_i then forces each r_i into W. Symmetry gives a symmetric coefficient matrix. Euler contraction gives the quadratic relation, and differentiating that relation proves the converse. No assumption that the original r_i are independent is needed.

## 2. Cofactors, target dimension, and relation rank

Generic Jacobian rank four makes its maximal-minor vector nonzero. Division by the degree-two gcd gives a nonzero vector of homogeneous quadrics and preserves the syzygy identity. A nonzero symmetric matrix of rank at most two defines a product of one or two nonzero linear forms over C; substituting the independent q_i into that product cannot vanish in the polynomial domain. Hence its rank is at least three.

Euler's identity identifies the radial direction in the rank-four affine differential with the radial target direction at a general nonbase point. Passing to the projective tangent quotients therefore gives rank three. The image closure is an irreducible three-dimensional closed subvariety of the irreducible three-dimensional target quadric and consequently equals it. Its homogeneous prime ideal is generated by that quadratic equation, so the degree-two relation space is one-dimensional. This does not assume generic injectivity of the quadratic map.

## 3. Rank-three factorization and common factors

For nonzero homogeneous quadrics satisfying q_0 q_2=q_1², the standard UFD reduction gives h(a²,ab,b²), with gcd(a,b)=1, after constant adjustments. For example, divide by the common gcd of the three entries; the remaining first and third entries are coprime, and the relation then forces both to be squares. Equal degrees force deg a=deg b. If that degree were zero, the three entries would be proportional, contradicting independence. Their degree is therefore one, and deg h=0. Thus no positive-degree common factor has been overlooked.

An integral nonplanar Q is not contained in either of the planes a=0 or b=0. At its general point, the derivative equations of a² and b² force the partner into the fixed two-dimensional vector space ker a intersect ker b. Its projectivization is only a line, contradicting the prescribed surface image.

## 4. Rank-four UFD factorization

The displayed gcd construction is valid, including homogeneity: gcds of homogeneous polynomials can be chosen homogeneous. Coprimality of m_1,m_2 in a m_1 q_3=a m_2 q_2 forces q_2=b m_1 and q_3=b m_2. Removing gcd(a,b) leaves the asserted two primitive pairs and one common factor h.

Each pair has a common degree. Degree zero for either pair would make its entries constants and yield linear dependence among the four q_i. Both pair degrees are therefore positive; since their sum plus deg h is two, both are one and h is constant. There are no residual common-factor or higher-degree cases.

## 5. Overlap and complementarity

If the two two-dimensional spaces L,H coincide, all four products belong to the three-dimensional space Sym² L, contradicting independence. If their intersection is one-dimensional, the tensor derivative equation implies l(y)=t l(x), m(y)=-t m(x). A common nonzero linear form z then gives 2t z(x)=0. Since Q is nonplanar, z(x) is nonzero generically, so t=0. Every generic partner lies in the one-dimensional annihilator of L+H. The resulting fixed projective point contradicts a surface kernel image.

Thus L and H are complementary. Their evaluation maps jointly identify E with C² direct-sum C². The tensor derivative equation then has precisely the vector-line solution y=t Tx, for the balanced involution T defined in the proof. The full Jacobian kernel, where nonzero on Q, is that same line. Closure extends the incidence condition xT(x) in K over all of Q, exactly as required by the cited fixed-linear-correspondence theorem.

## 6. Fifth equation and application

The polynomial Q_4(x,Tx) is nonzero: identically zero would give the nonzero kernel vector Tx for the full Jacobian on a dense ambient open and contradict generic rank four. It vanishes on the integral quadric Q, so equal degree makes it a scalar multiple of g. Splitting q_4 into the two pure blocks and a mixed block gives A(u)-B(v), with cancellation of the mixed polar terms.

The cited exclusion requires precisely the additional linear compatibility map and square-avoidance condition stated here. Its argument covers both smooth and conical integral quadrics. Consequently rank four is excluded with those extra hypotheses; rank five remains an actual unaddressed case.
