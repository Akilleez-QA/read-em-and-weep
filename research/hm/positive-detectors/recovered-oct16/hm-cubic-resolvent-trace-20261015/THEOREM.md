# Cubic-resolvent reduction of the normalized four-detector trace

## The exact result

Work over a characteristic-zero field k. Let H≤S4 be the order-eight stabilizer of the unordered pairing {12|34}, generated by (12),(34),(13)(24). Use the coherently gerbe-normalized completed scalar sections from historical imported premise hm-pair-symmetry-trace-20261015/THEOREM.md. (see PUBLICATION-SCOPE.md) Its proved character identities, not an assumption about the raw representative, are the input here.

Write v0,...,v4 for the homogeneous elementary binary-product coordinates on P4, with v0=∏Y_i. Put

u = X1 X2 Y3 Y4 + Y1 Y2 X3 X4,
a = (X1 Y2−X2 Y1)(X3 Y4−X4 Y3).

Both u and a have multidegree (1,1,1,1). If a normalized section has a symmetric owned clearing denominator D=∏σ*H_b^{n_b} of equal multidegree m, then uniquely

Y = a S/D,
S = A(v)+u B(v)+u² C(v),

where A,B,C are homogeneous polynomials of degrees m−5,m−6,m−7, respectively. Negative-degree components are zero. The denominator is unchanged.

For q=X3²X4²Y1²Y2² and Δ=∏_{i<j}(X_iY_j−X_jY_i), using the UNNORMALIZED alternation of all 24 permutations, the exact finite trace coefficient is

Alt(qY)/Δ
 = 4 [A+v2 B+(v2²−v1 v3+2v0 v4) C]/D.             (1)

The numerator is homogeneous of degree m−5 on P4, so this is a section of O_P4(−5) on the same carrier intersection. No v0 or discriminant inversion is needed in the representation or final formula. Formula (1) is therefore compatible with the global finite-duality theorem, including infinity and ramification. It does not evaluate the actual A,B,C or claim inexpensive acquisition.

## 1. Cubic equation with exact homogeneous conventions

On v0=1 put x_i=X_i/Y_i. Then v_j=e_j(x),

u1=x1x2+x3x4,
u2=x1x3+x2x4,
u3=x1x4+x2x3.

Direct multiplication gives

u1+u2+u3=v2,
u1u2+u1u3+u2u3=v1v3−4v4,
u1u2u3=v3²+v1²v4−4v2v4.

Homogenizing in the Segre degree (not ordinary total x-degree) gives

R(T)=T³−v2 T²+(v1v3−4v0v4)T
     −(v0v3²+v1²v4−4v0v2v4).                     (2)

Here every v_j, u, and T has degree 1. Each coefficient of T^(3−j) has degree j. In particular u is not being treated as ordinary total x-degree 1: on the affine chart it has ordinary degree 2, as does e2. The two gradings must not be confused.

The homogeneous roots are U1=u, U2=X1X3Y2Y4+Y1Y3X2X4, U3=X1X4Y2Y3+Y1Y4X2X3. Equation (2) and the symmetric root identities hold globally as polynomial identities, since they hold on the dense all-finite chart.

## 2. The global invariant Segre ring is free of rank three

Let R_X=⊕_{r≥0}H0((P1)^4,O(r,r,r,r)) be the Segre section ring and T=k[v0,...,v4] its symmetric polynomial subring. The map

T ⊕ T(−1) ⊕ T(−2) → R_X^H,
(A,B,C) ↦ A+uB+u²C                                  (3)

is an isomorphism of graded T-modules.

First, it is injective. On the v0≠0 chart, the generic symmetric field extension k(x1,...,x4)/k(e1,...,e4) is Galois with group S4. The stabilizer of u1 is exactly H: its three orbit polynomials u1,u2,u3 are distinct. Thus u1 has minimal polynomial of degree three over the symmetric field. No relation of degree at most two is possible. This also proves injectivity before localization, since R_X is a domain and v0 is nonzero.

Next compare each graded dimension. Put n_r=binom(r+2,2). In degree r, invariance under the first pair swap turns the first two (r+1)-dimensional factor spaces into their symmetric square, of dimension n_r. The same holds for the second pair. Invariance under exchanging pairs then gives the symmetric square of this n_r-dimensional space. Therefore

dim (R_X^H)_r = n_r(n_r+1)/2
 = binom(r+4,4)+binom(r+3,4)+binom(r+2,4).

The last expression is exactly the dimension in degree r of the source of (3), with binomial coefficients below degree zero interpreted as zero. The displayed polynomial identity holds for every r≥0. Degreewise injectivity plus equality of dimensions proves surjectivity. Thus this is a global polynomial basis, not a merely generic or localized basis. Equation (2) then supplies the algebra presentation

R_X^H ≅ k[v0,...,v4,u]/(R(u)).

In particular, representing a polynomial in this basis requires no added v0, discriminant, or other denominator. This is an existence-and-uniqueness theorem; an implementation of the coordinate conversion still needs its own cost certificate.

Dehomogenizing at v0 gives the corresponding ordinary polynomial invariant statement

k[x1,x2,x3,x4]^H
 = k[e1,e2,e3,e4] ⊕ u1 k[e1,e2,e3,e4]
   ⊕ u1² k[e1,e2,e3,e4].

Indeed localization and taking degree zero commute with the H invariants, since v0 is invariant. No assertion of S4 semisimplicity in characteristic 2 or 3 is used; the theorem has characteristic-zero scope.

## 3. Why normalized source numerators lie in this module

The given Y has line-bundle degree (−4,−4,−4,−4). Clearing its symmetric owned denominator D gives a polynomial P=DY of equal multidegree m−4. By the normalized pair-character theorem, P is odd under (12) and (34) and even under (13)(24).

Oddness forces P to vanish on each corresponding pair diagonal. In the polynomial Cox ring, each irreducible bracket X1Y2−X2Y1 and X3Y4−X4Y3 divides P. This follows either by reduction modulo its prime ideal or by vanishing on its dense finite part and closure. The brackets are coprime, so their product a divides P. Its character under H is exactly the character of P. The quotient S=P/a is therefore H-invariant, polynomial, and of equal multidegree m−5. Apply (3) to obtain A,B,C with the degrees stated above.

All this is polynomial division by proved factors. It changes neither the owned denominator powers nor the carrier domain. It remains valid on both pair diagonals and at infinity. No separate factorization of each production branch is assumed: the argument applies to the completed normalized section.

## 4. Proof of the trace functional

The existing pair-coset theorem, with tuple representatives (1,2,3,4),(1,3,2,4),(1,4,2,3) of signs +,−,+, gives

Alt(qY)/Δ
 = (4/D) ∑_{i=1}^3 (Ui²−2v0v4) S_i / R'(Ui),       (4)

on the dense locus of distinct Ui. Here S_i=A+Ui B+Ui²C. To check every sign and factor: q plus its pair exchange equals (X1X2Y3Y4)²+(Y1Y2X3X4)² = U1²−2v0v4; the eight-element H coset sum combined with averaging these two weights contributes 4. Also

U1−U2=(X1Y4−X4Y1)(X2Y3−X3Y2),
U1−U3=(X1Y3−X3Y1)(X2Y4−X4Y2),

so R'(U1)=Δ/a. For the second and third cosets the corresponding signed brackets give the same identity with their coset signs. Thus the quotient in (4) has exactly the Vandermonde orientation of the canonical trace theorem.

For a monic cubic T³−s1T²+s2T−s3 with distinct roots Ui, let

L_k=∑ Ui^k/R'(Ui).

Lagrange interpolation of polynomials of degree at most two gives L0=L1=0 and L2=1: compare coefficients of T² in the interpolation formula. Reducing Ui³ by the cubic gives

L3=s1,
L4=s1²−s2.

Apply this with s1=v2, s2=v1v3−4v0v4. The coefficient of A in (4) is L2−2v0v4 L0=1. That of B is L3−2v0v4 L1=v2. That of C is L4−2v0v4 L2=v2²−v1v3+2v0v4. This proves (1) on the dense locus.

Both sides of (1), after multiplication by DΔ, are polynomial identities. Therefore equality extends across the resolvent discriminant and v0=0. More strongly, its right side already has only the original D in its denominator. The intermediate R'(Ui) expressions are proof devices, not new denominators in the answer or instructions to evaluate at collision points.

## 6. Global kernel and exact structural exclusions

Let g=v2²−v1v3+2v0v4 and let L:R_X^H→T denote the degree-preserving T-linear map from (1) before division by D:

L(A+uB+u²C)=4(A+v2B+gC).

It is a split surjection, with section f↦f/4. Set

k1=u−v2,                 degree 1,
k2=u²−v2²+v1v3−2v0v4,   degree 2.

The change from (1,u,u²) to (1,k1,k2) is triangular with diagonal 1 over T. Hence

R_X^H = T·1 ⊕ T·k1 ⊕ T·k2,
ker L = T·k1 ⊕ T·k2 ≅ T(−1)⊕T(−2).

For a homogeneous numerator S of degree r, L(S)=0 if and only if S=k1 B+k2 C with B∈T_(r−1), C∈T_(r−2). More generally, an independently proved identity S=P+k1 B+k2 C gives L(S)=4P. These statements globalize without new poles, and after division by any symmetric owned D the trace is respectively zero or 4P/D.

This can help before acquiring all three old-basis coefficients: a source-level symbolic identity placing a completed numerator in the kernel submodule proves its trace entry zero, or a certificate for its representative P proves the entry using only P. Such a proof might use the exact source formulas and cancellation identities rather than materializing A,B,C. No actual completed source is claimed here to have this membership or a cheap certificate.

The kernel is a T-submodule, not an ideal in R_X^H. For example L(u·k1)=4(−v1v3+2v0v4), generically nonzero. Thus only symmetric polynomial multipliers automatically preserve these exclusions; arbitrary H-invariant multiplication, nonlinear residual reconstruction, or differentiation need not preserve them. A branchwise exclusion is legitimate only when the relevant branch lies in the authenticated module and is being combined by operations preserving this kernel. The safest scope is the completed normalized cleared section.
