# Cubic Hilbert-Burch syzygies on the order-three exceptional gate line

## Theorem and boundary

On the nonzero universal gate line ell3=span(R1-2R2), let

    F=N L (R1-2R2)

be the four normalized nonics of PR1036. Their exact homogeneous component
ideal has an equivariant Hilbert-Burch resolution

    0 -> A O(-12) -> P O(-9) -> I_B -> 0.                 (1)

Equivalently the normalized nonic kernel bundle is

    K_F = A O(-3),                                       (2)

not V O(-3). The ideal is saturated, and its scheme has length54. The
splitting follows from an explicit cubic syzygy matrix whose full signed
maximal-minor row is a nonzero scalar times F, not from matching Chern
classes, stability assumptions, or a generic-rank argument.

Through PR1049, the original-fourth D kernel fits

    0 -> O(-4) -> K_H -> E -> 0,
    0 -> E -> A O(-2) -> C_L -> 0.                       (3)

Here C_L has length ten; E is locally R^2 plus m at its ten points, and
the first sequence is the nontrivial local Koszul extension there. In
particular K_H has rank four, c1=-10 and c2=46. This does NOT assert that
K_H splits or that the actual unknown gate occupies ell3.

## 2. Full matrix and maximal-minor certificate

S.json contains the complete four-by-three matrix S of cubic polynomials,
with rational and sqrt5 coefficients. Its columns are three independent
solutions of C. The JSON file is part of the certificate, not a private
scratch calculation. The checker loads every entry and verifies

    F S = 0,
    (-1)^i det(S with row i deleted) = (9sqrt5/2050) F_i
                          for each i=0,1,2,3.             (4)

All four equations in (4) are exact polynomial identities in p,q,t.
They are not just equalities of radicals, matching zero sets, or isolated
nonzero minors. In particular the ideal of maximal minors of S is exactly
the homogeneous ideal generated by F, with no saturation required for this
algebraic equality. The scalar 9sqrt5/2050 is nonzero.

The checker also reconstructs the entire degree-twelve coefficient matrix,
certifies its rank37 over the number field, and checks that the coefficient
vectors of S have rank three. Thus the displayed columns exhaust ALL cubic
syzygies, not only an exhibited equivariant subspace.

## 3. Hilbert-Burch exactness and saturation

Let R=K[p,q,t] and I=(F_0,F_1,F_2,F_3). PR1037 proves that the projective
zero scheme is nonempty and finite. Hence the homogeneous ideal I has
height two: it has no height-one component, and the affine cone over its
nonempty projective scheme has dimension one.

By (4), I is the ideal of maximal minors of the four-by-three matrix S.
The Hilbert-Burch theorem therefore gives the exact graded resolution

    0 -> R(-12)^3 --S--> R(-9)^4 --F--> I -> 0.             (5)

Signs are consistent because the signed minors in (4) are the row F
up to one common nonzero scalar. This is a genuine resolution, not only
a generically exact complex. This graded resolution is minimal: every
entry of S has positive degree three (and the generators F have degree nine),
so its differentials have no degree-zero unit entries. Since R/I has depth one by (5) and height
two, it has no submodule supported at the irrelevant maximal ideal.
Consequently I is saturated. Its sheaf is exactly I_B, so sheafifying
(5) and twisting by nine gives the nonequivariant splitting
K_F=O(-3)^3.

For an independent consistency check, its Hilbert numerator is

    1-4t^9+3t^12.

It has a double zero at t=1, and its second derivative there divided by
two is54. This recovers the already known length, rather than using that
length or Chern data to infer splitting.

## 4. Exact representation identification

The cubic syzygy space U=H0(K_F(3)) is three-dimensional. It is stable
under the binary icosahedral group because the universal map F is equivariant.
The center acts trivially on P and on polynomials in A, so U factors through
A5. The only possible three-dimensional A5 representations are A, V, or
three trivial representations.

We distinguish them by a full coefficient-space action, not by evaluating
a section at a chosen point. Put z=sqrt5, phi=(1+z)/2. The pinned binary
group contains the generator

    g = [[phi/2+i/2, (phi-1)/2],
         [-(phi-1)/2, phi/2-i/2]].

Its determinant is one and g^5=-I, so it induces an order-five projective
element. Let A_g=Sym2(g) and P_g=g tensor g', where g' replaces z by -z.
The pinned Pi intertwining identity is checked again in the same basis.
The action on a polynomial syzygy column is

    S_j(a) |-> P_g S_j(A_g^(-1)a).

The checker expands ALL forty coefficient coordinates of each column,
solves the resulting three-dimensional change-of-basis matrix T, and
verifies the whole coefficient identity, not merely its trace. It finds

    trace(T)=(1+sqrt5)/2 = trace(A_g).

The trace on V at this element is (1-sqrt5)/2; that of three trivial
representations is3. Thus U=A. The canonical evaluation map
U O(-3)->K_F is the isomorphism already certified by (5), and is
equivariant. This proves (1)-(2) with the correct representation.

## 5. Consequence for the original-fourth kernel

PR1049 applies to the actual determinant-identified original-fourth D
row, not just an unrelated degree-ten polynomial system. Substituting
K_F=A O(-3) into its exact snake sequence yields (3). It identifies the
middle quotient precisely as the elementary modification of A O(-2)
by the induced surjection to C_L, not as a bundle of matching Chern data.

The retained local description is uniform: E=R^2 plus m and the extension
by O(-4) is locally nontrivial Koszul at each of the ten C3 axes. Its double
dual is A O(-2), and E**/E=C_L. With b=54 the Chern values are

    c2(K_F)=27,       c2(E)=22,       c2(K_H)=46.

These are exact structural restrictions on the original-fourth kernel
IF the unknown nonzero gate lies on ell3. They do not prove that it does,
exclude that source case, or identify an actual scalar coefficient. The
new information is the explicit equivariant Hilbert-Burch kernel, whereas
the prior Chern match alone did not justify any splitting.
