# The primary incidence is an integral Gorenstein complete intersection

Work over the complex numbers. Put A=M_0, S=S_2 and M=M_2, of dimensions 5, 15 and 10. Let m:S tensor A -> M be primary multiplication, and define

    I = {([x],[q]) in P(A) x P(S) : m(q,x)=0}

with the scheme structure given by its ten bilinear equations. Write O(a,b) for the restriction of the usual product line bundle.

## 2. Integral local complete intersection

The ambient product is smooth of dimension 18. Over X10 the incidence is the projective bundle of the rank-five kernel of e, hence smooth, irreducible and of dimension eight. Over the rank-nine locus its dimension is at most 2+5=7. Over Omega its dimension is 6.

On the other hand, an ideal generated by ten elements in the smooth eighteen-dimensional ambient variety has every irreducible component of its zero locus of dimension at least eight. Thus no component of I can lie wholly over the complement of X10. Every component meets the irreducible open projective bundle, which is dense in I; consequently I has a unique irreducible component and dimension eight.

At any closed point of I the ten local defining equations generate an ideal of height ten in a regular local ring. They form a regular sequence: in a Cohen-Macaulay local ring an ideal of height equal to its number of generators is generated by a regular sequence. Therefore I is a local complete intersection and Cohen-Macaulay. Its dense open projective bundle is smooth, so I is generically reduced. A Cohen-Macaulay scheme has no embedded associated primes, and an irreducible generically reduced scheme with this property is reduced. Hence I is integral.

Projection pi:I -> B is proper. By the scheme-level graph description on the rank-four locus from PR555, it is birational. This produces an integral Gorenstein proper birational model of B. It is not asserted to be a smooth resolution.

## 3. Dualizing line bundle and Koszul presentation

The ten equations form a regular section of E=M tensor O(1,1). The conormal bundle is E* restricted to I, so adjunction gives

    omega_I = (omega_(P4 x P14) tensor det E)|I
            = O_I(5,-5),

where all one-dimensional constant factors are suppressed. With the convention that projective space parametrizes lines, the full constant factor is det(A*) tensor det(S*) tensor det(M); retain it when tracking equivariant linearizations. This is the dualizing line bundle on the possibly singular scheme, not a claim of smoothness.

The regular sequence also gives the global Koszul resolution of O_I, whose j-th term is

    exterior^j M* tensor O(-j,-j),  0 <= j <= 10.

Thus, for all integers a,b, its Euler characteristic is

    chi(O_I(a,b)) = sum_(j=0)^10 (-1)^j binom(10,j)
                   binom(a-j+4,4) binom(b-j+14,14),

with binomial coefficients interpreted as polynomial expressions. This follows from the resolution and the Euler-characteristic polynomial of projective space; it does not assert higher-cohomology vanishing for general twists.

There is, however, an unconditional vanishing at the untwisted structure sheaf:

    H^0(I,O_I)=C,    H^i(I,O_I)=0 for every i>0.

For each j=1,...,10 the line bundle O(-j) on P14 has zero cohomology in all degrees (its twist lies strictly between -15 and 0). By the product cohomology formula, O(-j,-j) on the ambient product is therefore acyclic, regardless of its P4 factor. The Koszul resolution has only its j=0 term contributing to cohomology; that term O on the product has H^0=C and no higher cohomology. This proves the displayed vanishing without assuming normality or rational singularities.

## 4. Exact singular locus in each rank-eight fiber

Use the universal symbolic bases of PR553. The Heisenberg-equivariant exterior isomorphism has the form

    phi(e_a wedge e_c) = v_(a+c) tensor w_(c-a),
    w_0=0,  w_(-d)=-w_d,

where w_1,w_2 are independent. The primary parameter space is S=A tensor sl(W), with W two-dimensional, and, up to one common nonzero scalar,

    m(e_a tensor D, e_c) = v_(a+c) tensor D w_(c-a).

At x=e_0, evaluation has image the sum of v_b tensor W for b not equal to zero. Its cokernel is v_0 tensor W. Its kernel consists of arbitrary e_0 tensor D_0 and the four lines e_b tensor D_b satisfying D_b w_b=0. Indeed, sl(W) -> W, D -> D w_b, is surjective and has one-dimensional kernel.

Choose a nonzero generator N_b of that kernel. A nonzero traceless endomorphism of a two-dimensional space killing w_b has image exactly the line spanned by w_b: in a basis beginning with w_b it has matrix with sole possibly nonzero entry in the upper-right position. Thus

    ker e_(e0) = (e_0 tensor sl(W)) direct-sum
                 direct-sum_(b != 0) C(e_b tensor N_b).

Write a point of its P6 as [D_0,t_1,t_-1,t_2,t_-2]. The derivatives of the incidence equations in the q directions fill image e_(e0), of dimension eight, even after passing to the projective tangent space, since e_(e0)(q)=0. Smoothness therefore depends precisely on surjectivity of the residual derivative in the x directions onto v_0 tensor W.

For the tangent direction e_c, c not equal to zero, only the summand a=-c survives this projection. The residual column is

    t_(-c) N_(-c) w_(2c).

It is a nonzero scalar multiple of t_(-c) w_(-c), because w_(2c) and w_(-c) are independent. The two columns c=+/-1 lie in Cw_1, and the two columns c=+/-2 lie in Cw_2. These lines are independent. Accordingly the residual derivative has rank two exactly when both pairs

    (t_1,t_-1) and (t_2,t_-2)

are nonzero. Since I is an eight-dimensional local complete intersection, the Jacobian criterion now gives the following exact set-theoretic statement:

    Sing(I) intersect pi_X^(-1)([e_0])
      = {t_1=t_-1=0} union {t_2=t_-2=0}.

These are two projective four-spaces inside the projective six-dimensional fiber. They meet in the projective plane {t_1=t_-1=t_2=t_-2=0}, parametrized by D_0. At points on either P4 outside their intersection the tangent dimension of I is nine; at the P2 intersection it is ten; elsewhere on the fiber it is eight. The same argument applies to each of the thirty eigenlines.

In particular the singular points over Omega form a set of dimension four. They do not form a divisor in I. This does not establish normality of I: the singularities over the rank-nine locus still require analysis.

## 5. Verification status and contribution

The proof is structural algebraic geometry with a universal Heisenberg weight computation. The rank-eight singularity calculation depends only on the independence of the two multiplicity vectors, and is valid for every equivariant isomorphism of the indicated representation types. The new conclusions are the integral local-complete-intersection structure of the full incidence, its explicit dualizing line bundle, and its exact singular set and tangent dimensions on all thirty rank-eight fibers. No formal proof-assistant verification, normality, rational-singularity claim, or smooth resolution is asserted.
