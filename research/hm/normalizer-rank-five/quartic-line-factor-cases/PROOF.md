# Quartic line-factor families: square-avoiding classification

Work over C. Let E have dimension four, X=<x,y>, and K have dimension five, full support, and be spanned by the products lW_l, where l=sx+ty and W is the saturated rank-two kernel subbundle. Suppose deg W=-2, with splitting (0,-2) or (-1,-1). A correction v:K->E is compatible if v(lm) belongs to <l,m> for general l,m in W_l. Square avoidance means v(e^2) is not proportional to e whenever e^2 belongs to K.

Before using fibers, note that K is contained in XE, so its quotient there has dimension two. The pencil is therefore a map O^4 -> (XE/K) tensor O(1)=O(1)^2 with kernel W. Its rank-two image has degree -deg W=2. Its torsion cokernel in O(1)^2 thus has length zero, proving surjectivity everywhere. Consequently W_l={m:lm belongs to K} at every base point, not merely generically.

## 1. Contact lemma

Suppose l(0)=e belongs to W_0 and l(t) does not belong to W_t for nearby nonzero t. Compatibility forces v(e^2) in Ce.

Indeed fiber approaches at t=0 give limiting support W_0. Choose a fixed complement U to W_0; it remains complementary to W_t locally. Write l(t)=m(t)+u(t), with m(t) in W_t and u(t) in U. Then m(0)=e and u has nonzero leading term t^k u_0, with u_0 in U nonzero. The tensors l(t)m(t) tend to e^2 and their support planes tend to <e,u_0>. Compatibility extends to support-plane limits by closed incidence, so v(e^2) belongs to W_0 intersect <e,u_0>=Ce.

If l belongs to W_l for every l, then O(-1) embeds as a subbundle of W. This is impossible for O direct-sum O(-2): its map to O(-2) vanishes and its map to O has a zero. For W=O(-1)^2, extend l to a linear second generator m=s p+t r. Full support says p,r project independently to E/X. Consequently Sym^2 X and the three coefficients of lm are independent, giving span dimension six, contrary to dim K=5. Thus the all-squares case is impossible here.

It follows that any square-avoiding compatible quartic family must have no square l^2 in K. Since K is contained in XE, every square in K is a square in X. Thus K must be entirely squarefree.

## 2. Normal forms

Since dim XE=7, K has codimension two in XE. Squarefreeness forces K intersect Sym^2 X to be a one-dimensional line generated by a nonsquare binary quadratic: a two-dimensional subspace would meet the Veronese conic. Choose x,y so this line is Cxy.

The two defining equations of K restrict to independent x^2 and y^2 coefficient functionals. The pencil defining W can consequently be written

    [s, 0, a s+b t, c s+d t]
    [0, t, e s+f t, g s+h t].

Replacing the complement basis z,w by z+alpha x+beta y and w+gamma x+delta y removes a,c,f,h. Thus

    s m_x+t B(u)=0,   t m_y+s C(u)=0,  u in U=<z,w>.

Because deg W=-2, the map O^4 -> O(1)^2 is surjective: its rank-two image has degree two and hence its torsion cokernel has length zero. Evaluation at [0:1] and [1:0] therefore implies B and C are both nonzero.

There are just two possibilities, after GL(U) and rescaling x,y. In the proportional case first set B=z*, C=c z* with c nonzero. Under x'=alpha x, y'=beta y, z'=(alpha^2/beta)z, the normalized coefficient c becomes c(alpha/beta)^3, so a cube root over C makes it one:

A. B,C independent:

    K=<xy, xz, xw-y^2, yz-x^2, yw>,
    W_l=<-t x+s z, -s y+t w>.

The displayed columns have independent values everywhere, so W=O(-1)^2.

B. B,C proportional and nonzero:

    K=<xy, xz-y^2, xw, yz-x^2, yw>,
    W_l=<w, -t^2 x-s^2 y+st z>.

The quadratic column never vanishes or becomes proportional to w, so W=O direct-sum O(-2).

Both K have dimension five and full support. Squares must lie in X, but K intersect Sym^2 X=Cxy, proving squarefreeness directly. In particular both are genuine examples, with v=0 satisfying compatibility and square avoidance.

## 3. All compatible corrections

For a squarefree family the subbundles L=O(-1) and W of E O are fiberwise disjoint. Compatibility on each ruling and linearity imply, modulo L, that the map L tensor W -> E/L is scalar times W. Hence that scalar is a global section b of L*=O(1). The remainder has the form a(m)l with a in H^0(W*). Thus, globally,

    v(lm)=a(m)l+b(l)m.

In A write b=mu s+nu t and a on the two linear columns as A s+B t and C s+D t. The two coefficient tensors at t^2 in the first product and s^2 in the second are both -xy. Their images coincide exactly when B=-mu and C=-nu. Consequently the four remaining parameters give precisely

    v(xy)=nu x+mu y,
    v(xz)=A x+mu z,
    v(yz-x^2)=-2mu x+A y+nu z,
    v(xw-y^2)=D x-2nu y+mu w,
    v(yw)=D y+nu w.

These are the contractions v(lm)=d(l)m+d(m)l with d(x)=mu,d(y)=nu,d(z)=A,d(w)=D.

In B write a(w)=lambda and a(-t^2 x-s^2 y+st z)=A s^2+B st+C t^2. The s^3 and t^3 coefficient tensors in the quadratic-column product are both -xy. Their images coincide exactly when A=-nu and C=-mu. The four remaining parameters again give precisely the contractions, now with d(x)=mu,d(y)=nu,d(z)=B,d(w)=lambda.

Therefore every square-avoiding compatible quartic family under the stated hypotheses is one of A or B, and every compatible correction in either case is a contraction. This classifies pairs admitting square avoidance; square-containing K may exist but cannot admit such a correction.

## 4. No two-by-two product rectangle

Neither quartic K can contain A B for two-dimensional subspaces A,B of E. Indeed projecting K subset XE to Sym^2(E/X) gives zero. Products of nonzero vectors in the symmetric algebra are nonzero, so the projected product of A and B can vanish identically only if A or B is contained in X. That two-plane is therefore X itself. The other two-plane would then be contained in W_l for every l, making W the corresponding constant subbundle of degree zero, a contradiction.
