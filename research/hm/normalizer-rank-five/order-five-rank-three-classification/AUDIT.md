## 1. Cocycles and the torus action

For a fixed U=⟨u(1)⟩, every subgroup projecting isomorphically onto U has exactly one element projecting to u(1). Writing it as (a,u(1)) therefore gives a genuine parametrization of subgroups, with no duplication. Its fifth power is trivial because

    sum(i=0,...,4) u(i)(α,β) = (5α,5β+10α) = 0 in F5².

Every a gives an element of exact order five, since its projection has order five. Thus there are 25 subgroups above U.

Translation conjugation changes a by (1−u(1))v=(0,−v_x), so precisely β changes. The five α values give precisely the five A-conjugacy classes over this U.

Conjugation by h_s=diag(s,s⁻¹) changes the unipotent parameter to s⁻² and changes the first translation coordinate to sα. Taking the power m with m=s² in F5 restores parameter one. The first coordinate of a power is m times the first coordinate, because lower unipotents leave that coordinate unchanged. The resulting coordinate is therefore s³α, as claimed. Cubing permutes F5× because gcd(3,4)=1. Hence the four nonzero classes are fused, while zero stays zero.

For completeness, these two types cannot fuse by a further conjugation: any conjugation between subgroups above this same U projects to N_SL2(F5)(U), the lower triangular subgroup generated by U and the diagonal torus. Its U part leaves α unchanged and its diagonal part multiplies α by a nonzero scalar; A-conjugation also leaves α unchanged. Thus zero versus nonzero is invariant. Since SL2(F5) has six Sylow-five subgroups, the two full N-conjugacy classes have sizes 30 and 120 respectively.

## 2. Both standard eigenspace lines

D has eigenvalue multiplicities 1,2,2, with projective fixed locus [e0], L1, L2 exactly as written. On L_r=P⟨e_r,e_−r⟩, r=1 or 2, write a non-endpoint as [e_r+t e_−r]. The induced transformations are

    Z: t ↦ ζ^(−2r)t,
    R: t ↦ 1/t.

Because −2r is nonzero modulo five, the rotation has exact order five; inversion gives a faithful dihedral action of order ten on either line. No nonidentity rotation fixes a non-endpoint. Every other possible nonidentity stabilizer contains a reflection Z^kR. These are actual involutions in N because RZR=Z⁻¹. The only remaining exceptional orbit is the two endpoints, which are Heisenberg eigenpoints.

After removing endpoints and all reflection-fixed points, every orbit therefore has ten distinct points. N-equivariance puts the entire ten-point orbit in Z3 whenever one point lies in Z3. A restricted degree-eight minor is a section of O_P1(8); ten distinct zeros force it to vanish identically. Doing this for every defining minor puts the whole line in Z3, contradicting finiteness. This correctly excludes both lines and every conjugate zero-class fixed locus. It requires neither simple zeros nor reducedness of the original determinantal scheme.

## 4. Exact stabilizers, orbit uniqueness, and cardinality

The argument excluding E is sound. A Heisenberg eigenpoint has a unique one-dimensional A-stabilizer B'. If it is fixed by g, then g normalizes B'. A nontrivial two-dimensional unipotent has exactly one invariant line, namely B. Hence B'=B, whose eigenpoints are the coordinate endpoints. A monomial five-cycle fixes none of these. Thus these five simple eigenpoints lie outside E, and the stipulated A-freeness gives an injection S=N_p→SL2(F5).

If t∈S were an involution, its image would be −I, the unique involution in SL2(F5). Its commutator with g would consequently lie in A∩S=1. Therefore t would centralize C, contradicting the odd order 25 of N_N(C). By Cauchy's theorem, |S| is odd; since it divides 120 and contains C, it is 5 or 15.

An order-15 subgroup would inject into PSL2(F5)=A5, since it has odd order. Its Sylow-five subgroup would be unique: the number divides three and is one modulo five. It would then lie in that Sylow subgroup's order-ten normalizer in A5, an impossibility. Hence S=C exactly.

Every point under consideration therefore has orbit size 3000/5=600. Conjugacy of the 120 subgroups and normalizer transitivity on each five-point fixed set show that their union is one orbit. Exact stabilizers also show that different subgroups' fixed sets are disjoint. Thus the count 120·5=600 and uniqueness agree independently. The exclusion of zero-class fixed loci then proves there is at most one eligible 600-point orbit.
