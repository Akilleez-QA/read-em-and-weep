## 1. Exact plane orbit and conic avoidance

A genuine 750-point orbit under N of order 3000 has stabilizer S of order four. The central-involution cocycle argument conjugates this stabilizer into K=SL2(F5). After the same translation of the point, S is still its full N-stabilizer, not merely a subgroup fixing it. Therefore its K-stabilizer is S intersect K=S. The K-orbit has exactly 120/4=30 distinct points.

The finite-support classification places a surviving candidate in P(T), on the negative eigenspace line of an order-four element, after excluding its negative-space eigenpoints and its positive axis. The axis has the larger Q8 stabilizer and belongs to the 375-point orbit. No enlargement of the candidate's stabilizer can be overlooked because the argument starts with a genuine full orbit of size 750.

The candidate avoids the canonical conic by the prior pointwise theorem. Since the conic is K-invariant, its entire 30-point plane orbit avoids the conic. Similarly it avoids E. Avoidance of E is needed in the imported stabilizer/location analysis and in the optional unique-positive-plane observation, but is not needed in the interpolation lemma itself.

The center of K acts trivially on T, so the same orbit is an A5-orbit with stabilizer of order two. The use of an honest linear action on T, not an arbitrary projective lift, is important for speaking about polynomial semi-invariants. This action is supplied by the prior representation setup.

## 2. Independent proof of the low-degree invariant claim

The displayed symmetric-character table is consistent with the generating series and gives invariant dimensions 1,0,1,0,1,0 in degrees zero through five. There is also a direct geometric verification that avoids a character table entirely.

Let F be a nonzero invariant homogeneous polynomial on T of degree d, with 1<=d<=5. Its restriction to the smooth invariant conic C is a section of O_C(d)=O_P1(2d). If this section is nonzero, its zero divisor is an invariant effective divisor of positive degree 2d<=10. Every point orbit on C has at least twelve points. Any nonempty invariant effective divisor must therefore have degree at least twelve, a contradiction.

Thus F restricts to zero on C. Since the plane ideal of the smooth conic is generated by its irreducible invariant quadratic q, the polynomial F is divisible by q. The quotient F/q is again invariant because q is invariant. Inducting on degree proves that the only invariant forms through degree five are constants, scalar multiples of q in degree two, and scalar multiples of q^2 in degree four. There are no nonzero invariants in degrees one, three, or five.

The conic orbit bound used here is already an audited input to the canonical-conic theorem: the effective faithful A5 action on P1 has cyclic point stabilizers of order at most five. This verification neither identifies T with the symmetric square of the negative representation nor introduces a target twist.

## 3. Stable gcd and the interpolation lemma

For an off-conic A5-orbit O, the full degree-five vanishing space L=I(O)_5 is A5-stable. Suppose L is nonzero. A homogeneous gcd D of a finite basis is also a gcd of the whole space. Any group element takes D to another gcd of the same space, so the line spanned by D is stable.

Its scalar action is a genuine one-dimensional character because the action on the polynomial ring is honest. A5 is perfect; consequently D is invariant after choosing any nonzero representative. Its degree is at most five. Section 2 gives D constant, proportional to q, or proportional to q^2. The possibility that all forms are proportional and D has degree five is thus automatically excluded.

Divide every member of L by D. The resulting homogeneous system L' has common degree m=5-deg(D), hence m is 5, 3, or 1. It has gcd one. All its members still vanish at O because D is nonzero at every point of O. Removing a fixed divisor without this last condition would have been invalid; conic avoidance supplies exactly what is needed.

Choose any nonzero F in L'. For every irreducible factor H of F there is some member of L' not divisible by H, because the common gcd is one. The members divisible by H form a proper linear subspace. Over C a finite union of these proper subspaces cannot exhaust L', so one can choose G in L' sharing no factor with F. This is a precise justification for passing from gcd one for the entire linear system to a coprime pair; simply choosing an arbitrary pair would not suffice.

The plane curves F=0 and G=0 have degrees m and m and no common component. Their intersection has length m^2 by Bezout. Every distinct point of O contributes at least one to that length, so |O|<=m^2<=25. Therefore an off-conic orbit with more than 25 points cannot lie on any nonzero plane quintic.

This argument uses the full stable vanishing space, not the false assertion that an individual minor is invariant.

## 4. A single fixed parity block supplies the quintics

For middle parameter u in the positive space T, central equivariance decomposes the fixed source Sym^2(V) into its positive and negative subspaces and maps them to the corresponding fixed eigenspaces J+ and J- of the actual induced target action. Thus the family is block diagonal on P(T). Each block is a matrix of linear forms in u, and total rank is the sum of the two block ranks.

Since the central involution commutes with K, K preserves each of these source and target subspaces. Equivariance therefore preserves the rank of each block separately along a K-orbit. It does not merely preserve the unordered pair of ranks, and it does not exchange the two blocks.

At a putative Z5 point, rank b=rank Q+4<=9. Hence at least one block has rank at most four. Fix that block once and for all. Its rank is at most four at every one of the 30 plane-orbit points. Every five-minor of this same block vanishes there.

Each such minor is a homogeneous plane polynomial of degree five, possibly identically zero. The interpolation lemma forces every minor to be identically zero. The block therefore has rank at most four on the entire plane. The canonical conic lies in this plane, while the imported pointwise theorem gives rank at least five for both blocks at every conic point. Whichever block was selected yields a contradiction.

Only the existence of an appropriate nonzero five-minor at a conic point is ultimately needed; the stronger pointwise conic result supplies it for each block. There is no assumption that the even block has rank at least five away from the conic, nor any need to make such a claim.

The target action used here is the actual induced one. The previously audited target discussion supplies positive and negative dimensions eight and twelve and compatibility with the operation; perfectness precludes an additional scalar-character twist. No identification of the historical spinorial target representation with the negative summand of V is introduced.

## 5. Optional orbit-distribution and polar-line remarks

Outside E, a point cannot be fixed by two distinct normalizer involutions: their product is a nonzero translation, contradicting free translation action. Each of the 25 positive planes is fixed pointwise projectively by its defining involution. Thus a point of the candidate orbit lies in at most one such plane. Normalizer equivariance and the standard-plane orbit of size 30 then account for all 25 times 30 points. This observation is consistent but unnecessary for the exclusion.

The order-four generator's negative eigenspace line is polar to its positive axis. Since the latter has K-stabilizer Q8, this line has setwise K-stabilizer Q8, not the binary tetrahedral normalizer of Q8. On the line the chosen involution is scalar, leaving an effective group of order two. A generic orbit on this one line can therefore have only two points. The proof correctly avoids trying to contradict a degree-eight line bound with those two points; it instead uses the full 30-point plane orbit and degree-five parity minors.
