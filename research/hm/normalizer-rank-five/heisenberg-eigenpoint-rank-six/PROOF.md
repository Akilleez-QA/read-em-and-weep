# Residual rank at least six at the thirty canonical Heisenberg eigenpoints

## Statement and scope

Work over C. Let V have dimension five with Schrödinger basis e_i indexed by F_5. Let f:V³→J be outer-symmetric, cyclic-zero, and equivariant under the projective Heisenberg normalizer N, with its homogeneous linearization. Assume:

1. For each nonzero a, the map V/Ca→J, b↦f(a,a,b), is injective.
2. The linear span of all values of f has dimension at least twenty.

Let Q_u:Sym²(V/Cu)→J/R_u be the residual middle map, where R_u={f(u,u,v):v∈V}. Then at each of the thirty canonical Heisenberg eigenpoints,

    rank Q_u ∈ {6,8,10}.

The image-dimension hypothesis is additional to the hypotheses of ../heisenberg-eigenpoint-rank-parity/PROOF.md and must not be silently omitted. For the actual operation in the standing problem, its target J3 is the irreducible twenty-dimensional N-module A*⊗Sym³W. Repeated-input injectivity makes the operation nonzero, so its N-stable linear image is all J3. Thus the extra hypothesis holds structurally, without evaluating an operation value.

## 1. Rank four forces vanishing on every distinct coordinate triple

At u=e_0 put E=span(e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4). The audited parity proof, Sections 3–7, establishes that the residual kernel K_0⊂Sym²E is a sum of a subset of the two nonsquare monomial orbits, of dimensions four and two. The four square monomials cannot lie in K_0. Its possible residual ranks are consequently 4,6,8,10.

Suppose the rank is four. Then dim K_0=6, and necessarily

    K_0=span{e_i e_j: i,j≠0, i≠j}.

The same proof shows that the unreduced middle map vanishes on this kernel. To recall the crucial point, for B generated by e_i↦ζ^i e_i and e_i↦ζ^(i²)e_i, the characters of E and Sym²E are disjoint. The unique correction K_0→E obtained by identifying E with R_e0 via v↦f(e_0,e_0,v) is B-equivariant, hence zero. Therefore

    f(e_i,e_0,e_j)=0   for distinct nonzero i,j.

The Heisenberg shift X e_i=e_(i+1) is in N. Applying its kth power gives

    f(e_a,e_k,e_b)=0   whenever a,k,b are pairwise distinct.

Indeed write i=a−k and j=b−k. Both are nonzero and unequal. Scalar choices in lifting X have no effect on these zero identities.

## 2. The universal distinct-index space has dimension twenty

Use the universal Schur model

    T=ker(V⊗Λ²V → Λ³V),    dim T=50−10=40,
    F(a,b,c)=a⊗(b∧c)+c⊗(b∧a).

Its values span T, satisfy outer symmetry and cyclic zero, and yield the factorization f=q∘F through a linear map q:T→J. Let K=ker q. Its N-invariance follows from equivariance, even if lifts are changed by scalar factors.

Let D⊂T be spanned by F(e_a,e_b,e_c) with three distinct indices. For each unordered triple {a,b,c}, its ambient tensor block has dimension three, with basis

    e_a⊗(e_b∧e_c), e_b⊗(e_a∧e_c), e_c⊗(e_a∧e_b)

for a<b<c. Exterior multiplication maps this block onto its one-dimensional Λ³V coordinate, with coefficients (1,−1,1). Its kernel has dimension two and is spanned by the F tensors with these indices. Distinct unordered triples have disjoint tensor coordinates. Hence

    dim D=2·binomial(5,3)=20.

All tensors in D have zero coefficient on every repeated-index coordinate e_a⊗(e_a∧e_b). Section 1 implies D⊂K. Assumption 2 gives dim K≤40−20=20. Consequently

    K=D.

In particular D would have to be N-invariant.

## 3. Fourier transformation contradicts this invariance

Let H be the unnormalized Fourier transformation

    H e_i=Σ_(j∈F_5) ζ^(ij)e_j.

It is invertible and its projective class belongs to N: with Z e_i=ζ^i e_i,

    H X H⁻¹=Z,    H Z H⁻¹=X⁻¹.

The corresponding Heisenberg-label transformation has determinant one. A scalar multiple of H may be used to satisfy a preferred determinant or finite-group normalization; on T that changes the following transformed tensor by a nonzero scalar only.

The tensor t=F(e_1,e_0,e_2) belongs to D. In

    Ht=(H e_1)⊗((H e_0)∧(H e_2))
        +(H e_2)⊗((H e_0)∧(H e_1)),

the coefficient of e_0⊗(e_0∧e_1) is

    (ζ²−1)+(ζ−1)=ζ²+ζ−2.

Here each outer e_0 coefficient is 1. The two wedge coefficients are respectively ζ²−1 and ζ−1, using the displayed orientation e_0∧e_1. The result is nonzero: if ζ²+ζ−2=0, then (ζ−1)(ζ+2)=0, impossible for a primitive fifth root of unity.

Thus Ht∉D. This contradicts D=K and N-invariance of K, and excludes rank four at e_0.

## 4. Transport and limits

The thirty canonical eigenpoints form one N-orbit. Equivariance transports the exclusion at e_0 to all of them. Together with the established evenness and lower bound four, this proves the theorem.

This is a necessary rank restriction, not an existence claim for any of 6,8,10. It says nothing about ranks outside this canonical orbit. The Fourier calculation concerns only the universal Schur representation; it is not an evaluation of f, an actual source coefficient, or a matrix of an actual operation. The kernel identification uses the global image-dimension theorem and the hypothetical rank-four condition.
