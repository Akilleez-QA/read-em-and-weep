## 1. Six cross terms and zero correction

The characters of the complement E are (k,k²). Equality with a quadratic character would give k=i+j and 2ij=0, impossible for nonzero i,j in F_5. Therefore every B-equivariant map from any B-submodule of Sym²E to E is zero.

The squares cannot belong to this kernel, because f(e_i,e_0,e_i)=-2f(e_i,e_i,e_0) is nonzero by repeated-input injectivity. Consequently rank four forces precisely the six cross-term kernel. Applying shifts then gives vanishing for every ordered triple of pairwise distinct coordinate indices. No assertion about actual operation coefficients is needed.

## 4. Degree-eight minor and order-two vanishing

The universal binary formula identifies a fixed two-dimensional image H_U on every point of P(U). Hence quotienting by Sym²U and H_U removes exactly two image dimensions at every point, and produces a global matrix pencil with entries in O(1).

Since b_u(uV)=R_u has dimension four, rank b_u=rank Q_u+4; the quotient pencil therefore has rank Q_u+2. At a canonical endpoint this is at least eight. Some eight-minor is thus a nonzero section of O(8), with divisor of total degree eight on P¹, including any zero at infinity.

Every eight-minor belongs to its (8−r)-th power. Invertible operations preserve the ideal generated by eight-minors, so this conclusion applies to the originally chosen global minor as well. Its vanishing order is at least two. Thus there are at most four distinct intersection points. This is valid without reducedness or any global finiteness hypothesis for Z4.

## 5. C5 exclusion and the exact line count

On a coordinate pair line, Z acts by [a:b]↦[ζ^i a:ζ^j b]. Its only fixed points are the endpoints, and every other point has orbit of size five. Both endpoints are excluded from Z4 by the main theorem. Equivariance makes the finite intersection invariant, while its cardinality is at most four; hence it is empty. No reflection argument is needed. Normalizer transport applies this to all six axes.

There are ten distinct pair lines for one axis because its five eigenvectors are a basis. For a different axis, any noncentral representative X^aZ^b has a≠0 in these coordinates. Its eigenvector equation propagates every coordinate around a length-five cycle, so one zero coordinate would force the whole vector to vanish. Every eigenvector for the other axis therefore has all five coordinates nonzero and cannot lie on a coordinate pair line. Equality of pair lines from different axes is impossible. The count is exactly six times ten, or sixty distinct lines.
