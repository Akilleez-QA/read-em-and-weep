# The only projective point orbit of size at most 150

## Setting and conclusion

Work over C with the five-dimensional Schrödinger representation V of H₅. Let A=H₅/Z(H₅)=F₅² and let N=A⋊G₀ be its projective normalizer, where G₀≅G=SL₂(F₅) is the canonical intertwiner complement. The construction of this complement, its reflection, and the explicit operators below are justified in ../../rank-four/rank-three-line-rank-constancy/PROOF.md, Sections 2 and 7.

**Theorem.** The only N-orbit in P(V) with at most 150 points is the set E of thirty Heisenberg eigenpoints: the five eigenlines of each of the six order-five subgroups of A. Consequently every nonempty N-invariant finite set with at most 150 points is exactly E.

This is an abstract representation-theoretic theorem. It performs no computation of the source map or of actual ranks of a determinantal matrix.

## 1. Points with a nontrivial A-stabilizer

For any point p, its A-stabilizer is an F₅-linear subspace of A. It cannot be all of A: a line invariant under every projective Heisenberg operator would be an invariant line in the irreducible five-dimensional H₅ representation.

For every nonzero a∈A, a Weyl lift W_a has the five fifth roots of unity as distinct eigenvalues. Indeed W_a⁵=I and tr(W_a^k)=0 for 1≤k≤4, so Fourier inversion gives eigenvalue multiplicity one. Thus each order-five subgroup of A fixes exactly five projective points.

Two different order-five subgroups have no common fixed point. Their independent generators have Weyl lifts with nontrivial scalar commutator; two operators with a common eigenline must have trivial commutator on that line, a contradiction. There are six such subgroups, so their fixed-point sets form thirty distinct points E. The group G acts transitively on the six subgroups. For any one subgroup, the nondegenerate Heisenberg commutator pairing shows that A acts transitively on its five eigenlines, by shifting eigenvalues. Therefore E is a single N-orbit. These are exactly the points with nontrivial A-stabilizer.

## 2. Reduction for points with trivial A-stabilizer

Suppose A_p=1. Let Γ=N_p and let S be its injective image in G=N/A. Then Γ is the graph of a cocycle c:S→A, and

    |N·p| = 25 [G:S].

An orbit of size at most 150 therefore has m=[G:S]≤6.

The group G is perfect. One elementary proof is that upper and lower elementary unipotents generate G, and conjugation by diag(2,3) multiplies the upper unipotent parameter by 4; the resulting commutators exhaust upper unipotents, and likewise lower ones. If 2≤m≤4, the action on G/S gives a nontrivial transitive homomorphism from a perfect group to the solvable group S_m, impossible. The remaining indices are m=1,5,6.

Put z=−I∈G. Whenever z∈S, cocycle evaluation on zg=gz gives

    2c(g)=(1−g)c(z).

Consequently c(g)=a−ga for a=c(z)/2, so Γ is A-conjugate to the standard copy of S in G₀. After translating p by A, we may assume that p is fixed by standard S. Triviality of its A-stabilizer is unaffected.

## 3. Index one and index six

In a Schrödinger basis indexed by j∈F₅, define

    D e_j=ζ^(j²)e_j,   T e_j=e_(2j),   F e_j=Σ_k ζ^(jk)e_k.

Their projective classes lie in G₀, and the chosen actual operators satisfy

    D⁵=I,   TDT⁻¹=D⁻¹,   ker(D−I)=Ce₀.

A line stable under both D and T has D-eigenvalue λ with λ=λ⁻¹ and λ⁵=1, so it must be Ce₀. The Fourier operator does not preserve Ce₀. Thus G₀ has no invariant line, excluding m=1.

If m=6, S has order twenty. Its Sylow-five subgroup P is unique, because the number of such subgroups divides four and is 1 modulo five. Hence S⊂N_G(P). The normalizer of a Sylow-five subgroup in SL₂(F₅) is a Borel subgroup of order twenty: for the standard unipotents it consists of the determinant-one triangular matrices. Thus S=N_G(P), and all such S are conjugate in G. In particular z∈S.

After conjugation, the standard projective Borel is generated by [D] and [T]: these generate a group of order twenty, with D of order five and T of order four. Its only invariant line is Ce₀, by the preceding calculation. But [e₀] is fixed by a nontrivial order-five subgroup of A, contradicting A_p=1. This excludes m=6.

## 4. Index five: the subgroup and an honest linear lift

If m=5, |S|=24. Necessarily z∈S, since otherwise S would inject into G/⟨z⟩≅A₅, and 24 does not divide 60. Its image H in A₅ has order twelve and is a point-stabilizer A₄. To see the latter without a subgroup table, its action on five letters cannot be transitive. If it had no fixed letter, its orbits would have sizes three and two, placing it in (S₃×S₂)∩A₅ of order six. Hence it fixes a letter and equals the corresponding A₄.

The inverse image Q of the normal Klein-four subgroup of H has order eight and is Q₈. Indeed the only nonidentity involution in SL₂(F₅) is z: an order-two matrix is diagonalizable with eigenvalues ±1, and determinant one forces it to be ±I. A group of order eight with a unique involution is cyclic or quaternion, and Q/⟨z⟩≅V₄ rules out the cyclic case. The quotient S/Q has order three and cyclically permutes the three nonidentity elements of Q/⟨z⟩.

We need an honest linear representation of this S, not merely an unjustified choice of phases. Take the inverse image E_S of its projective representation under SL(V)→PGL(V). It is a central extension

    1 → μ₅ → E_S → S → 1.

It splits because 5 is coprime to |S|=24. Explicitly write a section's factor set as an additive F₅-valued cocycle f(s,t). Set b(s)=24⁻¹Σ_t f(s,t). Summing the cocycle equation over its last argument gives f(s,t)=b(s)+b(t)−b(st). Modifying the section by −b gives a homomorphic section. This establishes the splitting without assuming a global linear Weil representation.

Let R e_j=e_(−j). It has eigenvalue multiplicities three and two, trace one, determinant one, and order two; its projective class is z. The lift of z under the splitting must be R: its possible lifts in SL(V) are ξR with ξ∈μ₅, and only ξ=1 has order two. Therefore the honest S-action decomposes

    V=V₊⊕V₋,   dim V₊=3,   dim V₋=2,

with z acting as +I and −I respectively.

## 5. Index five: neither summand has an invariant line

On V₋, no S-invariant line can exist, since its restriction to Q₈ would be a one-dimensional character. Every such character kills the commutator z of Q₈, whereas z acts as −1 on V₋.

On V₊, first establish projective faithfulness. Every canonical intertwiner commutes with R as an actual operator: projective centrality yields R_s R R_s⁻¹=cR, and trace one forces c=1. Thus the projective G-action on V₊ factors through A₅. It is nontrivial, because D has eigenvalues 1, ζ, ζ⁴ on V₊. Simplicity of A₅ therefore makes this projective action faithful. In particular its restriction to the Klein-four subgroup is projectively faithful.

For the honest S-representation on V₊, the Q-action factors through V₄, since z acts trivially. Its determinant is trivial on Q. Indeed Q⊂[S,S]: commutators with a lift of an order-three element span Q/⟨z⟩, because that element cycles its three nonzero elements, and z already belongs to [Q,Q]. Every determinant character kills [S,S].

The three characters (with multiplicity) in V₊|V₄ must consequently be the three distinct nontrivial characters. Here is a direct check: their product is trivial. If two coincide, the remaining character must be trivial; that representation is not projectively faithful. If all three are distinct and one is trivial, their product is nontrivial. Thus only the three distinct nontrivial characters remain. Their one-dimensional character spaces are cyclically permuted by S/Q, and none can be S-invariant. An S-invariant line would have to be one of these spaces, so no such line exists.

Finally any S-invariant line in V is R-invariant and hence lies in V₊ or V₋. Both alternatives have been excluded. This excludes m=5 and completes the orbit theorem.

## 6. Conditional application to a rank-one residual locus

Suppose b is an N-equivariant linear matrix on P⁴ with rank b=4+rank Q, and that Z₂={rank Q≤2} is finite. Its scheme-theoretic ideal can be taken to be generated by the degree-seven minors of b. Four sufficiently general linear combinations of these minors have zero-dimensional common zero scheme C: successively choose each combination to avoid the positive-dimensional components of the previous intersection outside the finite base locus. They form a projective complete intersection of four hypersurfaces of degree seven, so

    length(C)=7⁴=2401.

At any p∈Z₁={rank Q≤1}, rank b_p≤5. Every seven-minor vanishes to order at least two at p. This follows by constant invertible row and column operations placing b_p in rank-normal form: a determinant of size seven needs at least two factors vanishing at p, or equivalently from the standard expansion of minors at a matrix of rank at most five. Hence all four local defining equations of C belong to m_p².

In the regular local ring O_(P⁴,p), an m-primary ideal generated by four elements in m² has colength at least 2⁴=16. For example, its colength is its parameter-ideal Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity, and multiplicity monotonicity for I⊂m² gives e(I)≥e(m²)=16. Therefore

    16·|Z₁,red| ≤ 2401,   so |Z₁,red|≤150.

Equivariance makes this finite set N-invariant. The orbit theorem proves:

    Z₁,red is empty or exactly E, the thirty canonical Heisenberg eigenpoints.

This last dichotomy is conditional on Z₂ finiteness. It neither computes the rank at the thirty points nor establishes their membership in Z₁; that requires a separate argument. The complete-intersection length bound concerns C and is not an assertion about the scheme-theoretic length of Z₁ itself.

## Independent audit

An independent mathematical audit verified the subgroup/orbit reduction, the central-involution complement conjugacy, the coprime central-extension splitting and reflection normalization, the binary-tetrahedral character obstruction, and the local complete-intersection multiplicity bound. In particular, the multiplicity argument uses e(I)≥e(m²) and does not assume that the lowest-degree tangent forms themselves have an isolated common zero. The operator T above has determinant −1; it is used only as a projective intertwiner, so no SL(V) assertion about T is required (−T is an SL(V) representative if desired).
