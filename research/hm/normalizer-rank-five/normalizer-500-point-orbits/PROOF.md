# Exactly two 500-point orbits, both excluding simple isolated defects

## Result

Let G=A⋊K, A=F5², K=SL2(F5), act on P(V), dim V=5, through the standard projective Schrödinger normalizer, with its stipulated determinant-one lift N=H5⋊K⊂SL(V).

There are exactly two G-orbits of size 500. Their projective stabilizers are A-conjugate to C6=⟨a,c⟩, where a has order three and c=−I has order two. Their full linear stabilizers are correspondingly conjugate to μ5×C6. The two orbits are distinguished by the eigenvalue +1 or −1 of their unique linear-stabilizer involution on the represented line.

Neither orbit can support a length-one isolated defect Ext²(B,O) of an N-equivariant rank-two reflexive sheaf with equivariantly trivial determinant. Thus each supported 500-point orbit contributes at least 1000 to total defect length.

Together with the preceding classifications, every orbit other than the canonical 30-point orbit, the unique 250-point orbit, the two 300-point orbits, the unique 375-point orbit, and these two 500-point orbits has at least 600 points. This is an orbit-size gap, not an assertion about the actual minimum defect length or realization of any defect.

## 1. Order-six stabilizers and cocycles

Outside the canonical orbit E, A acts freely. A point of orbit size 500 therefore has a projective stabilizer S of order six, projecting isomorphically to L≤K of order six; S is the graph of a cocycle f:L→A.

The group K has a unique involution c=−I, so c∈L by Cauchy's theorem. An element a of order three together with the central c generates L, giving L=⟨a⟩×⟨c⟩≅C6. All such subgroups are conjugate by Sylow-three conjugacy in K. Centrality of c and its action −1 on A give

    2f(g)=f(c)−g f(c).

Thus f(g)=b−gb for b=f(c)/2. Conjugation by A reduces S to L. Lifting this conjugation to H5 preserves the corresponding linear-stabilizer representations in N.

## 2. Five stable lines and their exact stabilizers

Let ω be a primitive complex cube root of unity. The universal representation calculation in Sections 2–3 of ../normalizer-250-simple-defect/PROOF.md gives

    Spec(a|V+)={1,ω,ω²},     Spec(a|V−)={ω,ω²},

where V± are the c-eigenspaces. Each displayed simultaneous character has multiplicity one. Thus L has exactly five stable lines, denoted

    ℓ0,+,  ℓ1,+,  ℓ2,+,  ℓ1,−,  ℓ2,−,

where a acts on ℓj,ε by ω^j and c by ε.

Recall briefly the derivation: the inverse image in K of the order-six normalizer of a Sylow-three subgroup of A5 is D≅Dic3 of order twelve, generated by a and an element u with u²=c and uau⁻¹=a⁻¹. This forces equal ω and ω² multiplicities separately in V+ and V−. The universal Weyl trace identity gives |tr(a|V)|²=1, forcing total multiplicities 1,2,2. Since dim V+=3 and dim V−=2, the displayed spectra follow. The inversion relation also shows that the sole D-stable line is ℓ0,+. The earlier proof establishes that its point lies on the unique 250-point orbit.

We now check the other four lines have exact projective stabilizer L, including possible stabilizer elements outside K. No L-stable point can belong to E: its stabilizer would have order 100, which cannot contain an order-six subgroup. The established noncanonical gap bounds its full stabilizer S′ by |S′|≤12, and S′∩A=1. Since L⊂S′, the only possibilities are |S′|=6 or 12.

Write S′ as a cocycle graph over its image L′≤K. It contains the actual element c of the chosen complement, so its cocycle satisfies f(c)=0. The same identity as in Section 1 forces f(g)=0 for every g∈L′. Consequently S′ lies in K.

If |S′|=12, its quotient by ⟨c⟩ is an order-six subgroup of A5, hence S3: a cyclic order-six subgroup is impossible because A5 has no element of order six. Its order-three subgroup is normal. Its inverse image has order six and a unique subgroup of order three, namely ⟨a⟩. Therefore S′ normalizes ⟨a⟩. The normalizer N_K(⟨a⟩) has order twelve: its quotient in A5 is the order-six normalizer of the image of ⟨a⟩, and lifting an element normalizing that image preserves ⟨a⟩ because the other lifts have order six. Hence S′=D. But the only D-stable line is ℓ0,+. Thus each of the four lines ℓ1,± and ℓ2,± has exact stabilizer L and orbit size 500.

Its full inverse-image stabilizer in N is μ5×L: the central kernel is μ5, L is an actual linear complement, and their orders are coprime.

## 3. Exactly two orbits

The element u∈D exchanges the a-eigenvalues ω and ω² and commutes with c. It therefore interchanges ℓ1,+ with ℓ2,+ and ℓ1,− with ℓ2,−. These four lines lie in at most two orbits. Conversely every 500-point orbit has a stabilizer conjugate to L by Section 1, so it has a representative among these four lines.

The signs cannot be conjugate in G. The full linear stabilizer μ5×C6 has exactly one involution, c. A lift in N of any proposed point-conjugating element would conjugate the unique involution of one linear stabilizer to that of the other. Its eigenvalue on the represented line is preserved by conjugation. These eigenvalues are +1 and −1, respectively, a contradiction. There are therefore exactly two orbits of size 500.

## 4. Local length-one obstruction

Let W=m/m² be the cotangent representation at such a point and δ=det W. The local theorem in ../../local-defect/minimal-local-defect-character/PROOF.md applies to a rank-two reflexive stalk M, free on the punctured spectrum, with equivariantly trivial determinant. If

    Ext²_R(M,R)=kχ

has length one, that theorem gives both necessary conditions

    δ³=χ⁻⁴,
    W admits a nondegenerate alternating form with values in η=χ⁻²δ⁻¹.

These are necessary conditions only. For g∈SL(V) acting on the represented line by λ, the cotangent determinant is δ(g)=λ⁵. The central μ5 acts trivially on cotangent directions, so δ is trivial there.

### Minus orbit

Choose the representative ℓ1,−. Then δ(c)=−1. Because c is an actual order-two element in N, every character χ satisfies χ(c)⁴=1. The first necessary identity gives −1=1 at c, excluding a length-one defect.

### Plus orbit

Choose the representative ℓ1,+. Its line character is (a,c)↦(ω,1), so

    δ(a)=ω²,    δ(c)=1,    δ|μ5=1.

Since a has order three, evaluation of δ³=χ⁻⁴ at a forces χ(a)=1. Evaluation on μ5 similarly forces χ|μ5=1. The remaining value χ(c) can be either sign. Thus for either determinant-compatible character,

    η(a)=ω,    η(c)=1,    η|μ5=1.

For the cotangent representation, each complementary V-character is inverted and multiplied by the line character. The four (a-exponent modulo three, c-sign) weights on W are therefore

    (1,+), (2,+), (0,−), (2,−).

An η-valued bilinear form can pair weights only when their a-exponents sum to one modulo three and their c-signs multiply to +. Cross-sign pairs are forbidden. Within the two-dimensional c-positive space, the only pair of distinct weight spaces has exponent sum 1+2=0. Within the two-dimensional c-negative space, the only such pair has sum 0+2=2. Neither is one. An alternating form vanishes on each one-dimensional weight space itself. Consequently every η-valued alternating form is zero, and in particular none is nondegenerate.

This excludes a length-one defect on the plus orbit as well. Conjugation transports both exclusions to every point of the corresponding orbit. The isolated-singularity hypothesis supplies the punctured-spectrum local freeness needed above.

## 5. The gap to 600 and the scope of defect consequences

Every noncanonical orbit has size 25k with k=[K:L] a divisor of 120. The preceding 250-, 300-, and 375-point classifications account for k=10,12,15; the earlier gap excludes smaller noncanonical possibilities. This proof accounts for k=20. There are no divisors of 120 strictly between 15 and 20 or strictly between 20 and 24. Thus all remaining point orbits have size at least 25·24=600.

Local lengths of an equivariant finite-length defect are constant along each orbit. Both 500-point orbits have local length at least two if supported, hence contribute at least 1000 each. The preceding proofs give minimum contributions 500 from the 250-point orbit, 600 from either 300-point orbit, and 750 from the 375-point orbit. Therefore a nonzero total defect of length strictly below 600 can only have support on E and/or the unique 250-point orbit. This statement does not classify the admissible local lengths on either orbit; it must be combined with separately proved canonical-stalk constraints. No actual defect minimum, existence, or realization follows merely from the point-orbit gap.
