# The full HM normalizer has no quintic semi-invariant

## Result

Let H=H5 and N=H⋊SL2(F5)⊂SL(V), dim V=5, be the classical HM normalizer with its faithful Schrödinger restriction to H. Then

    (Sym^5 V*)^N = 0.

Moreover N is perfect, so Sym^5 V* has no one-dimensional N-subrepresentation at all. Consequently there is no N-stable effective divisor of degree five in P(V). In the equivariant rank-ten setup, this excludes the branch (k,d)=(7,5). Together with the previous exclusions, rank ten necessarily has (k,d)=(12,0), and its saturation defect is zero.

All computations below are abstract finite-group character identities, not actual operation/source matrices, products, ranks, selected inputs, or polynomial, jet, or residue evaluations.

## 1. The six-dimensional Heisenberg-invariant space

Put Q=(Sym^5 V*)^H. The group H has order 125 and center of order five. Its center acts by fifth roots of unity on V*, hence trivially on Sym^5 V*. Every noncentral h∈H has the five distinct fifth roots of unity as its eigenvalues on V*: this follows from the Schrödinger character, which is zero off the center, and the exponent-five property of H.

Thus for noncentral h,

    sum_{m≥0} tr(h | Sym^m V*) s^m = 1/(1-s^5),

and tr(h | Sym^5 V*)=1. Character averaging gives

    dim Q = (5·126 + 120·1)/125 = 6.

Here 126=dim Sym^5(C^5). The action on Q factors through N/H=SL2(F5).

## 2. A conjugacy-and-averaging observation

Suppose a∈SL2(F5) has no eigenvalue one on H/Z(H)≅F5². Every ha, h∈H, is conjugate by H to za for some central z∈H. Indeed, modulo the center this is exactly the surjectivity of I−a on F5². Since z acts trivially on Sym^5 V*, the projector onto H-invariants gives

    tr(a | Q) = (1/125) sum_{h∈H} tr(ha | Sym^5 V*)
              = tr(a | Sym^5 V*).

This applies both to a=−I and to a=diag(2,3).

We also use a standard Schrödinger identity: End(V), under conjugation by H, is the direct sum of the 25 distinct one-dimensional character spaces indexed by H/Z(H), with the identity operator indexed by zero. The normalizer permutes these character lines by its action on F5². If a has no eigenvalue one there, its only fixed line is the identity line, where its action is one. Consequently

    tr(a | End(V)) = 1,
    |tr(a | V)|² = 1.

Possible scalars on the moved character lines do not affect this trace.

## 3. The central involution acts trivially on Q

Let c=−I∈SL2(F5). Its lift in the given semidirect product has order two. Therefore its eigenvalues on V are ±1. The preceding End(V) identity gives tr(c | V)=±1. Since c∈SL(V) and dim V=5, the number of negative eigenvalues is even; this forces trace +1, with multiplicities (+1)^3(−1)^2.

The same multiplicities hold on V*. Hence

    tr(c | Sym^5 V*)
       = [s^5] 1/((1-s)^3(1+s)^2)
       = [s^5] 1/((1-s)(1-s²)^2)
       = 1+2+3 = 6.

Section 2 gives tr(c | Q)=6. Since Q has dimension six and c is an involution, c acts as the identity. Thus Q is an honest representation of PSL2(F5)≅A5.

## 4. Every A5 involution has trace minus two on Q

Take a=diag(2,3)∈SL2(F5). It has order four, a²=c, and it is conjugate in SL2(F5) to a⁻¹. On V write its eigenvalue multiplicities at 1,−1,i,−i as A,B,C,D. The preceding spectrum of a² gives

    A+B=3,  C+D=2.

Conjugacy to its inverse gives C=D, hence C=D=1. Since det(a | V)=1, B is even. The identity |tr(a | V)|²=1 excludes B=0 (which would give trace three), leaving B=2 and A=1. Thus its spectrum on V and V* is

    {1, −1, −1, i, −i}.

It follows that

    tr(a | Sym^5 V*)
       = [s^5] 1/((1-s)(1+s)^2(1+s²))
       = [s^5] 1/((1+s)(1-s⁴))
       = −2.

By Section 2 the same is tr(a | Q). All involutions in A5 are conjugate, so every nonidentity element of a Klein four subgroup K⊂A5 has trace −2 on Q. Averaging over K yields

    dim Q^K = (6+3·(−2))/4 = 0.

In particular Q^A5=0, proving (Sym^5 V*)^N=0. More precisely, Q restricted to K is twice the direct sum of the three nontrivial characters of K; no classification of the remaining A5 character values is needed for the exclusion.

## 5. Semi-invariants and divisors

For completeness, N is perfect. First SL2(F5) is generated by its upper and lower unipotent subgroups. Conjugating an upper unipotent u(x) by diag(2,3) gives u(4x), so its commutator with u(x) is u(3x). Since 3 is invertible in F5, every upper unipotent lies in the commutator subgroup; the same argument applies to the lower subgroup. Thus SL2(F5) is perfect.

Also [H,H]=Z(H), and the action of diag(2,3)−I on H/Z(H) is invertible. The commutators with this element therefore generate H modulo its center, while the center already lies in [H,H]. Hence H⊂[N,N], and perfection of the complement proves N=[N,N]. Every complex one-dimensional character of N is therefore trivial.

An N-stable effective degree-five divisor is cut out by a nonzero homogeneous quintic whose line is N-stable. That line would give a one-dimensional character of N, hence an invariant quintic, contradicting Section 4. This includes nonreduced and reducible divisors.

Finally the divisorial cycle of an equivariant saturation defect is N-stable. The degree-five cycle required by the rank-ten (k,d)=(7,5) branch is therefore impossible. The already proved purity statement then gives zero defect in the remaining (12,0) branch.
