# Independent audit: universal incidence dependencies

## Verdict

The linear-algebra claims in PROOF.md are correct under the stated hypotheses. In particular, the refined bound

    I(L) <= 4 + rho + p

is valid; the cycle branch has I(L) <= 5; and I(L)=6 holds exactly for L=D. The two displayed rank-five examples establish sharpness for the abstract arrangement. The projector-collinearity condition exactly characterizes the cycle branch, but does not by itself establish intersection sum five.

No mathematical correction was required. One scope-wording correction was requested and incorporated: the abstract examples are **not shown** to satisfy the actual full-normalizer fixed-space condition. Their failure to satisfy that condition has not been proved and is not asserted. The final Section 4A was also independently audited and its seven numerical strata are exhaustive and abstractly realizable, as detailed below.

## 1. Arrangement and relation count

The eight-space is the direct sum of the four-dimensional spaces R and D. The edge planes are the edges of the cycle with vertex order r1,r2,r4,r3; the pairs (P1,P4) and (P2,P3) are opposite edges and have zero intersection. This agrees with the cited Heisenberg proof.

For each j, S_j maps to the one-dimensional space C tj, with kernel S_j intersect R. Thus

    dim S_j = epsilon_j + delta_j.

The coordinate-line exclusion makes epsilon_j at most one: dimension two would make S_j intersect R equal to P_j. Every nonzero vector of an allowed edge line has both endpoint coefficients nonzero. A proper set of cycle edges is a forest. Removing a leaf successively proves independence of any proper collection of these nonzero edge vectors.

It follows that the relation space has dimension rho either zero or one; rho=1 requires all four edges, and their span then has dimension three. For all cases its dimension is e-rho. Since that span is contained in L intersect R, the inequality r >= e-rho follows.

## 2. Projection inequality

The indices with delta_j=1 contribute the independent coordinate directions tj to the image of L in D, so W_A has dimension k. The two generators of H0 have disjoint supports. A vector

    a(t1+t4) + b(t2+t3)

belongs to W_A exactly when each pair with nonzero coefficient is wholly contained in A. Hence dim(W_A intersect H0)=p, as claimed.

Because S_0 is a subspace of both H0 and the projection of L, one has

    dim(W_A intersect S_0) <= p,
    d >= dim(W_A+S_0) >= k+h-p.

Combining this with r >= e-rho and r+d=4 gives e+k+h <= 4+rho+p. No additional direct-sum assumption about S_0 and W_A is being made.

## 3. Cycle bound and exact equality case

When rho=1, the four edge lines span a three-space in R. The exclusions prohibit r=4, since then L=R. Thus r=3 and d=1. At most one coordinate direction tj can occur in the projection, so k<=1 and p=0. The projection inequality gives k+h<=1, proving I<=5. Equality occurs exactly when (k,h) is (0,1) or (1,0), within this branch.

For I=6 the cycle case is impossible. With rho=0, the refined bound requires p=2. Both complete pairs then occur, so k=4, d=4, r=0, and e=0. The equality I=6 now forces h=2.

The projection L to D is an isomorphism, so L is the graph of a unique map A:D to R. Each coordinate direction contributes to S_j exactly as the vector tj+A(tj), giving A(tj) in P_j. The inclusion H0 in L forces A(t1)=-A(t4) and A(t2)=-A(t3). Opposite edge planes intersect trivially, so all four images vanish. Hence L=D.

Conversely, L=D has S_0=H0 and S_j=C tj for j=1,...,4. It contains none of the lines C ri and attains I=6. This proves both the universal upper bound and the uniqueness of its equality case.

## 4. Rank-five disjunction and formal examples

If I=5, the refined bound gives rho+p>=1. Thus there is either a full dependent edge cycle or a complete opposite pair among the projected incidence directions. In fact the alternatives are disjoint here, since the cycle case has p=0. The paired condition is necessary only; no cancellation of the R-parts of its two lifts is assumed.

For the cycle example R0={sum x_i r_i : sum x_i=0}, every edge intersection is generated by the difference of its two endpoint basis vectors. Any three of these four vectors span R0. Taking L=R0 plus C(t1+t4) gives e=4,k=0,h=1,rho=1,p=0 and I=5. The hyperplane contains no coordinate line.

The other cycle subcase is also formally realizable: L=R0 plus C t1 has e=4,k=1,h=0 and I=5. This additional example verifies that neither subcase in Section 2 is vacuous.

For the noncycle example L=span(t1,t4,r1+r2,r3+r4), its R-part is the plane of vectors (a,a,b,b). Its intersections with P1 and P4 are zero; with P2 and P3 they are respectively C(r1+r2) and C(r3+r4). Its D-part is span(t1,t4), whose intersection with H0 is C(t1+t4). Therefore the four nonzero S_j and S_0 are exactly those listed in PROOF.md, and the parameters are e=2,k=2,h=1,rho=0,p=1. No coordinate line lies in this plane or in L. It attains I=5.

These checks establish sharpness of the abstract statements, without asserting global repeated-input injectivity or realization by the actual normalizer kernel.

## 4A. Exhaustive seven-stratum classification

The list in the final Section 4A is correct. For r<=2 the four-edge dependency is impossible, so rho=0 and e<=r. Together with k<=4-r, h<=2, e+k+h=5, and k+h-p<=4-r with p<=floor(k/2), this gives:

- r=0: e=0 and k+h=5, leaving only (k,h)=(3,2) or (4,1).
- r=1: e=0 would force (k,h)=(3,2), but then k+h-p>=4>3. Hence e=1 and (k,h) is (2,2) or (3,1).
- r=2: e<=1 would force k=h=2 in the only potentially feasible case, giving k+h-p>=3>2. Hence e=2 and k+h=3. The pair (1,2) has p=0 and violates the projection inequality; only (2,1) remains.
- r=3: L intersect R is a hyperplane whose defining functional has four nonzero coefficients, since no coordinate line is contained in it. Every edge plane meets it in a line. Thus e=4 and rho=1, and Section 2 leaves (k,h)=(0,1) or (1,0).
- r=4 is excluded.

This produces precisely the stated seven quadruples, without an omitted r=3 noncycle case.

Each additional representative was checked directly:

1. For (0,0,3,2), the graph with A(t1)=r1, A(t4)=-r1, and other columns zero has trivial intersection with R. The columns for indices 1,2,3 lie in their corresponding P_j, whereas -r1 does not lie in P4. Thus k=3. A annihilates both generators of H0, so h=2.

2. For (0,0,4,1), the graph with A(t1)=A(t2)=r1 and other columns zero again has trivial intersection with R. Every column lies in its P_j, giving k=4. Both generators of H0 map to the same nonzero vector r1, so the kernel of A on H0 is one-dimensional and h=1.

3. For (1,1,2,2), the space H0 plus C t1 plus C(r1+r3) is a direct sum of a three-dimensional D-subspace and one R-line. Its D-subspace is span(t1,t4,t2+t3), containing exactly the coordinate axes t1,t4. Its sole R-line lies only in P1. Consequently r=e=1, k=2, and h=2.

4. For (1,1,3,1), the space span(t1,t4,t2,r3+r4) has a three-dimensional coordinate D-part and one R-line, which lies only in P3. Thus r=e=1, k=3, and its intersection with H0 is C(t1+t4), giving h=1.

5. The representative for (2,2,2,1) was checked in Section 4 above.

6. The representative for (3,4,0,1) was checked in Section 4 above.

7. The representative for (3,4,1,0) was checked in Section 4 above.

All graph examples have dimension four and meet R trivially. In the mixed examples the stated R-lines, R-plane, or R-hyperplane contain no coordinate line, and the R and D summands are independent. Thus all examples satisfy every arrangement hypothesis. The list is sharp as a classification of the numerical quadruple (r,e,k,h); it is not a classification of individual planes or of globally admissible representation kernels.

## 5. Full-normalizer consequences and dependencies

The cited class-sum proof identifies the actual universal kernel with the +1 eigenspace of M=C/5. Since M commutes with the full normalizer, it preserves the chosen exceptional-clock eight-space U. The cited Heisenberg and Sylow arguments give a four-dimensional kernel there. Thus ker((I-M)|U)=L is the correct normalization and sign.

In the cycle case, dim(L intersect R)=3, so the restriction of I-M to R has one-dimensional image. Each r_i has nonzero image by the coordinate-line exclusion, proving their nonzero collinearity.

Conversely, nonzero collinearity gives (I-M)r_i=c_i w with every c_i nonzero. Its kernel in R is the hyperplane sum c_i x_i=0. Each edge meets this hyperplane in a line with both endpoint coefficients nonzero. The forest argument shows that any three of these four edge lines are independent; their common containment in the hyperplane forces exactly one relation. Hence rho=1. This proves the claimed equivalence.

The qualification about I=4 versus I=5 is necessary. For instance, the formal plane L=R0 plus C(t1+t2) has the same full dependent edge cycle, but k=h=0 and I=4. Thus cycle detection alone does not establish residual rank five.

The corrected Sylow operator B commutes with M: B is constructed from normalizer actions and a polynomial in one such action, while M is central in the normalizer group-algebra action. The cited Sylow proof supplies the two-dimensional E_a and the four fixed lines ell_a. The present argument correctly treats their incidence conditions as constraints on that specified fixed space, without claiming they are satisfied or excluded.

Finally, the imported formula rank Q=10-I(L) makes residual rank four equivalent to L=D under these hypotheses. Since L is the fixed space of M, this is equivalent to M fixing D pointwise. No assertion that this happens, or fails, is made.
