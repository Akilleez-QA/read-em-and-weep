# Independent audit: minimal projective point orbit

Status: PASS. Reviewed all sections of PROOF.md. No outstanding corrections.

The symmetric Weyl convention is internally consistent with ZX=zeta XZ: its product scalar is (bc-ad)/2. The chirp and Fourier conjugations have the stated exact phases and label transformations. Thus their projective subgroup is an SL2(F5) complement: a word with identity label action commutes with both generators exactly and is scalar. The described centralizer argument also identifies the full projective normalizer.

The common-eigenline argument excludes a fixed point of this complement. The cocycle computation using central -I gives 2f(g)=f(-I)-g f(-I), proving every complement is conjugate by Hbar. Therefore a free transitive twenty-five-point orbit would have an impossible point stabilizer complement.

Each nonzero Weyl element has five distinct one-dimensional eigenspaces, cycled by a transverse Weyl operator. The six stabilizer lines therefore give disjoint five-point sets forming a single thirty-point normalizer orbit, and these exhaust all points with nontrivial Hbar stabilizer. Every other orbit has size 25k. The elementary commutator proof of perfectness of SL2(F5) and solvability of S_k for k<=4 exclude k=2,3,4, while the complement argument excludes k=1. Hence every other orbit has at least 125 points.

For an equivariant finite-length sheaf of length below 125, its support must be exactly the unique thirty-point orbit, and equivariance forces equal stalk lengths. This proves the list 30,60,90,120 and the stated length-one description at thirty, without claiming an equivariant structure is uniquely determined. The applications to T and S0 respect the distinction between finite defect support and the whole singular locus.
