# The projective Heisenberg normalizer has minimum point-orbit size 30

## Result and hypotheses

Let V be the five-dimensional complex Schrödinger representation of the Heisenberg group H5, with nontrivial central character, and let

    G = Hbar ⋊ SL2(F5),       Hbar = H5/Z(H5) = F5²,

be its standard projective normalizer, acting on P(V). Then:

- Every nonempty finite G-invariant subset of P(V) has at least 30 distinct points.
- There is a G-orbit of exactly 30 points: the eigenlines of the six maximal abelian subgroups of H5 containing its center.
- The 30-point orbit is unique; every other G-orbit has at least 125 points. No existence of a 125-point orbit is asserted.
- In particular there is no G-orbit of size 25.

The proof below is phase-independent. It neither assumes a choice of linear Weil representation nor imports the decomposition of that representation into dimensions three and two.

For the previously established conditional rank-eight branch in `../../local-defect/rank-eight-singularity-chern/PROOF.md`, this strengthens all occurrences of the finite-support bound 25 to 30, and all resulting bounds q≥5 to q≥6. In particular, writing T=Ext²(B,O) and length(T)=5q,

    T ≠ 0  ⇒  |Supp(T)| ≥ 30  ⇒  length(T) ≥ 30  ⇒  q ≥ 6.

If n=0 in that proof, the nonempty finite singular locus likewise has at least 30 distinct points and q≥6. This does not exclude the conditional rank-eight branch or establish equality for any singularity sheaf.

## 1. Symmetric Weyl operators and a canonical projective complement

Choose indices i in F5, a primitive fifth root ζ, and a basis e_i of V. Set

    X e_i = e_(i+1),       Z e_i = ζ^i e_i,
    W(a,b) = ζ^(ab/2) X^a Z^b.

Exponents are in F5, and 1/2 denotes the inverse of 2 in F5. Since ZX=ζXZ,

    W(a,b)W(c,d) = ζ^((bc−ad)/2) W(a+c,b+d).

Consequently the commutator scalar is ζ^(bc−ad), and the projective classes of W(a,b) form Hbar.

Define universal operators

    D e_i = ζ^(i²/2) e_i,
    F e_j = (1/√5) Σ_i ζ^(ij) e_i.

Directly from their definitions,

    D W(a,b) D^(-1) = W(a,b+a),
    F W(a,b) F^(-1) = W(−b,a).

For example, D X D^(-1)=ζ^(1/2)XZ, DZD^(-1)=Z, FXF^(-1)=Z, and FZF^(-1)=X^(-1). These identities determine the displayed formulas, including their scalar factors.

Let K be the subgroup of PGL(V) generated by [D] and [F]. Its action on the Weyl labels maps onto SL2(F5): the label transformations are the elementary lower shear and the Fourier interchange, which generate SL2(F5). Every word in D and F acts on W(a,b) by exactly the corresponding label transformation, without an extra scalar depending on (a,b). A word acting trivially on labels therefore commutes with X and Z. Commuting with the diagonal operator Z makes it diagonal, and commuting also with the cyclic shift X makes it scalar. Thus the map K→SL2(F5) is injective as well as surjective.

Also K∩Hbar is trivial. A nonidentity Weyl class has nontrivial commutator with some Weyl operator, whereas an element of K with trivial label action commutes with every Weyl operator exactly. Therefore Hbar K is the projective semidirect product Hbar⋊K, and K is a complement.

These constructions identify the standard projective normalizer intrinsically. Indeed an element acting trivially on Hbar projectively conjugates X and Z to scalar multiples of themselves. Those scalars are fifth roots, so multiplication by a suitable Weyl operator makes the element commute with X and Z exactly; it is then scalar. Thus the projective kernel of the normalizer action on Hbar is precisely Hbar. The commutator form forces the quotient to lie in SL2(F5), and D,F realize all of SL2(F5). In particular the standard order-3000 projective normalizer used in the prior proof is Hbar K.

No determinant normalization or metaplectic phase is required: only projective classes and the exact Weyl-conjugation identities above are used.

## 2. The complement K has no fixed point in P(V)

The eigenspaces of D are

    C e_0,       span(e_1,e_−1),       span(e_2,e_−2).

Indeed i²/2 takes exactly three distinct values as i runs through F5, with the displayed fibers. If a projective point were fixed by K, a vector spanning it would be an eigenvector of both D and F.

The line C e_0 is not preserved by F, since F e_0 has all five coefficients nonzero.

For either nonzero pair {a,−a}, suppose v=αe_a+βe_−a is nonzero and Fv is proportional to v. The coefficient of e_0 in Fv is (α+β)/√5. It must vanish, so β=−α and α≠0. Choose b≠0 outside {a,−a}. The coefficient of e_b in Fv is then

    α(ζ^(ab)−ζ^(−ab))/√5.

This is nonzero, because 2ab≠0 in F5. But the coefficient of e_b in v is zero, a contradiction. Thus no such common eigenvector exists, and K fixes no projective point.

This directly proves the needed projective assertion. In particular it avoids incorrectly treating a projective fixed line as a trivial vector without checking linearization or characters.

## 3. All complements are conjugate by Hbar

Write A=F5² additively, so G=A⋊K. Any complement K' to A is the graph of a cocycle

    f:K→A,       f(gh)=f(g)+g f(h).

Let z=−I in the center of K. It acts on A as multiplication by −1. Comparing f(zg) and f(gz) gives

    f(z)−f(g) = f(g)+g f(z),
    2f(g) = f(z)−g f(z).

Set a=f(z)/2. Then f(g)=a−ga, so

    K' = (a,1) K (a,1)^(-1).

Every complement therefore has no fixed projective point, by Section 2 and conjugacy.

## 4. Excluding a 25-point G-orbit

An A-orbit on P(V) has size 1, 5, or 25. Size one is impossible: an A-fixed projective point would be a line preserved by both X and Z. Their restrictions to that one-dimensional space would commute, contrary to ZX=ζXZ with ζ≠1.

Suppose a G-orbit O had size 25. Since A is normal, all its orbits inside O have the same size. They cannot have size five: an A-orbit of size five has a one-dimensional stabilizer L⊂A, and G-equivariance would force all six one-dimensional subspaces of A to occur as stabilizers in O. Different such stabilizers require different A-orbits, giving at least 6·5=30 points. Hence A acts freely and transitively on O.

For p∈O, its stabilizer G_p has order |G|/25=120 and has trivial intersection with A. Its projection to K, also of order 120, is therefore an isomorphism. Thus G_p is a complement to A. Section 3 says that no such complement can fix a projective point, a contradiction.

## 5. The lower bound for every finite invariant set

Let Y⊂P(V) be a nonempty finite G-invariant set.

If Y contains an A-orbit of size 25, the G-orbit containing it is a disjoint union of A-orbits of size 25. It cannot be just one such orbit by Section 4. Consequently that G-orbit has at least 50 points, so |Y|≥50.

Otherwise every A-orbit in Y has size five. Its stabilizer is a line in A. The induced SL2(F5) action is transitive on the six lines in A, so all six stabilizers must occur. Since A is abelian, all points of a given A-orbit have the same stabilizer. Thus at least six distinct five-point A-orbits occur, proving |Y|≥30.

In particular no point orbit has size less than 30.

## 6. A single orbit of exactly 30 points

For every line L⊂A, take a nonzero u∈L and the eigenlines of W(u). The operator W(u) has order five, hence is diagonalizable. Choose v∈A with nonzero alternating pairing with u. Conjugation by W(v) multiplies W(u) by a primitive fifth root. Thus it permutes its eigenspaces transitively through all five possible eigenvalues; since dim V=5, all five eigenspaces are one-dimensional. The collection E_L of those five eigenlines is independent of the choice of nonzero u∈L.

The five lines in E_L are a single A-orbit. Every line in E_L has A-stabilizer exactly L: elements of L preserve it, while an element outside L has a nontrivial commutator with W(u) and cannot preserve an eigenline of W(u). Equivalently, the eigenvalue-cycling argument gives orbit size five.

The six sets E_L are disjoint. A shared eigenline for two distinct lines L,M would be preserved by two independent Weyl operators, contradicting their nontrivial scalar commutator.

Finally G takes E_L to E_(gL), and its quotient SL2(F5) acts transitively on the six labels L. Combined with A-transitivity within every E_L, this shows that

    E = ⋃_(L∈P¹(F5)) E_L

is a single G-orbit of exactly 6·5=30 points. The lower bound is therefore sharp for the universal projective action.

## 7. Uniqueness, the next-orbit gap, and short equivariant sheaves

Any point with A-stabilizer L of order five is an eigenline of W(u) for u∈L nonzero. It therefore belongs to E_L. Section 6 accounts for every A-orbit of size five, so E is the unique G-orbit meeting that locus. Every other G-orbit is a union of free A-orbits, and hence has size 25k for some positive integer k.

The action on those k A-orbits factors through K=SL2(F5) and is transitive. The group K is perfect, as follows directly from elementary matrices. Write U(s) for the upper unipotent matrix with upper-right entry s, and h=diag(2,3)∈SL2(F5). Then

    [h,U(s)] = U(3s).

As s varies, all upper unipotents lie in the commutator subgroup. Conjugation gives the same conclusion for lower unipotents, and upper and lower unipotents generate SL2(F5). Hence K equals its commutator subgroup.

For k≤4, the symmetric group S_k is solvable. For example S4 has the normal series 1⊂V4⊂A4⊂S4 with abelian successive quotients, and its subgroups are solvable. The image of a perfect group is perfect, and a solvable perfect group is trivial. Thus K cannot act transitively on k objects when 2≤k≤4. The remaining possibility k=1 was excluded in Section 4. It follows that every G-orbit other than E has at least 25·5=125 points. This is only a lower bound for those other orbits.

Now let Q be any nonzero finite-length coherent sheaf on P(V) equivariant under the normalizer (an equivariant central cover suffices). Suppose

    0 < length(Q) < 125.

Its support is a nonempty G-invariant finite set of at most length(Q) points. The orbit gap forces Supp(Q)=E. Equivariance identifies stalk modules under the corresponding local-ring isomorphisms, so every stalk has the same positive integral length m. Consequently

    length(Q) = 30m ∈ {30,60,90,120}.

If length(Q)=30, every stalk has length one. As a nonequivariant coherent sheaf, Q is therefore the direct sum of one residue-field skyscraper at each of the 30 points; equivalently it is isomorphic to O_E for the reduced finite subscheme E. This does not specify its equivariant structure.

Apply this to T in the rank-eight branch. In addition to q=0 or q≥6, one obtains

    0 < q < 25  ⇒  q ∈ {6,12,18,24},

because length(T)=5q. For each such q the support is exactly E, and if q=6 then T has length one at every point of E. The same finite-length statements apply to S_0, whose length equals length(T) in the cited singularity-sheaf proof. When n=0, the singular locus is exactly E whenever 0<q<25. None of these statements realizes any of the allowed lengths in the rank-eight geometric problem.
