# Even residual ranks at least four at all thirty Heisenberg eigenpoints

## Statement and scope

Work over C. Let V be the five-dimensional Schrödinger representation with basis e_i, i in F_5, and primitive fifth root ζ. Let N be the standard projective Heisenberg normalizer A ⋊ SL_2(F_5), A ≅ F_5². Suppose an actual operation f: V³ → J is trilinear, outer symmetric, cyclic zero, and N-equivariant (with its natural homogeneous linearization). Suppose also its repeated-input maps are injective on V/Cu for every nonzero u. Write Q_u for the residual middle-input map, whose source is Sym²(V/Cu), and r(u)=rank Q_u.

**Theorem.** At each of the thirty canonical Heisenberg eigenpoints,

    r(u) ∈ {4,6,8,10}.

In particular, every residual rank at most three, and every odd residual rank, is impossible there. The evenness assertion uses equivariance alone; the lower bound four uses repeated-input injectivity.

This is a necessary condition, not an existence claim for any listed rank. It uses no evaluation of an actual source operation and no coefficients of such an operation. The result does not assert that any global low-rank locus is empty: that requires a separate support-orbit theorem.

## 1. The residual kernel and equivariance

Fix u=e_0 and let E=V/Cu. Define

    b_u(xz)=f(x,u,z),
    R_u={f(u,u,v):v∈V} ⊂ J.

Outer symmetry and the cyclic identity imply f(u,u,u)=0 and f(v,u,u)=f(u,u,v). Consequently b_u annihilates u² and sends uV into R_u. Reduction modulo R_u therefore descends to

    h_u: Sym²E → J/R_u.

This is the residual map Q_u after fixing the vector u, so rank h_u=r(u). Repeated-input injectivity gives dim R_u=4, but is not needed for the parity argument itself.

For each stabilizer element fixing u exactly, equivariance preserves R_u and makes h_u equivariant. In particular K_u=ker h_u is preserved by its induced action on Sym²E. This kernel formulation also accommodates projective linearizations: if the comparison between the chosen lift on V and an equivariant lift introduces a scalar or a one-dimensional target twist, the vanishing condition h_u(q)=0 is unchanged. Thus K_u is invariant under the explicit chosen matrices below regardless of such scalar choices.

The natural U factor in a family formulation, U⊗Sym²(V/U), is trivial on this subgroup: every chosen generator fixes u=e_0 exactly. Even if a convention uses a dual tautological twist, it is equally trivial here. Alternatively, the kernel-invariance formulation alone proves the result and avoids choosing any target linearization.

## 2. Three exact lifts in the point stabilizer

Set

    Z e_i = ζ^i e_i,
    D e_i = ζ^(i²) e_i,
    T e_i = e_(2i).

All three fix e_0 and preserve span(e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4), which identifies with E. Their projective classes lie in N. For Z this is the Heisenberg clock. If X e_i=e_(i+1), then

    D X D⁻¹ = ζ X Z²,       D Z D⁻¹ = Z,
    T X T⁻¹ = X²,          T Z T⁻¹ = Z³.

Their induced transformations on the Heisenberg labels have determinant one; equivalently D and T are the shear and multiplier normalizer elements. The exact identities needed here are

    ZD=DZ,  Z⁵=D⁵=I,  T⁴=I,
    T Z T⁻¹=Z³,  T D T⁻¹=D⁴=D⁻¹.

The diagonal subgroup B=⟨Z,D⟩ is C_5²: if Z^aD^b=I on E, evaluating at i=1 and i=4 gives a+b=0 and −a+b=0, whence a=b=0. The finite group generated by these matrices is B⋊⟨T⟩, of order 100. Its use concerns only the abstract input representation.

## 3. All quadratic characters are distinct

For nonzero i,j∈F_5, the monomial e_i e_j ∈ Sym²E has B-character

    (s,t)=(i+j, i²+j²),

meaning that Z acts by ζ^s and D by ζ^t. These ten characters are pairwise distinct. Indeed s,t determine ij=(s²−t)/2 and hence the unordered roots {i,j} of

    x²−s x+(s²−t)/2.

There is no trivial character: s=0 implies j=−i, and then t=2i²≠0.

Every B-stable subspace of Sym²E is therefore a direct sum of some of these ten one-dimensional character spaces. This follows, for example, by applying the character projectors of the finite abelian group B to the invariant subspace.

The multiplier T takes e_i e_j to e_(2i)e_(2j), and hence acts on the character labels of these monomials by

    (s,t) ↦ (2s,4t).

This label map is consistent with T Z T⁻¹=Z³: the character of Tv is evaluated using T⁻¹ Z T=Z². It is important not to confuse the conjugation exponent 3 with the monomial-label exponent 2.

## 4. The three character orbits

Multiplication of both indices by 2 gives exactly these three monomial orbits:

    O_square = {e₁²,e₂²,e₄²,e₃²},                       size 4;
    O_adjacent = {e₁e₂,e₂e₄,e₃e₄,e₁e₃},                 size 4;
    O_opposite = {e₁e₄,e₂e₃},                           size 2.

Because K_u is stable under both B and T, its subset of character spaces must be a union of entire listed orbits. Hence

    dim K_u is a subset sum of 4,4,2,
    rank h_u = 10−dim K_u ∈ {0,2,4,6,8,10}.

Equivalently, Sym²E is a direct sum of three irreducible modules for ⟨Z,D,T⟩ of dimensions 4,4,2. The elementary orbit argument proves irreducibility directly and does not require a representation table. The three modules have disjoint B-character supports, so no diagonal mixing between them is possible.

Before imposing repeated-input injectivity, a rank-two quotient could only be the opposite-pair module. Section 7 excludes this possibility under the full standing hypotheses.

## 5. Direct rank-one contradiction

For an independent short argument, suppose rank h_u=1. The genuine one-dimensional quotient M=Sym²E/K_u is a character of ⟨Z,D,T⟩. The relation T Z T⁻¹=Z³ forces its Z eigenvalue μ to satisfy μ=μ³; together with μ⁵=1 this gives μ=1. Similarly T D T⁻¹=D⁻¹ forces its D eigenvalue λ to satisfy λ=λ⁻¹, and λ⁵=1 gives λ=1.

Thus M would be a trivial B-character quotient, but Section 3 shows Sym²E has no trivial B-character. This contradiction excludes rank one without choosing any action on the actual target J/R_u.

Equivalently, the only Z-weight-zero quadratic monomials are e₁e₄ and e₂e₃; their D weights are respectively 2 and 3, neither zero, and T swaps them. They form a two-dimensional irreducible module and cannot have a one-dimensional invariant quotient.

## 6. Transport to the thirty eigenpoints

The six one-dimensional subspaces of A determine the six maximal abelian Heisenberg subgroups containing the center. Each has five eigenlines. Their thirty eigenlines form a single N-orbit, as established in ../normalizer-minimal-point-orbit/PROOF.md. The point [e_0] is one of them. Equivariance makes r(u) constant on every N-orbit, so the parity assertion holds at all thirty canonical eigenpoints.

This transport needs only that particular canonical orbit, not a classification of all small point orbits. Combining this theorem with a separate statement that a given finite rank-one support is contained in the canonical orbit would exclude that support. Such a containment is not proved or assumed implicitly here.


## 7. Repeated-input injectivity forces the square module to survive

There is a stronger conclusion under the stated full hypotheses. Identify E with the B-stable complement span(e₁,e₂,e₃,e₄) in V. Its four B-characters are

    (j,j²),   j∈F_5*,

and none occurs in Sym²E. Indeed equality with (i+k,i²+k²), i,k∈F_5*, would give j=i+k and j²=i²+k², hence 2ik=0, a contradiction. Thus

    Hom_B(Sym²E,E)=0,

and the same vanishing holds with any B-submodule K⊂Sym²E as source.

For q∈K_u, the unreduced map b_u(q)=f(x,u,z), interpreted linearly on Sym²E, lies in R_u. The repeated-input map

    A_u:E → R_u,   v ↦ f(u,u,v),

is an isomorphism by repeated-input injectivity. Define the unique correction

    v_u:K_u → E,   v_u=A_u⁻¹∘b_u|_(K_u).

This correction is B-equivariant. All chosen elements of B fix u exactly, so b_u has input character Sym²E while A_u has input character E; any common scalar factor or target twist in equivariance cancels between these two maps, since both arise from the same trilinear operation f. More explicitly, if a chosen lift g∈B satisfies f(gx,gy,gz)=c_g ρ_g f(x,y,z), then

    b_u(gq)=c_g ρ_g b_u(q),
    A_u(gv)=c_g ρ_g A_u(v),

and injectivity of A_u implies v_u(gq)=g v_u(q). Hence the disjointness of B-character supports gives v_u=0. In particular b_u vanishes on K_u.

No square e_i², i≠0, can belong to K_u. If it did, then

    0=b_u(e_i²)=f(e_i,u,e_i).

But outer symmetry and the cyclic identity give

    f(e_i,u,e_i)=−2 f(e_i,e_i,u),

and f(e_i,e_i,u)≠0 by repeated-input injectivity, because u and e_i are independent. This contradiction shows that the size-four square-character orbit is disjoint from K_u.

By Section 4, K_u is therefore a sum of a subset of only the other two orbit modules, of dimensions 4 and 2. Consequently

    dim K_u ∈ {0,2,4,6},
    r(u)=10−dim K_u ∈ {4,6,8,10}.

Section 6 transports this strengthened statement to all thirty canonical eigenpoints. In particular those points are disjoint from every residual rank-at-most-three locus. Nothing here bounds or excludes low-rank points outside the canonical orbit.
