# Canonical weight-two downstairs Massey reduction

## Scope and result

Work in characteristic zero with the stipulated symmetric quotient σ:Y=(P¹)^4→P⁴ and rank-two HM bundle H. Put D=End H, B=σ_*O_Y, K=H¹(D⊗B), T=H²(D⊗B). Import the proved ordered vanishing K·K=0 and the canonical invariant Massey operation from the canonical massey detector manuscript (PROOF.md). This note establishes a canonical associated-graded reduction, not an evaluation or a nonvanishing theorem.

Write U₀=H¹(D), U₁=H¹(D(−1)), U₂=H²(D(−2)), with dimensions 24,5,20. There are canonical ordered trilinear operations for each ordering of weights (0,1,1), with output U₂. For two coefficient directions v,w∈V₁ and one weight-zero class, the weight-two symbol of the full operation is the coefficient μ₁₁(v,w) times the corresponding downstairs operation. The coefficient multiplication Sym²V₁→V₂ is surjective. No multiplicative splitting of B is assumed.

## 1. Canonical generation filtration and its algebra

Let L=O_Y(1,1,1,1), R=⊕_{n≥0}H⁰(Y,Lⁿ), and S=Sym H⁰(P⁴,O(1)), embedded in R by σ. The five elements of S₁ form a homogeneous system of parameters: σ is finite and σ*O(1)=L. The Segre section ring R is standard graded. Indeed multiplication of symmetric powers in each of the four two-dimensional factors is surjective, and regrouping tensor factors proves that R is generated by R₁. The intermediate cohomology Hⁱ(Y,Lⁿ) vanishes for 0<i<4 and all integers n by Künneth, so this projectively normal section ring is Cohen–Macaulay. Projective normality here follows directly from the Segre section-ring description; it does not require a selected polynomial instance.

Equivalently, the already proved bundle splitting B≅O⊕O(−1)^11⊕O(−2)^11⊕O(−3), together with section modules in all degrees, makes R a graded free S-module with generator degrees 0,1,2,3. In particular the five parameters are regular, and A=R/S₊R is an Artinian graded algebra with Hilbert function (1,11,11,1). Because R is standard graded, A is standard graded too. Thus

    μ₁₁: Sym² A₁ → A₂

is surjective. This is an abstract graded-algebra conclusion; no parameters, points, matrices, or ranks are evaluated.

Define F_dB to be the subsheaf generated by sections of B(n) for 0≤n≤d, with their evaluation twists O(−n); set F_d=0 for d<0 and F_d=B for d≥3. This is canonical and multiplicative: F_pB·F_qB⊂F_{p+q}B. Any homogeneous free S-basis of R, used only to prove properties, identifies F_dB with the sum of generator summands of degrees ≤d. Hence these are subbundles, the short exact sequences split as O-modules, and canonically

    gr_d B ≅ A_d⊗O(−d).

The canonical identification follows by taking sections in degree d modulo all lower-degree S-multiples. Under it, associated-graded multiplication is the multiplication of A: the coefficients of generators of degree p+q are constants, and coefficients of lower-degree generators disappear in gr_{p+q}. This argument explicitly permits such lower-degree terms in the actual algebra B.

Put V_d=A_d. The stipulated S₄ decompositions are V₀=1, V₁=3[31]⊕[22], V₂=3[211]⊕[22], V₃=sgn. The construction and μ are equivariant for symmetries preserving σ and its polarization, including the established inversion action with its prescribed linearizations.

## 2. Strict cohomology and filtered primitives

Tensor the filtration with D and take a multiplicative Čech resolution, or over C the Dolbeault complex C=A^{0,*}(D⊗B). Let F_dC=A^{0,*}(D⊗F_dB). Products respect filtration and its associated-graded complexes are V_d⊗A^{0,*}(D(−d)).

The O-module splittings above imply that, in every cohomological degree, Hʲ(F_dC)→Hʲ(C) is injective, and

    gr_d Hʲ(C) ≅ V_d⊗Hʲ(D(−d)).

These claims do not require a splitting compatible with multiplication. In particular, a closed element in F_dC that is exact in C has a primitive in F_dC. This strictness assertion, rather than an arbitrary contracting homotopy on C, is the needed homotopy compatibility.

The known H¹(D(−2))=H¹(D(−3))=0 yields F₁K=K and a canonical exact sequence

    0 → K₀=U₀ → K → V₁⊗U₁ → 0.

All ordered products of two classes of K vanish by the imported theorem. Taking their symbols proves all ordered downstairs products of the following weights vanish:

    U₀⊗U₀ → H²(D),
    U₀⊗U₁ → H²(D(−1)),
    U₁⊗U₀ → H²(D(−1)),
    U₁⊗U₁ → H²(D(−2)).

For the mixed products the coefficient multiplication 1·v=v is injective for any nonzero v. For the last product, lift arbitrary v⊗u,w⊗u′ to K; strictness makes their product zero already in H²(F₂C), so its symbol is

    μ₁₁(v,w) ⊗ (u∪u′)=0.

Since μ₁₁ is surjective onto the nonzero space V₂, some abstract v,w have nonzero product. Fixing such a pair and applying a linear functional nonzero on that coefficient proves u∪u′=0 for every u,u′. This is an existence argument, not the selection or evaluation of an instance. It is valid for the ordered, not merely polarized, products.

## 3. Canonical downstairs operations

Use the graded collection C_r=A^{0,*}(D(−r)) with composition C_p⊗C_q→C_{p+q}. For weights (p,q,r) any permutation of (0,1,1), choose closed degree-one representatives α,β,γ. By Section 2 choose degree-one primitives

    d h_ab=αβ in C_{p+q},    d h_bc=βγ in C_{q+r}.

Define

    m_{pqr}(a,b,c)=[α h_bc+h_ab γ] ∈ H²(D(−2))=U₂.

The usual Leibniz signs make the displayed cocycle closed. The indeterminacy is

    a·H¹(D(−q−r)) + H¹(D(−p−q))·c.

Each term is zero: a sum of adjacent weights 2 gives H¹(D(−2))=0, and a sum 1 leaves a product U₁·U₁, already zero. Changing representatives is handled by the same degree-zero boundary identities as in canonical-massey-detector/PROOF.md, now respecting twists. Thus each operation is well-defined, trilinear, natural, and independent of every representative and primitive. It is not the choice of a minimal A∞ structure.

## 4. Exact leading-symbol comparison

Let a,b,c∈K have filtration weights at most p,q,r with (p,q,r) a permutation of (0,1,1). Choose representatives in those filtered subcomplexes. Each adjacent product is zero in H²(C); Section 2 supplies primitives in F_{p+q}C and F_{q+r}C. The resulting full Massey representative lies in F₂C. Its class is the canonical full M(a,b,c), so M on these triples lands in F₂T.

Projecting this filtered defining system to the associated graded gives a defining system for the symbols. Suppose its two weight-one symbols are v⊗u and w⊗u′ and its weight-zero symbol is t. Then, in the corresponding order,

    gr₂ M = μ₁₁(v,w) ⊗ m_{pqr}(t,u,u′).

To justify this equality without a factorized homotopy, choose downstairs primitives and tensor them with the relevant coefficient products. They form a valid graded defining system. The actual projected primitives differ from them by closed degree-one graded cochains. Their effects on the output vanish by the same product vanishings used in Section 3 (including H¹(D(−2))=0). Thus the two graded output classes agree. There is no requirement that actual filtered primitives factor, and no use of a multiplicative B splitting.

Changing either weight-one lift by K₀ changes M by a triple of total filtration ≤1, whose defining system likewise lies in F₁C. Consequently the weight-two symbol depends only on the symbols and on the K₀ input. The comparison therefore defines canonical maps on U₀ and K/K₀, not merely formulas for chosen lifts. General non-pure symbols follow by trilinearity.

Surjectivity of μ₁₁ means the span of this leading image for a fixed ordering is exactly

    V₂ ⊗ im(m_{pqr}) ⊂ V₂⊗U₂.

Here im(m_{pqr}) means the image of the linearized tensor map, equivalently the span of its trilinear values. This equality concerns the unprojected weight-two channel. Subsequent equivariant projectors impose their own parity and representation constraints; it is not a claim that im(m_{pqr})=U₂.

## 5. Symmetry, eligible quotient, and limits

The full operation's outer-reversal and cyclic-zero identities descend to the associated-graded operation. Since some coefficient μ₁₁(v,w) is nonzero and the coefficient algebra is commutative, the downstairs operations obey the corresponding reversal and cyclic relations across the three orderings. The construction is equivariant for the stipulated inversion and S₄ actions. S₄ acts trivially on downstairs cohomology, so the [22] piece of this weight-two channel is the unique [22] coefficient copy in V₂ tensored with U₂. Projecting onto the trivial D₈ line selects one coefficient line, leaving the appropriate inversion eigenspace of U₂ (with the coefficient-line character included).

The companion [the filtered massey target manuscript](FILTERED-TARGET.md) §4 supplies the separate equivariant calculation: the [22] coefficient line in V₂ is inversion-positive, and the trace on U₂ is −4. The latter follows from formal Euler additivity at twist −2: the internal Hom terms of degrees 2,3,−3 contribute −8,+2,+2 respectively; the last term uses H⁴(O(−5)) and has odd total degree. Since dim U₂=20 and all its other cohomology vanishes, dim U₂,+=8. Ordinary dimensions alone would not prove this. Thus the canonical quotient is

    Q₂,+ = e_{D₈,triv} e_[22] e_+ gr₂ T,

of dimension 8, canonically the positive weight-two quotient of the retained detector target by its four-dimensional weight-at-most-one subspace. The reduced detector into it is the same projector applied to μ₁₁⊗m_{pqr}. This identifies an eligible quotient and a precise canonical operation addressing it. Under the sign-valued Serre pairing, its physical dual is the annihilator of that four-dimensional detector subspace in the twelve-dimensional positive physical retained sector. Thus this quotient must not be confused with a chosen eight-dimensional physical summand. It proves neither that this operation is nonzero nor that it spans or separates that quotient. It also does not remove any lower-weight contribution or identify a preferred splitting of the full positive retained detector space.
