# Canonical coefficient filtration of the ordered Massey detector

## Result and limits

For the stipulated HM carrier, put F=End H on P⁴ and B=σ_*O_Y, where Y=(P¹)⁴. Use the canonical invariant ordered Massey operation M:K³→T from [the canonical massey detector manuscript](CANONICAL-MASSEY.md), with K=H¹(F⊗B), T=H²(F⊗B), and its established hypothesis K·K=0. Let K₀=H¹(F)⊂K. This is precisely the 24-dimensional S4-trivial part of K, including the projective-target directions.

There is a canonical S4- and inversion-stable filtration T≤p=im(H²(F⊗B≤p)→T). For the retained dual detector

D=e_D8,triv e_[22] (1+ι)/2 M,

its target Tret has dimension 12. Its canonical filtered subspace Tret,≤1 has dimension 4, and Tret/Tret,≤1 has dimension 8. Every ordered triple with at least two arguments in K₀ has D-value in Tret,≤1. Consequently the quotient detector vanishes on all such triples. A nonzero quotient value requires at least two arguments outside K₀ (equivalently, at least two nontrivial coefficient components after expanding multilinearly). No value, nonzero image, spanning assertion, or separation of the physical obstruction is proved.

Triples with at least one K₀ argument lie in T≤2. The entire retained target is already in T≤2. This removes this filtration obstruction for triples with two nontrivial coefficient components and one K₀ component; it does NOT establish accessibility or nonvanishing of any direction for the actual M.

## 1. Canonical graded-generation filtration

Let R=Sym H⁰(P⁴,O(1)), and let N=⊕n≥0 H⁰(B(n)). Define N≤p to be the graded R-submodule generated by all N_d for d≤p, and B≤p its sheafification. Equivalently B≤p is the image of the sum of evaluation maps H⁰(B(d))⊗O(-d)→B for 0≤d≤p. Put B≤p=0 for p<0 and B≤p=B for p≥3.

The proved equivariant splitting theorem gives N free, with minimal generators in degrees 0,1,2,3. Any degree-respecting free splitting identifies B≤p with the sum of the summands of degrees at most p. Thus these images are vector subbundles with locally free quotients, independently of the splitting. Their associated graded bundles are canonically

gr₀B=O, gr₁B=O(-1)⊗V₁, gr₂B=O(-2)⊗V₂, gr₃B=O(-3)⊗sgn,

where V₁=3[31]⊕[22] and V₂=3[211]⊕[22]. Here the intrinsic multiplicity spaces are (N/R₊N)_p; the identifications with the named representations are isomorphism statements, not chosen canonical bases. The degree-zero evaluation is the unit inclusion O→B.

The grading and evaluation maps commute with geometric S4 and with the stipulated simultaneous coordinate interchange on Y and coordinate reversal on P⁴. Thus the filtration is equivariant even though a splitting need not be canonical. Equivariant graded generator lifts may be chosen by averaging degreewise over S4×⟨ι⟩, with its semilinear action on R. Their R-linear extension gives an equivariant bundle splitting for this finite group. We use its existence only to compute associated graded cohomology and characters.

Multiplication satisfies B≤p·B≤q⊂B≤p+q. One proof uses a degree-compatible splitting: a component O(-a)⊗O(-b)→O(-c) is zero if c>a+b, because H⁰(O(a+b-c))=0. For a≤p,b≤q, every component outside B≤p+q therefore vanishes. Since the images defining the filtration are intrinsic, this proves intrinsic multiplicativity; it does not assert a multiplicative splitting.

## 2. Cohomological filtration and the special role of B≤0

The independent classical cohomology input gives

h¹(F)=24, h¹(F(-1))=5, H¹(F(-a))=0 for a≥2,
h²(F)=2, h²(F(-1))=10, h²(F(-2))=h²(F(-3))=20,
H⁰(F(-a))=0 for a>0.

Consequently H¹(F⊗B≤1)→K is an isomorphism. This follows directly from H⁰(F⊗B/B≤1)=H¹(F⊗B/B≤1)=0. Also H²(F⊗B≤1)→T is injective. The same assertions of H²-injectivity hold at level two, since H¹(F⊗B/B≤2)=0.

At level ZERO the latter vanishing argument cannot be used: H¹(F⊗B/O) is nonzero. Instead, the unit inclusion has its canonical S4 Reynolds retraction B→B^S4=O. Tensoring with F proves H²(F)→T injective. Alternatively the established bundle splitting proves injection. This distinction is needed for the primitive of a pair of K₀ inputs.

We obtain K=K₀⊕Knt canonically as S4 representations, where K₀ is the trivial isotypic part and Knt is the sum of the other isotypic parts. In associated graded notation Knt≅V₁⊗H¹(F(-1)). No trivial representation occurs in V₁. Both summands are inversion-stable. The splitting between classical twists in the full sheaf is not being declared canonical.

In fact every B≤p⊂B splits as a vector bundle by the graded-generator argument, so all the displayed H² maps are injections; the preceding vanishing/retraction arguments identify the precise minimal reasons needed here.

## 3. Filtered representatives and primitives

Use the associative algebra sheaf F⊗B and a multiplicative algebraic Čech cochain model A computing its cohomology. It is a DGA for the ordered cup product. Its subcomplex A≤p is obtained from F⊗B≤p. Then d preserves filtration and A≤p A≤q⊂A≤p+q. This computes the same invariant derived-endomorphism algebra used to define M; all cochains are in the invariant theory on the quotient, not arbitrary unrestricted primitives on X.

Every K₀ class has a closed degree-one representative in A≤0. Every K class has one in A≤1. For a pair of representatives with filtration degrees (0,0), their product lies in A≤0 and its full cohomology class vanishes by K·K=0. H²-injectivity at level zero implies a primitive in A≤0. For degrees (0,1) or (1,0), use H²-injectivity at level one to choose a primitive in A≤1. For degrees (1,1), use level two to choose a primitive in A≤2.

For M(a,b,c)=[α·h(β,γ)+h(α,β)·γ], take a defining primitive for each adjacent ordered pair at the level just specified. If two arguments lie in K₀ and the remaining one has level at most one, both terms have filtration at most one, in every ordering. The pair of K₀ arguments, if adjacent, uses its LEVEL-ZERO primitive. Merely taking every primitive at level one would not prove the desired bound.

If all three arguments lie in K₀, the value lies in H²(F), so its [22] projection is zero. More generally, the same split-inclusion argument gives M(K≤p,K≤q,K≤r)⊂T≤p+q+r, where K≤p is the cohomological filtration on K.

If one argument lies in K₀ and the other two have level at most one, the same argument puts both terms in filtration at most two. This includes the ordering with the K₀ argument in the middle.

These are bounds on the canonical operation, not a choice-dependent alternative operation. The established full M is independent of arbitrary invariant primitive choices: replacing either primitive by a closed degree-one cochain changes its class by a product of two K classes, hence by zero. Its representative independence follows from the usual explicit degree-zero boundary corrections in the canonical-M proof. We have exhibited ONE allowed defining system in the asserted subcomplex, so the unique full class belongs to its canonical H² image. Since those H² maps are injective, its preimage there is also unique. Thus changes of representatives, boundary primitives, filtered splittings, and invariant defining primitives cannot change either the filtered class or its projection. No assertion that arbitrary unfiltered primitives themselves stay filtered is needed.

## 4. The 4+8 inversion split on the retained dual target

Under S4 the [22] part occurs only in gr₁ and gr₂. Thus the retained dual target at filtration one is the inversion-positive part of H²(F(-1)) tensored with the D8-trivial line in [22]. Its associated quotient at filtration two is the analogous part of H²(F(-2)). We now verify the inversion dimensions including the relevant linearizations.

Let J interchange the two coordinates on each P¹, and R reverse the five binary-quartic coefficient coordinates. Use the established σ*O(1)=L linearization. The [22] multiplicity of V₁ is det²(C²), on which J acts by +1. Equivariant finite duality B^∨=B(3)⊗sgn gives the [22] multiplicity of V₂ the same + action: the four det(J) factors on Y multiply to +1, and det(R)=+1 on the base. Thus no extra inversion sign appears in these [22] graded factors.

The stipulated full-coefficient KS identification is K≅(W⊗C⁵)/Cv, with W=(C²)^⊗4 and action J^⊗4⊗R. Its [22] multiplicity is R, whose trace is 1. Comparing to the degree-one [22] factor above shows tr(ι|H¹(F(-1)))=1.

For completeness, compute the two H² traces by formal equivariant Euler additivity, without any source or matrix evaluation. In the audited four-term HM presentation, the coefficient involution traces are (1,3,-2,-2). For the internal Hom coefficients U^r, the resulting traces for r=1,2,3 are respectively (1,-8,-2); negative degrees have the same traces. On P⁴, O(-1),…,O(-4) are acyclic, tr(ι|H⁰O(1))=tr R=1, and tr(ι|H⁰O(2))=((tr R)²+tr R²)/2=3. The standard O(-5) has H⁴ trace +1 because det R=+1.

Twisting the Hom complex by -1 leaves only r=1,2,3 in its Euler character:

χ_ι(F(-1))= -1 + (-8)·1 - (-2)·3 = -3.

The classical row has only H¹,H² at this twist. Therefore tr H²(F(-1))= -3+1=-2. Its total dimension is 10, so its plus/minus dimensions are (4,6).

At twist -2, r=2,3 contribute H⁰ and r=-3 contributes H⁴(O(-5)); the latter has total degree r+4=1 and a minus Euler sign. Hence

χ_ι(F(-2))= (-8) - (-2)·1 - (-2)·1 = -4.

Only H² is nonzero. Its total dimension 20 gives plus/minus dimensions (8,12). Omitting the negative internal-degree endpoint would give an incorrect answer.

It follows that dim Tret,≤1=4 and dim(Tret/Tret,≤1)=8. Their sum agrees with the independently established retained dual dimension 12. These are canonical subspace/quotient dimensions; no preferred direct-sum decomposition Tret= C⁴⊕C⁸ is asserted.

## 5. Serre pairing and the physical-versus-dual distinction

Let V be the full-deck Ext² and T the invariant Ext². The trace/Serre pairing T×V→C has value character sgn under S4 and trivial character under inversion. For D8 stabilizing {{1,2},{3,4}}, write ε=sgn|D8. The physical retained positive sector is the ε-line in [22] tensored with its 12-dimensional positive multiplicity space. Its perfect dual under this pairing is the TRIVIAL D8 line in the invariant [22], positive target Tret. Indeed characters must multiply to ε, so pairing a physical ε-vector requires a trivial-character detector. The ε projector on the detector would be wrong.

The four-dimensional canonical detector subspace Tret,≤1 therefore has an eight-dimensional annihilator in the twelve-dimensional physical retained sector. Equivalently (Tret/Tret,≤1)^* identifies, under the sign-valued Serre pairing, with that annihilator. One must not identify the detector quotient with an arbitrarily named eight-dimensional physical summand. The pairing is perfect on the full retained sectors, but no proof here says M spans the four-dimensional subspace or the eight-dimensional quotient.

## 6. Consequence for the abstract witness and exact scope

The earlier formal witness F(a,b,c)=ℓ(a)ℓ(b)φ(Pc)+ℓ(b)ℓ(c)φ(Pa)-2ℓ(a)ℓ(c)φ(Pb) used a positive S4-trivial line and a positive [22] coefficient component. Every trivial line is in K₀. The new geometric filtration theorem imposes the additional condition that the corresponding actual M-values land in Tret,≤1 after retained projection. An arbitrary abstract choice of φ with nonzero quotient component still obeys reversal and cyclic-zero, but cannot be the restriction of this actual filtered Massey operation. Thus that simple representation-theoretic witness does not establish eligibility of all twelve retained detector directions for these inputs.

The quotient operation factors through K³ modulo the linear span of all tensors having two K₀ slots, in any positions. This is a precise necessary condition. Triples with two nontrivial coefficient components and one K₀ component encounter no further restriction from this filtration because Tret⊂T≤2. Other identities or geometric vanishing may still exclude them. The actual nonvanishing, image, and separation questions remain open.
