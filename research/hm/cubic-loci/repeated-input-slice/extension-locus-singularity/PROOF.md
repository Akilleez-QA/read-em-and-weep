# Conditional geometry of the filtered extension locus

## Statement and scope

Work over C. Import the audited filtered-extension representation theorem in the cubic filtered extension obstruction manuscript and its `AUDIT.md`. In particular, the framed filtered rank-eight bundle functor is represented by Z_f, the scheme-theoretic zero locus of the canonical ordered trilinear map on three independently labeled copies of V.

Assume dim V=5, dim J3=20, and f=lambda Phi with lambda nonzero. Import the established surjectivity of the linearization T: V tensor V tensor V -> J3 in this branch (equivalently, nonzero equivariance with irreducible target). No assertion about surjectivity of the polynomial map V^3 -> J3 is needed.

Set S=Sym((V direct_sum V direct_sum V)^*), m=S_+, I=I_f, R=S_m, A=R/IR, and n=mR/IR.

Then:

1. The twenty coordinate cubics of f are linearly independent and minimally generate IR.
2. The ambient quotient map induces canonical isomorphisms A/n^2 = R/(mR)^2 and A/n^3 = R/(mR)^3. The tangent space at the origin has dimension 15.
3. Each coordinate 10-plane u=0, v=0, or w=0 is contained scheme-theoretically in Z_f.
4. Both the global dimension and the local dimension at the origin lie between 10 and 14. Also 1 <= height_R(IR) <= 5.
5. The origin is singular and is not a local complete intersection.
6. Multiplying f by any nonzero scalar leaves its defining ideal, and therefore all these conclusions, unchanged.

This lemma remains conditional on lambda nonzero. It does not establish or evaluate that scalar. A separate nonvanishing theorem, if established, can discharge that hypothesis without altering this proof.

## Proof

### Independent equations

Surjectivity of T makes its dual J3^* -> V^* tensor V^* tensor V^* injective. Because the three input copies have independent labels, the target is canonically the multidegree (1,1,1) component of S. A basis of J3^* therefore supplies twenty linearly independent coordinate polynomials g_1,...,g_20, each of total degree three.

### Local minimal generators

The ideal I is generated by I_3=span_C(g_1,...,g_20). Every homogeneous element of I of degree greater than three belongs to mI, whereas (mI)_3=0. Thus I/mI is canonically I_3. Localizing preserves this C-vector space, so

    IR/(mR)IR = (I/mI)_m = C^20.

Nakayama's lemma gives mu_R(IR)=20. In particular, localization cannot introduce a redundancy among these twenty generators.

### Formal neighborhoods and tangent space

Since I is contained in m^3, for k=2,3 the ambient quotient map induces

    A/n^k = R/(IR+(mR)^k) = R/(mR)^k.

These describe the first and second infinitesimal neighborhoods under the convention that neighborhood number r uses maximal-ideal power r+1. Also n/n^2 is isomorphic to mR/(mR)^2, whose dimension over C is 15. These statements use only ideal degrees and formal maximal-ideal quotients, with no evaluation of an actual instance.

### Dimension and height

Multidegree (1,1,1) implies I is contained in each of the ideals (u), (v), and (w), where each ideal is generated by the five linear coordinates of its indicated input slot. This is scheme-theoretic containment of the corresponding affine 10-planes in Z_f. It gives dim(S/I)>=10 and dim A>=10, since each plane passes through the origin and its local ring there has dimension 10.

The independent coordinate cubics give I nonzero, and IR is nonzero because S is a domain. A nonzero ideal in S, or in its origin localization R, has quotient dimension at most 14. For the local assertion one can prepend the zero prime to every prime chain above IR in the 15-dimensional local domain R; the same argument applies globally to S. Thus dim(S/I)<=14 and dim A<=14.

Finally IR lies in the height-five prime (u)R, so height_R(IR)<=5. It is nonzero in a domain, so every prime containing it has positive height. Hence 1<=height_R(IR)<=5.

### Singularity and non-complete-intersection property

The embedding dimension of A is 15, strictly greater than its Krull dimension, which is at most 14. Therefore A is not regular and the origin is singular. Properness of an ideal alone would not prove singularity; here both its nonvanishing and its containment in m^3 are essential.

If IR were generated by a regular sequence, its minimal number of generators would equal its height. This contradicts mu_R(IR)=20>5>=height_R(IR). This also rules out the intrinsic local-complete-intersection property, rather than merely regularity of this particular embedding. For a quotient of a regular ambient ring, the completion of the local quotient is a complete intersection if and only if its localized defining ideal is generated by a regular sequence; see [Stacks Project, Lemma 23.8.3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/09Q1). The equivalent finite-type geometric criterion appears in [Stacks Project, Lemma 10.135.4](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00SC).

### Scalar invariance

For c in C^*, the ideals (c g_1,...,c g_20) and (g_1,...,g_20) are literally equal. Hence I_cf=I_f. In particular every nonzero value of lambda gives the same scheme Z_Phi.

## Limits and evidence boundary

No exact dimension or codimension, radicalness, reducedness, or irreducible-component description is claimed. The proof uses formal graded commutative algebra and the imported linearized surjectivity only. No source/model product or rank, actual polynomial instance, point, instance jet, residual, or Massey value was evaluated. The argument about infinitesimal neighborhoods is exclusively an ideal-degree theorem. No external writes or large computations were performed.
