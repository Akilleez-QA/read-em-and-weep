# Equivariant reduction of the paired full-deck sector

## Status and scope

This is a dimension-free theorem at the original symmetric-quotient coefficient tuple. It uses no numerical Ext certificate or actual detector evaluation. It does not prove a detector value, a vanishing, or an identification of any scalar selector with a perfect cohomological pairing. Identification with a particular source requires the precise permutation character stated in section 5.

Work over C. Put Y=(P¹)^4, σ:Y→Sym⁴(P¹)=P⁴, L=O_Y(1,1,1,1), and B=σ_*O_Y. The group S4 acts by factor permutations, trivially on P⁴. All tensor powers of L have the ordinary permutation linearization unless explicitly stated otherwise. Let [λ] denote the irreducible S4 representation attached to the partition λ. Write 1=[4] and sgn=[1111].

## 1. Equivariant splitting exists

The finite quotient σ is flat, by Cohen–Macaulayness of Y and regularity of P⁴. Projection formula gives H^i(B(n))=H^i(Y,L^n). Künneth gives vanishing for 1≤i≤3 and every integer n. Horrocks splitting and the Hilbert series

Σ_{n≥0}(n+1)^4 z^n = (1+11z+11z²+z³)/(1-z)^5

therefore give the ordinary bundle splitting O⊕O(-1)^11⊕O(-2)^11⊕O(-3).

This splitting can be made equivariant; simply averaging an arbitrary bundle isomorphism would not prove that assertion. Instead let R=Sym H⁰(P⁴,O(1)) and M=⊕_{n≥0}H⁰(P⁴,B(n)). S4 acts trivially on R. The ordinary splitting makes M a graded free R-module. The finite-dimensional graded generator space Q=M/R_+M has dimensions 1,11,11,1 in degrees 0,1,2,3. Maschke's theorem supplies equivariant degree-preserving lifts Q→M. Their R-linear extension R⊗Q→M is surjective by graded Nakayama, and is an isomorphism since its Hilbert series equals that of M (or because it is a surjection between free modules with the same graded ranks). Sheafification proves

B ≅ ⊕_{a=0}³ O(-a)⊗W_a,

where W_a=Q_a as S4 representations. The W_a are intrinsic up to representation isomorphism; the chosen bundle splitting is generally noncanonical. No algebra splitting is asserted.

## 2. Identification of the representations and the duality sign

Degree zero gives W_0=1. Degree one gives the exact sequence

0→H⁰(P⁴,O(1))⊗W_0→H⁰(Y,L)→W_1→0.

The first term is five copies of 1, and H⁰(Y,L)=(C²)^{⊗4} with the ordinary factor-permutation action. Schur–Weyl decomposition gives

(C²)^{⊗4} ≅ 5·[4] ⊕ 3·[31] ⊕ [22],

so W_1=3·[31]⊕[22]. Equivalently, its character at the classes 1,(12),(12)(34),(123),(1234) is (11,3,-1,-1,-3), obtained from 2^(number of cycles)-5.

The ordinary line L^{-2} is isomorphic to ω_Y as a nonequivariant line, but not with its ordinary linearization. On an affine chart the canonical generator dx_1∧dx_2∧dx_3∧dx_4 changes by sgn(g) under a permutation g, whereas the ordinary tensor product of the four O(-2) frames changes with no extra sign. Thus, equivariantly,

ω_Y ≅ L^{-2}⊗sgn.

The base has trivial S4 action, including on its canonical bundle O(-5). Finite duality therefore gives

B^∨ ≅ σ_*(ω_Y⊗σ*ω_{P⁴}^{-1}) ≅ B(3)⊗sgn.

Comparing minimal graded generators after the common twist, or using uniqueness of the equivariant multiplicity representations proved above, gives W_a^∨≅sgn⊗W_{3-a}. All irreducible S4 representations are self-dual; sign twists [31] to [211] and fixes [22]. Consequently

B ≅ O⊗1
 ⊕ O(-1)⊗(3·[31]⊕[22])
 ⊕ O(-2)⊗(3·[211]⊕[22])
 ⊕ O(-3)⊗sgn.

The sign in finite duality is essential: omitting it would exchange the wrong representations.

## 3. The full-deck line has ordinary linearization

For the product q:X=C⁴→Y of identical elliptic double covers, the deck algebra is ⊕_S O_Y(-2S). Its full-deck-character line is L^{-2}. This is an algebra of ordinary functions. On a finite chart its frame is y_1y_2y_3y_4, which factor permutations reorder without a sign. Thus the full-deck line is L^{-2} with the ordinary permutation linearization, not ω_Y with its canonical linearization. The extra canonical sign in section 2 must not be inserted into this branch eigensheaf.

For E=(σq)*H⊗N, the twist N cancels in End E. The canonical pullback permutation action on End E identifies its full-deck-character Ext groups with

V^j := Ext^j_X(E,E)_{full-deck} ≅ H^j(Y,σ*End H⊗L^{-2}).

Finiteness and projection formula now give the equivariant additive decomposition

V^j ≅ H^j(End H(-2))⊗1
 ⊕ H^j(End H(-3))⊗(3·[31]⊕[22])
 ⊕ H^j(End H(-4))⊗(3·[211]⊕[22])
 ⊕ H^j(End H(-5))⊗sgn.

The cohomology on the right is on P⁴, with trivial S4 action. This holds for every j, without any classical HM cohomology dimension input.

## 4. The paired character selects exactly three groups

Let D≤S4 be the stabilizer of the unordered pairing {{1,2},{3,4}}, generated by a=(12), b=(34), c=(13)(24). It has order eight. Let λ be its character with λ(a)=λ(b)=-1 and λ(c)=+1. These are exactly the values of sgn|_D.

The permutation representation on the three unordered pairings is Ind_D^{S4}1=1⊕[22]: it is the three-point permutation action through S4→S3, whose reduced representation is [22]. Tensoring by sgn gives

Ind_D^{S4}λ=sgn⊕[22].

Frobenius reciprocity consequently gives dim Hom_D(λ,[μ])=1 for μ=[1111],[22] and zero for the other irreducibles. Define the paired subspace V^j_λ={v:dv=λ(d)v for every d∈D}. Applying this exact character projection yields

V^j_λ ≅ H^j(End H(-5)) ⊕ H^j(End H(-3)) ⊕ H^j(End H(-4)).

In particular the entire S4-sign subspace V^j_sgn is canonically contained in V^j_λ and is isomorphic to H^j(End H(-5)). The quotient therefore satisfies

V^j_λ / V^j_sgn ≅ H^j(End H(-3)) ⊕ H^j(End H(-4)).

The quotient is canonical; the displayed additive identification inherits the noncanonical splitting choice. Equivalently, the central S4-sign idempotent canonically separates the sign sector from the [22] sector, while the two classical cohomological summands of the latter need not be canonically split. The normalized D-character projector is e_λ=(1/8)Σ_{d∈D}λ(d)d. No dimension or actual matrix is evaluated here.

## 5. Exact source-matching requirement and limitations

The theorem applies to the actual paired source precisely when the induced action on its cohomology class is the geometric factor-permutation action from section 3, and its pair symmetrization is antisymmetric in each pair and symmetric under exchanging the two pairs. On generators this is (-,-,+). Merely counting four directional labels or seeing the word “antisymmetric” is insufficient: exchanging the two pairs with a minus sign selects a different D-character, and a dual/Serre trace convention can introduce a canonical sign twist.

Likewise, a proof that a particular source's full S4 antisymmetrization is a boundary shows that its class has zero sign component. It does not say that the entire H²(End H(-5)) summand vanishes. Under the source-matching hypotheses the remaining class lies in a space additively isomorphic to the TWO classical groups H²(End H(-3)) and H²(End H(-4)). This alone proves neither that these remaining components vanish nor that thirty named detector functionals form its full dual.

Conditional numerical comparison only: if the separate classical cohomology input establishes dimensions 2,20,10 for the twists -5,-3,-4, respectively, then the paired space has dimension 32 and the quotient by the S4-sign subspace has dimension 30. No new proof of those numerical inputs is made here.
