## Checks

1. Outer reversal and cyclic zero give the stated eight tensor coefficients. The universal tensor tau=x tensor (y wedge z)−z tensor (x wedge y) has exactly those coefficients under T(u tensor omega)=A and T(v tensor omega)=−B. Its image spans the rank-two source, proving uniqueness and coordinate independence.
2. For any hypothetical nonzero kernel vector a u−b v, the repeated-input formula and an independent second vector contradict structural nonvanishing. This proves actual fiber injectivity, not just nonzeroness of the map.
3. Local regular frame constructions glue by uniqueness. Closed-point fiber injectivity on the finite-type complex Grassmannian makes the degeneracy locus empty. An invertible maximal minor gives a local left inverse and locally free cokernel. This is enough to preserve the component ideal equality under arbitrary base change.
4. The incidence space of spanning triples identifies scheme-theoretically with the exact-rank-two determinantal stratum, not with an open subset of the entire ambient V^3. Invertible-minor charts give the unique locally free image subbundle and verify the inverse map.
5. All three chart formulas are correct: for frames (x,z), (x,y), and (y,z), the coefficients of tau are respectively (a,−b), (−2a,−b), and (a,2b). Their ideals equal the two coefficient variables in characteristic zero. They cut out exactly one zero input and an independent remaining pair, with no nilpotents on this stratum.
6. The three components are disjoint, smooth independent-pair opens of dimension ten. The rank-two ambient stratum has dimension twelve. This proves the claimed codimension-two regular embeddings. It does not assert anything about transverse deformations leaving that stratum.
7. Field-valued triples of rank at most one vanish by the diagonal identity. A rank-two triple with all inputs nonzero cannot vanish: independent outer inputs force the middle input to zero, while proportional nonzero outer inputs give the nonzero scalar factor −2. These are universal arguments, with no chosen physical/source input evaluation.

## Scope clarification

The imported filtered-extension representation theorem was explicitly established over arbitrary affine complex bases. To state the extension-family classification for every test scheme, add Zariski descent: affine-locally the family is uniquely isomorphic to the universal pullback; uniqueness makes overlap isomorphisms agree and satisfy the cocycle condition, hence they glue. The universal pullback itself exists on every test scheme. Equivalently, restricting the extension-family sentence to affine bases would suffice. The arbitrary-base-change ideal equality and universal-bundle pullback statements are already valid without this clarification.
