# Coordinate components: generic multiplicity one and exact transverse degeneracy

## Theorem

Work over C. Let dim V=5, dim J=20, let f:V^3 -> J be the canonical trilinear operation, and let Z be its scheme-theoretic zero locus. Write C_i for the coordinate ten-plane on which input i is zero. Import the two-plane classification, structural repeated-input nonvanishing, and the bound dim Z^(3) <=9 for the independent-triple locus from the proof files below. Then:

1. Each C_i has a dense open U_i whose complement has dimension at most eight, and the full scheme Z equals C_i on an ambient open neighborhood of U_i. Thus Z is smooth of dimension ten there, and C_i has generic multiplicity one in Z.
2. For the fixed complementary pair b, let L_i(b):V -> J be the operation linear in slot i. The singular points of Z on C_i are exactly D_i={b:rank L_i(b)<5}, with the identification C_i=V^2. Its determinantal scheme is defined by the five-minors of this universal bundle map. The tangent space at (0,b) has dimension 10+dim ker L_i(b).
3. Over the independent-pair open B, kernel dimension is at most three, and dim {b in B:dim ker L_i(b)>=k} <=9-k for k=1,2,3. For nonzero proportional fixed inputs the kernel has dimension one. If either fixed input is zero, the kernel is all V. In particular dim D_i <=8.
4. Set-theoretically, D_i is exactly C_i intersected with the union of all other irreducible components of Z.
5. The top-dimensional cycle is [Z]_10=[C_1]+[C_2]+[C_3]. For the standard graded homogeneous coordinate ring A, Proj(A) has top-dimensional degree three and Hilbert polynomial 3 n^9/9! + O(n^8). The Hilbert-Samuel multiplicity of Z at the origin is three.

Neither global reducedness nor generic reducedness of the smaller components is asserted. No exact description of the independent singular locus, or dimension/equations of an actual D_i beyond these structural bounds, is asserted.

## 1. The universal fixed-pair map

Fix a slot i, denote its variable by u, and denote the ordered remaining pair by b. Over the affine ten-space B_all=V^2 there is a bundle map

    L_i: V tensor O -> J tensor O,

whose entries are bilinear functions of the two remaining variables. Trilinearity gives the identity f=L_i(b)u, with the original slot order understood. Therefore Z is precisely the kernel scheme of this bundle map, including its possibly nonreduced structure. There is no assumption that this kernel scheme is a vector bundle on all of B_all.

On the open set U_i where some five-minor is invertible, a left inverse for L_i exists locally. The ideals generated by f and by the five coordinates of u are therefore equal there: containment (f) subset (u) is automatic, and the left inverse gives the reverse containment. Consequently

    Z restricted over U_i = {u=0} x U_i

scheme-theoretically. This proves the full-neighborhood assertion and smoothness once nonemptiness and the complement bound are established.

## 2. Independent pairs and the dimension bound

Let B subset V^2 be the independent-pair open. The two-plane classification implies

    ker L_i(b) intersect span(b) = {0}

for every geometric b in B: a nonzero vector in that intersection would give a nonzero, rank-two zero triple. Hence dim ker L_i(b)<=3, since span(b) has dimension two in V.

More importantly, every nonzero vector in that kernel gives an independent zero triple. Conversely every independent zero triple gives precisely such a vector over its complementary pair.

For j=1,2,3, let B_j be the reduced locally closed locus on which dim ker L_i=j. On B_j the map has constant rank, so its kernel is a rank-j vector bundle. This can be seen on an invertible rank-(5-j) minor: the complementary block vanishes on the reduced stratum, by its higher-minor equations. The punctured kernel bundle is exactly the underlying independent-zero incidence above B_j. Thus, for every nonempty B_j,

    dim B_j + j <= dim Z^(3) <=9.

For B_{>=k}=union_(j>=k) B_j it follows that

    dim B_{>=k} <= max_(j>=k)(9-j) <=9-k.

In particular the noninjective locus in B has dimension at most eight, whereas B has dimension ten. Therefore the injectivity open U_i is nonempty and dense. These arguments apply separately to all three slots; no permutation symmetry stronger than the imported identities is assumed.

A weaker proof of nonemptiness alone uses dim Z=10: a nonzero generic kernel over all of V^2 would make the kernel scheme have dimension at least eleven. The independent-pair argument is needed for the codimension-two improvement.

## 3. Dependent complementary pairs

Suppose the fixed inputs are nonzero proportional vectors a v and b v with a,b nonzero. Outer reversal and cyclic zero give, as identities of the remaining variable u, that the fixed-pair operation is a nonzero scalar multiple of either

    f(v,v,u), or f(v,u,v)=-2 f(v,v,u).

The first expression is zero for u in Cv and nonzero for every u outside Cv by structural repeated-input nonvanishing. Thus its kernel is exactly Cv and its rank is four. This is a universal deduction, with no selected input evaluated.

The locus of nonzero proportional ordered pairs has dimension six: it is the open both-columns-nonzero part of the rank-one 5-by-2 determinantal cone. If either input is zero, L_i is identically zero by trilinearity; this union of two five-planes has dimension five.

These dependent strata together with Section 2 exhaust the fixed-pair space. Therefore dim D_i<=max(8,6,5)=8, and every point of U_i has an independent complementary pair.

More precisely, write D_(i,>=k) for the global kernel-dimension-at-least-k determinantal locus. Then

    dim D_(i,>=1)<=8, dim D_(i,>=2)<=7,
    dim D_(i,>=3)<=6,
    D_(i,>=4)=D_(i,>=5)={one fixed input is zero},

where the last equalities are set-theoretic and this last union has dimension five. Indeed independent pairs have kernel dimension at most three, nonzero proportional pairs have kernel dimension one, and zero-input pairs have kernel dimension five. In particular kernel dimension four never occurs.

## 4. Tangent spaces and the singular locus along C_i

At a point of C_i, the formal differential of f is

    d f_(0,b)(delta u,delta b) = L_i(b) delta u.

All terms differentiating b contain u and hence vanish there. Consequently

    T_(0,b) Z = T_b(V^2) direct-sum ker L_i(b),
    dim T_(0,b) Z = 10 + dim ker L_i(b).

At every closed point of C_i the local dimension of Z is exactly ten: C_i supplies the lower bound, and dim Z=10 supplies the upper bound. Thus a rank drop gives tangent dimension greater than local dimension and the point is singular. Conversely rank five gives the scheme equality of Section 1 and hence smoothness. This proves the exact set-theoretic identity

    Sing(Z) intersect C_i = D_i.

For an unambiguous scheme statement, the closed subscheme of Z defined by Fitt_10(Omega_(Z/C)) restricts to the five-minor determinantal scheme D_i on C_i. Indeed the presentation of Omega uses the full Jacobian, and its five-minors restrict to those of the block L_i, the other two blocks restricting to zero. Because Z is non-equidimensional, this Fitting subscheme is not being identified with a universal singular-locus scheme on all of Z.

Over U_i the normal bundle of Z in V^3 is the trivial rank-five bundle V tensor O_(U_i) in the omitted slot. Its differential map into J tensor O_(U_i) is L_i, a subbundle inclusion with locally free rank-fifteen cokernel. D_i is exactly where this normal differential fails to be injective. At nonzero proportional pairs the tangent dimension is eleven; with either fixed input zero it is fifteen. At independent bad pairs it is eleven, twelve, or thirteen according to kernel dimension.

## 5. Rank drop is exactly component crossing along C_i

If b lies in D_i, choose a nonzero kernel vector u abstractly and consider the algebraic line

    t -> (t u,b)

with its entries placed in the original slots. This line is contained in Z by trilinearity. It is not contained in C_i, so its irreducible closure lies in an irreducible component other than C_i. At t=0 it passes through (0,b). This proves that every point of D_i lies on another component. The choice is existential; no input value or rank is computed.

Conversely, if b lies outside D_i, Section 1 gives an ambient neighborhood on which Z is just the smooth plane C_i. A different irreducible component cannot meet that neighborhood: its intersection would be a nonempty open subset contained in C_i, forcing its closure to lie in C_i, contrary to maximality of distinct irreducible components. Hence D_i is precisely the intersection with the other components, set-theoretically.

For independent b, the line away from t=0 consists of independent zero triples, so the other component can be chosen to meet Z^(3). In particular every independent rank-drop pair is an actual degeneration of independent zeros, not merely an infinitesimal tangent direction. The component-crossing statement does not imply that D_i is reduced in its determinantal structure.

## 6. Multiplicities, top cycle, and degree

The scheme equality over nonempty U_i implies that the local ring at the generic point of C_i is its function field. Its length is therefore one. Since the imported top-component theorem says these are exactly the dimension-ten components,

    [Z]_10 = sum_(i=1)^3 length(O_(Z,eta_i)) [C_i]
            = [C_1]+[C_2]+[C_3].

The ideal of f is homogeneous of degree three. Each C_i projectivizes to a linear P^9 in P^14, of degree one, with the same generic multiplicity. Thus Proj(A) has dimension nine and top-dimensional degree three. Smaller-dimensional components and embedded structure do not change this leading degree. Accordingly

    dim_C A_n = (3/9!) n^9 + O(n^8)

for n sufficiently large. Summing the graded pieces gives

    length(A_m / m^(N+1) A_m) = (3/10!) N^10 + O(N^9),

where m is the homogeneous maximal ideal. Hence the Hilbert-Samuel multiplicity at the origin is three. This is a top-dimensional multiplicity assertion, not a complete-intersection degree calculation or a claim of equidimensionality or Cohen-Macaulayness.
