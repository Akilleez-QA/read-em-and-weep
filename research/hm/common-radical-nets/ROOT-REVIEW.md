The universal theorem is correct over C. Polarization ensures that a nonzero wedge map has a nonzero square. The alternating adjugate identity identifies the radical of a rank-four form. In the zero-wedge case, the two support planes of independent decomposable forms meet in exactly a line. The third support plane either contains this line, giving support dimension four, or meets the first two in distinct lines and lies in their three-space. This establishes the exhaustive alpha/beta classification without assuming genericity.

The scheme-theoretic argument also passes: on a chart with a unit Pfaffian coordinate, rank-one minors make every wedge column proportional to the chosen square. In the polynomial identity P(omega_0+t omega)=a(t)x, the constant term of a is one. The coefficient of t in the alternating adjugate identity then gives contraction_x(omega)=0 over any C-algebra, without cancellation by a nonunit or assuming reducedness. Conversely the split radical line makes all wedges factor through a line bundle. The invertible alternating four-by-four pivot proves uniqueness of the radical line over arbitrary bases. These inverse morphisms give the stated isomorphism on the specified open set.

The application to the traceless orbit uses the three independent tensors q tensor epsilon_1, p tensor epsilon_2, p tensor epsilon_1 minus q tensor epsilon_2 for independent p,q. Their independence is preserved by an injective phi*. Thus the net really has dimension three. The application makes no scheme identification at zero-wedge nets; their radical fibers are explicitly different.

The universal theorem has no HM dependency. For the actual primary application, the exterior intertwiner and traceless evaluation are the interface in PR543 and PR553 (342e2e3754664477026e4fcc3bc6a135a1eab9cc); PR554 (eb16c0e38e5157dc2902273cea3939b3410057c7) identifies the thirty support-drop points. Away from those points a nonzero rank-one annihilator tensor would annihilate the full V-support, so the actual rank-nine annihilator has tensor rank two. These interfaces identify the net problem but do not prove any modular-surface correspondence.

This review is an independent mathematical check, not formal verification. No selected actual HM/source matrix, coefficient, numerical rank or source membership was evaluated.
