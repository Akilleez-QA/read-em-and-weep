# Exceptional surface fibers: universal cohomology from the unstable-plane extension

## Result and scope

Assume the geometric bundle and cover premises in `../relative-surface-0502/RESULT.md` and its `DECK_ADDENDUM.md`. Let E be either unstable normalized Horrocks–Mumford plane restriction. Use the supplied restriction-theorem data

    rank E=2, c1(E)=-H, c2(E)=4H²,
    h⁰(E)=1, h⁰(E(-1))=0.

Then, for A=End(E), universally over these unstable restrictions,

    h⁰(A)=4, h⁰(A(-1))=1,
    h⁰(A(-j))=0 for j≥2,
    h⁰(A⊗Ω¹_P²)=0.

For p:C²→P² of degree eight and F_b=N_b⊗p*E, set G_b=End₀(F_b). The exceptional fiber cohomology dimensions are exactly

    (h⁰(G_b),h¹(G_b),h²(G_b)) = (4,128,4).

The stable-fiber dimensions remain (0,120,0). Thus this computes dimensions conditional on instability, independently of which of the two unstable resolutions occurs. It does not locate exceptional parameters, prove that one occurs in the particular plane family, evaluate any physical matrix, source, or jet, or determine any of the thirty retained obstruction values.

## 1. Saturation and the zero scheme

Choose the nonzero section s:O→E. If it had a nonzero divisorial zero D, its saturated rank-one image would be O(D)=O(d), d≥1. This would give a nonzero section of E(-d), and multiplication by a nonzero degree-(d−1) polynomial would give a nonzero section of E(-1), a contradiction. Thus the section has no divisorial zero.

The quotient is rank-one torsion-free with determinant O(-1), hence

    0 → O --s--> E --q--> Q=I_Z(-1) → 0.                 (1)

The zero scheme Z is zero-dimensional and locally a complete intersection: in a local trivialization, its ideal is generated by the two components of the section, and absence of a divisor forces height two at every zero. The Chern-class identity gives length(Z)=c₂(E)=4. In particular Z is nonempty.

The extension class e∈Ext¹(Q,O) is nonzero. Otherwise E≅O⊕I_Z(-1), which is not locally free at Z. This uses the actual vector-bundle premise, not just a numerical Chern character.

## 2. Hom computations and extension compatibility

On the smooth plane, the codimension-two ideal has

    Hom_sheaf(I_Z,O)=O,
    Hom_sheaf(I_Z,I_Z)=O.

For the first identity, extend a regular function from the complement of Z across codimension two, equivalently use reflexive hulls. For the second, every ideal endomorphism is multiplication by such a regular function, and every regular function preserves the ideal. Consequently, for every integer t,

    Hom(Q,O(t)) = H⁰(O(t+1)),
    Hom(Q,Q(t)) = H⁰(O(t)).                             (2)

These statements do not require Z to be reduced, in general position, or on no special curve.

Every endomorphism φ:E→E preserves the section subbundle because H⁰(E)=C·s. It induces scalars a on O and b on Q. Functoriality of the extension class requires

    a e = b e.

Since e≠0, a=b. Subtracting a·id_E leaves exactly a map factoring as E→Q→O→E. Therefore

    End(E) on global sections = C·id_E ⊕ H⁰(O(1)),
    h⁰(A)=1+3=4.                                       (3)

Here the second summand is a square-zero ideal: its elements are s f q and products vanish because q s=0. In characteristic zero it is exactly H⁰(End₀(E)).

For t<0, H⁰(E(t))=0: a section in any lower twist would, by multiplication by a polynomial, give one in twist −1. Hence a map E→E(t) annihilates O and factors through Q. Its composite Q→E(t)→Q(t) is zero by (2), since H⁰(O(t))=0. It therefore factors uniquely through O(t). Conversely every map Q→O(t) gives such an endomorphism. Thus

    Hom(E,E(t)) ≅ Hom(Q,O(t)) = H⁰(O(t+1)),  t<0.       (4)

This is an actual Hom calculation using exactness and the nonzero extension, rather than an Euler-characteristic inference. Possible higher Ext groups of I_Z introduce no additional ordinary Hom maps: the relevant potential obstruction for the diagonal pair is precisely (a−b)e, and the off-diagonal factorizations are established directly.

## 3. The Ω¹ term and its map, not just its dimensions

Tensor the Euler sequence by A:

    0 → A⊗Ω¹ → A(-1)^⊕3 --(x₀,x₁,x₂)--> A → 0.

By (4), H⁰(A(-1)) is one-dimensional, generated by the composition

    E --q--> I_Z(-1) → O(-1) --s(-1)--> E(-1).

Multiplication by x_i gives the nilpotent in (3) corresponding to x_i∈H⁰(O(1)). Therefore the map on H⁰ is the inclusion

    C³ ≅ H⁰(O(1)) → C·id_E ⊕ H⁰(O(1)).

It is injective for every Z, including all nonreduced or otherwise special schemes that can occur. Its kernel is zero, proving h⁰(A⊗Ω¹)=0. By A*=A and T_P²(-3)≅Ω¹_P², Serre duality also gives h²(A⊗Ω¹)=0.

## 4. Complete plane cohomology needed for the cover

As before, ch(A)=4−15H², so χ(A(t))=2t²+6t−11 and

    h²(A(t))=h⁰(A(-t−3)).

The triples (h⁰,h¹,h²) are

    A:            (4,15,0),
    A(-1):        (1,16,0),
    A(-2):        (0,16,1),
    A(-3):        (0,15,4),
    A⊗Ω¹:         (0,34,0).

For the last line χ(A⊗Ω¹)=3χ(A(-1))−χ(A)=−34. In particular the Ω¹ contribution does not jump between stable and unstable restrictions.

## 5. Exceptional surface dimensions and fiber deck characters

Retain the fiber-deck decomposition of p_*O from the supplied report:

    00: O ⊕ O(-1),
    10: Ω¹,
    01: Ω¹,
    11: O(-2) ⊕ O(-3).

Projection formula gives End(F_b) cohomology by deck character:

    character       h⁰       h¹       h²
    00               5       31        0
    10               0       34        0
    01               0       34        0
    11               0       31        5

The scalar O_(C²) contributes h⁰=1 in 00, h¹=1 in each of 10 and 01, and h²=1 in 11. Subtracting the scalar direct summand gives G_b=End₀(F_b):

    character       h⁰       h¹       h²
    00               4       31        0
    10               0       33        0
    01               0       33        0
    11               0       31        4

Totals are (4,128,4), consistently χ(G_b)=−120. The tracefree degree-two obstruction target is four-dimensional and entirely fiber-deck full-sign. This is a target dimension, not evidence that an actual obstruction is nonzero.

The five-dimensional global endomorphism algebra of F_b is also C plus a four-dimensional square-zero ideal. Indeed the pullback of (1) stays exact because p is finite flat. The pullback ideal is the ideal of a finite subscheme, so its dual is O. Moreover h⁰(p*E)=1, as follows from the same pushforward decomposition and H⁰(E(t))=0 for t<0 together with Ω¹↪O(-1)^3. The identical extension argument applies; equivalently its nilpotents are H⁰(p*O(1)), of dimension 3+1=4. This algebra description is optional for the cohomology calculation.

## 6. Inner-swap refinement

Use the actual equivariant descent established in the supplied deck addendum. On character 00, O is swap-even and O(-1) swap-odd. On character 11, O(-2) is swap-even and O(-3) swap-odd. The scalar H²(O_(C²)) is swap-odd; the scalar H⁰ is swap-even.

Thus the simultaneous character/swap dimensions for G_b are:

    character 00:
        h⁰: (+,−)=(3,1),
        h¹: (+,−)=(15,16),
        h²: (+,−)=(0,0).

    character 11:
        h⁰: (+,−)=(0,0),
        h¹: (+,−)=(16,15),
        h²: (+,−)=(1,3).

Swap exchanges characters 10 and 01, rather than preserving either separately. On their direct sum, h¹ has swap-even and swap-odd dimensions (33,33); h⁰ and h² vanish.

For comparison, on good fibers the character-11 h¹ swap dimensions were (15,12), and h² vanished. On exceptional fibers these become (16,15) and (1,3). Therefore the fiber-full-sign, inner-swap-odd degree-one summand jumps from dimension 12 to 15, while the corresponding fiber degree-two target has dimension 3.

Equivariant Serre duality checks these signs: the canonical two-form on C² has deck character 11 and swap sign −. It sends the h⁰ split (3,1) in character 00 to h² split (1,3) in character 11.

## 7. Relative interpretation and limits

Over the full integral parameter base with generically stable fibers, r_*G=0 still holds, as in the supplied report. The exceptional h⁰=4 does not contradict this: formation of r_*G fails to commute with base change there. Likewise the exceptional h¹=128 does not make R¹r_*G a vector bundle of rank 128 on a neighborhood containing good fibers.

Top-degree proper base change identifies the fiber of R²r_*G with H²(G_b). Accordingly its nonzero fibers, if any, are supported on the unstable-parameter locus and are four-dimensional. The fiber-deck character is 11; the inner-swap-even and odd fibers have dimensions 1 and 3 at every unstable parameter. All other top-degree character summands vanish. These fiber dimensions alone do not determine scheme-theoretic support, module length, transverse structure, or gluing behavior.

After restriction to any smooth locally closed stratum consisting entirely of unstable parameters, proper cohomology and base change apply with the constant dimensions above. The restricted-family direct images are vector bundles with those ranks and equivariant ranks. These are direct images of the restricted family; one must not identify them with ordinary restrictions of all original-base direct images without the relevant base-change theorem.

There is no implication that the original exceptional locus is nonempty, finite, reduced, or smooth, and no new bound on the global thirty-dimensional sector. On the good open, the previous rank-12 gluing summand remains correct. The present computation identifies the possible exceptional fiber targets and their precise jumps; it does not remove them or evaluate any obstruction class.
