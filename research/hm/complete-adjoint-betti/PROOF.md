# The complete adjoint cohomology Betti family

Work over C. Let F be the normalized Horrocks–Mumford bundle on P(V)=P^4, T=V*, S=Sym(T), B=End_0(F), and

    K_j=H^1(B(j-1)), H_j=H^2(B(j-5)), G_j=H^0(B(j-1)).

All modules and multiplication maps are the actual ones. Write beta_(i,d)=dim Tor_i^S(K,C)_d. There is a single unresolved integer r in {0,15}; the entire minimal free resolution of K has free terms

    F0 = 5S,
    F1 = S(-1) + 15S(-2) + rS(-3),
    F2 = rS(-3) + 10S(-4) + 10S(-6) + 60S(-7) + 25S(-8),
    F3 = 2S(-6) + 140S(-8) + 75S(-9),
    F4 = 80S(-9) + 75S(-10),
    F5 = 32S(-11).

Thus 0 -> F5 -> F4 -> F3 -> F2 -> F1 -> F0 -> K -> 0 is its minimal resolution, with the displayed ranks and shifts. This does not assert that both alternatives for r are realized. The actual K has one of these two tables; deciding which remains open. The new degree-four assertion needed to complete the family is

    Tor_2(K,C)_4 = A tensor W',   dim=10,
    Tor_i(K,C)_4 = 0 for i other than 2.

Here A is the five-dimensional normalizer representation used in the pinned inputs and W' is its two-dimensional representation. The isomorphism is understood with the determinant factor of T; this factor is trivial for the stated normalizer.

## 1. Pinned inputs and low-degree minimality

PR476 proves that K is generated by its five-dimensional degree-zero piece. Its degree-one minimal relation is the Euler relation epsilon, and its degree-two minimal relation space has dimension fifteen. There is no degree-two second syzygy: the Euler column is nonzero, hence injective over the domain S, and the fifteen quadratic lifts are independent modulo T epsilon. More explicitly, any degree-two relation among these columns would give a constant dependence among the quadratic classes modulo T epsilon; those constants vanish, and injectivity of the Euler column kills the remaining linear coefficient. This is independence of actual generators, not a Hilbert-numerator argument.

PR479 proves that the only possible cubic minimal relation space is the irreducible A* tensor U', of dimension fifteen, and that the adjacent cubic second-syzygy space has the same dimension r. Thus

    beta_(1,3)=beta_(2,3)=r in {0,15}.

PR481 proves that there are no quartic or higher minimal relations. Therefore F1 has exactly the terms displayed above. Generation in degree zero also gives beta_(0,d)=0 for d different from zero.

For completeness, minimality excludes hidden low-degree higher syzygies. F1 has minimum degree one. There are no second syzygies of degree at most one, since the only possible constant dependence in degree one is on the nonzero Euler column. The preceding independence argument also excludes degree two. Hence F2 has minimum degree at least three. In a minimal graded resolution every nonzero column of each differential has entries in the homogeneous maximal ideal, so successive minimum generator degrees increase by at least one. A zero column cannot occur: exactness would place its basis vector in the image of the next differential, which lies in the maximal ideal times that free module. Consequently F3 has minimum degree at least four, F4 at least five, and F5 at least six. This already exhausts all homological indices in internal degrees at most three.

## 2. Sections and the dual Koszul module

PR484 proves H^0(B(t))=0 for t<=4. In particular, its positive-twist argument at t=3 is essential here and can be recalled explicitly. Riemann–Roch gives

    chi(B(t))=3 binom(t+4,4)-(15/2)t^2-(75/2)t-25,
    chi(B(3))=-100.

PR477 gives h^1(B(3))=100. The structural bounds give H^2(B(3))=H^3(B(3))=0, and Serre duality gives H^4(B(3))=H^0(B(-8))*=0. Therefore h^0(B(3))=0. Lower positive twists are audited by PR479; negative twists vanish by stability and twist zero by simplicity. No positive-twist section vanishing is inferred from stability alone.

The actual S-compatible pairing H_j tensor H_(5-j) -> C gives Koszul duality

    Tor_i(H,C)_d = Tor_(5-i)(H,C)_(10-d)* tensor det T.

PR482 proves that H is generated in degree zero, has no linear relations, and has quadratic minimal relation space A* tensor W', of dimension ten. Its two relevant degree-eight groups are therefore

    Tor_4(H,C)_8 = Tor_1(H,C)_2* tensor det T
                     = A tensor W',
    Tor_5(H,C)_8 = Tor_0(H,C)_2* tensor det T = 0.

These assertions need no choice of the unresolved cubic scalar or of the middle parameter in the separate H Betti family.

## 3. The missing degree-four syzygies

Tensor the exact coordinate Koszul sheaf complex by B(3). Its zero-abutment hypercohomology spectral sequence is

    E1^(-i,q)=Lambda^i T tensor H^q(B(3-i)),

with differential d_a of bidegree (a,1-a). The q=1 row on E2 is Tor_i(K,C)_4; the q=2 row is Tor_i(H,C)_8. All q=0 terms vanish by section 2. All q=3 terms vanish as well: their twists run from -2 to 3, PR476 handles twists at least -1, and PR466 supplies H^3(B(-2))=0. All q=4 terms vanish by Serre duality, since the corresponding dual section twists run from -8 to -3. These vanishings are at E1 and consequently persist on every later page.

The only possible differential between the two remaining rows is d2. For each 0<=i<=5 it sends the H row in column -i to the K row in column -i+2, when that column exists. No higher differential can connect two nonzero rows. In particular,

    Tor_4(H,C)_8 at (-4,2) -> Tor_2(K,C)_4 at (-2,1)

must be an isomorphism: the source has no incoming differential, the target has no other incoming differential, and neither can survive the zero abutment. Explicitly the possible later outgoing source target (-1,0), the outgoing target (0,0), and the possible incoming d3 source (-5,3) all vanish by the E1 bounds just established. This proves Tor_2(K,C)_4=A tensor W', dimension ten.

Likewise Tor_3(K,C)_4 at (-3,1) can receive only d2 from Tor_5(H,C)_8=0. Its outgoing target (-1,0) is zero and every other incoming source is outside the five-step complex. It survives unchanged to the zero abutment and is zero. The minimum-degree bounds in section 1 give Tor_4(K,C)_4=Tor_5(K,C)_4=0; degree-zero generation and PR481 give the remaining indices zero. Thus every entry in internal degree four is now determined.

## 4. Middle degrees and the exact high tail

PR484 proves that every Tor_i(K,C)_5 vanishes. In degree six it proves that the only nonzero groups are

    Tor_2(K,C)_6 = H^0(B(5)) = Sym^2 H^0(F(3)), dim=10,
    Tor_3(K,C)_6 = W', dim=2.

These are actual spectral-sequence isomorphisms, not guesses from an alternating sum.

PR485 proves, for every n>=7 and 2<=i<=5, actual isomorphisms

    Tor_i(K,C)_n = Tor_(i-2)(G,C)_n.

PR486 determines the entire minimal G resolution, not merely its shifts:

    0 -> 32S(-11) -> 80S(-9)+75S(-10)
      -> 140S(-8)+75S(-9)
      -> 10S(-6)+60S(-7)+25S(-8) -> G -> 0.

Applying the comparison gives precisely

    beta_(2,7)=60, beta_(2,8)=25,
    beta_(3,8)=140, beta_(3,9)=75,
    beta_(4,9)=80, beta_(4,10)=75,
    beta_(5,11)=32,

with all other entries in n>=7 and i>=2 zero. Generation and the complete relation bound already give zero in these degrees for i=0,1. Hilbert's syzygy theorem over the five-variable ring excludes all homological indices above five. Together with sections 1–3 and the middle-degree results this exhausts every integer internal degree and every homological index, proving the stated full family.

As a consistency check, the alternating numerator from the displayed terms is

    5-t-15t^2+10t^4+8t^6+60t^7-115t^8+5t^9+75t^10-32t^11.

It equals (1-t)^5 times the established Hilbert series

    5+24t+55t^2+85t^3+100t^4+85t^5+32t^6.

The two r terms cancel in this identity. The identity is not used to infer their value, nor to exclude an unexamined pair of adjacent syzygies.
