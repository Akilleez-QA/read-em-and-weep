# Minimal relations of the HM middle-cohomology module

## Result

Let S=Sym X, X=H⁰(O(1))=V*, and reindex the audited middle-cohomology module by L_d=H²(End₀F(d−5)). It is generated by U=L₀=W′, has Hilbert function (2,10,20,20,10,2), and its trace/Serre pairing is perfect between degrees d and 5−d.

The quadratic relation space is the irreducible ten-dimensional module

    R₂ = V₃⊗W′.

There is only one possible irreducible obstruction to generating its cubic relations by quadrics. If A denotes either the minimal cubic relations or the linear syzygies of R₂, then

    A ≅ 0 or V₁⊗χ₃′.

In particular their common dimension is 0 or 15. The apparent third, thirty-dimensional obstruction V₁⊗χ₆ is excluded by a universal polynomial-shadow bound, without evaluating any multiplication matrix. The five-dimensional block V₁ is also forced nonzero by ordinary polynomial surjectivity and an irreducible-representation exclusion. Thus the cubic presentation test reduces to one scalar block, rather than an unspecified rank-50 map or three scalar blocks.

This does not prove the surviving scalar nonzero, and does not establish quadratic generation. Even the vanishing of this cubic obstruction would still leave possible quartic relations. Section 5 gives the necessary self-dual Betti shape and a further consequence of vanishing cubic obstructions.

## 1. Inputs and conventions

The generation, Hilbert function, socle, and actual N-representations are proved and independently audited in ../quintic-scalar-propagation/PROOF.md and AUDIT.md, with representation sources in ../quintic-pairing-representation and ../full-hm-image-symmetry. In particular

    L₀=W′, L₂=V₃⊗Q, L₃=V₁⊗Q,

where Q=χ₄#=Sym³W is the spinorial irreducible four-dimensional representation. V₁ has Heisenberg central character ζ² and V₃=V₁* has character ζ³. All quotient-group representations are inflated from SL₂(F₅). The other representations χ₃,χ₃′,χ₅,χ₆ have dimensions indicated by their subscripts; W′=χ₂′.

The primary source is Horrocks–Mumford, Topology 12 (1973), 63–81:
https://www.dam.brown.edu/people/mumford/alg_geom/papers/1973a--Rk2BdleP%5E4-NC.pdf

Its Lemma 3.3(v), dualized, gives Sym²X=V₃⊗χ₃′. Lemma 2.4(ii) gives directly

    X⊗V₃ = V₁⊗(χ₃⊕W′).

Lemma 3.3(vi), dualized, gives

    Sym³X = V₁⊗(χ₅⊕W′).

These are finite-N identities, not assertions of continuous SL₂ equivariance. Its appendix character table gives

    χ₃′⊗W′ = W′⊕Q,
    χ₃⊗W′ = χ₆,
    W′⊗W′ = 1⊕χ₃′,
    χ₅⊗W′ = Q⊕χ₆.

The first also follows by the two-dimensional Clebsch identity and the fact Sym³W′≅Q. As a small check, in the source's nine-class order put η+η′=−1, ηη′=−1. The characters of W and W′ are (2,−2,0,−1,1,η,η′,−η,−η′) and its η↔η′ conjugate. Symmetric-square and cubic formulas produce χ₃,χ₃′ and Q; the displayed tensor identities follow by multiplication and comparison with the table.

For any irreducible quotient representation T, V₁⊗T and V₃⊗T are irreducible N-representations by the Heisenberg-isotypic evaluation equivalence. Distinct T remain nonisomorphic.

## 2. Quadratic relations and the three initial cubic blocks

Let R=ker(S⊗U→L); write R_d for its degree-d part. There are no degree-one relations. Generation gives exact sequences

    0→R₂→Sym²X⊗U→L₂→0,
    0→R₃→Sym³X⊗U→L₃→0.

Semisimplicity and the identities above yield

    Sym²X⊗U = V₃⊗(W′⊕Q),
    R₂ = V₃⊗W′,

and

    Sym³X⊗U = V₁⊗(1⊕χ₃′⊕Q⊕χ₆),
    R₃ = V₁⊗(1⊕χ₃′⊕χ₆).

In particular the quadratic inclusion is the unique copy of its irreducible type in Sym²X⊗U. Meanwhile

    X⊗R₂ = V₁⊗(χ₃⊗W′ ⊕ W′⊗W′)
           = V₁⊗(1⊕χ₃′⊕χ₆).

The natural multiplication map

    m:X⊗R₂ → R₃

therefore has three multiplicity-one scalar blocks, on irreducibles of dimensions 5, 15, and 30. Its kernel is exactly Tor₂^S(L,C)₃, the linear syzygies among quadratic relations. Its cokernel is exactly Tor₁^S(L,C)₃, the minimal cubic relations. On each irreducible block the map is either an isomorphism or zero. Consequently its kernel and cokernel are isomorphic as N-modules (not canonically unless identifications are chosen).

## 3. A universal polynomial-shadow inequality

**Lemma.** For a finite-dimensional vector space E, n-dimensional X, and a subspace T⊂SymᵈX⊗E,

    dim(XT) / dim Symᵈ⁺¹X ≥ dim(T) / dim SymᵈX.

**Proof.** Choose a module monomial order and replace T by its degree-d leading-monomial subspace. It has the same dimension, and every variable times a leading monomial of T is a leading monomial of an element of XT. Thus it suffices to prove the bound for a monomial subspace, separately for each basis vector of E and then sum.

There are N_d=binom(n+d−1,d) monomials of degree d. Choose an exponent vector a uniformly among these, and then choose index i with probability (a_i+1)/(n+d). The resulting b=a+e_i is uniform among degree-(d+1) exponents: its probability is

    Σ_{i:b_i>0} b_i/[N_d(n+d)]
      = (d+1)/[N_d(n+d)] = 1/N_{d+1}.

If a belongs to a chosen monomial set, b belongs to its shadow under multiplication by variables. Thus the probability of landing in that shadow is at least the probability of starting in the chosen set. This is the desired normalized inequality. ∎

This lemma concerns ordinary polynomial multiplication on arbitrary subspaces. It is not an evaluation of an HM multiplication map or a rank instance.

## 4. Elimination of the thirty- and five-dimensional obstructions

Apply the lemma to T=R₂⊂Sym²X⊗U, with n=5 and dim T=10. Since dim Sym²X=15 and dim Sym³X=35,

    dim(XR₂) ≥ ceil(35·10/15)=24.

The source of m has dimension 50, so dim ker m≤26. It cannot contain the irreducible thirty-dimensional summand V₁⊗χ₆. Hence the χ₆ scalar block is necessarily nonzero and an isomorphism.

To eliminate the five-dimensional block, use full polynomial multiplication

    X⊗(Sym²X⊗U) → Sym³X⊗U,

which is surjective. Its source splits equivariantly as (X⊗R₂)⊕(X⊗L₂), since finite representations are semisimple. The second summand has representation

    X⊗L₂ = V₁⊗((χ₃⊕W′)⊗Q).

There is no trivial quotient-group constituent in (χ₃⊕W′)⊗Q: all three factors χ₃,W′,Q are self-dual irreducibles and Q is inequivalent to the other two. Thus X⊗L₂ contains no V₁. The unique V₁ constituent of Sym³X⊗U must therefore be reached from X⊗R₂. Its multiplicity-one V₁ scalar is nonzero. This V₁ target lies in R₃, since L₃=V₁⊗Q has no V₁ constituent.

Only the fifteen-dimensional block V₁⊗χ₃′ can vanish. Its zero/nonzero choices give the two alternatives in the Result. These are necessary possibilities, not a claim that both are realized by modules satisfying the full HM geometry. In particular

    dim(XR₂) ∈ {50,35}.

The proof establishes two blocks' nonvanishing structurally. It makes no corresponding claim about the remaining block.

## 5. Necessary numerical self-dual Betti shape

Write β_{i,j}=dim Tor_i^S(L,C)_j. Local duality, det X=1, and the perfect degree-five pairing give

    β_{i,j}=β_{5−i,10−j}.

For clarity, the dual module D(L)_d=Hom(L_{−d},C) satisfies D(L)≅L(5), and Ext⁵_S(L,S(−5))≅D(L), yielding the indicated degree-ten resolution duality. There are only two degree-zero generators and two degree-five socle elements, so β_{0,0}=β_{5,10}=2 and no other endpoint Betti numbers occur. Minimality and absence of linear relations give j≥i+1 for 1≤i≤4; duality also gives j≤i+4.

The Hilbert numerator is

    (1−t)⁵H_L(t)=2−10t²+20t⁴−20t⁶+10t⁸−2t¹⁰.

Therefore every possible Betti table has the following form, with nonnegative integers a,b,c,d,e:

    F₀ = S²;
    F₁ = S(−2)¹⁰ ⊕ S(−3)^a ⊕ S(−4)^b ⊕ S(−5)^c;
    F₂ = S(−3)^a ⊕ S(−4)^(20+b+d) ⊕ S(−5)^e ⊕ S(−6)^d;
    F₃ = S(−4)^d ⊕ S(−5)^e ⊕ S(−6)^(20+b+d) ⊕ S(−7)^a;
    F₄ = S(−5)^c ⊕ S(−6)^b ⊕ S(−7)^a ⊕ S(−8)¹⁰;
    F₅ = S(−10)².

Here a∈{0,15}, and its representation is specified in Section 4. This is a necessary form, not a realization theorem. In addition b and d are multiples of five, since their internal degrees have nontrivial Heisenberg central character. Other finite-representation constraints are not asserted to be exhausted.

If a=0, then d=c=0. Indeed a degree-four generator of F₃ would have to map into F₂ in smaller degree; the only possible such degree is three, absent when a=0. A zero differential on such a minimal generator contradicts exactness and minimality. Hence d=0. Similarly a degree-five generator of F₄ would have to map into degree four of F₃, now absent, so c=0.

Thus absence of cubic relations implies absence of quintic relations, but possible quartic relations b remain. Even a=b=c=0 does not by this argument exclude equal-degree middle ghost terms e, so the aesthetically pure even-degree resolution cannot be inferred from the Hilbert numerator.

## Evidence boundary

Only audited generation/cohomology, primary finite representation decompositions, a universal polynomial-shadow inequality, and formal minimal-resolution duality are used. No actual HM, source, cofactor, or obstruction matrix was selected or evaluated. No physical/source obstruction conclusion is drawn. The remaining cubic scalar block and possible higher relations require a genuinely additional argument.
