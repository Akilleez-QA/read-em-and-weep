# Characteristic limits of Laurent divergence certificates

## Scope and review status

This note qualifies the characteristic-zero Laurent divergence theorem in positive characteristic; it does not correct that theorem as stated. The [pinned residue-certificate route](https://github.com/Akilleez-QA/read-em-and-weep/tree/4659e4425c6b5ab6b1899e6af93656ac03ff0862/research/hm/null-residue-certificate-route) explicitly assumes characteristic zero. Its single-resonance and per-factor multiplicity arguments remain valid under that assumption.

**This positive-characteristic qualification has NOT been independently reviewed.** Reviews of the characteristic-zero dependency do not constitute review of this note.

Everything here concerns ordinary coordinate derivatives of a complete finite Laurent scalar H. It assumes, rather than proves, any source identification, trace orientation, geometric specialization, or Laurentness needed by an application. No source-specific value or geometric obstruction is established.

## 1. Exact criterion over an arbitrary field

Let L=k[x_1^{+-1},...,x_n^{+-1}] and H=sum_a c_a x^a have finite support. Put r=(-1,...,-1). If char(k)=ell>0, define

  R_ell={a in Z^n : ell divides a_i+1 for every i}.

Then the following are equivalent:

1. c_a=0 for every a in R_ell.
2. H=sum_i partial_i G_i with G_i in L.
3. H=sum_i partial_i G_i with G_i in k(x_1,...,x_n).

For characteristic zero replace R_ell by {r}.

Proof of 1=>2: for every nonzero c_a choose i for which a_i+1 is nonzero in k and put c_a x^(a+e_i)/(a_i+1) in G_i. All sums are finite. 2=>3 is immediate.

For 3=>1 embed the rational field in K=k((x_1))...((x_n)) in this fixed order. All coordinate derivatives extend coefficientwise and agree with rational differentiation. For any fixed multi-index a, successive coefficient extraction defines [x^a]:K->k. Directly,

  [x^a] partial_i G = (a_i+1)[x^(a+e_i)]G.

This follows coefficientwise, even for negative exponents; extraction in other variables commutes with the derivative. For a in R_ell the multiplier is zero for every i. Thus every such coefficient of a rational divergence vanishes. On a Laurent left side these are its usual coefficients, independent of expansion order. Extra poles of G cannot evade the functionals.

Consequently L/(sum_i partial_i L) is, as a k-vector space, freely spanned by x^a for a in R_ell. As a module over k[x_1^{+-ell},...,x_n^{+-ell}] it is free of rank one, represented by x^r. No perfectness hypothesis on k is needed. The characteristic-zero quotient has just its one usual residue class.

In particular, zero ordinary residue in characteristic ell is necessary but generally insufficient. The monomial x_1^(ell-1) product_{j>1}x_j^-1 has ordinary residue zero and no rational divergence certificate, while over Q it is partial_1(x_1^ell product_{j>1}x_j^-1/ell).

## 2. Exact bad-prime set and safe support bounds

For a!=r define g(a)=gcd(|a_1+1|,...,|a_n+1|)>0, with zeros ignored. Then a is an additional resonance in characteristic ell exactly when ell divides g(a). This gives the sharp coefficient-independent forbidden-prime set for a finite permitted support S:

  B(S)=union_{a in S, a!=r}{prime divisors of g(a)}.

If ell is outside B(S), the characteristic-ell divergence criterion on S is exactly vanishing of the ordinary residue. For a particular reduced H, an exponent in this set only obstructs if its coefficient survives reduction; the coefficient-aware condition is still section 1.

A simple sufficient bound is ell > max_{a in S,i}|a_i+1|. If S lies in the box L_i<=a_i<=U_i, it suffices that ell exceeds max_i(max(|L_i+1|,|U_i+1|)). Bounds must apply to the complete weighted scalar H, not merely an unweighted numerator or individual summand. A smaller sufficient bound is max_{a!=r} min_{i:a_i!=-1}|a_i+1|, with empty maxima zero. The gcd criterion is sharper still: a=(1,2) has coordinate multipliers (2,3) and no bad prime despite both nonunit single-coordinate choices at some primes. Support width alone is insufficient: a singleton at a=(ell-1,...,ell-1) has width zero and is resonant.

A useful integral refinement: for one monomial c*x^a, let integers u_i satisfy sum_i u_i(a_i+1)=g(a). Then

  c*x^a = sum_i partial_i((c*u_i/g(a))*x^(a+e_i)).

This Bezout primitive uses only primes dividing g(a), in addition to coefficient denominators; it avoids spurious bad primes caused by committing to one coordinate. Over Z_(ell), the monomial has a Laurent primitive exactly when c belongs to the ideal generated by the integers a_i+1. Equivalently v_ell(c)>=v_ell(g(a)); c must be ell-integral to state this criterion. This follows by comparing the coefficient x^a on both sides; sufficiency is Bezout. Different monomials do not interact in that equation. Reduction can therefore lose an integral lifting obstruction: ell*x^(ell^2-1) is zero mod ell but its primitive x^(ell^2)/ell is not ell-integral.

## 3. Denominator-one and logarithmic certificates

Let R=k[x_1,...,x_n], D!=0, and H=P/D in L, with P polynomial (P may already incorporate a test weight). Write D=x^d C, no coordinate variable dividing C. Unique factorization and localization imply P=C T for a polynomial T: every irreducible factor of C remains a nonunit after inverting coordinates. This argument is valid in every characteristic.

The exact certificate equation is

  P D = sum_i(D partial_i A_i - A_i partial_i D).       (1)

By section 1, (1) has polynomial solutions iff every monomial t_b x^b of T with b_i+1-d_i=0 in k for all i has t_b=0. For a nonresonant b choose i with nonzero multiplier and assign t_b x^b/(b_i+1-d_i) to h_i. Set

  A_i=C x_i h_i.

Then L_D(A):=div A-A(D)/D obeys

  L_D(A)=C sum_i(x_i partial_i+1-d_i)h_i=P.

This proves completeness of denominator exponent one and of this explicit baseline in any characteristic, with the MODIFIED full resonance criterion. It also proves the same bounds as the characteristic-zero packet:

  deg A_i <= deg P+1,
  deg_j A_i <= deg_j P+delta_ij.

For P=0 use zero witnesses. Degree additivity for nonzero polynomials holds over every field. A modular failure at these complete bounds proves a modular resonant coefficient is nonzero; it does not by itself say that coefficient is the ordinary residue.

Full logarithmic tangentness M_D={A:A(D) in (D)} is necessary for any solution of (1) in every characteristic, since A(D)=D(div A-P). It is the PER-FACTOR description that needs repair. If D=c product q_a^m_a with distinct irreducible q_a, then in characteristic ell:

  M_D = intersection_{a: ell does not divide m_a}{A:A(q_a) in (q_a)}.

Proof: A(D)/D=sum m_a A(q_a)/q_a; terms with ell|m_a vanish. Localizing at an active prime q_a forces its numerator to be divisible by q_a, and the converse follows by the product rule. Inactive factors impose no condition. No smoothness or perfectness assumption is needed.

Example: D=x^ell, A_1=1 gives A(D)=0, so A is tangent to D, but A(x)=1 is not divisible by x. Insisting on tangentness to every reduced factor may discard valid members of M_D. The unfactored identity always remains exact. For cross-characteristic transfer of a factored parametrization, one must also recheck factorization after reduction: factors can collide and multiplicities can change. The complete C x_i baseline bypasses that factor-module issue under Laurentness.

## 4. What a modular test can establish about Q

Choose an explicit coefficient ring B=Z[1/M] containing all coefficients used in the input identities, and all claimed reductions. Require ell not dividing M. Require D modulo ell to be nonzero. To transfer a specific C,T factorization and Laurent support, establish P=C T and the Laurent equality over B (enlarge M if needed); a rational equality over Q alone is not a license to reduce coefficients with ell in their denominators. Integer denominator clearing must not silently multiply by a scalar that becomes zero mod ell.

For an authenticated H in B[x_i^{+-1}]:

- A nonzero ordinary residue mod ell proves the rational ordinary residue is nonzero. No no-extra-resonance bound is needed for this direct assertion.
- A complete modular certificate failure outside B(S) proves its ordinary residue mod ell is nonzero, hence the rational ordinary residue is nonzero. This uses the complete bounds or equivalent full-image proof, good reduction, and actual support inclusion.
- At an extra-resonance prime, failure may coexist with zero rational residue and a rational certificate; the example in section 1 exhibits exactly this.
- Modular success proves only residue zero modulo ell, not rational zero. H=ell*x^r reduces to zero but has rational residue ell. Finitely many primes cannot prove rational zero without an independent coefficient/height bound or an exact characteristic-zero identity.
- A rational witness can be reduced only if its coefficients are ell-integral and its rational denominators remain nonzero. The input can have good reduction while a chosen witness does not: the primitive divided by ell in section 1 is an example.
- A reconstructed rational certificate must be verified by the exact polynomial identity over Q. A failed sparse tangent family is inconclusive even at a safe prime; full degree bounds on A do not automatically make a chosen generator/multiplier parametrization complete.

No geometric reduction theorem is implied by these formal algebraic statements. In particular they do not identify a modular physical pairing with a characteristic-zero geometric obstruction without the existing separate comparison premises.
