# Exact local ideals and lengths on the two exceptional nonic jet lines

## Theorem and scope

Work in the universal two-dimensional pure-V nonic gate, with the normalized
quadratic lift basis R1,R2 of PR1031. The two exceptional jet lines are
ell5=span(R1+2R2) and ell3=span(R1−2R2), as established by the placement
packet. For any nonzero member of either line, its component ideal has the
following exact local form on the projective plane of directions:

- On ell5, it is m^3 at each of the six order-five axes, and m at each of
  the ten order-three axes. Its base scheme has length46.
- On ell3, it is the fixed length-four ideal (u^2,v^2) at each of the six
  order-five axes, and m^2 at each of the ten order-three axes. Its base
  scheme has length54.

Here m is the maximal ideal of the indicated smooth surface point. The
normal form (u^2,v^2) is in the local coordinates supplied by the prescribed
scheme Z; the assertion is equality with that prescribed ideal, not a new
choice of actual axis coordinates. The base support is exactly the sixteen
prescribed axes in either case. These are universal conditional statements
on distinguished lines of the gate, not a selection or identification of
an actual source coefficient. No original obstruction vanishing follows.

## 2. Whole-orbit polynomial setup

Let a=p x^2+q xy+t y^2. Over K=Q(sqrt(5)), form

    B = Pi times multiplication by a^2 from Sym^2 to Sym^6,
    L = Pi times multiplication by a from Sym^4 to Sym^6.

Thus B is4 by3 and L is4 by5. Let N be the signed3 by3 cofactor row of B,
so NB=0. Its four entries are homogeneous of degree6. The normalized
nonic component rows corresponding to the two quadratic lifts are

    F1=N L R1,     F2=N L R2.

This is the same determinant realization of the torsion-free target
I_Sigma(6) and of its subsheaf J used in the placement theorem. In particular,
these are actual component rows of the universal nonic gate, not merely
some rows with the same generic ranks. Multiplication by a nonzero constant
does not change their component ideals.

Set p=1 but retain q,t as independent indeterminates throughout. This is
an entire affine chart, not a chosen direction. Let I5 be the ideal generated
by Pi(a^3), and let I3 be generated by the maximal minors of L. Their exact
reduced lexicographic Groebner bases, with q>t and z=sqrt(5), are:

    I5:
      q^2+5t^3+t+2z,
      q t^2−q,
      t^4−(2z/5)t^3+(2z/5)t−1;

    I3:
      q^2+(27/2)t^5+(27z/10)t^4+6t^3−3zt^2−(11/2)t−57z/10,
      q t^4−q,
      t^6+(2z/3)t^5+t^4−t^2−(2z/3)t−1.

The quotient lengths are6 and10. By the pinned reduced-orbit identification,
these are the entire relevant geometric axis orbits, with no missing point
at p=0. Consequently, after extension to an algebraic closure and
localization at any indicated axis, I5 or I3 becomes its maximal ideal m.
Ordinary powers therefore localize to the corresponding m powers.

## 3. Two bounded exact certificates

Write parentheses around a row for the ideal generated by its components,
and in this section read each row after p=1. The checker proves the exact
polynomial ideal identities in K[q,t]

    (F1+2F2)+I5^4 = I5^3,                         (1)
    (F1−2F2)+I3^3 = I3^2.                         (2)

In detail it constructs the reduced Groebner bases of I5^3,I5^4 and
I3^2,I3^3 using products of generators. It checks every component of the
appropriate nonic row belongs to the right-hand power. It then reduces
those components modulo the higher power, adjoins those remainders to its
basis, and computes the reduced basis of the combined ideal. That basis
is EXACTLY equal to the reduced basis of the lower power. Thus (1),(2)
are ideal equalities, not merely matching dimensions or numerical ranks.

The finite dimensions provide transparent cross-checks:

    dim K[q,t]/I5^4 =60,   dim K[q,t]/I5^3 =36;
    dim K[q,t]/I3^3 =60,   dim K[q,t]/I3^2 =30.

Only quotients of dimension60 are used for the new certificates. There
is no search over coefficient values and no unbounded global nonic
base-locus computation. All arithmetic is exact over K.

## 4. Local Nakayama upgrade

For either identity localize at a point of its orbit and extend the field
if needed. Write R for the regular local surface ring, m its maximal
ideal, J for the ideal generated by the corresponding nonic row, and
r=3 for (1), r=2 for (2). The identities give

    J subset m^r,      J+m^(r+1)=m^r.

The finitely generated R-module M=m^r/J therefore satisfies

    M=m M.

Nakayama's lemma gives M=0. Thus J=m^r exactly. This determines the
entire local ideal, including all higher-order terms, rather than only
its initial homogeneous piece. The lengths are

    length(R/m^3)=6,       length(R/m^2)=3,

because a smooth surface has two-dimensional cotangent space and the
monomials of total degree less than r form a basis of R/m^r.

At the other orbit, the two exceptional lines are distinct, so PR1030
already identifies the component ideal with the prescribed one: m at
the ten order-three axes for ell5, and the length-four ideal of Z at the
six order-five axes for ell3.

## 5. Total lengths and support

PR1035 excludes all additional off-conic support on either exceptional
line. Their distinctness from the conic-base line excludes conic support.
The fixed scheme Z supplies all sixteen axes, so the support is exactly
those axes. Summing the now exact local lengths gives

    ell5: 6*6+10*1 =46;
    ell3: 6*4+10*3 =54.

This closes the local-length gap left by the lower bounds and support
theorem. It does not determine which, if any, distinguished gate line
an actual source occupies.
