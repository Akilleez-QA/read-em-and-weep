Status: independently mathematically reviewed (PASS); see REVIEW.md. Not formally checked. Work over C. No selected HM/source coefficient, point, matrix, or rank is evaluated. The argument uses the universal relative Pfaffian resolution and natural cohomology maps. It concerns the primary S15 normalization defect only, not the separate Gamma-conductor/value-map problem.

0→O_S→nu_*O_X→T→0.

The sheaf T is supported on thirty distinct nodes, with one-dimensional fiber at each. Let rho_n:H0(X,nH)→H0(T(n)) and r_n=rank(rho_n). The center of H5 acts with primitive character on H, so any image of rho_n has dimension divisible by five when 5 does not divide n.

Barth–Hulek–Moore, “Shioda's Modular Surface S(5) and the Horrocks–Mumford Bundle,” §3.4, printed pp.51–52, defines the smooth surface S25 in Y=P4×P1 by

(lambda f+(x)+mu f−(x)) wedge (lambda f+(x)+mu f−(x))=0.

Here f+ and f− are linear bivectors, and the equation is precisely the five submaximal Pfaffians of a 5×5 alternating matrix whose entries are sections of O_Y(1,1). ## 2. Relative resolution and the actual conductor surjection

Let p:Y→P4 be the first projection and J=I_{X/Y}. The codimension-three Pfaffian resolution is

0→A→B→C→J→0,

where A=O_Y(-5,-5), B=O_Y(-3,-3)^5 and C=O_Y(-2,-2)^5. This is the standard Buchsbaum–Eisenbud Pfaffian complex: its grade-three hypothesis holds since the defining Pfaffian subscheme is the smooth codimension-three X. All twists follow from the fact that the alternating matrix has bidegree (1,1).

Set K=coker(A→B), so that

0→A→B→K→0,
0→K→C→J→0.                                            (1)

The projection p has cohomological dimension one. Therefore the second sequence gives a surjection

R1 p_* C → R1 p_* J →0.                                (2)

On the other hand, applying p_* to 0→J→O_Y→O_X→0 gives

0→I_S→O_P4→nu_*O_X→R1 p_*J→0,

since R1 p_*O_Y=0. The image of O_P4 in nu_*O_X is O_S. Consequently the identification

R1 p_*J=T                                               (3)

is the actual normalization quotient, not merely a sheaf of the same length. Finally,

R1 p_*C=H1(P1,O(-2)) tensor O_P4(-2)^5=O_P4(-2)^5.

Combining (2) and (3) produces a sheaf surjection

O_P4(-2)^5 → T →0.                                     (4)

In particular T(2) is globally generated by these five distinguished cohomological sections. This does not assert a splitting of nu_*O_X or of its normalization sequence.

## 3. Those five sections are actual quadratic normalization differences

It is essential to check that the sections in (4) belong to im(rho_2). This is not automatic from a sheaf presentation in general.

Twist (1) by O_Y(2,0). The bundles A(2,0)=O_Y(-3,-5) and B(2,0)=O_Y(-1,-3)^5 have zero cohomology in every degree, since O_P4(-3) and O_P4(-1) do. Hence K(2,0) is acyclic. The second sequence of (1) now gives a natural isomorphism

H1(Y,C(2,0)) ≅ H1(Y,J(2,0)).                            (5)

The left side is H0(P4,O)^5 tensor H1(P1,O(-2)), of dimension five. Also 0→J(2,0)→O_Y(2,0)→O_X(2H)→0 gives a surjection

H0(X,2H) → H1(Y,J(2,0)),                               (6)

with kernel the ambient quadratic sections, because H1(Y,O_Y(2,0))=0.

The natural Leray edge map

H1(Y,J(2,0)) → H0(P4,R1 p_*J(2,0))=H0(T(2))            (7)

composed with (6) is exactly rho_2, by naturality of the connecting maps in 0→J→O_Y→O_X→0. Likewise naturality for C→J identifies (7), under (5), with the map on global sections induced by (4) after twist two. Thus the five generating sections of T(2) in (4) are precisely im(rho_2).

Since (4) is surjective, projection of im(rho_2) onto EVERY one-dimensional node fiber is nonzero. In particular r_2>0. Equation (5) gives r_2≤5, and Heisenberg divisibility gives 5|r_2. Therefore

r_2=5.                                                  (8)

This proves both the exact rank and the all-coordinate nonvanishing. Equivalently, the five-dimensional nonambient quadratic quotient maps injectively to H0(T(2)). Since h0(X,2H)=20 and ambient quadratics inject, h0(O_S(2))=15 and h1(O_S(2))=25. Also H1(P4,I_S(2))=0.

## 4. The full image module is generated in degree two

The same argument supplies a useful stronger statement, without asserting all sections of T arise in degree two. For every n≥0, the Kunneth formula and H1,H2,H3(P4,O(d))=0 give

H2(Y,A(n,0))=H3(Y,A(n,0))=H2(Y,B(n,0))=0.

Thus H2(Y,K(n,0))=0, and the second sequence of (1) yields a surjection

H1(Y,C(n,0))→H1(Y,J(n,0)).

For n≥0, H1(Y,O_Y(n,0))=0, so the latter group is the quotient of H0(X,nH) by ambient sections. The maps commute with multiplication by ambient polynomials. Since

H1(Y,C(n,0))=H0(P4,O(n−2)) tensor C5,

the nonambient normalization section module, and hence the image of its conductor differences, is generated in degree two. More precisely, for n≥2,

im(rho_n)=H0(P4,O(n−2)) · im(rho_2).                    (9)

This is an equality of images in H0(T(n)); it is not a claim that T's complete graded section module is generated in degree two.

## 5. Combined theorem: full conductor rank from degree six

The strict-growth proof of PR578 only needs nonzero projection to every node fiber. Section 3 establishes this already in degree two. Consequently its strict-growth lemma applies for every n≥2. Together with (8) and Heisenberg divisibility this gives, independently of any new interpolation result,

r2=5, r3≥10, r4≥15, r5≥16, r6≥20, r7≥25, r8=30.

Thus H1(O_S(n))=0 for n≥8 and O_S is 9-regular, using the existing normalization cohomology vanishing.

That proof uses PR578, not the present quadratic-generation theorem, so there is no circular dependence.

**Theorem.** The actual primary normalization differences satisfy

r2=5, r3≥10, r4≥15, r5≥25, and r_n=30 for every n≥6.

In particular H1(S,O_S(n))=0 for every n≥6, and O_S is 7-regular on P4.

**Proof.** By Section 3, each coordinate functional of rho_2 is nonzero. Finite hyperplane avoidance over C therefore supplies a section s∈H0(X,2H) with rho_2(s) nonzero at all thirty nodes. This is an existence argument, not a selected-coefficient search. Since the normalization quotient is O_S-linear, for every ambient polynomial p of degree d one has

rho_(d+2)((nu* p)s)=(p|Z) rho_2(s).                       (10)

Multiplication by this nowhere-zero section of the rank-one O_Z-module T(2) identifies

H0(Z,O_Z(d)) ≅ H0(T(d+2)).

For d=4, the pinned quartic-interpolation theorem makes ambient polynomial restriction onto the left side surjective. Equation (10) proves r6=30. For d=3, ambient polynomial restriction onto the twenty-five chirp summands is surjective, so r5≥25. Multiplying by any ambient linear form avoiding the finite set Z preserves full rank; hence r_n=30 for every n≥6. The stated degree-three and degree-four bounds are the independent strict-growth consequences above.

The normalization long exact sequence and H1(X,nH)=0 for n≥1 give H1(S,O_S(n))=coker(rho_n), proving the claimed vanishing. The other required regularity group is H2(S,O_S(5)). Since T is zero-dimensional and nu finite,

H2(S,O_S(5))=H2(X,5H)=H0(X,K_X−5H)*.

The fiber class F is nef, K_X=3F, H=I+2F and I.F=5, so

(K_X−5H).F=−25.

An effective divisor cannot have negative intersection with a nef divisor. Thus H0(X,K_X−5H)=0 and H2(S,O_S(5))=0. Together with H1(S,O_S(6))=0 and the vanishing of cohomology above the two-dimensional support, this proves that O_S is 7-regular.

Consequently complete-section multiplication H0(O_S(n)) tensor H0(P4,O(1))→H0(O_S(n+1)) is surjective for n≥7. This is not a claim of ambient polynomial surjectivity onto those complete section spaces.

## 6. Boundary

The nonambient quotient H0(X,nH)/im H0(P4,O(n)) and its conductor image im(rho_n) are distinct objects: the latter is a quotient of the former. Their equality in dimension is proved here only in degree two, not assumed in later degrees. In particular no exact r3, r4, or r5 is claimed. Their normalizer multiplicity representations have not been identified merely from dimension. The argument instead proves the exact quadratic rank and an actual global-generation theorem using the relative defining equations, their grade-three resolution, and natural pushforward maps. It neither substitutes a generic surface nor enters the separate Gamma-conductor calculation, and implies no original-target lift or algebraicity result.
