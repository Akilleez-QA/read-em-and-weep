# Universal exclusion of determinant degree two

## Result

Let J be an irreducible twenty-dimensional representation of the finite group acting on P^4. Let F be a nonzero proper equivariant saturated subsheaf of J tensor O. Suppose det(F)=O(-2). Then no such F exists.

Consequently every k=2 branch in the existing generic-rank table is impossible, not only the rank-ten branch. Combining this with the independently audited rank-five restriction in ../rank-five-degree-two-obstruction/PROOF.md excludes generic rank five. The generic local lengths 11 and 12 are therefore impossible; the remaining necessary length set is {5,6,7,8,9,10,16}.

## Proof

Put R=(J tensor O)/F, n=rank(F), and m=rank(R)=20-n. Saturation makes R torsion-free and F reflexive. The sheaf R is globally generated, while F* is reflexive and generated by J* outside codimension two. Their first Chern classes are both 2.

Irreducibility gives H0(F)=0: a nonzero invariant subspace of J would be all of J and would force F to contain all constant sections, contradicting its proper rank. Therefore J injects into H0(R), so h0(R)>=20. Likewise the natural map J* -> H0(F*) is nonzero, and irreducibility of J* makes it injective. Thus h0(F*)>=20.

Apply the positivity and reflexive zero-c2 splitting arguments from ../rank-nine-defect-two-sided/PROOF.md. They give c2(F*)>0 and c2(R)>0. For clarity, equality on the F* side gives F=O^(n-1) direct-sum O(-2); if n>1, its constants contradict irreducibility, while if n=1 the twenty independent sections of F*=O(2) are already impossible. On the R side, passing to R** gives R*=O^(m-1) direct-sum O(-2); if m>1 its constants contradict irreducibility, while if m=1 the injection of R into its double dual O(2) contradicts h0(R)>=20. Thus the strict positivity claim includes both rank-one edge cases.

Let M be either R or F*, of rank s. Choose s-1 general global sections, using generation outside codimension two. The established section lemma gives
   0 -> O^(s-1) -> M -> I_Z(2) -> 0,
with Z of codimension at least two and [Z]_2=c2(M)>0. In particular Z contains a reduced irreducible surface Y.

Any projective surface Y in P^4 imposes at least six independent conditions on quadrics. Indeed, choose three linear forms defining a dominant linear projection Y --> P^2. No nonzero polynomial in those three forms vanishes on Y, since its image is dense in P^2. Their six quadratic monomials therefore remain linearly independent upon restriction to Y. Consequently
   h0(I_Z(2)) <= h0(I_Y(2)) <= 15-6=9.

Since H1(O)=0, the section sequence gives
   20 <= h0(M)=s-1+h0(I_Z(2)) <= s+8.
Hence s>=12. Applying this once to F* and once to R forces n>=12 and m>=12, contradicting n+m=20. This excludes det(F)=O(-2).

## Evidence boundary

The argument uses no operation evaluation, no actual matrix or rank instance, and no invariant-polynomial calculation. The rank-five consequence imports the separately audited elimination of residual degree two in ../rank-five-degree-two-obstruction/PROOF.md and AUDIT.md.

## Corollary: rank five and lengths eleven and twelve are excluded

The audited theorem in ../rank-five-degree-two-obstruction/PROOF.md (see its AUDIT.md) proves that generic rank five would force (k,d,e)=(2,0,7), excluding both degree-two residual-kernel branches. The universal determinant-degree-two exclusion above contradicts k=2. Therefore generic rank five cannot occur under the established operation hypotheses.

By the rank-conditioned Hilbert-function table in ../rank-one-even-sharpened-spectrum/PROOF.md, lengths 11 and 12 can occur only at generic rank five. They are therefore excluded for the actual operation. The remaining necessary possibilities are ranks {4,6,7,8,9,10} and lengths {5,6,7,8,9,10,16}. This does not determine the actual generic rank or prove realizability of any remaining length for this operation.
