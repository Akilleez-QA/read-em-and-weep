# Full local length-ten exclusion and global lower bound 330

Under the established actual-rank-eight hypotheses, B is a stable equivariant rank-two reflexive sheaf on P4 with equivariantly trivial determinant and isolated singularities. Its nonzero finite defect T=Ext²(B,O) and saturation defect D=F/G have identical support and local lengths.

The square-zero and non-square-zero theorems exhaust local length ten at every canonical point. The first combines numerical equality, necessary representation self-duality, and an incompatible Koszul representation identity. The second uses strict Betti bounds and exact filtered normal forms; it does not assume that all modules with three radical layers are graded. Thus local length ten is impossible, in addition to the earlier exclusions of lengths one through nine.

Every supported canonical point has local length at least eleven. The canonical orbit therefore contributes at least 30·11=330. Previously audited noncanonical orbit results show that any supported noncanonical orbit contributes at least 600: the contributions for sizes 250,300,375,500 are at least 750,600,750,1000 respectively, and every other orbit has at least 600 points.

Consequently

    length(T)=length(D) >= 330.

For total length below 600, support is exactly the canonical thirty-point orbit, with common local length m∈{11,12,13,14,15,16,17,18,19}. The only necessary totals in that range are

    330, 360, 390, 420, 450, 480, 510, 540, 570.

If length(T)=5q, then q>=66, and 0<q<120 forces q∈{66,72,78,84,90,96,102,108,114}.

This excludes the previous total300 possibility. It does not realize any remaining value, bound the total from above, select the actual generic rank, or settle a retained physical obstruction value. The rank-eight branch itself remains unresolved.

The abstract length-ten wedge module is still a valid counterexample to the blanket strict numerical inequality for arbitrary equivariant finite modules. Its exclusion as a defect, and the complete length-ten exclusion, use additional realizability and full-stabilizer information. These statements are compatible.
