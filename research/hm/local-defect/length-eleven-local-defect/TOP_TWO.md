# Exclusion of the two final length-eleven families

Retain the hypotheses and conventions of PROOF.md and TOP_THREE.md. The only cases remaining there have finite-dual Hilbert functions (2,4,5) and (2,4,4,1). We exclude both. In either case top T is clock-trivial of dimension two and the first radical layer U is one V. Under full H, T is either a sum of two characters (both B-trivial), or an irreducible two-dimensional representation with B-weights (0,b),(0,−b), b≠0. Central characters and extension linearizations do not change the following weight obstructions.

## 1. The three-layer case (2,4,5)

The bottom S=m²P has L-type V plus a character, so its one-dimensional clock-trivial summand is an H-character and therefore B-trivial. If T is a sum of H-characters, the surjection Sym²W⊗T→S cannot generate that summand: the only zero-clock B-weights of Sym²W are (0,1),(0,−1), neither trivial. Thus T must be irreducible of dimension two.

Then W⊗T has two distinct four-dimensional orbits O_(3+b), O_(3−b). Write U=O_c, where c equals one of 3±b. For each parameter x_i, only the unique top line of weight (0,(c−3)i²) maps nontrivially to U; its coefficient is nonzero. The initial linear relations are exactly x_i applied to the other top line. Consequently the quotient by the submodule generated by these four linear relations is the direct sum of two polynomial modules, one over each opposite variable pair {x_i,x_(−i)}, with all other parameters killing that summand.

Its degree-two monomials consist of four squares, whose weights form O_d with d=(3+c)/4=2+4c, and two opposite mixed monomials, with weights (0,±(c+3)). The character summand of S can be generated only if c+3=0, so c=2, b=±1, and d=0. Therefore S has weights O0 plus one trivial B-weight. W⊗T has slopes 2 and 4, disjoint from these bottom weights. Hence in invariant vector-space splittings all top-to-bottom correction terms vanish. With m³P=0 this makes P graded; this assertion follows from the explicit weight disjointness rather than from its number of layers.

There are four independent minimal linear relations. Modulo their generated submodule, the quadratic domain has dimension six and S has dimension five. Its four pure squares map isomorphically to the four distinct weights of O0, and its two mixed monomials map to the bottom character. Thus there is one additional minimal quadratic relation, supported only on those mixed monomials.

There are also at least four independent minimal cubic relations. For each of the four active parameter/top combinations take x_i³ times its active top vector. It is a relation because m³P=0. In the quotient by the linear relations, the coefficient projection onto each such pure cubic vanishes on all parameter multiples of the quadratic relation, since that relation is supported on mixed monomials. These four projections show that the four pure cubic classes are independent modulo mK, where K is the presentation kernel. Therefore

    β1(P)≥4+1+4=9,
    β1(P)−2β0(P)≥5>2.

This excludes (2,4,5).

## 2. The four-layer case (2,4,4,1)

We work entirely in the associated graded module, so filtered corrections cannot affect the argument. Its terminal one-dimensional layer is an H-character and hence has trivial B-weight.

If T is a sum of H-characters, the terminal layer is a quotient of Sym³W⊗T. But Sym³W has no trivial B-weight. To verify this exhaustively, a degree-three monomial of clock weight zero has indices i,j,k∈F5× with i+j+k=0. Three distinct nonzero indices cannot sum to zero, since all four sum to zero and the missing one is nonzero. Three equal indices cannot sum to zero either. Thus the multiset is {i,i,−2i}. Its second B-coordinate is 3(i²+i²+4i²)=18i²=3i²≠0. No monomial has both coordinates zero. This rules out the character-only top.

If T is irreducible of dimension two, the four independent initial linear relations again kill the wrong top line for each x_i, leaving a quotient of two polynomial modules over opposite variable pairs, as in Section 1. A degree-three monomial in a pair {x_i,x_(−i)} has first coordinate among 3i,i,−i,−3i, all nonzero in F5. Top weights have first coordinate zero, so this quotient has no degree-three clock-trivial constituent at all. Any further quadratic or higher relations only take a quotient; they cannot create one. The terminal character is therefore impossible.

This excludes (2,4,4,1) without requiring a graded normal form of P.

## Combined conclusion

PROOF.md, TOP_THREE.md, and this file exhaust and exclude all length-eleven canonical defects under the stated hypotheses. In conjunction with the already audited local exclusions through ten, every supported canonical point has local length at least twelve. The existing noncanonical-orbit lower bounds then give total defect length at least 360; below 600 only the canonical orbit remains, with common local length in {12,13,14,15,16,17,18,19}. This numerical consequence depends on independent acceptance of all three local proofs and the previously audited orbit bounds; it asserts no realization or actual-rank selection.
