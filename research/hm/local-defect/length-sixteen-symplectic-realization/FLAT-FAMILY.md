# A flat algebraic parameter family of the abstract realizations

## Statement and algebraicity boundary

Put B=C[lambda,lambda^{-1},(lambda^4-1)^{-1}] and S_B=B[x1,x2,x3,x4]. The epsilon=-1 construction defines a finitely generated graded S_B-module M_B, flat over B, whose fiber at each lambda=c is the algebraic rank-two reflexive realization M_c. Its projective homogenization defines a B-flat coherent sheaf on P4_B with every geometric fiber the stable isolated-singularity cone of GLOBAL-CONE.md.

The localizations/completions at the respective vertices recover the formal local examples. The pairwise nonisomorphism result for fixed H-action on the polynomial/local ring persists. This is an algebraic flat family of the explicitly constructed abstract examples, not algebraization of an HM/source deformation, not an all-order obstruction theorem, and not a fine or universal moduli space.

## 1. The candidate module is free over the parameter ring

Form the same ten linear relations over B, and let P_B=coker L. The candidate normal-form argument works over B itself: its only scalar divisions are by lambda and lambda^4-1, both units by definition. It gives four independent top generators, six degree-one normal forms, the four quadratic F_j, and two cubic G_j with G_-j=G_j for epsilon=-1. The normal-form maps in degrees two and three annihilate the relations over B, proving independence over B, not merely after specialization. Degree four and higher vanish identically.

In degree one, the four monomial relations, the four two-term relations with unit leading coefficient, and the two opposite-index relations with unit coefficients eliminate ten distinct pivots among sixteen monomials. Their disjoint B-weight blocks and the displayed relations give six free normal forms. Thus P_B is free of rank sixteen as a B-module, with graded ranks (4,6,4,2). In particular tensoring its presentation with any B-algebra gives the corresponding candidate presentation; at each allowed closed parameter its finite-length description is unchanged.

## 2. Flatness of the kernel modules

Let A_B=S_B tensor T, E_B=S_B tensor V, I_B=im L and K_B=ker L. The two exact sequences

    0 -> I_B -> A_B -> P_B -> 0,
    0 -> K_B -> E_B -> I_B -> 0

show that I_B and K_B are B-flat: in each sequence the middle and quotient are B-flat. Consequently both sequences remain exact after arbitrary base change. Thus K_B tensor k(c) is exactly the fiber kernel ker L_c, rather than an unverified submodule of it.

The alternating J_B is invertible over B, with determinant lambda^2 a unit, and L J_B L^t=0 identically. Define b_B=J_B L^t and M_B=K_B/im b_B. The map b_B is injective: over the fraction field of S_B, L has rank four, for example because one allowed fiber has rank four and a nonzero minor is therefore a nonzero polynomial. Thus L^t has full column rank over this field, and the free source of b_B has no torsion.

## 3. Flatness of the middle cohomology

For any closed point lambda=c of Spec B, base change of the preceding kernel sequences identifies b_B tensor k(c) with b_c:A_c* -> K_c. The latter is injective by the local construction: L_c has finite-length cokernel and J_c is invertible. From

    0 -> A_B* --b_B--> K_B -> M_B -> 0

and B-flatness of the first two terms, the Tor exact sequence therefore gives

    Tor_1^B(M_B,k(c))=0

at every closed point.

The ring B is a localization of the principal ideal domain C[lambda], so it is a principal ideal domain. Every nonzero prime is generated by lambda-c for an allowed c. The displayed Tor vanishing says M_B has no element killed by any such prime generator. Any nonzero B-torsion element would yield one killed by a prime generator by factoring its annihilator. Therefore M_B is B-torsion-free, and hence B-flat. This argument does not require M_B to be finite as a B-module; torsion-free modules over a PID are flat.

Flatness also shows that the quotient sequence commutes with arbitrary base change. In particular every closed fiber is exactly K_c/im b_c, with no extra Tor term. The same argument applies at the generic parameter, where the normal forms and invertibility remain valid. Since B is a complex curve, these are all its geometric fiber types after field extension.

## 4. Projective family and interpretation

Adjoin a degree-one variable y, extend the graded module M_B, shift by one and sheafify on P4_B. Polynomial extension preserves B-flatness. Standard affine Proj charts are degree-zero parts of graded localizations; localization preserves B-flatness, and the degree-zero part is a direct summand as a B-module. Hence the resulting coherent sheaf is B-flat. Base change on these charts identifies its fibers with the centered cone sheaves constructed in GLOBAL-CONE.md.

Every geometric fiber is stable, reflexive, symplectic of rank two, has a unique singular vertex and the stated local defects. Its Chern classes are c1=c3=0, c2=4 and c4=-86. These statements follow fiberwise from the previously proved monad argument. They do not assert that the total family is a vector bundle at the vertex section, and do not replace the actual HM requirements c2=5 and full projective-normalizer equivariance.

No selected physical/HM instance was evaluated. Flatness is proved from the universal normal forms, exact sequences and elementary PID algebra, rather than inferred from a numerical sample.
