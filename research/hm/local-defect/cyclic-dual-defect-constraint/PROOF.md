# Cyclic finite duals force complete intersections and canonical length at least twenty-two

## Theorem

Let R be a regular local complex algebra of dimension four, with residue field C. Let M be reflexive of rank two, free on the punctured spectrum, and self-dual. Put Q=Ext²_R(M,R), D(N)=Ext⁴_R(N,R), and P=D(Q). Suppose Q is nonzero and P is cyclic.

Then P is R/I for an m-primary ideal generated by a regular sequence of four elements. In particular Q is cyclic as well. If a finite group H acts and self-duality is equivariant, there is a character χ such that

    P ≅ (R/I)⊗χ,
    V=(I/mI)⊗χ ≅ V*.

At the canonical stabilizer and cotangent representation specified below, necessarily

    length(Q)=length(P) ≥ 22.

This excludes ALL canonical cyclic-finite-dual defects of lengths 1 through 21. In particular it excludes length sixteen without an equality-in-multiplicity theorem. It is a necessary condition only, and does not assert realization at twenty-two or improve the unrestricted global defect bound.

## 1. Cyclicity forces a complete intersection

The rank inequality in ../representation-defect-bound/PROOF.md gives

    β₁(P)−2β₀(P) ≤ 2.

Since β₀(P)=1, write P=R/I nonequivariantly, with I m-primary. Then β₁(P)=μ(I)≤4. Krull's height bound gives μ(I)≥height(I)=4. Thus μ(I)=4. Since R is Cohen–Macaulay, these four parameters form a regular sequence. The Koszul resolution gives D(R/I)≅R/I nonequivariantly, hence Q=D(P) is cyclic too.

Equivariantly, averaging lifts the one-dimensional generator quotient P/mP to a character line inside P. Its generator induces P≅(R/I)⊗χ, with H-stable I. The relation representation is V=(I/mI)⊗χ. Here d=β₁(P)−2β₀(P)=2. The same rank inequality says 2≥2+a_H(V), so a_H(V)=0 and V≅V*. Equivalently one can use the equality-case Tor isomorphism in ../wedge-defect-equivariant-obstruction/PROOF.md. No determinant character is added to this identity; D is Ext⁴(-,R).

For completeness, if L=I/mI, an equivariant Koszul resolution identifies Q with (R/I)⊗(det L)*⊗χ⁻¹. Thus both cyclicity statements retain any character twists.

References for the standard commutative algebra: regular local rings are Cohen–Macaulay by Stacks Lemma 10.106.3, https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00NN; a system of parameters in a Cohen–Macaulay local ring is regular by Lemma 10.104.2, https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00N7.

## 2. The canonical linear and quadratic representations

Use the established abstract stabilizer

    H=μ₅×(B⋊C₄), B≅C₅²,
    gZg⁻¹=Z³, gDg⁻¹=D⁴.

Every H-character is trivial on B. The cotangent representation W=m/m² has four distinct B-weights

    (i,3i²), i∈F₅×,

permuted transitively by C₄. Therefore W is irreducible. The B-weights of W* are (i,2i²), disjoint from those of W.

If I has a nonzero linear image in W, irreducibility makes that image all W. Nakayama then gives I=m. In that case V=W⊗χ is not self-dual on B, contradicting §1. Hence I⊂m².

The ten distinct B-weights of Sym²W form three C₄-orbits:

    S₄={(a,4a²): a≠0}, dimension four (the squares);
    S₀={(a,0): a≠0}, dimension four (nonopposite mixed products);
    T={(0,1),(0,4)}, dimension two (opposite products).

The orbit S₄ is not self-dual: −S₄={(a,a²):a≠0}, disjoint from all ten displayed weights. Both S₀ and T are stable under negation.

Let J₂=(I+m³)/m³⊂Sym²W and let r=dim J₂. Since mI⊂m³, this is an H-quotient of L=I/mI, which has dimension four. If S₄ occurred in J₂, it would exhaust L|B and contradict the self-duality of L⊗χ, since χ|B=1. An H-stable subspace of Sym²W is a sum of whole listed weight orbits: distinct B-weights have one-dimensional weight spaces and C₄ permutes each orbit transitively. It follows that precisely the following possibilities need be considered:

    r=0;
    r=2 with J₂ the opposite-product subspace;
    r=4 with J₂ the nonopposite mixed-product subspace.

There is no assumption here that all four minimal relations start in degree two.

## 3. Elementary filtered length estimates

Let h_j=dim_C m^j(R/I)/m^{j+1}(R/I). Then h₀=1, h₁=4, h₂=10−r. The associated graded ring of R is Sym W (Stacks Lemma 10.106.1, https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00NN).

Choose four minimal generators f₁,…,f₄ of I so that the first r have independent quadratic initial forms spanning J₂ and the other 4−r lie in m³. Every element of I∩m³ is a sum Σa_i f_i. Its quadratic term vanishes only if the constant coefficients of a₁,…,a_r vanish. Consequently its cubic initial form belongs to the span of WJ₂ and the cubic initial forms of f_{r+1},…,f₄. In particular

    dim(initial ideal in degree 3) ≤ 4r+(4−r),
    h₃ ≥ 20−4r−(4−r)=16−3r.

This counts all cubic initial forms, including possible cancellation, rather than merely the ideal generated by the leading forms.

If r=0, the sum through degree three is at least

    1+4+10+16=31.

If r=2, it is at least

    1+4+8+10=23.

If r=4, use the exact mixed-product subspace instead of the coarse estimate. Write the variables as x₁,x₂,x₃,x₄ indexed by F₅×. The nonopposite pairs are

    x₁x₂, x₁x₃, x₄x₂, x₄x₃.

They generate the product ideal (x₁,x₄)(x₂,x₃) in the polynomial associated graded ring. Its degree-three quotient has dimension eight: the four degree-three monomials in x₁,x₄ and the four in x₂,x₃. Because all four generators have independent quadratic terms, the preceding cancellation argument shows that the degree-three initial ideal is EXACTLY WJ₂. Higher terms of the f_i cannot add cubic initial relations. Hence

    h₀+h₁+h₂+h₃=1+4+6+8=19.

All later h_j are nonnegative. This proves length(R/I)≥19 in every case, and finite duality preserves length.

## 4. The simpler multiplicity bound, independently sourced

Even before the refined quadratic analysis, I⊂m² and the complete-intersection property give length(R/I)≥16. Here is the precise short multiplicity argument, not needed for the stronger theorem.

For any m-primary ideal J, e_J(R) is 4! times the leading coefficient of length(R/J^n). Because I^n⊂m^{2n}, one has length(R/I^n)≥length(R/m^{2n}), hence e_I(R)≥16. The Koszul multiplicity formula gives e_I(R)=length(R/I), since the four generators form a regular sequence. The definition and formula are respectively Stacks Definition 43.15.1 and Theorem 43.15.5, https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0AZU. Also length(R/m^{2n})=binomial(2n+3,4) by the polynomial associated graded ring. No multiplicity equality criterion is used anywhere.

## 5. Conditional cycle consequence and scope

If the canonical orbit E of thirty points occurs in the support of an equivariant defect sheaf, and its finite dual stalk at one (equivalently every) point of E is cyclic, then its contribution to the total length is at least

    30·22=660 (using the socle refinement in §6).

This is conditional on cyclicity. Noncyclic finite duals are not excluded, and no unrestricted lower bound of 660 is asserted. Even if the total defect has other orbits, the E contribution alone satisfies this bound.

## 6. Gorenstein socle refinement: the bound improves to twenty-two

The lower bound nineteen above can be strengthened without additional representation calculations:

    length(Q) ≥ 22.

Consequently a canonical cyclic finite dual is excluded at every length 1 through 21, and its contribution on the thirty-point orbit is at least 660. This refinement is included in the headline theorem and §5; all scope qualifications remain unchanged.

To prove this, put A=R/I. Its socle Soc(A)={a∈A:ma=0} has dimension one. One elementary verification uses the parameter Koszul resolution of k over R: its top homology after tensoring with A is Soc(A)⊗det(W), whereas the complete-intersection Koszul resolution of A gives β₄(A)=1. Thus dim Soc(A)=1, without invoking a separate Gorenstein theorem.

Cases r=0 and r=2 already have lengths at least 31 and 23. For r=4, §3 proves EXACTLY h₀,h₁,h₂,h₃=(1,4,6,8). Set t=dim_C m⁴A. Then dim_C m³A=8+t, and length(A)=19+t. Choose four regular parameters x₁,…,x₄ of R. Multiplication defines a C-linear map

    m³A → (m⁴A)^4,   a ↦ (x₁a,x₂a,x₃a,x₄a).

Its kernel is m³A∩Soc(A), of dimension at most one. Consequently

    8+t−1 ≤ 4t,
    t ≥ ceiling(7/3)=3.

It follows that length(A)≥22. This argument allows arbitrary higher terms in the four relations and arbitrary later Hilbert pieces. It needs neither symmetry of the Hilbert function nor a homogeneous complete intersection.
