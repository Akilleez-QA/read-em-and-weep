# Flat boundary at the fourth roots of unity

## Statement and scope

Let B=C[λ,λ^{-1}], S=B[x1,x2,x3,x4], and let indices be nonzero residues modulo five. Let A=S^4 have degree-zero basis t_j and let E=S(-1)^10 have basis m_i,l_i,c1,c2. Define L:E→A by

- L(m_i)=x_i t_{3i};
- L(l_i)=x_i t_i−λx_{−i}t_{3i};
- L(c1)=x1t4−x4t1, L(c2)=x2t3−x3t2.

Let J be the alternating matrix with J[m_i,l_{3i}]=1, J[l_{3i},m_i]=−1, J[c1,c2]=−λ, J[c2,c1]=λ, all other entries zero. The grading-correct map b=JL^t has source S(-2)^4. Put P=coker L, I=im L, K=ker L, and N=K/im b.

Then N is B-flat. For c≠0 with c^4≠1, its fiber is the known rank-two reflexive module of length-sixteen defect. For c^4=1, write K_c^true=ker L_c and M_c=K_c^true/im b_c. There is an exact sequence

    0 → N⊗_B C_c → M_c → Q_c → 0,

where Q_c has length one and is concentrated in internal degree two. The true fiber M_c is reflexive of depth two; the flat fiber N⊗C_c is torsion-free of depth one, and its reflexive hull is M_c.

The centered projective cones give a flat family of stable torsion-free rank-two sheaves on P^4 over C*. At c^4=1 the flat fiber is a length-one elementary modification of a stable reflexive sheaf with c2=4 and c4=−92. The flat fiber retains c4=−86. This is only a partial flat completion of the abstract family, not a proper compactification, a family at λ=0 or ∞, an actual Horrocks–Mumford/source instance, or a higher-order realization claim.

Dependencies: the displayed L,J formulas and the elementary finite-defect symplectic-monad argument in ../length-sixteen-symplectic-realization/PROOF.md; the centered cone convention in GLOBAL-CONE.md there. The boundary normal forms and base-change correction are proved below independently.

## 1. Integral normal forms, without inverting λ^4−1

Write f=λ^4−1. On t_j, x_{2j} vanishes. Name the remaining quadratic monomials

    A_j=x_j²t_j, B_j=x_jx_{3j}t_j, C_j=x_jx_{−j}t_j,
    D_j=x_{3j}²t_j, E_j=x_{3j}x_{−j}t_j, F_j=x_{−j}²t_j.

The defining relations imply precisely the quadratic reductions

    A_j=E_j=0,
    B_j=λB_{3j},
    C_j=F_{−j},
    D_j=λ^{-1}F_{3j}.

For completeness, these are a complete presentation of degree two, not merely necessary identities. Define a map from the free quadratic monomials to the module with these displayed generators and relations; send forbidden monomials containing x_{2j} on t_j to zero. Multiples of m_i map to zero. The four multipliers x_i,x_{2i},x_{3i},x_{−i} of l_i respectively give 0=0, 0=0, B_i=λB_{3i}, C_i=λD_{3i}. The clock relation x_{−j}t_j=x_jt_{−j}, with multipliers x_j,x_{−j},x_{2j},x_{3j}, respectively gives C_j=F_{−j}, F_j=C_{−j}, 0=0, 0=0. Thus the map annihilates every degree-two relation; conversely every displayed reduction is a consequence of these relations. It induces mutually inverse normal-form and generator maps.

The four B_j form a cyclic module B/(f). Taking b0=B_1, one may write

    B_1=b0, B_2=λb0, B_4=λ²b0, B_3=λ³b0, f b0=0.

Consequently P_2=B^4⊕B/(f), with the free summand generated by F_j. The ten independent degree-one relations have unit pivots (their monomial supports separate into four monomial, four two-term, and two clock blocks), so P_0=B^4 and P_1=B^6.

All variables annihilate B_j, including over B itself:

- x_j B_j=x_{3j} A_j=0;
- x_{2j} B_j=0 by the forbidden variable;
- x_{−j} B_j=x_j E_j=0;
- x_{3j} B_j=x_j D_j=λ^{-1}x_jF_{3j}=0, because j=2(3j) modulo five.

For F_j the identities x_{2j}F_j=x_{3j}F_j=x_{−j}F_j=0 remain valid. The first uses the forbidden variable, the second E_j, and the third follows from F_j=λD_{2j}=λx_j²t_{2j}, since x_{−j} kills t_{2j}. Put G_j=x_jF_j. Multiplying C_j=F_{−j} by x_{−j} gives G_j=G_{−j}.

No new relation on G_j arises at f=0. Indeed define a cubic normal-form map to B^2, with symbols g_j=g_{−j}, by

    x_jx_{−j}²t_j ↦ g_j,
    x_{3j}³t_j ↦ λ^{-1}g_{3j},

and all other cubic monomials ↦0. It kills all multiples of forbidden monomials, A_j,E_j and B_j−λB_{3j}. For C_j−F_{−j}, only the multiplier x_{−j} is nonzero and gives g_j−g_{−j}=0. For D_j−λ^{-1}F_{3j}, only x_{3j} is nonzero and gives the displayed value. As the quadratic presentation has already been shown complete, this checks every cubic ambiguity and proves P_3=B^2. Finally every variable kills G_j: three kill F_j and the remaining x_j gives a multiple of A_j. Hence P_d=0 for d≥4.

Altogether, as a graded B-module,

    P = B^4[degree 0] ⊕ B^6[degree 1]
        ⊕ (B^4⊕B/(f))[degree 2] ⊕ B^2[degree 3].

In particular the generic fibers have Hilbert function (4,6,4,2); at every fourth root c the true cokernel P_c has Hilbert function (4,6,5,2), length seventeen. All fibers are supported at the origin. This calculation uses only the unit λ, not f^{-1}.

## 2. Equivariant character of the boundary correction

Use the established action g:x_i↦x_{2i}, t_i↦t_{2i}. The normal subgroup C5² has weights wt(x_i)=(i,3i²), wt(t_j)=(j,0). Every B_j has weight zero. Since g b0=B_2=λb0 modulo f, at λ=c the extra quadratic socle has g-character c. If the central μ5 acts on variables by ω and on t_j by ω^{-1}, its character on this line is ω, not automatically trivial.

The same character occurs on Tor_1^B(P,C_c). This can be seen from the equivariant exact sequence multiplying by λ−c on P: Tor_1 identifies with the kernel of λ−c, supported on the c-component of B/(f), and g acts there by c. Equivalently use the Chinese remainder decomposition of B/(f) into its four supported characters. One must not use a B-free equivariant rank-one resolution whose g-action is multiplication by λ: that would violate g^4=1 away from f=0.

Thus Q_c is the origin skyscraper with internal degree two, C5²-trivial character, C4-character c, and central character ω when that convention is used. A centered twist changes internal grading by one, as usual; the projective quotient remains length one.

## 3. Isotropic complex and its true fibers

Direct multiplication gives LJL^t=0 identically over B and det J=λ². Thus J is invertible everywhere on Spec B. On every fiber c≠0, P_c is finite-length, so L_c is surjective off the origin and has rank four over the fraction field. Hence b_c=J_cL_c^t is injective. The same reasoning over the fraction field of S gives injectivity of b over S.

For each c, the usual symplectic quotient of ker L_c by im b_c is rank two and locally free away from the origin. At the local origin ring R_c=C[x]_(x), the exact sequences

    0→I_c^true→A_c→P_c→0,
    0→K_c^true→E_c→I_c^true→0,
    0→R_c^4→K_c^true→M_c→0

give depths 1,2,2 respectively. In the last step one can either use the depth lemma plus its reverse inequality or the Ext calculation below. Torsion in M_c would be supported only at the origin, whereas depth at least two forbids any nonzero finite-length submodule. Thus M_c is torsion-free and satisfies S2, and is reflexive over the regular normal ring. These arguments apply unchanged when c^4=1.

## 4. Exact base-change correction and flatness

All uses of flatness here are over B, not over S. Since B is a PID, any torsion-free B-module is flat. I and K are submodules of the B-free modules A and E, so they are B-flat. The exact sequence 0→K→E→I→0 therefore remains exact after arbitrary base change.

On the other hand 0→I→A→P→0 gives on a fiber

    0→Tor_1^B(P,C_c)→I⊗C_c→A_c→P_c→0.

Factoring L_c through E_c→I⊗C_c therefore produces an exact sequence

    0→K⊗C_c→K_c^true→Tor_1^B(P,C_c)→0.

Its last map is surjective because E_c→I⊗C_c is surjective. At c^4=1 the last term is Q_c; away from these points it vanishes.

The map b_c is injective into E_c and its image lies in K⊗C_c because b already factors through K. Thus base change of 0→S(-2)^4→K→N→0 has an injective left map on every fiber. This proves Tor_1^B(N,C_c)=0 for every c≠0. Equivalently N has no (λ−c)-torsion for any irreducible of B; since every B-torsion element has a primary component for one such irreducible, N is B-torsion-free and hence B-flat.

Quotienting the previous inclusion of kernels by their common image im b_c proves

    0→N_c→M_c→Q_c→0.

In particular it is not correct to identify N_c with the middle cohomology of the specialized complex at c^4=1. That failure of cohomology base change is exactly the length-one quotient just computed.

## 5. Hull, depth, and the additional Ext jump

At a boundary point write R=R_c, k=R/(x), Q=Q_c. Since N_c⊂M_c and M_c is torsion-free, N_c is torsion-free. The quotient is supported at the origin. Taking duals in 0→N_c→M_c→Q→0, using Hom(Q,R)=Ext^1(Q,R)=0, gives N_c^*=M_c^*. Hence N_c^{**}=M_c.

The local cohomology sequence gives H_m^0(N_c)=0 and H_m^1(N_c)=Q: indeed H_m^0(M_c)=H_m^1(M_c)=0 and H_m^0(Q)=Q. Thus N_c has depth exactly one and is not reflexive.

There is a useful further homological distinction. Put D(U)=Ext_R^4(U,R) for finite-length U. The symplectic-monad dual sequences yield

    Ext_R^1(M_c,R)≅P_c,
    Ext_R^2(M_c,R)≅D(P_c),
    Ext_R^i(M_c,R)=0 for i≥3.

The first two identifications use the same dual-sequence calculation as for the non-boundary fibers; its only necessary input is finite length of P_c and invertibility of J. Applying Hom(-,R) to 0→N_c→M_c→Q→0 and using Ext_R^i(Q,R)=0 for i<4 gives

    Ext_R^1(N_c,R)≅P_c,
    Ext_R^2(N_c,R)≅D(P_c),
    Ext_R^3(N_c,R)≅D(Q).

These are ungraded local identifications; graded versions carry the twists of the chosen centered or uncentered convention. Consequently both Ext^1 and Ext^2 of the flat boundary fiber have length seventeen, and a new length-one Ext^3 appears. Auslander–Buchsbaum gives projective dimension three for N_c, versus two for M_c. This explains why flatness does not preserve either the length-sixteen defect or reflexivity at the boundary.

## 6. Projective cones and numerical invariants

Adjoin a variable y and sheafify after the centered shift by one on P^4_B. Flatness is preserved: polynomial extension is B-flat, graded localization remains B-flat, and degree-zero localization pieces are direct summands. Thus the resulting sheaf family F is B-flat. Fiberwise the complex is

    O(-1)^4 → O^10 → O(1)^4.

The true middle cohomology F_c^true is reflexive and singular only at v=[1:0:0:0:0], since the finite graded cokernel is supported at that vertex. At the four boundary points there is an exact sequence

    0→F_c→F_c^true→Q_{v,c}→0,

where Q_{v,c} is a length-one skyscraper. The completed stalk at v has the local description above. Away from v the two sheaves agree.

For each true fiber of length ℓ, in K-theory

    [F_c^true]=10[O]−4[O(-1)]−4[O(1)]+ℓ[k_v].

Thus ch(F_c^true)=2−4H²+(ℓ−1/3)H⁴. Since c1=c3=0 and c2=4, comparison with ch4=(2c2²−4c4)/24 gives

    c4=10−6ℓ.

At ℓ=17 this is −92, while subtracting one skyscraper gives the flat fiber ch4=47/3 and c4=−86. The Euler characteristics are respectively 7 and 6.

The ten columns of L remain linearly independent as degree-one vector-valued forms for every λ≠0. The monad sequences and H^1(P^4,O(d))=0 imply H^0(F_c^true(-d))=0 for d≥0. A saturated rank-one subsheaf of a reflexive sheaf on P^4 is reflexive and hence O(d); the vanishing excludes d≥0. Thus F_c^true is slope stable. Every rank-one subsheaf of F_c is also a subsheaf of F_c^true, and their slopes agree because the quotient is zero-dimensional. Therefore F_c is slope stable too. Stability here is slope stability with respect to O(1).
