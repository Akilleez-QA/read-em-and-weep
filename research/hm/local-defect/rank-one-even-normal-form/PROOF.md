# Even normal form and quadratic generic thickness

Work over C with the canonical cubic f:V^3→J, dim V=5, dim J=20. Import outer reversal, cyclic zero and repeated-input injection from ../rank-one-normal-obstruction/PROOF.md. Import from ../rank-one-quadratic-flag-nef/PROOF.md that the rank-one cone D is a component, its generic local algebra A has residue field K=C(D), embedding dimension four, maximal ideal m with m^5=0, and its quadratic obstruction ideal is geometrically primary to the origin.

## 1. Symmetric coordinates remove all odd residual terms

Choose a linear functional ℓ nonzero on the common line and normalize the two outer inputs by their ℓ-values relative to the middle input u. Write

    x=s(u+v+p), y=u, z=t(u+v−p),
    v,p∈H=ker ℓ, ℓ(u)≠0, s,t≠0.

These are invertible algebraic coordinates on this ambient open. The rank-one stratum is v=p=0. Put A_u(w)=f(u,u,w) and F_u(w)=f(w,u,w). Outer reversal and f(u,u,u)=0 give the exact equation

    f(x,y,z)/(st)=2A_u(v)+F_u(v)−F_u(p).

Take a local left inverse L of A_u and a complementary target projection π, so LA_u=1 and πA_u=0. The equations split as

    2v+LF_u(v)−LF_u(p)=0,
    πF_u(v)−πF_u(p)=0.

The first equation has invertible derivative 2I in v along the zero section. In the completed local ring it has a unique solution v=v(p). It is even in p, by uniqueness and F_u(−p)=F_u(p). With P(p)=LF_u(p) and q(p)=πF_u(p), homogeneous expansion gives

    v(p)=P(p)/2−P(P(p))/8+terms of order at least six,
    q(v(p))−q(p)=−q(p)+q(P(p))/4+terms of order at least six.

These are identities of universal formal expressions. No actual source value, matrix, polynomial instance or selected jet is evaluated. In particular there are no odd-degree residual equations in these coordinates; the cubic terms in asymmetric elimination coordinates are removable coordinate effects.

## 2. The associated graded algebra is exactly quadratic

At the generic point of D, the base coordinates embed K into A: after localizing, every nonzero rational function on D is a unit. Since A is Artinian it is complete. Let S=K[p1,p2,p3,p4], n=(p1,p2,p3,p4), and let W⊂S2 be the span of the components of q. The known m^5=0 and the preceding expansion give the exact finite presentation

    A ≅ S/(n^5, q_i−q_i(P(p))/4 for all components i).

Let ρ:W→S4 be q↦q(P(p))/4. This is a well-defined K-linear map: a constant linear relation among the q_i is an identity of quadratic polynomials and remains an identity after substitution P(p).

In S/n^5 the ideal generated by these equations is, as a filtered vector space, the direct sum

    {q−ρ(q):q∈W} + S1 W + S2 W.

Indeed multiplying q−ρ(q) by positive-degree polynomials discards ρ(q) modulo n^5, and only degrees one and two can contribute. The three summands have distinct least degrees two, three and four; the graph summand has zero degree-two term only when q=0, in which case ρ(q)=0 as well. Its initial ideal is therefore (W,n^5).

Since (W) is n-primary, four K-linear combinations in W form a regular sequence of quadrics. Their complete intersection has Hilbert series (1+z)^4 and contains n^5. Hence n^5⊂(W), and

    gr_m A ≅ S/(W).

This is equality, improving the previous normal-cone inclusion at the generic point. Thus the exact generic multiplicity is the length of the homogeneous quadratic algebra S/(W); higher residual terms cannot change it. No exact numerical rank or length is asserted.

## Assumptions and scope

The statement is generic along the rank-one component and uses the previously proved generic emptiness of the projective quadratic zero set. At exceptional rank-one points m^5 need not vanish and the truncation argument does not apply. Parity of the formal residual equations remains valid there, but cannot alone imply equality of normal cones. The result concerns the higher-order cubic zero scheme; it does not decide the retained physical obstruction or algebraicity of a solution to the original research goal.

## 3. The generic local algebra is noncanonically quadratic

In fact A is isomorphic as a K-algebra to B=S/(W), not merely equal in associated graded. Write r=dim W, necessarily 4≤r≤10.

If r≥5, choose four regular-sequence quadrics spanning W0⊂W. Their quotient B0 is an Artinian Gorenstein complete intersection with Hilbert function (1,4,6,4,1). An extra independent quadratic gives a nonzero class e∈(B0)2. Perfect multiplication (B0)2×(B0)2→(B0)4 shows that its ideal contains the degree-four socle. Therefore B4=0 and B has no higher degrees. Section 2 implies m^4=0 in A. The residual corrections all have order at least four, so the original coordinates p now satisfy q_i(p)=0 in A. The resulting surjection B→A is an isomorphism because Section 2 gives equal finite lengths.

If r=4, W is a regular sequence. B has Hilbert function (1,4,6,4,1). Consider the K-linear Jacobian map

    (B3)^4 → (B4)^4,
    (d_j) ↦ (sum_j (∂q_i/∂p_j)d_j)_i.

It is surjective. To prove this, identify B4 with K after choosing a socle generator. An element (c_i) of the dual cokernel would satisfy sum_i c_i ∂q_i/∂p_j=0 in B1 for every j, by the perfect pairing B1×B3→B4. There are no degree-one relations in B, so these are identities of linear polynomials. Euler's identity gives sum_i c_i q_i=0. Independence of the four q_i forces all c_i=0.

Thus choose homogeneous cubic polynomials d_j so the quartic corrections ρ(q_i) are congruent to sum_j(∂q_i/∂p_j)d_j modulo the degree-four part of (W). The substitution p↦p+d is an automorphism of S/n^5, with inverse p↦p−d modulo n^5. Since q_i is quadratic,

    q_i(p+d)=q_i(p)+sum_j(∂q_i/∂p_j)d_j modulo n^5.

It removes the quartic corrections from q_i−ρ(q_i), up to combinations of q_j with quadratic coefficients. These remaining combinations are absorbed by an invertible generator-change matrix I+C(p), whose entries C have degree two. Hence the deformed ideal is carried to (W) modulo n^5. Since n^5⊂(W), this proves A≅B.

The isomorphism is noncanonical, and the construction at rank four uses a choice of solution to the Jacobian equation. It does not say the actual cubic operation is formal, nor that its global zero scheme is quadratic. It resolves exactly the generic rank-one local thickness: its entire algebra is determined up to isomorphism by the quadratic normal obstruction.

## 4. Immediate consequences

- Rank r=4 gives exact generic length 16 and Hilbert function (1,4,6,4,1); the generic local algebra is a quadratic complete intersection.
- Rank r≥5 gives m^4=0, improving the earlier m^5=0 bound in this case, and length at most 14 from h2=10−r≤5 and h3≤4.
- The possibilities r=4 and r≥5 exhaust the generic cases, but the actual rank remains unevaluated. No actual exact generic multiplicity is claimed.
