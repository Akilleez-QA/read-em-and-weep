# Two extra quadrics force at most one cubic dimension

## Theorem

Let K be an infinite field of characteristic zero, let S=K[x1,x2,x3,x4], and let W be an r-dimensional subspace of S2 with geometrically empty projective base locus. Put A=S/(W), and write hi=dim A_i. These hypotheses apply to the exact generic local algebra established in ../rank-one-even-normal-form/PROOF.md. Then:

- r=4: Hilbert function (1,4,6,4,1), length 16.
- r=5: Hilbert function (1,4,5,t), 0<=t<=2, length 10 through 12.
- r=6: Hilbert function (1,4,4,t), 0<=t<=1, length 9 or 10.
- r=7: Hilbert function (1,4,3,t), 0<=t<=1, length 8 or 9.
- r=8,9,10: Hilbert functions (1,4,2), (1,4,1), (1,4), respectively, and lengths 7,6,5.

Thus the previously possible pairs (r,length)=(6,11) and (7,10) are impossible. This sharpens the rank-conditioned bounds, although it does not further shrink the unconditioned length set.

In the boundary case r=7 and h3=1, the algebra has a more rigid description: it is the graded fiber product of a three-variable quadratic complete intersection C and K[e]/(e^2), over their common residue field K. Equivalently A=C plus a one-dimensional degree-one socle, with e C_+=0.

No assertion is made about the actual generic rank, or whether any of these possibilities occurs for the operation. The argument is universal algebra; it evaluates no actual source operation, matrix, rank instance, selected input, jet, or residue.

## 1. A perfect-pairing bound

Choose a four-dimensional regular-sequence subspace W0 of W, and put B=S/(W0). Such a choice exists because W is basepoint-free and K is infinite. Then B is an Artinian quadratic complete intersection with Hilbert function (1,4,6,4,1). Set U=W/W0, viewed as a subspace of B2, so dim U=r-4. The algebra A is B/(U).

Use the perfect pairing B1 x B3 -> B4 and choose a nonzero identification B4=K. Define

    L = { l in B1 : l pairs to zero with U B1 in B3 }.

Then dim L=h3(A). Moreover, for each l in L and u in U, the element lu in B3 pairs to zero with every element of B1. Perfectness therefore implies lu=0. Thus

    U is contained in ann_B(L)_2.

The perfect pairing B2 x B2 -> B4 identifies ann_B(L)_2 with the dual of

    B2/(L B1) = (B/(L))2.

Indeed a degree-two element u annihilates L if and only if it pairs to zero with all products l b, for l in L and b in B1.

Write t=dim L and n=4-t. The ring B/(L) is a quotient of a polynomial ring in n variables by homogeneous quadrics. It is Artinian because B is. Consequently those quadrics have empty projective common zero locus and span a space of dimension at least n: an ideal generated by fewer than n elements cannot have height n. Hence

    dim (B/(L))2 <= binom(n+1,2)-n = binom(n,2).

We obtain the numerical constraint

    r-4 <= binom(4-h3(A),2).                  (*)

For r>4, inequality (*) excludes t>=3. If r>=6, it also excludes t=2, since binom(2,2)=1<r-4. Thus h3<=1 when r>=6. If r>=8, it excludes t=1 as well, since binom(3,2)=3<r-4. This reproves the degree-three vanishing at ranks eight through ten without inverse systems.

## 2. Length consequences

For r>=5 an extra nonzero element of B2 generates B4 under multiplication by B2, by the perfect degree-two pairing. Therefore A4=0, and all higher degrees vanish. Also h0=1, h1=4 and h2=10-r. Combining these facts with (*) proves the stated table.

For r=4 there are no additional quadrics, so A=B and its Hilbert function is (1,4,6,4,1).

## 3. Structure of the rank-seven upper boundary

Suppose r=7 and h3=1. Extend K to an algebraic closure for the argument concerning squares below; dimensions and subsequent conclusions descend.

Let H=W^perp be the three-dimensional dual space of quadrics, under the characteristic-zero differential pairing. Let F be a nonzero dual cubic representing the one-dimensional dual of A3. Every first directional derivative of F belongs to H. Write e for the dimension of their span. The cubic F depends essentially on e variables: the kernel of its directional derivative map is precisely the space of directions on which it is constant, and a basis adapted to that kernel eliminates all other variables.

The basepoint-free condition on W is equivalent to H containing no nonzero square of a linear form. If e=1, the derivative span contains a square. If e=2, its projectivization is a line in the projective plane of binary quadrics, which meets the conic of squares over the algebraic closure. Both are impossible. Since e<=dim H=3, it follows that e=3 and the derivative span equals H.

Choose dual coordinates Y1,Y2,Y3,Y4 so F depends only on the first three. Then every quadric in H depends only on these three variables. In primal coordinates x1,x2,x3,x4, put T=K[x1,x2,x3]. The space W therefore contains x4 S1 and decomposes as

    W = (x4 S1) direct sum W',
    W' = W intersect T2,    dim W'=3.

This coordinate construction is available over K itself, since the one-dimensional kernel of the derivative map of F is defined over K. The three quadrics in W' have no geometric projective common zero in P(T1^dual): otherwise that point with fourth coordinate zero would be a basepoint of W. Thus W' is a quadratic regular sequence in T, and C=T/(W') is a quadratic complete intersection with Hilbert function (1,3,3,1).

It follows directly from the displayed decomposition of W that

    A = S/(W) is isomorphic to C fiber-product_K K[e]/(e^2),

where e has degree one. In particular e annihilates every positive-degree element, and A has Hilbert function (1,4,3,1). Conversely this construction, applied to any three-variable quadratic complete intersection, gives a basepoint-free quadratic algebra with r=7 and h3=1. This converse is an abstract family construction, not an evaluation of any operation or selected input.

## Scope

The new essential restriction is h3<=1 at ranks six and seven; the rank-seven extremal case additionally has the explicit universal fiber-product characterization above. No extra global geometric assumptions or computational instances are used. All files are local.

## 4. Abstract sharpness at rank six

The rank-six upper bound is also attained by a universal algebra construction. Let C=T/(Q) be any three-variable quadratic complete intersection, let T=K[x1,x2,x3], and choose any nonzero linear functional on T1. Choose coordinates so its kernel is spanned by x2,x3. Define

    D = T[e]/(Q, e^2, e x2, e x3).

Its relation space has dimension six, so h2(D)=4. The quadrics are basepoint-free: the relation e^2 forces e=0 at every geometric projective zero, and then Q forces the other coordinates to vanish.

The quadrics Q cannot all have zero x1^2 coefficient, since that would give them the projective basepoint [1:0:0]. Thus multiplying an appropriate element of Q by e, and using e x2=e x3=0, gives e x1^2=0 in D. Every cubic involving e therefore vanishes. The degree-three piece of D is exactly C3, of dimension one, and all degree-four pieces vanish. Hence D has Hilbert function (1,4,4,1), length ten and rank six.

This is a symbolic construction valid for arbitrary ternary quadratic complete intersections and arbitrary nonzero linear functionals; it uses no actual operation or specific polynomial instance. Together with Section 3, it shows that both newly proved rank-conditioned upper bounds are optimal among all basepoint-free quadratic algebras in four variables.

## 5. Every listed rank-length pair occurs in abstract quadratic algebra

The following purely symbolic examples are unrelated to the original operation. They prove that the full table in the theorem is exact for arbitrary basepoint-free quadratic algebras in four variables. Put

    B = K[x,y,z,w]/(x^2,y^2,z^2,w^2).

All quotients considered retain the four square relations and are therefore basepoint-free. The squarefree monomials form a basis for B, and the perfect socle pairing is multiplication followed by extraction of the coefficient of xyzw.

At rank five add a single quadric q. The map q:B1 -> B3, expressed using the socle pairing to identify B3 with the dual of B1, has a symmetric matrix with zero diagonal. Its off-diagonal entry indexed by two distinct variables is the coefficient in q of the complementary pair of variables. Consequently:

- q=xy gives a single nonzero edge on z,w, so this matrix has rank two, and the quotient has length twelve.
- q=xy+xz+xw gives the adjacency matrix of a triangle on y,z,w and an isolated x. Its three-by-three nonzero block has determinant two, so the matrix has rank three, and the quotient has length eleven.
- q=xy+zw gives two disjoint nonzero edges. Its matrix is invertible, so the quotient has length ten.

Here the length formula is 14-rank(q:B1 -> B3), proved in ../rank-one-even-length-spectrum/PROOF.md. The determinant-two argument uses characteristic zero, as assumed.

At rank six, add two squarefree monomials:

- Adding xy and xz kills exactly the cubic basis elements xyz, xyw, xzw, leaving yzw. This gives length ten.
- Adding xy and zw kills all four squarefree cubic basis elements. This gives length nine.

At rank seven, add three squarefree monomials:

- Adding xy,xz,xw leaves precisely yzw in degree three and gives length nine.
- Adding xy,xz,yz kills all four squarefree cubic basis elements and gives length eight.

At ranks eight, nine and ten, add respectively any four, five or six distinct squarefree quadratic monomials. At most two quadratic monomials survive, so no squarefree cubic survives: a surviving cubic would require all three of its quadratic divisors to survive. The resulting lengths are seven, six and five.

At rank four, B itself has length sixteen. Thus every rank-length pair in the theorem is realized. In particular the unconditioned set

    {5,6,7,8,9,10,11,12,16}

is the exact length spectrum for all basepoint-free quadratic algebras of embedding dimension four. Any further exclusion for the original operation must use additional structure particular to that operation, rather than the basepoint-free quadratic presentation alone.
