# Low-degree polynomial lifts for kernels containing the Veronese line

Let V have dimension five, S=Sym²V, X=P(V), and q denote the universal square, giving the subbundle O(-2)→S⊗O. Put E=(S⊗O)/O(-2). Suppose a homogeneous polynomial κ of degree e with values in Λ^mS satisfies q∧κ=0. No assertion about an actual operation or its values is required.

## Polynomial lifting theorem

There exists a polynomial a of degree e−2 with values in Λ^(m−1)S such that κ=q∧a in each of the following ranges:

- m≤4, for every e;
- m=5, e≤3;
- m=6, e≤2.

For e<2 this means κ=0. The conclusion does not require κ to be decomposable. In particular, a degree-two rank-six kernel Plücker vector has the form q∧a with a constant five-vector. Decomposability of that constant five-vector is NOT claimed.

## Proof

Since q is nowhere zero as a map of bundles, q∧κ=0 identifies κ with a section of Λ^p E(t), where p=m−1 and t=e−2. Polynomial lifting is precisely surjectivity on global sections of

Λ^pS⊗O(t) → Λ^p E(t).

Resolve this exterior quotient by the exact complex whose term at homological position j is

C_j=Λ^(p−j)S⊗O(t−2j), 0≤j≤p.

The differentials are wedge multiplication by q. On P⁴, line bundles have no cohomology in degrees one, two, or three. Thus failure of the desired surjectivity can only come from the homology at j=4 of the H⁴ row of this resolution. For p≤3 no such term exists, proving the first assertion.

For p=4, dualizing this H⁴-row homology by Serre duality gives the cokernel of the multiplication map

S*⊗Sym^(3−e)V* → Sym^(5−e)V*,

with negative symmetric powers interpreted as zero. Here S*=Sym²V*. For e≤3 the multiplication map is surjective, proving the second assertion.

For p=5, the dual of the relevant H⁴-row homology is the first homology of

Λ²S*⊗Sym^(3−e)V* → S*⊗Sym^(5−e)V* → Sym^(7−e)V*.

This is the degree-(7−e) component of the first Koszul homology of the fifteen quadratic monomials generating the ideal n² in R=Sym(V*). This homology vanishes in degrees at least five:

1. The first syzygies of n² are generated in total degree three. An elementary verification uses the usual pairwise monomial syzygies. A degree-four least-common-multiple relation between two relatively prime quadratics decomposes through a mixed quadratic formed from one linear factor of each. All other nontrivial pairwise relations already have degree three.
2. Therefore the first Koszul homology is generated in degree three, since it is a quotient of the first syzygy module.
3. Every Koszul homology module is annihilated by the ideal generated by the Koszul generators: multiplication by each generator is chain-homotopic to zero, using wedge with its corresponding basis vector. Thus n² annihilates this homology.
4. Every coefficient of degree at least two belongs to n², so all homogeneous components of total degree at least five vanish.

When e≤2, 7−e≥5, completing the proof.

## Equivariance and its exact limits

For a finite group acting compatibly on V and on the polynomial κ, the lifting map is equivariant. Consequently any lift can be averaged, with the relevant character twist if necessary, to a lift of the same equivariance type. In the rank-six, degree-two case this reduces a possible branch to a constant vector in the appropriate isotypic part of Λ⁵S.

This is a reduction, not an exclusion: neither vanishing of that isotypic part nor decomposability of its vectors is established here. For m≤4 the lift is equally useful in other degrees; for example m=2,e=4 gives κ=q∧a with a quadratic S-valued polynomial, hence a linear endomorphism of S after polarization. An independently proved scalar-endomorphism condition under the symmetry group would then force κ=0.

Only universal bundle resolutions, elementary monomial syzygies, and finite-group averaging enter. No actual source matrix, operation product, input, polynomial instance, rank instance, jet, or residue is evaluated.
