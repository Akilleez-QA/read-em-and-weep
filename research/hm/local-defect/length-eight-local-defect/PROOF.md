# Exclusion of a local defect of length eight

## Theorem and scope

Let R be a regular local complex ring of dimension four, with residue field C, and let M be an equivariant reflexive rank-two R-module, free away from the closed point, with equivariantly trivial determinant. Suppose the stabilizer contains G=C5⋊C4, and W=m/m² is the irreducible four-dimensional representation V whose clock weights are the four nonzero elements of F5, transitively permuted by C4. Then

    length_R Ext²_R(M,R) ≠ 8.

In fact every G-equivariant finite R-module Q of length eight satisfies

    β3(Q) − 2β4(Q) ≥ 4.                         (A)

This contradicts the necessary defect inequality β3(Q)≤2β4(Q)+2. Only the order-twenty subgroup is needed. The proof explicitly treats the new two-V representation; it does not reuse a single-V classification outside its range.

Everything below is universal local algebra and abstract finite-group representation theory. No physical source/model matrix, operation, selected input, actual rank instance, jet, or residue is evaluated.

## 1. Inputs and linear parameters

The isolated self-duality and generator argument in ../length-five-local-defect/PROOF.md, Section 1, give, for Q=Ext²_R(M,R),

    β2(Q)−β4(Q) ≤ 2+β1(Q)−β0(Q).

Here finite-length duality D(Q)=Ext⁴_R(Q,R) reverses the minimal Betti numbers. Since the alternating sum of the Betti numbers of a finite module is zero, this is equivalent to

    β3(Q)−2β4(Q) ≤ 2.                          (1)

We use only this imported necessary condition, not any assumption that direct summands of Q are themselves defects.

Faithfully flat completion preserves lengths and minimal Betti numbers. Averaging gives a G-invariant parameter space W inside the completed maximal ideal, and identifies the completed ring with C[[x1,x2,x3,x4]] with its linear action. Index the parameters by i∈F5× so that x_i has clock weight i.

The irreducibles of G are its four one-dimensional characters, all clock-trivial, and V. Therefore a length-eight module has representation type: entirely clock-trivial; V plus four character dimensions; or 2V. Radical layers are semisimple G-representations, and each multiplication map W⊗L_j→L_(j+1) between successive nonzero layers is surjective.

If mQ=0, then Q≅k^8 and β3−2β4=32−16=16. An entirely clock-trivial module is necessarily of this type, because every parameter has nonzero clock weight. Henceforth assume mQ≠0.

## 2. Exhaustive radical shapes

For type 2V, each nonzero layer has dimension at least four. Nonzero action therefore forces exactly two layers (V,V), and m²Q=0.

For type V plus four character dimensions, two consecutive character-only layers are impossible: W times a clock-trivial representation has no clock-trivial quotient. Thus there are at most three nonzero layers. If there are three, the unique V is in the middle. The top is clock-trivial, so its parameter products cannot generate character constituents in the middle. Consequently the shapes are exactly

    (a,4,b),  a+b=4,  a,b>0,                 (2)

with clock-trivial top and bottom and m³Q=0. Otherwise m²Q=0. These alternatives include arbitrary character multiplicities and residue-field summands.

## 3. All square-zero modules

Write Q=T⊕S as vector spaces, where S=mQ, and parameters send T to S and kill S. Let r_i denote the rank of the Koszul differential

    δ_i:T⊗Λ^iW → S⊗Λ^(i−1)W.

Then

    β3=32−r3−r4,    β4=8−r4,
    β3−2β4=16−r3+r4.                         (3)

The kernel of δ4 is the common parameter annihilator in T, tensored with Λ⁴W. Hence the active quotient of T has dimension r4, and δ3 factors through its tensor product with Λ³W. Thus r3≤4r4. If dim T≤4, then r4≤4, so

    r3−r4≤3r4≤12.

Equation (3) gives β3−2β4≥4. This covers type 2V, and every square-zero case with top dimension at most four.

If dim T>4, type 2V is impossible. The unique V must be in T: if it were in S, then T would consist of at most four character dimensions. Thus S is clock-trivial, and T consists of V plus characters. All character constituents of T are killed by the parameters. The common parameter kernel on V is G-invariant and not all V, because S≠0 is generated by parameter products. Irreducibility makes that kernel zero, so r4=4.

Choose weight vectors v_i in this top V. Only x_(−i) can act nontrivially on v_i. Therefore the four independent tensors

    v_i ⊗ (the wedge of all x_j with j≠−i)

lie in ker δ3. The active source has dimension 16, so r3≤12. Equation (3) now gives β3−2β4≥8. All square-zero cases satisfy (A).

## 4. Exact multiplication structure in the three-layer case

Take (2), and choose G-invariant complements to write

    Q=T⊕V⊕S,  dim T=a, dim S=b,
    mQ=V⊕S,  m²Q=S.

Both T and S are clock-trivial. Weight considerations and the radical filtration imply that multiplication has precisely the form

    x_i t = a_i(t)v_i,        a_i∈T*,
    x_(−i)v_i=b_i,           b_i∈S,
    x_jv_i=0 for j≠−i,       W S=0.             (4)

There are no hidden higher-degree correction terms: W T has nonzero clock weights, hence lies entirely in V, and W V⊂S because V⊂mQ.

Every a_i is nonzero, since the images W T generate V. Every b_i is nonzero: the common kernel of W on V is a G-subrepresentation, not all V since W V=S≠0, and therefore zero. The vectors b_i span S.

Commutativity on T gives the exact tensor identities

    a_i⊗b_i = a_(−i)⊗b_(−i) in T*⊗S.         (5)

Equality of two nonzero rank-one tensors implies their first factors are proportional and their second factors are inversely proportional. There are only two opposite pairs {1,4} and {2,3}. Consequently

    dim span{b_i}≤2,   r:=dim span{a_i}≤2.      (6)

In particular b≤2, excluding (a,b)=(1,3).

Let K=intersection_i ker a_i in T. Formula (4) shows K is the common parameter annihilator in T and is therefore G-invariant. Choose a G-invariant complement T' to K by complete reducibility. Then T'⊕V⊕S is both an R-submodule and a G-subrepresentation, while K is a residue-field summand (with its given G-action). Thus

    Q ≅ k^(a−r) ⊕ N,
    N=T'⊕V⊕S,  dim T'=r.                    (7)

The socle of N is exactly S. Indeed a socle vector's T' component is zero because the a_i separate T', and its V component is zero because the common parameter kernel on V is zero. Therefore β4(N)=b.

## 5. Shape (3,4,1)

Here a=3, b=1, and r∈{1,2}. By (7), N has length 5+r, top dimension r, and socle dimension one. Its finite dual D(N) is cyclic, because its top dimension equals the socle dimension of N. Duality preserves length, m³ annihilation, and nonvanishing of m² action; the last assertion follows directly from the faithful C-linear dual action of any parameter monomial acting nontrivially on N.

As a G-module D(N) still has a single V and clock-trivial remaining constituents, up to an irrelevant one-dimensional duality twist. Its cyclicity and three-layer classification force its Hilbert function to be (1,4,r). Equivalently its first radical layer is V and its final layer has dimension r.

Write D(N)=R/I. Its embedding dimension is four, so I⊂m². Its quadratic relation space has dimension 10−r, and these relations are minimal: mI⊂m³ and I/mI surjects onto (I+m³)/m³. Thus

    β3(N)=β1(D(N))≥10−r,   β4(N)=1.

Using (7) and β3(k)=4, β4(k)=1 gives

    β3(Q)−2β4(Q)
       ≥ (10−r)−2 + 2(3−r)
       =14−3r ≥8.

This establishes (A) for this shape without assuming that N itself is a realizable defect.

## 6. Shape (2,4,2): a split top socle

Here a=b=2. If r=1, (7) gives Q≅k⊕N, where N is cyclic with Hilbert function (1,4,2) and socle dimension two. We recall the direct graded calculation, so that the numerical bound does not depend on realizability of N.

Since m³⊂I⊂m² for N=R/I, its quadratic relations can be taken homogeneous and all cubics are in I. It is the completed graded algebra with Hilbert series 1+4t+2t². Its Hilbert numerator is

    (1+4t+2t²)(1−t)^4
       =1−8t²+12t³−3t⁴−4t⁵+2t⁶.

There are no minimal ideal generators in degrees four or five. The coefficients in those degrees therefore give β_(3,4)≥3 and β_(3,5)≥4, since all other terms of even homological degree have nonnegative coefficients. Hence β3(N)≥7 and β4(N)=2. Additivity yields

    β3(Q)−2β4(Q)≥(4+7)−2(1+2)=5.

Thus (A) holds.

## 7. Shape (2,4,2): the nonsplit case and two complete intersections

It remains that r=2. Equations (5)–(6) say the forms belonging to each opposite pair are proportional. Since they span T*, the forms for the two different pairs are independent. Similarly the bottom vectors for the two pairs are independent, because they span the two-dimensional S.

Choose bases t_A,t_B of T dual to representatives of the two forms, and representatives s_A,s_B of their bottom lines. Then (4) exhibits an actual R-module direct sum

    Q=Q_A⊕Q_B,
    Q_A=span{t_A,v_1,v_4,s_A},
    Q_B=span{t_B,v_2,v_3,s_B}.               (8)

The parameters x_2,x_3 annihilate Q_A, and x_1,x_4 annihilate Q_B. Each summand is cyclic, generated by its t-vector. In Q_A, both x_1² and x_4² annihilate the generator, while x_1x_4 acts nontrivially, by (4) and the nonzero factors in (5). Thus there is a surjection

    R/(x_2,x_3,x_1²,x_4²) → Q_A.

Both sides have length four, so it is an isomorphism. Likewise

    Q_B ≅ R/(x_1,x_4,x_2²,x_3²).

Each displayed ideal is generated by a regular sequence of length four: kill the two distinct parameters first, leaving the squares of the other two parameters in a two-variable power-series ring. Its Koszul free resolution is minimal, with Betti numbers (1,4,6,4,1). Additivity in (8) gives

    β(Q)=(2,8,12,8,2),
    β3(Q)−2β4(Q)=4.

This proves (A) in the final case. The splitting is required only as an underlying R-module; it need not preserve the C4 action, which exchanges the opposite pairs.

## 8. Conclusion and conditional global consequence

Sections 2–7 exhaust every length-eight representation and every radical filtration. They prove the universal bound (A), contradicting the necessary defect bound (1), and hence prove the theorem.

Combining this new exclusion with the separately audited exclusions of local lengths one through seven and the unique canonical thirty-point orbit below the orbit-size threshold 250, a nonzero finite defect on the stipulated actual-rank-eight branch satisfies

    length(T) ≥250.

Indeed a defect supported on the canonical orbit has local length at least nine and therefore contributes at least 270; any noncanonical support orbit contributes at least 250. Thus no positive total defect below 250 survives. In notation length(T)=5q, this says q≥50. The equal-length saturation defect satisfies the same lower bound, under the previously established finite duality.

This is a necessary lower bound, not a rank-eight exclusion, a realization, an upper bound, or a classification at or above total length 250.
