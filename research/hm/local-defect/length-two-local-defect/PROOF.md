# Small local defects at the canonical normalizer stabilizer

## Result and scope

Let R be the local ring of a smooth complex fourfold at a canonical point of the thirty-point Heisenberg eigenline orbit. Let H be its stabilizer in the stipulated order-15000 determinant-one normalizer. Let M be an H-equivariant reflexive R-module of rank two, free off the closed point, with equivariantly trivial determinant. Write Q = Ext²_R(M,R).

**There is no such M with 1 ≤ length(Q) ≤ 4.**

The new length-two argument has two separate parts:

1. Every length-two H-equivariant finite R-module is annihilated by the maximal ideal. In particular a cyclic dual-number module cannot occur.
2. For any isolated self-dual rank-two reflexive module on any regular local fourfold, an Ext² module annihilated by the maximal ideal has length at most one.

The same representation argument proves annihilation for every length at most four. The previously audited length-one character obstruction then excludes the final remaining possibility.

Consequently, under the hypotheses of the existing conditional rank-eight branch, the finite defect length is at least 150; if it is below 250, its only remaining necessary values are 150, 180, 210, and 240. This is neither an existence theorem nor a claim that the actual rank-eight branch occurs or is excluded. Nothing is asserted about local length at least five beyond these necessary restrictions.

## 1. Stabilizer and its small representations

At the canonical point p = [e_0], use the universal abstract Schrödinger representation indexed by i in F5. The diagonal operators Z and D act by ζ^i and ζ^(i²/2), respectively, while T permutes indices by i ↦ 2i. Let g = -T, the determinant-one lift of order four. The prior stabilizer proof establishes the projective stabilizer of order 100 generated by Z, D, and T. Its full inverse image H has order 500.

The subgroup B = <Z,D> is C5 × C5, and

    g Z g⁻¹ = Z³,       g D g⁻¹ = D⁴.

The subgroup <B,g> has order 100. It intersects the scalar μ5 trivially: an element with nonzero g-power is not diagonal, while a scalar Z^aD^b forces the polynomial ai + bi²/2 to be constant for all i, and hence a=b=0. Thus

    H = μ5 × (B ⋊ <g>).

This explicitly retains the central linearization characters rather than assuming that representations descend to the projective group.

Here is the complete classification of irreducible complex H-representations of dimensions at most two. Tensor each of the following with an arbitrary character of the scalar μ5:

- Four one-dimensional characters, trivial on B, obtained from the four characters of <g> ≅ C4.
- Four two-dimensional representations. For each of the two pairs {b,-b} in F5×, choose the B-character trivial on Z and taking D to ζ^b. Its stabilizer in C4 is <g²>. Extend this character to B ⋊ <g²> by either of the two choices g² ↦ ±1, then induce to B ⋊ C4.

To verify completeness, restrict an irreducible representation to the abelian normal subgroup B. Its weight spaces form one C4-orbit. A weight with nonzero Z-exponent has orbit length four, since multiplication by 3 has order four on F5×, so the representation has dimension at least four. A weight with zero Z-exponent and nonzero D-exponent has orbit length two, with the stabilizer and extensions just described. The zero weight gives the C4 characters. The scalar μ5 acts by a character in an irreducible representation. This proves the classification without a character table.

In particular, Z acts trivially on every representation of dimension at most two.

## 2. Classification of length-two equivariant R-modules

Set m = m_R and k = R/m = C. For any length-two R-module Q, either mQ=0 or length(mQ)=length(Q/mQ)=1. In the latter case Nakayama gives m²Q=0, and Q is cyclic. Its annihilator quotient is a local length-two algebra isomorphic to k[ε]/(ε²). Thus these are precisely the two nonequivariant alternatives: k² or a cyclic dual-number module supported in a tangent direction.

For the equivariant cyclic alternative, write Q/mQ = k_χ and mQ = k_ψ for H-characters. Multiplication would induce a nonzero H-map

    (m/m²) ⊗ χ → ψ.

Let W = m/m². On W, the subgroup <Z> has the four distinct nontrivial characters: up to simultaneous inversion convention their exponents are 1,2,3,4. In contrast, χ and ψ are trivial on Z by Section 1. The displayed map must therefore be zero, a contradiction. Equivalently, W has no character quotient.

It follows that every equivariant length-two module is k ⊗ U, with m acting by zero and U a two-dimensional H-representation. By complete reducibility and Section 1, U is either a direct sum of two one-dimensional characters (allowing repetitions and different central characters), or one of the listed irreducible two-dimensional representations. Conversely every such U gives an equivariant R-module by the residue map R → k. This is a classification of finite modules, not of modules realizable as Ext² of rank-two reflexive modules.

## 3. Universal semisimple-defect bound

This section does not require a group action. Let R be a regular local complex ring of dimension four, and let M be a reflexive R-module of rank two, free on the punctured spectrum, with M ≅ M*. Suppose

    D = Ext²_R(M,R) ≅ k^n,       n > 0.

The natural isolated-singularity duality used in the existing proof gives

    A = Ext¹_R(M,R) ≅ Ext⁴_R(D,R) ≅ k^n.

Only this nonequivariant identity is needed here. Reflexivity gives projective dimension at most two. Take a minimal free resolution

    0 → F2 → F1 → F0 → M → 0.

The cokernel of F1* → F2* is D. Minimality and Nakayama give rank(F2)=n, and, because D is killed by m, the image is exactly mF2* ≅ mR^n. The latter has 4n minimal generators. Write rank(F1)=4n+u with u≥0.

A surjection from R^(4n+u) onto mR^n is, after changes of basis, the direct sum of a minimal free cover R^(4n) → mR^n and u zero columns. Indeed choose source elements mapping to a basis of (mR^n)/m(mR^n), extend them to a basis of the source, and subtract combinations of these chosen columns from every remaining column. A minimal cover of mR^n is the direct sum of n parameter rows. Therefore, if K0 is the kernel of a parameter row R^4 → m, then

    K := ker(F1* → F2*) ≅ K0^n ⊕ R^u.

The Koszul resolution of the regular parameter sequence gives μ(K0)=6, so

    μ(K) = 6n+u.

Let C be the image of F0* → F1*. The definition of A gives an exact sequence

    0 → C → K → A → 0.

After tensoring with k, right exactness implies that the image of C/mC in K/mK has dimension μ(K)−n. Hence

    μ(C) ≥ μ(K)−n = 5n+u.

On the other hand F0* surjects onto C, while the rank-two Euler characteristic of the free resolution gives

    rank(F0) = rank(F1)−rank(F2)+2 = 3n+u+2.

Combining the inequalities yields

    5n+u ≤ 3n+u+2,       hence n ≤ 1.

This proves the universal bound. In particular, semisimple length-two defects are impossible, regardless of which of the representations in Section 2 they carry. No assertion that the remaining n=1 case exists is needed.

## 4. Why the same exclusion extends through length four

Consider only the subgroup L = <Z,g> ≅ C5 ⋊ C4. Its generator g cyclically permutes the four nontrivial Z-characters. Let Q be an H-equivariant finite R-module with total length at most four. Over C, this length equals dim_C Q.

If a nontrivial Z-weight occurs in Q, all four nontrivial weights occur with the same positive multiplicity. The dimension bound forces length(Q)=4, each such weight to have multiplicity one, and the trivial weight to be absent. This makes Q irreducible as an L-representation: any L-invariant subspace is a sum of Z-weight spaces, and g acts transitively on those four spaces. Since mQ is L-invariant and is a proper subspace by Nakayama, mQ=0.

Otherwise Z acts trivially on Q. Choose Z-equivariant regular parameters lifting W; these exist by averaging a linear section W → m, and generate m by Nakayama. Each has nontrivial Z-weight. Its multiplication map on Q would carry the trivial weight to its own nontrivial weight, which is absent. Thus all these parameters annihilate Q, again proving mQ=0. One may equally pass to the faithfully flat completion before making these parameter choices.

Consequently every equivariant finite module of length at most four is semisimple as an R-module. Section 3 excludes lengths two, three, and four for D = Ext²(M,R). The already audited local character obstruction excludes length one: for a length-one character χ it requires δ³=χ⁻⁴, where δ=det W, whereas g has order four and δ(g)=-1.

## 5. Conditional global consequence

Use only the existing rank-eight hypotheses and established conclusions: the stable rank-two reflexive B has isolated singularities and equivariantly trivial determinant; T=Ext²(B,O) is nonzero and finite; and the unique orbit of size below 250 is the canonical orbit E of size thirty. The saturation defect has the same support and local lengths as T.

If 0<length(T)<250, support is E and every stalk has the same length m with 1≤m≤8. The local argument above forces m≥5. Therefore

    length(T) ∈ {150,180,210,240}   if length(T)<250.

If length(T)≥250, it is already at least 150. Thus length(T), and the equal total saturation-defect length, are at least 150 without assuming an upper bound. In notation length(T)=5q, this gives q≥30 and

    0<q<50  implies  q∈{30,36,42,48}.

These restrictions do not evaluate the actual defect, exclude rank eight, or prove realization of any surviving alternative. For length at least five, nontrivial maximal-ideal action is not ruled out by the small-representation argument, and no realizability conclusion is drawn.
