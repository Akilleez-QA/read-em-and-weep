# Generic rank nine is impossible

## Result and inputs

Under the established equivariance, irreducibility, saturated-image, and determinant hypotheses, generic rank(Q)=9 is impossible.

The inputs from `../rank-nine-kernel-splitting/PROOF.md`, its addendum, `../rank-nine-defect-two-sided/PROOF.md`, and `../heisenberg-defect-hilbert-congruence/PROOF.md` are:

- An equivariant exact sequence 0 -> F -> J tensor O -> R -> 0 on complex P4, with J irreducible of dimension twenty.
- E=F* is reflexive of rank thirteen, generated outside codimension two; R is globally generated and torsion-free of rank seven.
- c1(E)=c1(R)=4, and the ordered pair (c2(E),c2(R)) belongs to {(12,4),(7,9),(2,14)}.

We prove the necessary universal quartic section bounds below, allowing nonreduced schemes and embedded components. No arithmetic Cohen--Macaulay assumption is made.

## 1. Plane lemma

Let D be a zero-dimensional subscheme of P2 of length d>=9. Suppose a subspace W of H0(I_D(4)) has finite base locus. Then

    I_D(1)=I_D(2)=0;
    d=9:  h0(I_D(3))<=2 and h0(I_D(4))<=6;
    d>=10: h0(I_D(3))<=1 and h0(I_D(4))<=5.

Here and below dimensions refer to global sections of the ideal sheaf. Finite base locus means that the quartics in W have no common curve. In particular, for any fixed plane curve G, a sufficiently general member C of W shares no irreducible component with G. This follows by avoiding the finitely many proper linear subspaces consisting of quartics divisible by each component of G; the field is infinite.

If a nonzero conic q vanishes on D, choose C coprime to q. The complete intersection V(q,C) has length eight and contains D, contradiction. This treats reducible and nonreduced conics equally. A linear equation would yield a quadratic equation, so there are no linear equations either.

Consider two independent cubics f,g vanishing on D and let q be their greatest common divisor of degree e, so 0<=e<=2. Write f=qa, g=qb, with coprime a,b of degree 3-e. If e=0, the complete intersection V(f,g) has length nine, so d<=9. If e>0, choose C in W coprime to q and let X=V(f,g,C). Its support is finite: it is contained in the union of V(q,C) and V(a,b). It contains D. The residual exact sequence with respect to q is

    0 -> O_{X:q}(-e) -> O_X -> O_{X intersect V(q)} -> 0.

The ideal quotient defining X:q contains a,b, so its length is at most (3-e)^2. Also X intersect V(q)=V(q,C), a complete intersection of length 4e. Thus

    d <= length(X) <= 4e+(3-e)^2,

which is eight for e=1 and nine for e=2. All these are scheme-theoretic ideal quotients, so they remain valid for repeated factors and nonreduced schemes. It follows that d>=10 permits at most one independent cubic.

For d=9, suppose the entire cubic space V=H0(I_D(3)) has dimension at least three. Let q be the greatest common divisor of all its members, of degree e. A sufficiently general pair of members has greatest common divisor exactly q: divide by q, choose one member, and choose the second outside the finitely many proper subspaces divisible by each irreducible factor of the first. Degree e is at most two since V has dimension at least three.

If e=0, a coprime pair cuts out a length-nine complete intersection containing D. Equality of lengths forces equality of schemes. A complete intersection of two cubics has exactly two independent cubic equations (by its Koszul resolution), contradicting dim V>=3. If e=1, the preceding residual bound gives d<=8. If e=2, V/q is a space of linear forms of dimension at least three, hence the whole three-dimensional linear-form space. Its generated ideal sheaf is O_{P2}. Consequently the ideal sheaf generated by V equals (q). Since all these cubics vanish on D, this implies D is contained scheme-theoretically in V(q), contradicting the no-conic result. Thus dim V<=2.

Finally let H(t) be the Hilbert function of the saturated homogeneous coordinate ring of D. Choose a linear form avoiding all support points. Multiplication by it is injective, and the first differences H(t)-H(t-1) form the Hilbert function of a standard graded Artinian algebra. These differences are nonnegative and cannot become zero and later positive; H eventually equals d. For d=9, the cubic bound gives H(3)>=10-2=8, hence H(4)>=9: otherwise H(3)=H(4)=8 would force the eventual value to be eight. For d>=10, H(3)>=9 similarly implies H(4)>=10. Since dim H0(O_P2(4))=15, the claimed quartic bounds follow.

## 2. Surface lemma without a Cohen--Macaulay hypothesis

Let Z be a closed subscheme of P4 of dimension two and degree d>=9, and suppose its complete quartic system has no fixed divisor. Then

    d=9:   h0(I_Z(4))<=10;
    d>=10: h0(I_Z(4))<=7.

Choose a general flag P2=L contained in P3=H contained in P4. Set C=Z intersect H and D=C intersect L. Choose each hyperplane equation to avoid all associated points of the preceding structure sheaf; these are open conditions. Then both are nonzerodivisor sections. Two successive differences of the Hilbert polynomial show that D is zero-dimensional of length d, regardless of embedded or lower-dimensional components of Z.

The base locus B of H0(I_Z(4)) has dimension at most two by the no-fixed-divisor hypothesis. A general L meets B in a finite set. The restricted quartics therefore constitute a subspace of H0(I_D(4)) with finite base locus. These requirements hold simultaneously on a dense open set of flags. The plane lemma applies.

Regular hyperplane sections yield exact ideal-sheaf sequences

    0 -> I_Z(t-1) -> I_Z(t) -> I_{C,H}(t) -> 0,
    0 -> I_{C,H}(t-1) -> I_{C,H}(t) -> I_{D,L}(t) -> 0.

Taking H0 gives inequalities without asserting restriction surjectivity. Write b_t=h0(I_D(t)), c_t=h0(I_C(t)), z_t=h0(I_Z(t)). Since C and Z are nonempty, c_0=z_0=0. Since b_1=b_2=0, induction gives c_1=c_2=z_1=z_2=0. Then

    c_3<=b_3, c_4<=b_3+b_4,
    z_3<=b_3, z_4<=2b_3+b_4.

The plane bounds give z_4<=2*2+6=10 for d=9 and z_4<=2*1+5=7 for d>=10. No intermediate cohomology vanishing has been used.

## 3. Many sections of E and R

The equivariant inclusion H0(F) -> J has invariant image. Irreducibility means this image is zero or all of J. The latter is impossible because it would make every constant section of J tensor O factor through the rank-thirteen F, forcing its generic rank to be at least twenty. Thus H0(F)=0 and J injects into H0(R), so h0(R)>=20.

Dualizing the original sequence gives an equivariant inclusion R* -> J* tensor O and a map J* tensor O -> E with kernel R*. The identical irreducibility argument, now using rank(R*)=7, gives H0(R*)=0. Hence J* injects into H0(E), and h0(E)>=20.

## 4. Section quotients and contradiction

The section-quotient argument established in Section 1 of `../rank-nine-defect-two-sided/PROOF.md` gives, for sufficiently general lists of rank-minus-one sections,

    0 -> O^12 -> E -> I_{Z_E}(4) -> 0,
    0 -> O^6  -> R -> I_{Z_R}(4) -> 0.

For clarity, it only needs torsion-freeness and generation off codimension two. General sections have no divisorial rank drop. At a point of codimension k>=2 the free source has depth k and the middle term has depth at least one, so the depth lemma gives depth of the cokernel at least min(1,k-1)=1. Together with codimension-one freeness this excludes all torsion in the rank-one cokernel, which consequently has the displayed ideal-sheaf form. The second Chern class equals the effective surface cycle of Z. Both degrees here are positive in every allowed pair, so these are dimension-two schemes.

Each ideal quotient is generated outside codimension two by the images of global sections of its middle term. Therefore its complete quartic system has no fixed divisor. Since H1(P4,O)=0, global sections of the two sequences give

    h0(I_{Z_E}(4))=h0(E)-12>=8,
    h0(I_{Z_R}(4))=h0(R)-6>=14.

Now exhaust the three possible second-Chern pairs:

- (12,4): Z_E has degree twelve, so the surface lemma gives at most seven quartics, contradicting at least eight.
- (7,9): Z_R has degree nine, so the surface lemma gives at most ten quartics, contradicting at least fourteen.
- (2,14): Z_R has degree fourteen, so the surface lemma gives at most seven quartics, contradicting at least fourteen.

All possibilities are excluded. Therefore generic rank(Q)=9 is impossible.
