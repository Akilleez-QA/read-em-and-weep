# Unavoidable non-reflexive stable boundary

## Dependency and precise conclusion

This note depends on the flat family and its special-fiber calculation in [PR228](https://github.com/Akilleez-QA/read-em-and-weep/pull/228), exact commit `06c7e2f13e902d819bb6945c033fd455a5f28eac`, file `research/hm/local-defect/length-sixteen-flat-boundary/PROOF.md`. That calculation supplies a flat family F on P^4 over C[lambda,lambda^{-1}]. Its fibers are slope-stable torsion-free rank-two sheaves, with c1=c3=0, c2=4, c4=-86 and Euler characteristic 6. At c^4=1 its fiber F_c has depth one at the vertex v and fits into

    0 -> F_c -> F_c^{**} -> Q_{v,c} -> 0,

where Q_{v,c} has length one, and F_c^{**} is stable reflexive with c4=-92 and Euler characteristic 7.

The new conclusion is a uniqueness obstruction: the non-reflexive F_c cannot be replaced by a reflexive slope-semistable special fiber in any flat extension of the same generic family on the fixed P^4, including after finite DVR base change. In fact the two flat families are isomorphic after rescaling their chosen generic isomorphism by a power of the uniformizer. The prescribed generic marking itself need not extend without that rescaling.

No choice of a moduli space, a proper compactification, or original Horrocks--Mumford/source instance is asserted or used.

## 1. Elementary stable-Hom lemma with its necessary numerical hypothesis

Let E be a slope-stable torsion-free sheaf and G a slope-semistable torsion-free sheaf on an integral smooth polarized projective variety over a field. Assume they have the same positive rank r, the same slope, and the same Hilbert polynomial. Then every nonzero map u:E -> G is an isomorphism.

Proof. Its image I is a torsion-free subsheaf of G and has positive rank s: a rank-zero image in a torsion-free sheaf must vanish. If 0<s<r, its kernel K has positive rank r-s. Stability of E and semistability of G give mu(K)<mu(E) and mu(I)<=mu(G), since K and I are proper positive-rank subsheaves of E and G. The first inequality and degree additivity in 0 -> K -> E -> I -> 0 give

    r mu(E) = (r-s) mu(K) + s mu(I),
    hence mu(I)>mu(E)=mu(G),

contradicting the second inequality. The convention testing only saturated subsheaves yields the same inequalities by saturating; saturation cannot decrease slope. Thus s=r. The kernel has rank zero and is a subsheaf of the torsion-free E, so it is zero. For C=coker u, Hilbert polynomial additivity gives P_C=P_G-P_E=0. A nonzero coherent sheaf on a projective variety has a nonzero Hilbert polynomial (equivalently, sufficiently positive twists have vanishing higher cohomology and are generated by global sections). Therefore C=0.

Only the source needs stability. The target need only be semistable: its weak slope inequality already contradicts the strict opposite inequality from the source. This asymmetry is essential to the strengthened application.

The Hilbert-polynomial hypothesis must not be dropped. For example I_p -> O on a smooth projective variety of dimension at least two is a nonisomorphic injection between rank-one slope-stable torsion-free sheaves of the same slope. Rank-one slope stability is vacuous, and their Hilbert polynomials differ by one. Thus a bare assertion that equal-slope stable sheaves admit only isomorphisms would be false in this category.

## 2. Uniqueness over a DVR

Let D be a noetherian DVR, pi a uniformizer, K its fraction field, k its residue field, and X=P^4_D. Let E and G be coherent D-flat sheaves on X. Suppose E_k is slope-stable and G_k is slope-semistable, both torsion-free, of equal rank, slope, and Hilbert polynomial. Suppose an isomorphism

    a_K:E_K -> G_K

is given. Then there is an integer n such that pi^n a_K extends to an isomorphism E -> G. In particular E_k is isomorphic to G_k.

### 2.1 Integral extension and primitive choice

Put H=Hom_X(E,G). It is a finite D-module: the internal Hom is coherent on the noetherian X, and global sections of a coherent sheaf on a proper scheme over the noetherian affine base are finite. It is torsion-free because multiplication by pi is injective on G, hence also on H. Therefore H is a finite free D-module.

Moreover

    H tensor_D K = Hom_{X_K}(E_K,G_K).

Only flat localization is involved in this identity, not arbitrary cohomology base change. One can see it directly on a finite affine open cover: finite presentation of E gives localization of module Hom on every open and intersection; the global maps are the kernel of the finite gluing-difference map, and localization preserves that kernel. The separated scheme X has affine intersections for the standard affine cover.

Thus the nonzero a_K belongs to H tensor K. In a basis of the finite free H, choose n so that all coordinates of pi^n a_K lie in D and at least one is a unit. The resulting a in H is not in pi H. This also handles a generic isomorphism with arbitrarily large poles; finiteness prevents infinite divisibility of a nonzero integral map.

### 2.2 Its reduction is nonzero

Flatness gives an exact sequence

    0 -> G --pi--> G -> i_*G_k -> 0,

where i:X_k -> X. Left exactness of Hom_X(E,-), followed by the closed-immersion adjunction, gives an injection

    H/pi H -> Hom_X(E,i_*G_k)
             = Hom_{X_k}(E_k,G_k).

Consequently a_k is nonzero. There is no claim that this injection is surjective. Lemma 1 now makes a_k an isomorphism.

### 2.3 The integral map is itself an isomorphism

Let C=coker a. Right exactness of restriction shows C/pi C=0 along the special fiber. At each special-fiber point, pi lies in the maximal ideal; coherence and Nakayama therefore give C=0 at that point. On the generic fiber C vanishes because a_K is an isomorphism up to a nonzero scalar. Every point of X lies over one of the two points of Spec D, so C=0 everywhere.

Now a is surjective. In 0 -> T -> E -> G -> 0, D-flatness of G ensures restriction to k is still left exact. Since a_k is an isomorphism, T/pi T=0. Nakayama and the vanishing of T_K show T=0 exactly as above. Hence a is an isomorphism on X.

This is family-level uniqueness, not extension of an unchanged marking. For example multiplication by pi^{-1} on the generic fiber of O_X cannot itself extend as an integral automorphism, though scaling it by pi does.

## 3. Application at the four punctures and after finite base change

Fix c in C with c^4=1 and take

    D=C[lambda,lambda^{-1}]_(lambda-c).

Restrict the PR228 family to P^4_D. Its generic fiber is the known stable reflexive length-sixteen-defect cone, whereas its special fiber F_c is the depth-one stable torsion-free sheaf above.

Let D -> D' be any finite extension of DVRs with the closed point lying above lambda=c; in particular ramification is allowed. The pullback F' is D'-flat, its generic fiber is the field extension of the same generic family, and its special fiber is the residue-field extension of F_c. Here the finite residue-field extension of C is C itself. It is therefore still stable, torsion-free, and non-reflexive of depth one.

Suppose G' is another coherent D'-flat family on the same P^4_{D'}, with slope-semistable torsion-free special fiber, and an isomorphism G'_{K'} isomorphic to F'_{K'} over the identity of P^4_{K'}. The Hilbert polynomials of their special fibers equal those of their generic fibers by projective flatness; hence they agree. Equal Hilbert polynomials on P^4 determine rank and degree, and therefore slope. Apply Section 2 with the known stable family F' as the source and the competing semistable family G' as the target (invert the generic isomorphism if necessary). It gives

    G' isomorphic to F',   G'_{k'} isomorphic to F_c.

In particular G'_{k'} cannot be reflexive; indeed it must itself be stable, since it is isomorphic to F_c. Thus neither a stable nor a strictly semistable reflexive special fiber can replace the known limit. This remains true even if G' is not required to carry the H-action: uniqueness already holds in the underlying nonequivariant category. If the generic isomorphism is H-equivariant for a compatible action fixing D', its integral extension is H-equivariant as well, because the commutator vanishes generically and the target is D'-torsion-free.

A family changed by a generic automorphism of the ambient projective space is not automatically a family satisfying the fixed-ambient generic-isomorphism hypothesis. Nor is an arbitrary unstable extension covered by this argument.

## 4. Next consequence: the elementary modification is forced, not a repairable choice

Among slope-semistable torsion-free flat extensions with this fixed generic sheaf, the length-one discrepancy is intrinsic to the limit, not an artifact of the chosen presentation. Since the special fiber is unique up to isomorphism, its reflexive hull and the quotient by it are recovered functorially:

    G'_{k'}^{**} isomorphic to F_c^{**},
    G'_{k'}^{**}/G'_{k'} isomorphic to Q_{v,c}.

In particular the support vertex and length-one quotient cannot be removed by choosing a different slope-semistable torsion-free filling. When the generic comparison is equivariant, its quotient also has the same character (C5^2-trivial, C4 eigenvalue c, with the specified central character convention of PR228).

Replacing the flat special fiber by its reflexive hull would change the Hilbert polynomial by +1, Euler characteristic from 6 to 7, and c4 from -86 to -92. Thus the hull is not an alternative flat special fiber of the same generic family. More strongly, no different slope-semistable reflexive sheaf with the correct Hilbert polynomial can fill the puncture either, by Section 3.

Equivalently, the given punctured family admits no extension through the puncture entirely within the class of slope-semistable reflexive sheaves, even after finite DVR base change. This is a concrete family-level valuative obstruction; it does not assume existence of a moduli space or identify a proper compactification. It says nothing about lambda=0, infinity, unstable alternative fillings, changed generic fibers, or original HM/source-instance realization.

## Verification and status

The proof was derived independently from the stated PR228 fiber calculation. Checks explicitly isolate the two common pitfalls: Hom reduction need only be injective, not surjective; and stability of the source together with semistability of the equal-slope target gives full rank, while the Hilbert polynomial is needed to kill the cokernel. A rank-one counterexample checks the necessity of the latter condition. The total-family argument separately uses target flatness to control the kernel after restriction. Finite ramification does not change the known special fiber.

This is an authored human-readable proof, not a machine-formal verification. The PR228 boundary calculation is a dependency, not reproved here. An independent reviewer should check this note before publication.
