# Adjoint section generators and the higher syzygy tail

## Statement

Work over C on P^4. Let F be the normalized Horrocks–Mumford bundle, E=F(3), B=End_0(F)=Sym^2(F)(1), and T=H^0(O(1)), S=Sym(T). Put V=H^0(E), dim V=4, and define the full graded modules

    G_j=H^0(B(j−1)),    K_j=H^1(B(j−1)).

The following assertions concern these actual modules, with their natural polynomial multiplication.

1. G has depth 2, projective dimension 3, and Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity 8.
2. Its complete minimal generator counts are ten in degree 6, sixty in degree 7, and twenty-five in degree 8. There are no generators in any other degree. More canonically,

    Tor_0^S(G,C)_6 = Sym^2 V,
    Tor_0^S(G,C)_7 = V tensor (H^0(E(1))/(T V)),
    Tor_0^S(G,C)_8 = H^0(C,L^2(2)),

where C is the union of 25 disjoint evaluation-degeneracy lines and L is the evaluation cokernel line bundle, of degree −1 on each line.
3. For n≥7, the coordinate Koszul spectral sequence supplies actual isomorphisms

    Tor_i^S(K,C)_n ≅ Tor_(i−2)^S(G,C)_n,    2≤i≤5.

Consequently the degree-seven and degree-eight second syzygy generator counts of K are exactly sixty and twenty-five. For i=2 and n≥7 they occur only in degrees 7 and 8. For i=3,4,5 and n≥7 its i-th syzygy generators can occur only in the interval i+5≤n≤i+6. Moreover dim Tor_3(K,C)_8=140. The degree-six exceptions are precisely those proved in PR484; no assertion here determines lower-degree cubic ambiguities.

These are structural statements, not evaluations of selected HM matrices or source instances.

## 1. Inputs and their exact scope

The pinned preceding proofs give:

- H^0(B(t))=0 for t≤4 and H^0(B(5))=Sym^2 V, dimension 10.
- H^1(B(t))=0 for t≥6; H^1(B(5)) has dimension 32.
- H^2(B(t))=0 for t≥1, and H^3(B(t))=0 for t≥−1.
- B is self-dual, so H^4(B(t))=H^0(B(−t−5))*.
- The actual primary section module has minimal resolution

    0 → 2S(−8) → 20S(−6) → 35S(−5)
      → 15S(−4) + 4S(−3) → H^0_*(F) → 0.

In particular H^0_*(E) is generated by V in degree zero and fifteen new generators in degree one, with first relations in degree two. Thus T V embeds in H^0(E(1)), its quotient has dimension 15, and T H^0(E(1)) surjects onto H^0(E(2)). Also H^1(E(2))=H^1(F(5))=0.

Finally the evaluation V tensor O → E has rank two off C and transverse rank one on C. Its cokernel is i_*L, and L restricts to O(−1) on each of the 25 disjoint lines. Locally at C the actual map is equivalent to

    R^4 → R^2,    (c0,c1,c2,c3) ↦ (c0, x c1+y c2+z c3),

where x,y,z form a regular sequence. This is the geometric local form from HM1973, not a selected numerical matrix.

## 2. A symmetric multiplication complex

Let D=det(V), a constant one-dimensional vector space. There is an exact complex

    0 → D tensor O(−5) → Lambda^2 V tensor O
      → V tensor E → Sym^2 E → i_*L^2 → 0.             (1)

The map Lambda^2 V → V tensor E sends u wedge v to u tensor ev(v)−v tensor ev(u); the next map is multiplication. The left map is given by the complementary two-by-two minors of evaluation, hence has precisely the global line D tensor det(E)^−1=D tensor O(−5).

For completeness, exactness can be checked in the transverse local form. Write the target basis a,b, with ev(e0)=a and ev(ei)=xi b for i=1,2,3. The image in Sym^2 E is R a^2+R ab+I b^2, I=(x1,x2,x3), so the cokernel is L^2. An element u tensor a+v tensor b in the kernel of multiplication satisfies u0=0, v0=−sum xi ui, and sum xi vi=0. The first two equations are accounted for by the images of ei wedge e0; after subtracting them, the last equation is a Koszul syzygy on x1,x2,x3, generated by images of ei wedge ej. This proves exactness at V tensor E. In the kernel at Lambda^2 V, the e0 components vanish, and the remaining bivector is annihilated by contraction with (x1,x2,x3). Exactness of the regular-sequence Koszul complex says it is a multiple of

    x3 e1 wedge e2 − x2 e1 wedge e3 + x1 e2 wedge e3.

This is the complementary-minor generator. Away from C the ordinary exact symmetric-power complex of a rank-two quotient gives the same assertion. Thus (1) is globally exact.

Set A_t equal to the image of Lambda^2 V tensor O(t) in V tensor E(t), and J_t equal to the image of V tensor E(t) in Sym^2 E(t). Twisting and splitting (1) gives

    0 → D tensor O(t−5) → Lambda^2 V tensor O(t) → A_t → 0,
    0 → A_t → V tensor E(t) → J_t → 0,
    0 → J_t → B(5+t) → i_*L^2(t) → 0.                (2)

For t=1,2, the first sequence and line-bundle cohomology on P^4 give

    H^0(A_t)=Lambda^2 V tensor S_t,
    H^1(A_t)=H^2(A_t)=0.

Here the absent H^0(O(t−5)) is also essential. Hence

    H^0(J_t)=(V tensor H^0(E(t)))/(Lambda^2 V tensor S_t),
    H^1(J_t)=V tensor H^1(E(t)).                     (3)

All identifications commute with multiplication by T.

## 3. Exact minimal generators

There are no sections below G_6, and G_6=Sym^2 V, so the first generator space is established.

At t=1, L^2(1) has degree −1 on each line, so has no H^0. Equations (2)–(3) identify

    G_7=(V tensor H^0(E(1)))/(Lambda^2 V tensor T).

The denominator lies inside V tensor (T V), where T V injects into H^0(E(1)). The resulting quotient of this subspace is T tensor Sym^2 V. It is exactly the natural multiplication image T G_6. In particular that multiplication is injective, and taking its quotient yields the canonical isomorphism

    G_7/(T G_6) ≅ V tensor (H^0(E(1))/(T V)).

Its dimension is 4 times 15 = 60. No noncanonical splitting or representation labeling is needed.

At t=2, H^1(E(2))=0, so H^1(J_2)=0. Therefore the last sequence in (2) gives a surjection

    G_8 → H^0(C,L^2(2))

with kernel H^0(J_2). Since T H^0(E(1)) → H^0(E(2)) is onto, compatibility in (3) shows

    H^0(J_2)=T H^0(J_1)=T G_7.

This is an equality of the actual multiplication images, not a dimension argument. Each component of L^2(2) is O on a line, so h^0(C,L^2(2))=25. Thus there are exactly 25 minimal degree-eight generators. No claim about the equivariant character of this 25-dimensional space is necessary.

The next section excludes all higher-degree generators.

## 4. Exact depth and regularity

The full section module G is finitely generated. For example the stated cohomological inputs show B is 7-regular, since H^1(B(6)), H^2(B(5)), H^3(B(4)), and H^4(B(3)) vanish. The standard regularity multiplication theorem yields finite generation of its full section module; its finite number of lower graded pieces causes no issue.

Let m=S_+ be the irrelevant ideal. The standard Cech description of sections on the five standard affine opens gives

    H_m^0(G)=H_m^1(G)=0,
    H_m^q(G)_j=H^(q−1)(B(j−1)),    2≤q≤5.           (4)

The first line uses that G is the full section module, not a truncation. The group H_m^2(G)_6=H^1(B(5)) is nonzero of dimension 32. Thus depth_m G=2. The Auslander–Buchsbaum formula over the five-variable polynomial ring gives pd_S G=5−2=3.

Use reg G=max_q(end H_m^q(G)+q), with the end of zero equal to minus infinity. The input vanishings bound these ends as follows:

    end H_m^2(G)=6;
    end H_m^3(G)≤1;
    end H_m^4(G)≤−1;
    end H_m^5(G)≤−9.

For the last bound, Serre duality gives H_m^5(G)_j=H^0(B(−j−4))* and sections of B start at twist 5. Therefore the maximum is exactly 6+2=8. This also proves the exact section-module regularity without relying on an unstated equality between sheaf and module regularity.

Equivalently a minimal free resolution of G has shifts at most 8+a in homological degree a. Since G starts in degree 6, minimality makes every such shift at least 6+a. The injection T G_6 → G_7 proved in Section 3 further gives Tor_1(G,C)_7=0. Hence minimality sharpens the possible shifts to 6..8, 8..9, 9..10, and 10..11 in homological degrees zero through three, respectively. Its zeroth free module is now completely determined:

    10S(−6) + 60S(−7) + 25S(−8).

The first syzygy generator count in degree eight is also exact. The primary section resolution gives

    h^0(E(2)) = 4 dim S_2 + 15 dim S_1 − 35 = 60+75−35 = 100.

There are no further free-resolution terms in this degree. Equations (2)–(3) therefore give

    dim G_8 = 4(100) − 6 dim S_2 + 25 = 335.

The minimal free cover displayed above has degree-eight dimension

    10 dim S_2 + 60 dim S_1 + 25 = 475.

Its kernel in degree eight has dimension 475−335=140. Since that kernel has no component in any smaller degree, all 140 are minimal first syzygies. Thus dim Tor_1(G,C)_8=140. These are dimension deductions from proved exact sequences, not computations of selected multiplication ranks. We do not determine the remaining differential matrices or Betti multiplicities here.

## 5. Transfer to the adjoint higher syzygies

Fix n≥7. Tensor the coordinate Koszul complex on P^4 with B(n−1). It is exact, because the homogeneous coordinates have no common projective zero. Its hypercohomology spectral sequence therefore abuts to zero and has

    E_1^(−a,q)=Lambda^a T tensor H^q(B(n−1−a)),    0≤a≤5.

The smallest twist is n−6≥1. The inputs in Section 1 therefore make every row q≥2 zero already on E_1. The q=0 and q=1 rows are precisely the internal-degree-n Koszul complexes for G and K. On E_2 they are Tor_a(G,C)_n and Tor_a(K,C)_n.

The only possible higher differential is

    d_2: E_2^(−i,1) → E_2^(2−i,0).

For 2≤i≤5 both terms have no other incoming or outgoing higher differentials. The zero abutment forces d_2 to be injective and surjective, hence supplies the asserted actual isomorphism. The unpaired boundary terms also give Tor_0(K,C)_n=Tor_1(K,C)_n=0 and Tor_4(G,C)_n=Tor_5(G,C)_n=0.

In particular Tor_2(K,C)_7 and Tor_2(K,C)_8 have dimensions 60 and 25. The additional first-syzygy calculation yields dim Tor_3(K,C)_8=140. Applying the sharpened shifts of Section 4 to homological degree i−2 shows that for i=3,4,5 the high-degree i-th syzygies of K occur only at i+5≤n≤i+6. PR484 supplies the separately established degree-five gap and degree-six terms; the comparison above does not need H-module duality or the unresolved Betti parameters of PR483.
