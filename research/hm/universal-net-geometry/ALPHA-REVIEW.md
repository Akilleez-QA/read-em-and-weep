The argument is sound. The contraction map has a one-dimensional kernel exactly at the rank-four points. An invertible four-by-four pivot identifies its maximal-minor scheme with the projective kernel incidence by exact Schur elimination. The incidence is the relative Grassmannian Gr(3,6) over P4 and is smooth irreducible of dimension thirteen. At alpha nets the contraction rank is four, so this comparison proves smoothness of the contraction scheme there before invoking reduced support.

PR560 supplies equality of closed-point supports, and the Nullstellensatz over C then identifies the reduction of the wedge scheme with that smooth scheme locally. Since all wedge entries vanish at a zero-wedge net, all two-minors have zero linear term. The wedge scheme has tangent dimension twenty-one, whereas its reduction has dimension and tangent dimension thirteen. This proves nonreducedness and the eight-dimensional cotangent reduction kernel, not a nilradical-length formula.

The global fiber argument is correct: a radical of dimension at least three would leave at most a two-dimensional covector support, whose exterior square cannot contain a three-dimensional net. Radical dimension two forces the beta net to equal the full exterior square of its three-dimensional support. This gives precisely P1 fibers over the six-dimensional beta locus. Every other point has contraction rank four and therefore the local inverse already proved. The seven-dimensional alpha flag description and seven-dimensional exceptional inverse image follow directly.

No inference of actual HM intersection, nonreducedness after arbitrary pullback, normality, or embedded-component structure is made.
