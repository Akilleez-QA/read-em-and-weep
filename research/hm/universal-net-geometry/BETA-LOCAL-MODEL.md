# Exact local model at a beta net

## Scope and theorem

Let A be a complex vector space of dimension five. In G=Gr(3,Λ²A*), let X be the reduced closed locus of nets with a nonzero common radical. Equivalently, by the universal common-radical criterion of PR560 at b9ee9b3b105967ee4d9e423d30cfa961ce0824d7, X is the reduced support of the rank-at-most-one wedge-map locus. Let

J={(N,[x]): contraction_x(N)=0} ⊂ G×P(A).

At every beta net N=Λ²B with dim B=3, there is an affine Grassmannian chart and polynomial coordinates in which

    X = A^6 × {H in Mat_(6×2): rank H≤1},

inside A^6×A^12×A^3, with the last three coordinates zero. The incidence J on this chart is the product with A^6 of

    {(H,[r]) : H r=0} -> {rank H≤1}.

This is the total space of O_(P1)(-1)^6, mapping properly to the rank-one cone. In particular the model is exact, not just a tangent-cone calculation.

Consequently X is normal and its singular locus is exactly the beta locus Gr(3,A*), of dimension six. J is a smooth proper birational resolution of X, with only P1 fibers over beta nets and point fibers elsewhere. Its positive structure-sheaf higher direct images vanish. These are statements about the universal net space, not about the intersection with any selected HM family. No actual HM normality or transversality is inferred.

## 1. A polynomial shear gives exact coordinates

Fix A*=B⊕C with bases b1,b2,b3 of B and c1,c2 of C. Put

    omega1=b2∧b3, omega2=b3∧b1, omega3=b1∧b2.

The Grassmannian chart where projection N->Λ²B is an isomorphism has unique basis

    eta_i=omega_i + sum_(l=1,2) sum_(j=1,2,3) M_l(i,j) b_j∧c_l
                    + z_i c1∧c2.

Thus it is affine 21-space, with two arbitrary 3×3 matrices M_l and three scalars z_i. Define the skew matrix K(a) by

    contraction_a(omega_i)=sum_j K(a)_(ij) b_j,
    K(a)_(ij)=sum_k epsilon_(ikj) a_k.

Here epsilon_123=1. The map a->K(a) identifies C^3 with the space of skew 3×3 matrices.

Write uniquely M_l=S_l+K(s_l), with S_l symmetric. Define new covectors

    b'_j=b_j-s_1(j)c1-s_2(j)c2.

For a shear b_j->b_j+t(j)c_l, the mixed coefficient matrix of omega_i changes by -K(t). Hence omega_i(b') has mixed matrices K(s_l). It follows that there are unique corrected scalars z'_i such that the SAME eta_i satisfy

    eta_i=omega_i(b') + sum_(l,j) S_l(i,j) b'_j∧c_l
                       + z'_i c1∧c2.

The correction from z to z' is polynomial in the entries of s_l and S_l; its coefficient on each original z_i is one. Therefore

    (M1,M2,z) <-> (s1,s2,S1,S2,z')

is a polynomial automorphism of the affine chart. This also proves the shear is valid scheme-theoretically over any complex algebra, not just pointwise. Only division by two is used to split symmetric and skew parts.

## 2. Exact contraction equations

Use the coframe (b',c) to write a vector x as coordinates (a1,a2,a3,r1,r2). The B' coefficients of contraction_x(eta_i)=0 are

    K(a)=r1 S1+r2 S2.

The left side is skew and the right side symmetric. Thus, over any complex algebra, these equations are equivalent to

    a=0,   r1 S1+r2 S2=0.

After imposing a=0, the two remaining coefficients of each contraction are

    z'_i r1=0,   z'_i r2=0.

On projective vector space, a=0 implies [r1:r2] is a projective line coordinate. On its two standard charts one r-coordinate is a unit, so the last equations are equivalent to z'_i=0. Consequently the incidence is exactly

    z'=0,  a=0,  H r=0,

where H has as its two columns the six independent entries of S1 and S2. The six entries of s1,s2 are free parameters.

At a closed point a nonzero r exists exactly when rank H≤1. Thus the reduced image is precisely the claimed rank-one determinantal cone, times affine six-space, with z'=0. The next section proves that the standard minor ideal is prime, so this description gives its reduced scheme structure, not only its support.

## 3. The rank-one cone is integral and normal

Let h_(i,l), 1≤i≤6, 1≤l≤2 be the entries of H. The homomorphism

    C[h_(i,l)] -> C[u1,...,u6,v1,v2],   h_(i,l) -> u_i v_l

has kernel generated by the two-by-two minors. One elementary proof sorts monomials using h_(i,1)h_(j,2)=h_(i,2)h_(j,1). Two monomials have the same image exactly when their row totals and two column totals agree. If two distributions differ, moving one column-one factor from a row with excess to a row with deficit, exchanging a column-two factor in the opposite direction, reduces the difference. Iteration connects them by the displayed minor relations. Grouping the monomials of any polynomial in the kernel by their images proves the kernel assertion.

Its image R consists exactly of the invariants for the multiplicative action with weights +1 on all u_i and -1 on v1,v2: invariant monomials have equal total u- and v-degree and factor into u_i v_l. Hence R is a domain. It is integrally closed as well. If f in Frac(R) is integral over R, then f is integral over the polynomial ring P=C[u,v], because its monic equation also has coefficients in P. Since f lies in Frac(P) and P is integrally closed, f lies in P. It remains invariant, since it lay in Frac(R), and therefore belongs to R.

A nonzero-entry chart, for example h_(1,1)≠0, is freely parametrized by that invertible entry, the other five entries of column one, and h_(1,2); all other column-two entries are determined by the minors. It is smooth of dimension seven. The origin has tangent dimension twelve because all minor equations are quadratic, while the cone has dimension seven. Thus the cone is singular exactly at its vertex.

In our net chart the vertex H=0,z'=0 means eta_i=omega_i(b'), or N=Λ²span(b'_1,b'_2,b'_3). The free shear parameters therefore describe exactly the local beta locus. This proves the stated singularity description near every beta net.

## 4. Incidence resolution and the complement of beta

Globally J is the relative Grassmannian over P(A) whose fiber at [x] is Gr(3,Λ²(x^perp)). Since x^perp has rank four, J is smooth and irreducible of dimension 4+3(6-3)=13. It is proper over G. By the common-radical classification, its image is X and every fiber is a point except at beta nets, where the common radical has dimension two and the fiber is P1.

For completeness, the projection is a scheme isomorphism over the complement of beta in X. Consider the universal contraction map

    A⊗O_G -> N*⊗A*.

At a net with one-dimensional radical its rank is four. On a chart with an invertible four-by-four pivot, imposing rank≤4 is equivalent to vanishing of its residual Schur column. The kernel is then a line subbundle, and projectivizing that kernel identifies its rank≤4 scheme with the radical incidence on the chart. The incidence is smooth, so this local scheme is reduced and equals X there. Therefore X is smooth away from beta, including the zero-wedge alpha nets. Combined with the beta charts, X is normal everywhere and singular exactly at beta.

Near beta, H r=0 identifies each matrix with a map C²/L -> C^6, where L is the chosen line [r]. The quotient C²/L is O_(P1)(1), so this incidence is Tot(O_(P1)(-1)^6). The morphism to the cone is proper because it is the closed incidence in the product of affine matrix space and projective line. It is an isomorphism for nonzero rank-one H and has fiber P1 at H=0. Its exceptional locus has dimension one in the seven-dimensional slice; globally it has dimension seven in J, hence codimension six. In particular it is a small resolution in the sense that there are no exceptional divisors.

## 5. Structure-sheaf direct images

For the local resolution E=Tot(O_(P1)(-1)^6), the affine projection to P1 gives

    H^j(E,O_E)=H^j(P1, direct_sum_(n≥0) Sym^n(C^6*)⊗O(n)).

All positive groups vanish: H^1(P1,O(n))=0 for n≥0, higher cohomology on P1 vanishes, and cohomology commutes with these direct sums, as can be checked on its finite standard affine cover. Its global functions are the rank-one cone ring, by the same degree-wise presentation as in section 3 (a choice of bases identifies the two symmetric-power factors).

The same statements hold after product with affine six-space. The target chart is affine and the resolution proper, so these cohomology calculations give pushforward O=O_X and all positive higher direct images zero on each beta chart. Away from beta the map is an isomorphism. Thus globally

    pi_* O_J=O_X,   R^j pi_* O_J=0 for j>0.

Together with normality and smoothness of J, this also verifies rational singularities for the universal reduced locus X by their defining resolution criterion. This property is NOT automatically inherited by an arbitrary family pullback or linear section.

## 6. The wedge-minor scheme is nonreduced at beta

Let D denote the scheme defined by two-by-two minors of the wedge map, before reduction. At a beta net the wedge map itself is zero, so the differential of every such minor is zero. Thus its tangent space is the full tangent space to G, of dimension 21. The reduced support X has tangent dimension 6+12=18 there by the exact local model. If D were reduced in a neighborhood of that point, its local ring would equal that of X and their tangent spaces would agree. Therefore D is nonreduced at every beta net. This is separate from the normality and rational singularities of X, and makes no assertion that nonreducedness persists under any particular HM family pullback.

## Verification and boundary

The shear, symmetric/skew splitting, contraction equations, rank-one ring presentation, and cohomology computation are exact universal arguments. No selected HM coefficients, numerical ranks, source-instance evaluations, or large computation enter. This proof requires independent mathematical review before publication and is not formally verified. The wedge-minor scheme itself may be nonreduced on zero-wedge strata; all global normality and resolution statements here concern its reduced support X.
