## 1. Seven general symmetric sections through one fixed point

Let `S=P4×P4`, and let `H` consist of symmetric bilinear forms annihilating `u²`. Its dimension is `15−1=14`. A point in its base locus has symmetric product `xz` proportional to `u²`. Unique factorization of quadratic polynomials forces both factors to be proportional to `u`; thus the set-theoretic base locus is exactly `p`.

The map to symmetric products has finite fibers: a rank-two symmetric product has its two factor lines determined up to interchange, and a square has one factor line. For a general rank-two symmetric tensor `q` whose support excludes `u`, the tensors `q+c u²` have rank three when `c≠0`. This can be checked in a basis consisting of the two-dimensional support of `q`, then `u`, and a complement. The line through `[q]` and `[u²]` is therefore not contained in the rank-at-most-two locus. Its intersection with that locus is finite. Projection from `[u²]`, and hence the map defined by `H`, is generically finite with image dimension eight.

The successive Bertini argument is valid, with the following precise reading. At stage `k≤6`, the current integral support has dimension `8−k`, and the restricted system has image dimension `8−k`. The latter follows on the open locus where the original map is quasi-finite: intersect its image with the same general hyperplanes, avoiding containment in the complement of that open locus. The restricted system is therefore not composed with a pencil while the next divisor is being chosen. Its only possible base point is `p`, of codimension at least two on the current support. Thus there is no fixed divisorial component, and the irreducibility form of Bertini applies. Generic smoothness/Bertini applies on the smooth open complement of `p` at each stage.

The base point does not introduce an isolated component: throughout these cuts a divisor on the current integral variety has pure codimension one; the final ambient ideal has height seven. In the regular local rings of `S`, an ideal generated by seven elements of height seven is a complete intersection. Its quotient is Cohen–Macaulay, and generic smoothness gives generic reducedness. The combination of generic reducedness and the absence of embedded associated primes implies reducedness. Therefore the final curve is indeed integral as a scheme.

The diagonal-incidence estimate is also correct. For any fixed `[a]≠[u]`, evaluation on `a²` is a nonzero functional on `H`, so seven vanishings have codimension seven in `H^7`. The punctured diagonal has dimension four; the incidence has dimension at most `98+4−7=95<98`. Its image is constructible, and its closure has no larger dimension. Properness of the punctured base is unnecessary. Consequently there is a nonempty open parameter set avoiding every other diagonal zero.

## 2. General first jets, net transversality, and simultaneous genericity

The decomposition `h(x,z)=x0 ell(b)+z0 ell(a)+c(a,b)` is exact. On the open locus of first-jet rank four, changes of equation basis give four vector equations with linear part `x0 b+z0 a`, together with three equations in the kernel of the first-jet map. Choosing arbitrary `B:Sym²E→E` and a surjective `q:Sym²E→C³` realizes such systems. This parameterization dominates the first-jet-rank-four locus; changes of residual basis and adding residual equations to the first four equations account for its redundancy.

A transverse net is a genuinely nonempty condition, not only an expected dimension count. For example, in coordinates `t1,t2,t3,t4`, the three quadrics

- `t1²−t4²`,
- `t2²−t4²`,
- `t3²−t4²`

have exactly the eight points `[±1:±1:±1:1]`, and their three projective derivatives are independent there. Transversality is open. Pulling this nonempty open set back under the above dominant parameterization proves the needed generic tangent-net claim. All the other generic conditions arise in the same irreducible space of ordered independent equation tuples (or its Grassmannian quotient), so their finitely many nonempty opens intersect.

At `p`, the formal implicit function theorem solves the four equations uniquely for an even series `s(t)` with order at least two. The remaining three equations have initial forms the three net quadrics. A regular sequence of these initial forms gives the associated graded ring

`C[t1,t2,t3,t4]/(q1,q2,q3)`.

Blowing up the origin, the strict-transform equations have independent derivatives in the three projective directions at each of the eight exceptional points. Each strict transform is a smooth branch, transverse to the exceptional divisor. There are exactly eight branches with distinct tangent lines; this also proves local reducedness.

The delta calculation can be checked without relying just on the branch count. The graded ring has Hilbert function `1,4,7,8,8,…`, whereas the associated graded normalization has dimension eight in every degree. The filtration induced from the normalization agrees with the maximal-ideal filtration: a nonzero initial form in the reduced cone cannot vanish on all eight tangent points. Therefore the normalization defect has length `7+4+1=12`. Branch count alone would not have sufficed for this number, but the regular reduced tangent cone does.

## 3. Square avoidance and exact reconstruction

Set `K=ker q` and `v=B|K`. If `a²∈K` and `v(a²)=lambda a`, the vector `a−(lambda/2)u` is a square zero of the seven-dimensional middle map. Since its image in `E` is nonzero, it is not proportional to `u`; this contradicts diagonal avoidance. The factor `1/2` is correct because the cross term is twice `b(u,a)`.

If the residual outer vectors are proportional and nonzero, one may rescale the two outer projective representatives independently to make these vectors equal. The middle equation then forces precisely the excluded condition `a²∈K`, `v(a²)∈Ca`. If one residual vector vanishes, `b(u,E)` being injective forces the other to vanish. This proves the unique dependent support point assertion.

For independent residual vectors `a,b`, expansion gives exactly

`b(a+alpha u,b+beta u)=(v(ab)+beta a+alpha b,0)`

provided `ab∈K`. Hence the correction has a unique solution precisely when `v(ab)` belongs to `span(a,b)`. There is no discrepancy of correction convention between this geometric fiber and the subsequent universal graph.

## 4. Universal tensors and uniform avoidance

The wedge map `V⊗Λ²V→Λ³V` is surjective, so its kernel has dimension `5·10−10=40`. Both outer symmetry and cyclic zero of the displayed `f0` follow by pairing opposite wedge terms; its values lie in that kernel.

For any nonzero middle `a`, split `V=Ca⊕U`. The images of `aU` under `f0(-,a,-)` are the four independent tensors `a⊗(a∧b)`. The images of `Sym²U` are the ten independent symmetric matrices in `U⊗(a∧U)`. They occupy distinct first-factor blocks. The image `F_a` thus has dimension fourteen, the middle-map kernel is `Ca²`, and quotienting by the repeated-input image gives `Sym²(V/Ca)`.

For `r∈K`, the graph tensor has block matrix `[-v(r)^T; 2M(r)]`. In particular projection to its lower symmetric block recovers `r`, so `L` is seven-dimensional and intersects the repeated-input image at `u` trivially. Its second-factor support is exactly `u∧(im r+Cv(r))`. For rank at least two this support has dimension at least two. For rank one, a nonzero symmetric rank-one tensor over C is a scalar multiple of a square, and square avoidance makes `v(r)` independent of its one-dimensional image. Thus every nonzero graph tensor again has support dimension at least two.

For distinct middle lines `[a]≠[u]`, one has the elementary identity

`(u∧V) ∩ (a∧V) = C(u∧a)`.

Indeed a nonzero decomposable bivector lying in both spaces must represent a plane containing both `u` and `a`. Every tensor of `F_a` has second-factor support inside `a∧V`. A nonzero tensor of `L` has at least two-dimensional second-factor support inside `u∧V`, so it cannot belong to `F_a`. This proves the required **uniform** intersection statement for all `[a]≠[u]`; a mere generic-middle assertion would not suffice.

## 5. The 20-dimensional target and Grassmannian incidence

After quotienting by `L`, the ambient space has dimension 33. The images of the `F_a` on the punctured projective middle space form a rank-fourteen subbundle: the original middle-image maps are algebraic of constant rank fourteen, and the already-proved uniform intersection with `L` is zero. Twisting by the tautological middle line if necessary describes this bundle intrinsically and does not alter its fibers or dimensions.

For a fixed middle, choose a line in its fourteen-dimensional image: this contributes dimension thirteen. For a fixed line in a 33-dimensional vector space, the thirteen-dimensional subspaces containing it form `Gr(12,32)`, of dimension `12·20=240`. Adding the four middle parameters gives

`4+13+240=257`.

The full parameter space `Gr(13,33)` has dimension `13·20=260`. The bad incidence image has proper closure, even though the middle base is punctured. Avoiding this closure provides a nonempty open set of thirteen-planes meeting none of the other middle images. Avoidance of the fixed seven-dimensional space `F_u/L` is another nonempty open condition (`13+7≤33`). The Grassmannian is irreducible, so these opens intersect.

Repeated-input images survive at every middle. Their dimensions are four; therefore residual rank is the middle-image dimension minus four, namely `14−dim(T∩F_a)−4`. ## 7. Added global symmetric-Segre invariant-ring argument

**Pass, including the scheme-theoretic linear-section conclusion.** Let `R` be the standard graded Segre ring, with degree-one generators `r_ij=x_i z_j`. The substitution `a_ij=r_ij+r_ji`, `b_ij=r_ij−r_ji` gives a surjection from a polynomial ring carrying the involution `a→a`, `b→−b` onto `R`. Averaging any polynomial lift of an invariant element proves surjectivity on invariants; linear independence among the displayed generators is not needed. Invariant polynomials are sums of monomials with an even number of `b` factors, hence are generated by the `a` variables and pairwise `b` products.

Expansion in `x,z` verifies the exact sign and coefficient in

`b_ij b_kl = a_il a_jk − a_ik a_jl`.

In particular, with `(k,l)=(i,j)` this reads `b_ij²=a_ij²−a_ii a_jj`, consistent with `a_ii=2x_i z_i`. Thus all invariant products reduce to polynomials in the fifteen symmetric degree-one coordinates. There is no missing factor of two.

It follows that `Proj(R^swap)` is a closed subscheme of the symmetric projective space. For a finite graded group action, `Proj(R^swap)` is the projective scheme quotient; standard invariant affine charts, or finiteness of `R` over its invariant subring, establish this identification. Its points are precisely rank-at-most-two symmetric tensors by factorization of binary quadratic forms over C. The invariant subring is a domain, so its Proj is reduced. Over C, a reduced closed subscheme is determined by its closed points. Hence this is exactly the reduced symmetric rank-at-most-two variety as a scheme. This reasoning does not need an assertion that its ideal is generated by all 3-by-3 minors.

For `I=(h1,…,h7)R` with invariant linear `h_i`, averaging coefficients proves `I∩R^swap=(h1,…,h7)R^swap`; averaging also proves the surjection `R^swap→(R/I)^swap`. Therefore the asserted graded-ring isomorphism is exact. Taking Proj gives the scheme-theoretic hyperplane section, including any multiplicities or nonreduced structure of that section. Saturating the ideal is not required to obtain the same Proj subscheme. In the generic construction the seven independent invariant linear equations leave eight degree-one coordinates, in agreement with the descended complete linear series and its ambient P7.
