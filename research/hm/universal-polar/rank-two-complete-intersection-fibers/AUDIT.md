# Independent audit

PASS. The opening hypotheses now explicitly include trilinearity. The audit accepts the cited universal K8 theorem as an existing dependency; it does not reprove that theorem.

- **Dependent zeros.** For independent a,b, A_a(b) and A_b(a) are nonzero. A relation A_a(b)=t A_b(a) gives f(a+tb,a+tb,b)=A_a(b)-t A_b(a)=0, contradicting injectivity at a+tb since b is not proportional to a+tb. Here the cross term is -A_b(a), by cyclic zero and outer symmetry. If x,z are distinct outer lines and u=alpha x+beta z, then f(x,u,z)=alpha A_x(z)+beta A_z(x) cannot vanish. If x,z coincide, cyclic zero gives f(x,u,x)=-2A_x(u), forcing u to coincide also. Thus the sole dependent support point in the fixed-middle fiber is indeed p.
- **Dimension and scheme structure.** The middle image has dimension 4+rank Q_u=6. Six sections on the smooth eightfold force every support component to have dimension at least two. Removing the sole dependent point leaves a dense independent open on every component. Exact residual reconstruction and the K8 upper bound make every component two-dimensional. At each point of X the six local equations have height six in the regular ambient local ring, hence form a regular sequence. This proves the local complete-intersection, Cohen--Macaulay, Gorenstein, and no-embedded-components assertions, including for nonreduced fibers. The Koszul resolution is therefore justified, rather than inferred merely from the number of equations.
- **Cycle and adjunction.** The regular section of O(1,1) raised to the sixth direct sum gives class (h_1+h_2)^6 and dualizing sheaf O_X(1,1). Direct coefficient extraction gives bidegrees 15,20,15 and Segre degree 70.
- **Cohomology.** For the Koszul terms O(-k,-k), k=1,...,4 are acyclic; k=5 and 6 contribute only H^8, of dimensions 1 and 25 before the exterior multiplicities. The only nonzero differential in that row is C^25 to C^6. Its dual is precisely the inclusion of the six independent defining sections into H^0(O(1,1)), so its rank is six. The remaining hypercohomology consists of C in degree zero and C^19 in degree two; there is no possible higher differential between these terms. Consequently h^0=1, h^1=0, h^2=19 and chi=20.
- **Canonical embedding.** Twisting the Koszul complex by O(1,1) leaves, in total degree zero, exactly the quotient of H^0(O(1,1)) by the six defining equations. The remaining nonzero negative Koszul term is O(-5,-5) in Koszul degree -6 with H^8, hence total degree two and no effect on this quotient. Thus the canonical sections form a 19-dimensional space, and their morphism is the restriction of the Segre embedding to the linear P^18 cut out by the six independent hyperplanes. This is a closed immersion of the full scheme.
- **Boundary model.** Expansion at p gives exactly a+b+B(a,b)=0 and q(a,b)=0. The first equations eliminate b formally because their b-Jacobian at zero is the identity. The remaining equations have independent quadratic parts -q(a,a), a basis of the residual annihilator pencil. No linear equation remains, so embedding dimension is four, while local dimension is two. For coprime initial quadrics the initial forms form a regular sequence, yielding the asserted associated graded complete intersection and multiplicity four. The proof correctly withholds that tangent-cone conclusion in the common-factor case.

- **Swap-equivariant refinement.** Symmetry cancels the cross terms under a=s+t, b=s-t. The implicit equation for s has invertible linear part 2I and is invariant under t to -t, so its unique solution is even, beginning with B(t,t)/2. Substitution into q(s,s)-q(t,t) gives exactly the stated quartic term q(B(t,t),B(t,t))/4, with remainder of order at least six. The induced swap is exactly t to -t; its only fixed support point is p by the dependent-zero calculation.

No claim of reducedness, irreducibility, existence of rank-two middles, or invariants of the residual projective closure is warranted or made. 

## Common-factor multiplicity supplement

PASS. The argument in COMMON_FACTOR_MULTIPLICITY.md is rigorous for both common-factor pencil types and does not assume reducedness of R.

- A two-dimensional Cohen--Macaulay local C-algebra has a minimal reduction of its maximal ideal generated by two sufficiently general linear combinations of any chosen minimal generators. Here the four tangent coordinates are such generators. Reduction preserves multiplicity, and the Cohen--Macaulay parameter-ideal formula identifies it with the length of the resulting Artinian plane slice. This is a nonempty open condition on the defining pair of linear forms, hence also on the corresponding Grassmannian chart.
- Since l_1,l_2 span a two-dimensional space, a basis can be chosen with neither proportional to z, even when z lies in that space. All three forms are then pairwise independent. Restriction to a general two-plane preserves each pair's independence. The finite intersection of these nonempty opens with the reduction open is nonempty. Thus the squared-factor case is genuinely included; no independence of all three forms is needed.
- On the slice each h_i has a nondegenerate binary quadratic initial term z*l_i. Its two smooth formal branches are distinct and uniquely determined by their tangent lines. Invariance of h_i under central inversion therefore preserves each branch separately. Writing the shared-tangent branches as s=phi_i(r) makes the phi_i odd and of order at least three.
- Finite colength of (h_1,h_2) excludes any common branch, so phi_1-phi_2 is nonzero. Its order is at least three. The remaining three cross-branch intersections are transverse, each of multiplicity one, because all three tangent lines are distinct. Additivity of intersection multiplicity gives e(R) at least six exactly as stated.

The parity strengthening also passes: the nonzero difference phi_1-phi_2 is odd, so its order is odd. Adding the three transverse intersection contributions makes e(R) even. Thus the precise audited conclusion is that e(R) is even and at least six.
