# Residual-rank-two fibers are complete-intersection surfaces

## Statement

Work over C. Let dim V=5 and let f:V^3 -> J be trilinear, outer symmetric and cyclic zero, with every A_a:V/Ca -> J, b -> f(a,a,b), injective. Fix a middle line [u] with residual rank rank Q_u=2. No equivariance, hypothesis (H), or finiteness of the middle stratum is required for this pointwise theorem.

Let X_u be the full scheme-theoretic ordered outer zero fiber

    X_u = {([x],[z]) in P(V) x P(V) : f(x,u,z)=0}.

Then:

1. X_u is a pure two-dimensional local complete intersection, cut out globally by six independent divisors of bidegree (1,1). It is Cohen--Macaulay and Gorenstein; no reducedness or irreducibility is asserted.
2. Its only dependent support point is p=([u],[u]). Every irreducible component has dense independent locus. Consequently the independent-zero fiber, equivalently the compatible residual rank-two locus, has dimension EXACTLY two. In particular an attempted universal improvement of the K8 upper bound to one is impossible whenever a rank-two middle exists.
3. Its cycle class is (h_1+h_2)^6. Its bidegrees are (15,20,15), its Segre degree is 70, its dualizing sheaf is O_X(1,1), and chi(O_X)=20. In fact h^0(O_X)=1, h^1(O_X)=0 and h^2(O_X)=19.
4. The boundary point p is singular, with embedding dimension four. In formal coordinates it is a complete intersection of two power series in four variables whose quadratic initial terms are precisely a basis of the annihilator pencil K_u^perp. If the two pencil quadrics have no common factor, its tangent cone is their complete intersection and its local multiplicity is four.

Degree and cohomology statements concern the full fiber scheme, with its possible multiplicities. They do not assign these invariants to each reduced component or to the residual projective closure in P(V/Cu) x P(V/Cu).

## 1. Six equations and the lower dimension bound

The image of the middle linear map

    b_u: Sym^2 V -> J,   xz -> f(x,u,z)

contains im A_u, which has dimension four. Its quotient by this subspace is exactly im Q_u. Therefore rank b_u=4+2=6. Choosing a basis of its image writes the equation f(x,u,z)=0 as six independent bilinear scalar equations on the smooth eightfold P^4 x P^4.

The point p lies in their common zero scheme by cyclic zero. The height theorem implies that every irreducible component of that nonempty zero scheme has dimension at least 8-6=2.

Repeated-input injectivity, together with outer symmetry and cyclic zero, implies that any zero triple with all three entries nonzero is either independent or all three projective lines coincide. The proof is recalled in ../full-support-rank-five-dimension-bound/PROOF.md, Section 1: for independent a,b the two repeated values A_a(b) and A_b(a) are linearly independent. A relation A_a(b)=t A_b(a) contradicts injectivity at a+t b; cyclic zero then excludes every other dependent zero triple. For our fixed middle, the sole dependent point is p.

Thus no component can consist of dependent points: every component has a dense open independent locus. Exact residual reconstruction identifies that locus with the compatible ordered residual pairs for dim K_u=8, with no extra lifting parameter. The universal K8 theorem in ../diagonal-hartogs-fiber-bound/UNSQUARED_PENCIL.md bounds its dimension by two. All components of X_u consequently have dimension exactly two.

## 2. Complete intersection and invariants

Locally the ideal is generated by six functions in a regular local ring, with height six. A regular local ring is Cohen--Macaulay; a height-six ideal generated by six elements is a complete-intersection ideal, and the displayed six generators form a regular sequence. This also rules out embedded associated components. Hence the global Koszul complex of these six (1,1) sections resolves O_X.

The cycle and adjunction formulas give

    [X]=(h_1+h_2)^6,
    omega_X = (O(-5,-5) tensor O(6,6))|_X = O_X(1,1).

In the Chow ring Z[h_1,h_2]/(h_1^5,h_2^5),

    integral_X h_1^2 = 15,
    integral_X h_1 h_2 = 20,
    integral_X h_2^2 = 15.

Thus integral_X (h_1+h_2)^2=70. Koszul Euler characteristics give

    chi(O_X) = sum_{k=0}^6 (-1)^k binom(6,k) chi(O(-k,-k))
             = 1-6+25 = 20.

For k=1,...,4 all cohomology of O(-k,-k) vanishes. For k=5,6 its only cohomology is H^8, of dimensions 1 and 25 respectively. The induced map from the k=6 term to the k=5 term is C^25 -> C^6; by Serre duality its dual sends the six coordinate basis vectors to the six independent bilinear equations, so it has rank six. The Koszul hypercohomology spectral sequence therefore gives h^0=1, h^1=0, h^2=25-6=19, with no higher cohomology.

The dualizing sheaf is very ample: it is the restriction of the Segre embedding line bundle. Twisting the Koszul resolution by O(1,1) shows that restriction of ambient bilinear sections is surjective with kernel the six-dimensional space of defining equations. (The intervening negative twists have no cohomology that can affect H^0 or H^1.) Hence h^0(omega_X)=25-6=19. Its complete dualizing linear system embeds X in P^18, as the scheme-theoretic codimension-six linear section of the Segre P^4 x P^4 in P^24. Thus X is a canonically embedded Gorenstein surface of degree 70, possibly reducible or nonreduced. This does not assert that X is a smooth minimal surface of general type.

These computations remain valid if X is nonreduced.

## 3. Boundary local model and its pencil tangent cone

Choose V=Cu direct-sum E, and split im b_u as im A_u direct-sum R, where dim R=2. Use A_u to identify its first summand with E. Write

    f(a,u,b)=A_u(B(a,b))+q(a,b),

with symmetric bilinear maps B:E x E -> E and q:E x E -> R. The linear map induced by q on Sym^2 E is Q_u after identifying its image with R. Its two scalar quadratic forms q(a,a) span K_u^perp.

In the affine chart x=u+a, z=u+b centered at p, the exact equations are

    a+b+B(a,b)=0,
    q(a,b)=0.

Put C(a)(b)=B(a,b). Since I+C(0)=I, the first four equations eliminate b uniquely near the origin:

    b=-(I+C(a))^{-1}a.

The completed local ring is therefore

    C[[a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4]] / (g_1(a),g_2(a)),
    g(a)=-q(a,(I+C(a))^{-1}a).

Its quadratic initial terms are -q(a,a); all other terms have degree at least three. The local dimension is two by Section 1 and the tangent space has dimension four, so p is singular with embedding dimension four.

There is a swap-equivariant refinement of this normal form. Put

    a=s+t, b=s-t.

The equations become

    2s+B(s,s)-B(t,t)=0,
    q(s,s)-q(t,t)=0.

The first equation determines a unique formal s=s(t), since its derivative in s is 2I. Uniqueness gives s(-t)=s(t), and

    s(t) = (1/2) B(t,t) + terms of degree at least four.

Consequently the two remaining equations can be chosen EVEN in t:

    -q(t,t) + (1/4) q(B(t,t),B(t,t)) + terms of degree at least six = 0.

In these coordinates the outer swap acts exactly by t -> -t. In particular it acts as -Id on the four-dimensional Zariski tangent space. There are no cubic terms in this equivariant presentation. The only fixed support point of the swap on X is p, since a fixed outer pair would be a dependent zero triple.

If the two initial quadrics are coprime, they form a regular sequence in C[a_1,...,a_4]. The regular-initial-sequence lemma then identifies the associated graded ring with their homogeneous quotient. For completeness, this standard filtered lemma follows by canceling successive lowest-degree terms of any relation: the only syzygies of a two-element homogeneous regular sequence are multiples of (q_2,-q_1), so cancellation never creates an additional initial generator. Its Hilbert series is (1-t^2)^2/(1-t)^4, giving multiplicity four.

If the pencil has a common factor, we make no equality assertion for the tangent cone or multiplicity: further initial equations can appear from cancellations. The six-bilinear complete-intersection theorem itself still holds.

## 4. Consequences and scope

Residual rank two thus forces actual independent zero surfaces. Exclusion of these specific rank-two fiber surfaces is exactly as strong as exclusion of rank-two middle points; it cannot follow from a stronger pointwise dimension bound. If the independently established finite rank-two middle locus is retained, these surfaces contribute projective dimension two and affine dimension five after restoring the three nonzero input scales.

No claim is made that rank-two middles exist for the actual equivariant operation. This theorem classifies what their fibers must be if they exist. It uses the proved K8 upper bound, the elementary six-equation lower bound, and exact reconstruction.
