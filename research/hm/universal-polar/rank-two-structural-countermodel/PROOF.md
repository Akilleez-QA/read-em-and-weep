# A synthetic rank-two polar model without normalizer equivariance

## Result and scope

Work over C. There exists a 20-dimensional vector space J and a trilinear
operation f: V × V × V → J, with dim V=5, satisfying all of the following:

1. f is symmetric in its outer variables and satisfies the cyclic-zero
   identity.
2. For every nonzero a, the repeated-input map
   A_a: V/Ca → J, [b] ↦ f(a,a,b), is injective.
3. The middle residual map Q_a has rank 10 at every line [a]≠[u].
4. At a specified nonzero middle vector u, Q_u has rank exactly 7 and
   its three-dimensional kernel is the transverse common-factor space
   x-bar · Z-bar in V/Cu = Cx-bar ⊕ Z-bar.
5. The polar map c ↦ f(x,u,c) has rank exactly 2 and kernel Z, where
   V = Cu ⊕ Cx ⊕ Z and dim Z=3.

This is a synthetic quotient family, not an evaluation or counterexample
for the actual source operation. It can be chosen demonstrably not
equivariant for the intended normalizer. Thus the identities, global
repeated-input injection, and generic residual rank 10 alone do not
exclude the surviving rank-two polar configuration. Additional structure
is necessary for such an exclusion; this argument does not prove that
normalizer equivariance by itself excludes it.

The construction places the configuration at one middle point. It does
not construct a two-dimensional middle surface or a seven-dimensional
affine independent-zero component. Its residual-rank-drop support is
exactly the single point [u], hence has codimension 4 in P(V). In
particular it also satisfies a codimension-at-least-two rank-drop
hypothesis. No reducedness assertion about the degeneracy scheme is
intended by the description of its support.

## 1. The universal tensor space and its middle images

Set

    W = ker(V ⊗ Λ²V → Λ³V),

where the arrow sends a ⊗ (b ∧ c) to a ∧ b ∧ c. The arrow is surjective,
so dim W = 5·10 − 10 = 40. Define

    f0(a,b,c) = a ⊗ (b ∧ c) + c ⊗ (b ∧ a).

Both terms have opposite exterior products, so f0 takes values in W.
Its outer symmetry is immediate, and its cyclic sum is zero by pairwise
cancellation. In particular,

    f0(a,a,b) = a ⊗ (a ∧ b) =: A0_a(b).

The values of f0 span W. To see this in a basis, terms with a repeated
index are generated by A0_a(b). For three distinct basis vectors, put

    t1 = e_i ⊗ (e_j ∧ e_k),
    t2 = e_j ⊗ (e_k ∧ e_i),
    t3 = e_k ⊗ (e_i ∧ e_j).

Exterior multiplication has the same value on all three, and the two
differences t1−t3 and t2−t1 are values of f0. These generate the kernel
on that three-dimensional summand.

For every nonzero a define

    F_a = im(Sym²V → W, bc ↦ f0(b,a,c)).

Here bc denotes multiplication in the symmetric algebra; in particular,
the image of b² is f0(b,a,b). Choose a complement V=Ca ⊕ U. Then

    Sym²V = Ca² ⊕ aU ⊕ Sym²U.

The first summand maps to zero, aU maps isomorphically onto
a ⊗ (a ∧ U), and Sym²U maps injectively into U ⊗ (a ∧ U): after
identifying a ∧ U with U, this is the ordinary symmetric-tensor inclusion
bc ↦ b ⊗ c + c ⊗ b. The two nonzero images have different first-factor
summands. Consequently

    ker(Sym²V → W) = Ca²,
    dim F_a = 4+10 = 14,
    A0_a(V) ⊂ F_a,
    dim A0_a(V) = 4.

In particular there is a canonical isomorphism

    Sym²(V/Ca) ≅ F_a/A0_a(V).

This verifies the universal rank-14 assertion without any source matrix
or numerical calculation.

## 2. The entire repeated-input variety is closed and has dimension 7

Let X be the projective variety of nonzero tensors A0_a(b):

    X = { [a ⊗ (a ∧ b)] : a ∧ b ≠ 0 } ⊂ P(W).

It is the image of the projective flag variety of a line ℓ in a
two-plane P in V, under

    (ℓ ⊂ P) ↦ [ℓ ⊗ Λ²P].

The line and plane are recovered from the two tensor factors, so this
identifies X with the corresponding Segre-Plücker flag embedding. In
particular X is closed and

    dim X = 4+3 = 7.

Equivalently, X is exactly the rank-one tensor locus in P(W), with tensor
rank taken in V ⊗ Λ²V. Indeed, if a ⊗ β belongs to W with a and β
nonzero, then a ∧ β=0. The decomposition V=Ca ⊕ U gives
β=a ∧ b, since exterior multiplication by a is injective on Λ²U.

Therefore a quotient q:W→J preserves repeated-input injection at every
nonzero a if and only if its projective kernel avoids X.

## 3. The prescribed three-plane and its intersections with F_a

Fix an abstract decomposition

    V = Cu ⊕ Cx ⊕ Z,       dim Z=3,

and set

    L = { f0(x,u,z) : z ∈ Z }
      = { x ⊗ (u ∧ z) + z ⊗ (u ∧ x) : z ∈ Z }.

Projection of the first factor to Cx shows that z ↦ f0(x,u,z) is
injective, so dim L=3. For z≠0, the first-factor vectors x,z are
independent, and the second-factor vectors u ∧ z,u ∧ x are independent.
Thus every nonzero element of L has tensor rank exactly 2. It follows
that

    P(L) ∩ X = ∅,       L ⊂ F_u.

There is also the important uniform statement

    L ∩ F_a = 0 whenever [a] ≠ [u].                         (1)

To prove it, note first that F_a ⊂ V ⊗ (a ∧ V). If a nonzero
f0(x,u,z) belongs to F_a, its second-factor support must therefore obey

    span(u ∧ z, u ∧ x) ⊂ a ∧ V.

The two generators are independent. The first inclusion implies
a ∈ span(u,z), and the second implies a ∈ span(u,x), since a ∧ u ∧ z
and a ∧ u ∧ x must vanish. Their intersection is Cu, proving (1).

## 4. Simultaneous generic choice of a 20-dimensional kernel

Fix any nonzero w with [w]≠[u]. Put E=W/L, so dim E=37. Kernels K of
dimension 20 containing L are parametrized by the irreducible
Grassmannian

    G = Gr(17,E),       dim G = 17·20 = 340,

with K the inverse image of the corresponding 17-plane M⊂E. We impose
three conditions on M.

### 4.1 Avoid every repeated-input tensor

Projection with center P(L) restricts to a morphism

    π|X : X → P(E)

because P(L)∩X is empty. Its image Y is closed: X is projective and the
restricted morphism is proper. Also dim Y≤7. This properness is what
allows a closed-incidence argument; no unclosed rational-projection
image is being used.

The bad incidence is

    I_X = { (y,M) ∈ Y × G : y ∈ P(M) }.

For a fixed y the fiber is Gr(16,36), of dimension 16·20=320. Hence
dim I_X≤327<340. Projection to G is proper, so its image is closed and
proper. Its complement is a nonempty open subset of G. On that open,

    P(M)∩Y=∅, equivalently P(K)∩X=∅.                       (2)

### 4.2 Retain exactly the prescribed kernel at u

The image H_u=F_u/L in E has dimension 11. The condition

    M ∩ H_u = 0

is nonempty open because 17+11≤37. Equivalently,

    K ∩ F_u = L.                                           (3)

Nonemptiness here can be seen by choosing a 17-plane inside a complement
to H_u; openness follows from the maximal-rank minors of the natural
map M→E/H_u.

### 4.3 Retain full rank at w

By (1), the image H_w of F_w in E has dimension 14. The condition

    M ∩ H_w = 0

is again nonempty open, since 17+14≤37. It is equivalent to

    K ∩ F_w = 0.                                           (4)

The equivalences in (3) and (4) follow by taking inverse images under
W→E; in (4) one uses L∩F_w=0.

The intersection of these three nonempty open subsets of the
irreducible G is nonempty. Thus K satisfying (2), (3), and (4) exists;
indeed these K form a nonempty Zariski-open family in G. This proves
existence algebraically without sampling, a large computation, or a
choice of actual source inputs.

### 4.4 Strengthening: retain full rank at every other middle line

In fact K can be chosen to satisfy (4) simultaneously for every
[w]≠[u]. Here the parameter base is not proper, so we explicitly take
the closure of the incidence image and bound its dimension.

Let B=P(V)\{[u]}, a variety of dimension 4. The spaces F_a form a
rank-14 subbundle F of W⊗O over P(V): the defining map

    Sym²V ⊗ O(−1) → W ⊗ O

has constant rank 14 by Section 1. On B, (1) says that the projection
F→E⊗O_B has rank 14 everywhere. Its image H is therefore a rank-14
subbundle, with fibers H_a=(F_a+L)/L.

Consider the relative incidence

    T = { (a,[h],M) : a∈B, [h]∈P(H_a), [h]∈P(M) }.

The base P_B(H) has dimension 4+13=17. Over each (a,[h]), the choices
of M are the 17-planes of E containing the line Ch, a Grassmannian
Gr(16,36) of dimension 320. Equivalently, T is the corresponding
relative Grassmannian over P_B(H). Consequently

    dim T = 17+320 = 337 < 340 = dim G.

The image of T→G is exactly the set of M meeting at least one H_a for
a∈B. This image is constructible; its Zariski closure has dimension
at most 337, by the dimension bound for images of finite-type
morphisms. Its complement in G is therefore nonempty open. Every M
in that complement satisfies

    M∩H_a=0 for every [a]≠[u],
    equivalently K∩F_a=0 for every [a]≠[u].                 (5)

Intersect this nonempty open with the nonempty opens in Sections 4.1
and 4.2. Irreducibility of G again gives a nonempty open family. From
now on choose K in this strengthened family. It automatically satisfies
the fixed-w condition in Section 4.3. The use of the closure above
avoids any assumption that a projection from the nonproper base B has
closed image.

## 5. The quotient operation and its exact ranks

Choose such a K, put J=W/K, and let f=q∘f0. Then dim J=20 and the two
trilinear identities descend. Condition (2) proves that all maps

    A_a:V/Ca→J,       [b]↦q(A0_a(b)),

are injective, simultaneously for every nonzero a.

Define the residual map intrinsically by

    Q_a:Sym²(V/Ca) → J/A_a(V/Ca),
    [b][c] ↦ f(b,a,c) modulo A_a(V/Ca).

Changing a lift by a multiple of a changes the displayed value only by
an A_a-value, so Q_a is well defined. Since q(A0_a(V)) has dimension 4,

    rank Q_a = dim q(F_a) − 4.

Conditions (3) and (5) consequently give

    rank Q_u = (14−3)−4 = 7,
    rank Q_a = 14−4 = 10 for every [a]≠[u].

The rank-14 locus of Sym²V→J, bc↦f(b,a,c), is open in P(V), as follows
from its maximal minors. It contains [w], and is therefore dense. On it
Q_a has rank 10. The stronger calculation above establishes that its
rank-drop support is exactly {[u]}, of dimension zero and codimension 4.
This is a statement about the support, not the possible nonreduced
structure of its determinantal scheme.

With the chosen splitting V/Cu=Cx-bar⊕Z-bar, the kernel of Q_u is
exactly the image of L in F_u/A0_u(V), namely

    ker Q_u = x-bar · Z-bar.

For completeness, the general kernel formula is

    ker Q_u ≅ ((K∩F_u)+A0_u(V))/A0_u(V).

Here K∩F_u=L, and L∩A0_u(V)=0. Each nonzero x-bar·z-bar has symmetric
tensor rank 2, and Cx-bar∩Z-bar=0. This is precisely the transverse
common-factor type, with no rank-one point in its projective kernel
plane. Moreover f(x,u,z)=0 in J for every z∈Z, so its compatibility
vanishes identically, not merely after quotienting by A_u.

## 6. Exact rank-two polar and the two outer-factor sheets

The polar map P:V→J, c↦f(x,u,c), vanishes on Z and has values

    P(u) = q(u ⊗ (u ∧ x)) = A_u(x),
    P(x) = q(2x ⊗ (u ∧ x)) = −2A_x(u).

These two values are linearly independent. Indeed every nonzero tensor
in

    span(u ⊗ (u ∧ x), x ⊗ (u ∧ x))

is a rank-one tensor in W, hence represents a point of X; condition (2)
says that q is injective on this two-plane. Thus

    rank P = 2,       ker P=Z.

At the middle line [u], the separately projectivized independent-zero
fiber is, set-theoretically, exactly

    {([x],[u],[z]) : [z]∈P(Z)}
      disjoint-union
    {([z],[u],[x]) : [z]∈P(Z)}.

Indeed an independent zero has nonzero residual product in x-bar·Z-bar,
so uniqueness of the two factors forces those residual directions.
After scaling, its outer lifts have the form x+αu and z+βu. Their f-value
is αA_u(z)+βA_u(x), which vanishes only for α=β=0 by repeated-input
injection. Conversely the prescribed pairs are zero by construction.

## 7. Making the lack of normalizer equivariance explicit

Suppose N is the intended linear or projective normalizer on V, and
choose an element g with [gu]≠[u]. By the strengthened construction,
every such g gives residual rank 10 at [gu]. For the original fixed-w
construction, taking w=gu would already have sufficed.
The standard Heisenberg normalizer admits such a g at every line: its
Heisenberg subgroup has no invariant line. Abstractly, the latter
follows from two Heisenberg generators whose commutator is a nontrivial
central scalar, which cannot hold on a common invariant line.

Then the quotient has residual rank 7 at [u] and 10 at [gu]. It cannot
be N-equivariant for any action of N on J compatible with f: such
equivariance would identify the residual maps along N-orbits and
preserve their ranks. Equivalently, K is not g-stable, because g carries
F_u to F_gu while dim(K∩F_u)=3 and dim(K∩F_gu)=0.

This uses only an abstract group element moving a line. No actual
source matrix, input, or operation value is evaluated. The conclusion
is a strict separation of hypotheses: the coarse tensor identities and
rank assumptions permit the surviving configuration, whereas the
constructed model deliberately fails the extra normalizer symmetry.

## 8. Consequence for structural exclusion arguments

A proof excluding every rank-two polar using only outer symmetry,
cyclic-zero, global repeated-input injection, and generic residual rank
10 would contradict the model above. Those assumptions alone are
therefore insufficient. Adding a codimension-at-least-two rank-drop
hypothesis still does not exclude the model: its sole exceptional
middle point has codimension 4.

This does not settle occurrence for the actual normalizer-equivariant
operation, exclude its exceptional middle surfaces, or prove its
determinant section nonzero. Those are separate questions requiring
additional information. The construction and proof are symbolic; the
only output is this local proof artifact.
