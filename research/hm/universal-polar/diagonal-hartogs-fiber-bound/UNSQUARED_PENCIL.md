# Closure of the unsquared common-factor pencil case

Work over C. Let E have dimension four, let K ⊂ Sym²E have dimension eight, and let v:K→E be linear. Assume square avoidance:

    e ≠ 0 and e² ∈ K imply v(e²) ∉ Ce.

An ordered pair ([a],[b]) of distinct projective points is compatible when ab∈K and v(ab)∈span(a,b). All dimensions concern reduced supports.

## 1. The remaining pencil

Suppose K⊥=span(zx,zy), where z,x,y are linearly independent elements of E*. Set A=ker z and R=ker x∩ker y. Then dim A=3, dim R=2, and dim(A∩R)=1. Choose w completing z,x,y to a basis of E*. The restrictions x,y,w are coordinates on A. Write

    ρ:E→E/R≅C²,    ρ(a)=(x(a),y(a)).

The product incidence I of pairs with ab∈K is the closed subvariety of P(E)×P(E) cut out by

    z(a)x(b)+x(a)z(b)=0,
    z(a)y(b)+y(a)z(b)=0.

A nonzero normalization factor from symmetric polarization has no effect on these equations. Its diagonal is the square locus

    B=P(A)∪P(R).

Indeed the square equations are z(e)x(e)=z(e)y(e)=0. Also Sym²A⊂K.

Assume for contradiction that an integral projective threefold Z⊂I has a dense open subset of compatible distinct pairs. If Z⊂P(A)×P(A), the three-dimensional subspace lemma of PROOF.md excludes Z. Hence assume Z is not contained there.

## 2. Proportionality without any division by a projected coordinate

The open subset Z°=Z\(P(A)×P(A)) is nonempty and dense. At every point of Z°, at least one of z(a),z(b) is nonzero. The incidence equations, written as

    z(a)ρ(b)+z(b)ρ(a)=0,

therefore imply

    ρ(a)∧ρ(b)=0.

For example, if z(a)≠0, then ρ(b)=−z(b)ρ(a)/z(a); if z(a)=0, then z(b)≠0 and ρ(a)=0. Thus points with one or both projections zero are included, and no projective map to P(E/R) is required.

The identity ρ(a)∧ρ(b)=0 is bihomogeneous and defines a closed condition, so it holds on all of Z. On the dense compatible open, v(ab) is a linear combination of a and b; consequently

    ρ(v(ab))∧ρ(a)=0.                                  (1)

This too is a polynomial identity on the product incidence: v is linear on K, and the tautological product ab is K-valued on I. More explicitly, either coordinate expression of ρ(v(ab)) is bilinear on I, and the wedge in (1) has bidegree (2,1). One can extend v linearly to Sym²E to obtain ambient polynomial representatives, whose restrictions to I are independent of the extension. Therefore (1) holds throughout Z by density, including all diagonal and zero-projection points.

## 3. Hartogs forces the whole plane diagonal

If dim(Z∩Δ)≤1, the diagonal–Hartogs extension lemma of PROOF.md gives a contradiction. Its codimension requirement is exactly satisfied: normalization is finite, so the inverse image of Z∩Δ has dimension at most one in the normal threefold and thus codimension at least two. The coefficients of v(ab) in the two independent tautological vectors extend as sections of invertible sheaves. Nonempty diagonal intersection follows from the positive intersection of any projective threefold in P³×P³ with Δ, as proved there. Their extended identity at a diagonal point contradicts square avoidance.

We may therefore assume dim(Z∩Δ)≥2. Every point of this intersection corresponds to a square in K, so

    Z∩Δ ⊂ Δ_A∪Δ_R,

where Δ_A≅P² and Δ_R≅P¹. A closed subset of this union of dimension at least two must contain Δ_A: its two-dimensional irreducible component must be the whole irreducible plane Δ_A. In particular ([a],[a])∈Z for every nonzero a∈A.

Restrict (1) to this plane diagonal. With

    ρ(v(a²))=(F(x,y,w),G(x,y,w)),

where F and G are homogeneous quadrics, we obtain the polynomial identity

    yF−xG=0                                               (2)

in C[x,y,w]. The identity holds as a polynomial because it vanishes on all of P(A), equivalently on all of A.

## 4. The two remaining quadrics force a forbidden square

The relatively prime polynomials x and y in C[x,y,w] satisfy x | yF by (2), hence x | F. Write F=xℓ with ℓ homogeneous of degree one (allowing ℓ=0). Equation (2) then gives G=yℓ. Thus

    ρ(v(a²)−ℓ(a)a)=0    for every a∈A.

The homogeneous quadratic map

    H:A→R,    H(a)=v(a²)−ℓ(a)a

is well-defined because Sym²A⊂K. Choose a basis of the two-dimensional vector space R; its two coordinate functions H₁,H₂ are homogeneous quadratic polynomials in x,y,w.

These two quadrics have a common nonzero zero in A. One algebraic justification, covering identically zero or dependent quadrics as well, is the height theorem: an ideal generated by two elements in C[x,y,w] has height at most two. If their only affine common zero were the origin, the Nullstellensatz would make its radical (x,y,w), of height three, a contradiction. Equivalently, two plane conics over C always have nonempty projective intersection, with the degenerate cases included.

Choose such e≠0. Then e∈A, so e²∈K, and H(e)=0 gives

    v(e²)=ℓ(e)e.

This is a forbidden square-compatible point, also when ℓ(e)=0. Contradiction. Thus no such threefold Z exists in the remaining pencil case.

If a compatible component had dimension greater than three, take successive general ample hyperplane sections of its integral projective closure to obtain an integral threefold meeting its dense compatible distinct-pair open. The preceding argument applies to that threefold. Hence the entire compatible ordered locus has dimension at most two.

## 5. Universal eight-dimensional-kernel theorem

Every pencil W=K⊥ of two independent quadrics falls into one of the following cases:

1. No common factor. Its projective square base has dimension at most one, so the diagonal–Hartogs lemma excludes a compatible threefold.
2. A common linear factor z, with W=zL and z∈L. This is the squared-factor case handled in COROLLARIES.md, Section 3, by the hyperplane lemma and universal finite-compatible-partner-plane lemma.
3. A common linear factor z, with W=zL and z∉L. Choosing a basis x,y of L gives precisely the case proved above.

A common quadratic factor cannot occur for two independent quadrics. These cases are exhaustive. Consequently:

**For every eight-dimensional subspace K⊂Sym²E with dim E=4 and every square-avoiding linear correction v:K→E, the compatible ordered distinct-pair locus has dimension at most two.**

No generality assumption on the pencil, transverse-square assumption, or generic-net assumption is used.

## 6. Exact global consequence under (H)

Use the trilinear setup and reconstruction established in ../full-support-rank-five-dimension-bound/PROOF.md: dim V=5; f is outer symmetric and cyclic zero; every repeated-input map A_a:V/Ca→J is injective; E_u=V/Cu; Q_u is the residual map; and K_u=ker Q_u. The induced correction is square-avoiding. A residual-rank-two middle has dim K_u=8. Exact outer reconstruction identifies its separately projectivized independent-zero fiber with the compatible ordered locus, without an additional lift parameter. Therefore every residual-rank-two fiber has dimension at most two.

Assume the previously established, separately sourced hypothesis

    dim { [u]∈P(V): rank Q_u<10 }≤2.                 (H)

Then the independent-zero locus over the residual-rank-two stratum has separately projectivized dimension at most 2+2=4. Restoring the three independent nonzero input scales adds exactly three, so its affine dimension is at most seven. The same bound holds for every irreducible component whose generic middle has residual rank two; lower-rank boundary fibers do not increase the dimension of that component beyond its dense open part.

Combining this result with the universal K7 theorem in PROOF.md and the previously established rank-four, rank-five, and rank-at-least-six bounds yields:

**Under (H), any independent-zero component of affine dimension at least eight must have generic residual rank at most one.**

Equivalently, all components generically of residual rank at least two have affine dimension at most seven. This does not resolve kernels of dimension nine or ten, does not exclude affine dimension seven, and does not derive (H) from the abstract trilinear assumptions alone.

The conclusions use abstract algebraic geometry and do not identify an actual source operation.
