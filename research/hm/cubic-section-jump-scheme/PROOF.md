# The nonreduced conductor section-jump schemes

Work over C. This is an authored structural proof conditional on the pinned inputs, not a formal verification. It concerns the algebraic conductor-gluing family for V51, not the original full-adjoint or algebraicity goals. No selected actual source coefficients, points, derivatives, matrices or ranks are evaluated.

Set R=C[lambda_1^{+/-1},...,lambda_12^{+/-1}], x_i=1-lambda_i, m=(x_1,...,x_12), and J=(x_i x_j : i<j). Let p:V x T->T. Define the natural determinantal h0>=r scheme, for 1<=r<=10, by

    Z_r = V(Fitt_(54+r)(R^1 p_* L)).                    (1)

The convention is that Fitt_q of a module presented by R^a->R^b is generated by its (b-q)-minors. These intrinsic Fitting ideals yield the usual degree-zero cohomology-jump determinantal schemes in this family. We prove

    I(Z_r) = J^(6-r) m^(r-1),       1<=r<=5,
    I(Z_r) = m^(11-r),              6<=r<=10.           (2)

In particular the positive-section scheme is V(J^5), not merely the reduced union V(J) of twelve coordinate axes. Along the generic point of each axis its transverse length is 1365.

## 2. The actual derived cohomology model

The normalization sequence on V x T is

    0 -> L -> nu_*D_T -> Q_T -> 0,

where Q_T has global sections T_0 tensor R, T_0=direct-sum_i T_i, of dimension 60. Because D_T is pulled back from M, its derived global sections are RΓ(M,D) tensor_C R. A complex of C-vector spaces splits into its cohomology and contractible terms. Hence this object is isomorphic, noncanonically, to S_R in degree 0 plus W_R in degree 1. The induced derived map to T_0 tensor R in degree 0 is determined by its degree-zero map: the possible contribution from W_R in degree 1 is zero, since Hom in the derived category from W_R[-1] to (T_0)_R is Ext^1_R(W_R,(T_0)_R)=0. The source W_R is free. The degree-zero map is exactly

    A:S_R -> (T_0)_R,       s |-> (x_i E_i(s))_i.

Taking the shifted cone therefore gives a two-term finite free model for Rp_*L:

    C^0=R^10 --d=(A,0)--> C^1=R^60 direct-sum R^5.     (3)

The five extra zero rows are essential: H1(M,D) has not been discarded. Thus R^1 p_*L=coker d, all higher direct images vanish, and the Euler characteristic is -55. Every base change R->A' gives the same derived model by tensoring (3). One can also obtain this directly from the normalization sequence after base change; its quotient is flat over the parameter ring. No assertion that H0 itself commutes with arbitrary base change is made.

At a field-valued parameter of differential rank q, h0=10-q and h1=65-q=55+h0. Formula (1) is consequently defined by I_(11-r)(d) and has precisely the field points with h0>=r. Its ideal is independent of the splitting, bases, and presentation: it is the Fitting ideal of the intrinsically defined coherent module R^1 p_*L. This also explains its compatibility with base change here, since the degree-one cohomology of the two-term complex is a cokernel. Restricting to the ample open or completing at the identity preserves these formulas.

These schemes impose determinantal conditions. They are not asserted to represent the functor 'there exists a nonzero section' over every nonreduced Artin algebra; torsion sections and freeness of section submodules are different conditions.

## 3. Multiplicity-space factorization

For completeness, a fixed nontrivial central character of H has a unique irreducible representation E, of dimension 5. Write generators g,s,z with g^5=s^5=z^5=1 and gs=zsg. If z acts as a primitive fifth root eta, s carries each g-eigenspace to the eigenspace whose eigenvalue is multiplied by eta. From any nonzero g-eigenvector v, the span of v,sv,...,s^4v is stable under g,s and is irreducible: g has five distinct eigenvalues and s cycles their lines. Changing the starting eigenvalue only cyclically relabels this model. Maschke's averaging argument decomposes any representation of H over C into these copies when the central character is fixed. Schur's lemma gives End_H(E)=C.

It follows that S=E tensor C^2 and T_i is isomorphic to E. Choose such intertwining identifications, with no numerical choice of source evaluations. Each map E_i becomes Id_E tensor ell_i for a nonzero linear form ell_i on C^2. Complementarity in PR640 means ell_i and ell_j are linearly independent whenever i!=j.

Let B be the 12-by-2 matrix whose row i is x_i ell_i. After reordering rows and columns, A is a block diagonal matrix of five copies of B. The entries of each ell_i are constants, at least one nonzero, and each two-row determinant is a nonzero constant. Consequently

    I_1(B)=m,                  I_2(B)=J.               (4)

No actual constants are selected or computed. Their nonvanishing alone suffices.

## 4. Every minor ideal

For a block diagonal matrix with five copies of B, a nonzero selected square minor must select equally many rows and columns in each block. Otherwise all determinant terms vanish, since nonzero entries connect only matching blocks. Within each block the number selected is 0, 1, or 2. In the balanced case the determinant is, up to sign, the product of the five corresponding minors. Conversely any product of five such minors is realized by selecting the indicated rows and columns block by block. Repeated orbit indices in different blocks are allowed, because those are distinct rows of A.

Writing a for the number of 2-minor blocks and b for the number of 1-minor blocks, we obtain an equality of ideals, not merely radicals:

    I_k(A) = sum_(2a+b=k, a+b<=5) J^a m^b.            (5)

Zero rows in d do not change any positive-size minor ideal. Since J is contained in m^2, the summands of (5) decrease by inclusion as a increases with k fixed. The smallest feasible a is max(0,k-5). Thus

    I_k(d)=m^k,                         1<=k<=5,
    I_k(d)=J^(k-5)m^(10-k),              5<=k<=10.     (6)

Substitution k=11-r proves (2). In particular I_10(d)=J^5: every product of five two-row minors is a maximal minor using the five blocks, and every maximal minor is such a product or zero.

## 5. Support, transverse thickness, and the next distinction

The squarefree ideal J is the reduced ideal of the twelve axes. Explicitly,

    J = intersection_i P_i,       P_i=(x_j : j!=i).

This follows already in C[x_1,...,x_12], since a monomial belongs to the intersection exactly when it involves at least two different variables; localization by the units 1-x_i preserves the equality. For r<=5 the radical of (2) is J. For 6<=r<=10 it is m. Thus the field-valued classification from PR640 is recovered, while the different thresholds now have different scheme structures.

At the generic point of axis i, x_i is a unit, m becomes the unit ideal, and J becomes P_i. For 1<=r<=5 the localized ideal of Z_r is therefore P_i^(6-r). The regular local ring R_(P_i) has dimension 11 and residue field C(lambda_i). Its quotient by this power has length

    binomial(11+(6-r)-1,11) = binomial(16-r,11).

This counts monomials of degree less than 6-r in eleven transverse variables. For r=1 the length is binomial(15,11)=1365; for r=5 it is 1. Thus Z_5 is generically reduced along each axis, whereas Z_1 has a fifth-power transverse thickening. The identity also has the explicit threshold ideals J^(6-r)m^(r-1) or m^(11-r). In fact Z_1 has an embedded associated point there, as the following direct witness shows. Put f=x_1^4 x_2^4 x_3. Then f is not in J^5, since its degree is 9 whereas every monomial of J^5 has degree at least 10. This remains true after localizing by the 1-x_i: every product of these inverted polynomials has constant term 1, so its product with f still has a nonzero degree-9 term. But

    x_1 f=(x_1 x_2)^4(x_1 x_3),
    x_2 f=(x_1 x_2)^4(x_2 x_3),
    x_3 f=(x_1 x_2)^3(x_1 x_3)(x_2 x_3),
    x_i f=(x_1 x_2)^3(x_1 x_3)(x_2 x_i),   i>=4.

All these products lie in J^5. Thus the proper ideal (J^5:f) contains the maximal ideal m and must equal m. The nonzero class of f has annihilator m, proving that m is associated to R/J^5. It is embedded because each minimal axis prime P_i is strictly contained in m. This proves embeddedness without claiming a full primary decomposition.

There is a useful distinction between the natural determinant scheme and symmetry-reduced rank conditions: the smaller matrix B has rank<=1 scheme V(J), whereas the ordinary h0>=1 determinantal scheme for the actual rank-ten section space is V(J^5). Their field-valued points agree, but their nilpotent structures do not. Formula (1) specifies which convention is being used and makes the latter intrinsic.

## Scope and contribution

This determines every natural determinantal section-jump threshold for the actual algebraic twelve-parameter conductor family, including its nonreduced positive-section structure and transverse lengths. It does not equate Artin-valued nonzero-section existence with factoring through Z_1, assert all torus polarizations ample, choose a source evaluation, identify new adjoint/source solutions, or settle any broader algebraicity claim. The embedded identity of the positive-section scheme is also proved by an explicit annihilator witness. A full primary decomposition or lengths of its embedded primary components is not claimed.
