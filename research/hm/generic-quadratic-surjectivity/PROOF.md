# Generic quadratic surjectivity and translated order-six vanishing

## Statement and boundary

Work over C. Let S = Sym(V) be a polynomial ring in finitely many
degree-one variables, and let M be a finitely generated graded S-module
generated by M_0. Suppose dim M_3 = 2. Then

    U_M = { [q] in P(S_2) : q M_1 = M_3 }

is a nonempty Zariski-open subset. In particular, when dim V = 5,
U_M contains nondegenerate quadrics.

Apply this to the fixed primary module M = H^1_*(F) of a normalized
Horrocks–Mumford bundle F on a fixed Y = P^4. The pinned primary
resolution proves degree-zero generation, not just its Hilbert
function. For every q in U_M and every cubic r,

    M_3 / (q M_1 + r M_0) = 0,
    Q(q,r) = H^1(B(6)) / (q H^1(B(4)) + r H^1(B(3))) = 0,

where B = End_0(F). In fact q:H^1(B(4)) -> H^1(B(6)) is already
surjective.

Consequently there is a nonempty Zariski-open subset of PGL_5 such
that the entire paired-character order-six ideal coefficient
vanishes for the corresponding translated ordered-root maps, with
F held fixed. The precise family is defined in Section 5.

This is a generic relative-position theorem. It does not decide
whether the fixed original t_2 belongs to U_M, and it does not
evaluate Q(t_2,t_3) for the original fixed geometry. It does not
assert that projective transformations preserve F. No actual source
membership, source primitive, pure-square condition, module lift,
or algebraicity conclusion is supplied.

## 1. An elementary rank-one-space lemma

Let A and W be finite-dimensional vector spaces over a field k, and
let L be a nonzero linear subspace of Hom(A,W) all of whose elements
have rank at most one. Then at least one of the following holds:

1. The images of all elements of L lie in a common line in W.
2. A fixed hyperplane in A lies in the kernel of every element of L.

Write one nonzero element as
T = w tensor lambda, with w != 0 and lambda != 0. If
T' = w' tensor lambda' is another nonzero element and both
w,w' and lambda,lambda' are independent, there are a,b in A with

    (lambda(a),lambda'(a)) = (1,0),
    (lambda(b),lambda'(b)) = (0,1).

Then (T+T')(a)=w and (T+T')(b)=w', contradicting rank(T+T') <= 1.
Thus every T' has either image kw or functional proportional to
lambda.

If every image is contained in kw, conclusion 1 holds. Otherwise
choose T' = w' tensor lambda with w,w' independent, absorbing a
nonzero scalar into w'. For any nonzero T'' = w'' tensor lambda'',
if lambda'' is not proportional to lambda, the preceding two-map
argument applied to (T,T'') forces w'' in kw, while its application
to (T',T'') forces w'' in kw'. This is impossible. Thus all the
functionals are proportional to lambda, and ker(lambda) is the
common hyperplane of conclusion 2.

In the application W has dimension two and L is nonzero.
No classification theorem for bounded-rank matrix spaces is imported.

## 2. Commutativity excludes the common-kernel alternative

For q in S_2 let mu_q:M_1 -> M_3 be multiplication by q, and put
L = {mu_q:q in S_2}. Suppose no mu_q is surjective. Since dim M_3=2,
all elements of L have rank at most one.

Degree-zero generation gives

    M_1 = S_1 M_0,             M_3 = S_2 M_1.         (1)

Consequently the sum of the images of L is all of M_3. In particular
L != 0, and the common-image alternative of Section 1 is impossible.
The other alternative gives a nonzero lambda in M_1^vee and a
linear map G:S_2 -> M_3 such that

    q a = lambda(a) G(q)       (q in S_2, a in M_1). (2)

To construct G without making any extra assumption, choose
a_0 in M_1 with lambda(a_0)=1 and set G(q)=q a_0.
Every mu_q vanishes on ker(lambda), so (2) follows.

The first equality in (1) allows us to choose z in S_1 and m in M_0
with c = lambda(zm) != 0. For arbitrary x,y in S_1, associativity
and commutativity of the S-action give

    c G(xy) = (xy)(zm)
            = (yz)(xm)
            = lambda(xm) G(yz),

and, using the same z and m,

    c G(yz) = (yz)(zm)
            = z^2(ym)
            = lambda(ym) G(z^2).

Therefore

    G(xy) = c^(-2) lambda(xm) lambda(ym) G(z^2).     (3)

Products xy span S_2. Equation (3) implies that G(S_2), and hence
all the images in L, lie in the span of the single vector G(z^2).
If that vector is zero, every image is zero; otherwise it spans a
line. Either case contradicts M_3 = S_2 M_1 and dim M_3=2.

Thus at least one quadratic multiplication is surjective. In any
bases, mu_q has entries linear in the coefficients of q. The
surjectivity locus is the complement of the common zero locus of
its 2-by-2 minors. It is therefore nonempty and Zariski open, and it
is invariant under nonzero rescaling of q. This proves the theorem
for U_M in projective quadratic space.

The module-theoretic contradiction itself works over any field.
The open-intersection and orbit arguments below are stated over C.
No individual matrix entries, ranks, or sample quadrics are evaluated.

## 3. Verify the primary-module hypothesis and pass to B

PR167, at commit 063da90fc4543304e87e701cdefcc08225412f17, records
the following published primary resolution for the normalized HM
module:

    0 -> 2S(-8) -> 20S(-6) -> 35S(-5) + 2S(-3)
      -> 15S(-4) + 4S(-3) + 10S(-2)
      -> 15S(-1) -> 5S -> M -> 0.                 (4)

In particular, the homogeneous degree-zero surjection 5S -> M
proves that M is generated in degree zero. This premise is not
inferred from the Hilbert function alone. The same pinned source
derives

    Hilb(M,z) = 5 + 10z + 10z^2 + 2z^3.

Thus dim M_3=2, and Section 2 applies to this fixed module.

PR440 supplies natural surjections

    15M_1 + 4M_2 -> H^1(B(4)),
    15M_3          -> H^1(B(6)).                  (5)

They are induced by the same section resolution and commute with
multiplication by every homogeneous polynomial. Since M_4=0,
multiplication by q from the first source to the second is

    (q on each of 15M_1, zero on 4M_2).

For q in U_M this map is surjective. The commuting square (5)
therefore makes q:H^1(B(4)) -> H^1(B(6)) surjective: lift a target
class to 15M_3, lift that vector through q:15M_1 -> 15M_3, and
then take its image in H^1(B(4)).

Equivalently, PR440's exact presentation, for bar M=M/(q,r)M, is

    35 bar M_2 -> 15 bar M_3 -> Q(q,r) -> 0.       (6)

Its Section 3-4 primary-module argument uses only multiplication
naturality, so it applies to arbitrary degree-two and degree-three
polynomials q,r; its separate surface description requires the
regular-sequence hypothesis. For q in U_M, bar M_3=0 and (6) gives
Q(q,r)=0 without any assumption on r. The incoming differential is
not evaluated or discarded: its target is now proved zero.

## 4. The binary-quartic quadratic is nondegenerate

This follows directly from the pinned ordered-root weight identities,
without a claim about an action on F. On the finite-root chart put

    p_1=x_1x_2+x_3x_4,
    p_2=x_1x_3+x_2x_4,
    p_3=x_1x_4+x_2x_3,

and let e_j be the elementary symmetric functions of the four roots.
Then p_1+p_2+p_3=e_2. The pinned differences

    sigma_1-sigma_2 = 3(x_1-x_4)(x_3-x_2),
    sigma_1-sigma_3 = 3(x_1-x_3)(x_4-x_2),

together with sum sigma_i=0 give

    sigma_i=e_2-3p_i.

Direct expansion of symmetric polynomials gives

    p_1p_2+p_1p_3+p_2p_3 = e_1e_3-4e_4,
    sum sigma_i^2 = 6(e_2^2-3e_1e_3+12e_4).

Since u_0=t_2=2 sum sigma_i^2, in ordinary coefficient coordinates

    f(X,Y)=a X^4+b X^3Y+c X^2Y^2+d XY^3+e Y^4

the corresponding global quadratic is

    u_0 = 12(c^2-3bd+12ae).                       (7)

The equality first holds on the monic chart and then globally,
because both sides are homogeneous quadratic sections and agree
on a dense open. Any fixed harmless determinant-line rescaling
changes only its nonzero overall scalar.

The form in (7) has the two nondegenerate paired blocks (a,e) and
(b,d), and the nonzero square c^2. Its rank is five. This is an
identity for the universal root/coefficients map, not an evaluation
of an HM multiplication map or selected source.

Nondegenerate quadratic forms constitute a nonempty Zariski-open
subset D of P(S_2). Since P(S_2) is irreducible and U_M is nonempty
open, U_M intersect D is nonempty open. Over C, any nondegenerate
quadratic form can be diagonalized by an invertible linear change,
and its nonzero diagonal coefficients have square roots. Thus
PGL_5 acts transitively on D.

## 5. Translate the root map while holding HM fixed

Keep the following fixed:

    Z=(P^1)^4,     Y=P^4,     F on Y,     B=End_0(F),
    I=I_D on Z,
    H=< (12),(34),(13)(24) >,  chi=sgn|_H.

Let q_0:Z -> Y be the original ordered-root quotient map, with
invariants u_0=t_2 and v_0=t_3. For g in PGL_5 set

    q_g = g o q_0,
    u_g = (g^(-1))^*u_0,      v_g = (g^(-1))^*v_0,
    A_g = q_g^*O_Y(1),       E_g = q_g^*B tensor A_g^(-2).     (8)

The polynomial representatives in (8) can be defined using a
GL_5 lift of g. Changing the lift rescales them by nonzero degree
powers. All ideals, multiplication-surjectivity assertions, and
cohomology vanishings here are unchanged. No global PGL_5
linearization of O_Y(1) or equivariant structure on F is required.

The orbit map g -> [u_g] has image D by Section 4. Therefore

    Omega = { g in PGL_5 : [u_g] in U_M }           (9)

is nonempty and Zariski open. PGL_5 is irreducible, so Omega is
dense. For every g in Omega, Section 3 proves Q(u_g,v_g)=0.

The exact full-character filtration of PR437 applies to every
locally free coefficient bundle. Apply it to g^*B and then push
forward along the isomorphism g. Projection formula gives

    (q_g*(I^k E_g))_chi
      = B(-3)K_(k-1)(u_g,v_g)
        + B(-4)K_(k-2)(u_g,v_g)
        + B(-5)K_(k-3)(u_g,v_g).                 (10)

Here q_g* denotes direct image, not pullback; the direct-sum symbol
is written as +. The ideals K_n are generated by u_g^a v_g^b with
2a+3b >= n, with K_n=O_Y for n<=0. The original regular sequence,
root-cover geometry, ramification, and quadruple collisions are
transported by an actual target isomorphism, not deleted.

PR439's adjacent-syzygy calculation consequently applies with
u_g,v_g. The only cohomological inputs are the same fixed bundle B
and its pinned vanishings H^2(B(t))=0 for t>=2 and H^1(B(t))=0
for t>=7. It yields

    H^2(Z,I^6 E_g)_chi^vee = Q(u_g,v_g) + Q(u_g,v_g).

Thus for every g in Omega,

    H^2(Z,I^6 E_g)_chi = 0.                       (11)

In particular restriction H^2(Z,E_g)_chi ->
H^2(Z,E_g/I^6E_g)_chi is injective. On a quotient-adapted affine
cover every closed I^6E_g-valued chi two-cochain has an
I^6E_g-valued chi primitive. These are whole-coefficient existence
consequences; no primitive or high-order representative for an
actual source is constructed.

The ordinary root action on Z and I remain fixed, while the
pulled-back coefficient E_g varies. It would be incorrect to
replace (11) by a statement that every g preserves the original
E_0 or HM bundle. This is not merely rewriting coordinates of
the same fixed pair.

## 6. Why the fixed original quadratic is still unresolved

The module theorem does not say that every nondegenerate quadratic
is good. For a purely abstract counterexample, let

    S=C[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4],
    M=S/(x_2,x_3,x_4,x_0x_1,x_0^4,x_1^4).

This finite-length module is generated in degree zero, and M_3 is
spanned by x_0^3,x_1^3. The nondegenerate quadratic

    x_0x_1+x_2^2+x_3^2+x_4^2

annihilates M and is therefore not good, whereas x_0^2+x_1^2
maps M_1 onto M_3. This formal example is not an HM instance and
is not used to evaluate the actual bad locus. It shows why the
generic-to-fixed inference is unavailable from the stated module
hypotheses alone.

In the HM application the identity element need not have been
shown to lie in Omega. Neither a rank of the fixed t_2 map nor
a value of Q(t_2,t_3) is supplied. The original fixed source
and every separate source-comparison/membership condition remain
outside the conclusion.
