# A fixed cubic as interpolation on the twenty-five HM lines

Work over C on Y=P^4. Let E=F(3) be the positive Horrocks–Mumford bundle, B=End_0(F), V=H^0(E), and D=det V. Thus dim V=4, det E=O(5), and Sym^2E=B(5). Let C be the disjoint union of the twenty-five evaluation-degeneracy lines, i:C→Y. Write T=coker(V⊗O→E)=i_*L, where L has degree -1 on each component.

For any nonzero cubic v in S_3=H^0(O_Y(3)), this note identifies the fixed multiplication map on the non-primary quotient

    m_v:C_2→K_5,
    C_2=H^1(B(2))/(V⊗H^1(F)),
    K_5=H^1(B(5))/(V⊗H^1(E)).

The natural cup-product images in these definitions are injective. Their dimensions are 20 and 8; dim C_2=65 and dim K_5=24. The principal conclusion is an exact interpolation criterion, not an evaluation of any selected cubic.

## 1. A locally free resolution of the squared evaluation cokernel

There is an exact complex

    0→D⊗O(-5)→Λ^2V⊗O→V⊗E→Sym^2E→i_*L^2→0.       (1)

The second map sends a∧b to a⊗ev(b)-b⊗ev(a), and the third is symmetric multiplication through ev. The first map is the complementary-minor bivector of ev.

Here is a local proof including the degeneracy locus. By the transverse evaluation theorem used in PR456, evaluation has normal form

    e0↦a, e1↦x b, e2↦y b, e3↦z b,

where x,y,z is a regular sequence defining C. The image of V⊗E in Sym^2E is R a^2⊕R ab⊕(x,y,z)b^2, so its cokernel is (R/(x,y,z))b^2=L^2 locally. In the kernel of V⊗E→Sym^2E, the e0∧ei images eliminate all mixed a-coefficients. The remaining condition is a syzygy of (x,y,z) on the b-coefficients, generated by the Koszul relations. This proves exactness at V⊗E. A bivector in the kernel of Λ^2V→V⊗E has all e0∧ei coefficients zero; its remaining three coefficients satisfy the Koszul condition and are a multiple of

    z e1∧e2-y e1∧e3+x e2∧e3.

Regular-sequence exactness makes this a free rank-one kernel. This is precisely the complementary-minor map, globally with source D⊗(det E)^-1. Away from C, (1) is the usual exterior-symmetric sequence for a surjection of vector bundles. Thus (1) is a locally free resolution, not an assertion that the evaluation kernel itself is locally free.

## 2. Duality identifies the boundary with ordinary restriction

Dualize (1) by ω_Y=O(-5). Since i is a regular codimension-three embedding,

    Ext^3_Y(i_*L^2,ω_Y)=i_*(ω_C⊗L^-2).

The final map in the dual complex is

    Λ^2V*⊗O(-5)→D*⊗O.

Its entries are the minors of evaluation, which generate the reduced ideal I_C scheme-theoretically by the local normal form. Its cokernel is therefore D*⊗O_C. Consequently (1) induces the canonical identification

    ω_C⊗L^-2 ≅ D*⊗O_C.                              (2)

This keeps track of the constant determinant line rather than independently choosing twenty-five unrelated normalizations.

The boundary H^1(i_*L^2(t))→D⊗H^4(O(t-5)) arising from (1) has, by Serre duality, transpose

    D*⊗H^0(O(-t))→D*⊗H^0(O_C(-t)),                 (3)

equal to restriction. Indeed the resolution class is a morphism i_*L^2(t)→D⊗O(t-5)[3]; its dual is exactly the quotient map D*⊗O(-t)→Ext^3(i_*L^2(t),ω_Y) in the dual resolution. Formula (2) identifies that quotient with restriction. This proves the map identification, not merely its dimensions.

## 3. The two exact cohomology sequences

Write R_1=im(Λ^2V⊗O→V⊗E) and R_0=im(V⊗E→Sym^2E). Split (1) into three short exact sequences. For t=-3 and t=0, line-bundle cohomology gives

    H^1(R_1(t))=H^2(R_1(t))=0,
    H^3(R_1(t))=D⊗H^4(O(t-5)).

Using H^2(E(t))=H^3(E(t))=0 at these two twists gives

    H^1(R_0(t))=V⊗H^1(E(t)),
    H^2(R_0(t))=D⊗H^4(O(t-5)).

Here the inputs at t=-3 are H^2(F)=H^3(F)=0, and at t=0 are H^2(E)=H^3(E)=0. Since H^0(L^2(t))=0 and H^2(B(5+t))=0, the final short exact sequence yields

    0→V⊗H^1(F)→H^1(B(2))→H^1(L^2(-3))
      →D⊗H^4(O(-8))→0,                            (4)

    0→V⊗H^1(E)→H^1(B(5))→H^1(L^2)
      →D⊗H^4(O(-5))→0.                            (5)

The first arrows are the natural cup products, by their definition in (1). The respective dimensions are 20,85,100,35 and 8,32,25,1. In particular (4) identifies the earlier degree-two defect without choosing its normalizer-equivariant splitting.

Dualizing (4) and (5), using (2)-(3), gives

    C_2* ≅ D*⊗[H^0(O_C(3))/res(S_3)],
    K_5* ≅ D*⊗[H^0(O_C)/C].                       (6)

In particular res:S_3→H^0(O_C(3)) is injective. This also proves H^0(I_C(3))=0 from the cohomological inputs; no separate rank computation on restrictions is needed.

## 4. The exact fixed-cubic criterion

Multiplication by a fixed cubic v commutes with (1) and its twists. It carries the primary degree-two image into the primary degree-five image, so defines m_v:C_2→K_5. The transpose, after canceling the common constant line D*, is

    H^0(O_C)/C → H^0(O_C(3))/res(S_3),
    [λ] ↦ [λ·v|C].                                (7)

It is well-defined because constant λ multiplies the global cubic v. Since C is a disjoint union of lines, λ is a tuple of twenty-five constants. Thus m_v is surjective if and only if the following implication holds:

    f∈S_3 and f|ell=λ_ell v|ell on every component ell
    imply that all λ_ell are equal.

The quantification includes components where v vanishes identically. On such a component the equation does not constrain λ_ell, a fact essential to the criterion.

## 5. Intersection geometry and a structural failure condition

Let k be the number of component lines on which the nonzero cubic v vanishes identically. Let Z=C∩V(v) be the scheme-theoretic intersection, viewed as a subscheme of Y. Then

    dim coker(m_v)=h^0(I_Z(3))+k-1.                (8)

To prove this, multiplication by v on H^0(O_C) has kernel of dimension k. Its image consists of sections which are a constant multiple of v|ell on each component. A global cubic f restricts to this image exactly when it vanishes on Z. On a noncontained line, divisibility by the degree-three section v|ell and equality of degrees make the quotient a constant. On a contained line, vanishing on Z means f vanishes on the whole line. Restriction of global cubics is injective by (6). Therefore the space of tuples λ for which λv|C extends to a global cubic has dimension h^0(I_Z(3))+k. Quotienting by constant tuples gives (8), since the transpose kernel has dimension equal to the original map's cokernel.

If k=0, Z has length 25·3=75, with scheme multiplicities retained, and

    m_v is surjective ⇔ H^0(I_Z(3))=C v.

Thus residual surjectivity says exactly that v is the unique cubic through its intersection with the twenty-five lines. If k≥1, then v itself belongs to H^0(I_Z(3)), so dim coker(m_v)≥k. In particular a cubic containing even one component line cannot surject onto K_5. This excludes a geometric class of fixed cubics without evaluating any selected source coefficients.
