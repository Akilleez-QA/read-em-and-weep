# The conic gate line has twelve reduced ambient basepoints

## Statement and scope

In the immutable universal quadratic-lift normalization of PR1036, the
unique conic gate line is ellDelta=span(R1). Its nonic component row is
F1=N L R1. At every point of the twelve-point conic orbit E12, the ideal
generated by these four nonics in the AMBIENT plane is the maximal ideal.
Thus the twelve conic basepoints are reduced as plane basepoints, not only
as their scheme-theoretic intersection with the conic. Combining the pinned
support exclusions, its complete base scheme is Z disjoint-union E12,
of total length 34+12=46.

This is a universal conditional statement about the entire coefficient
line. It does not identify the unknown actual gate with that line, assert
that the actual gate is nonzero, or evaluate any actual coefficient or root.

## 2. A complete-intersection ideal for the whole conic orbit

Let I12 be the homogeneous ideal (delta,f6). The restriction of f6 to the
square conic is not zero: after the whole symbolic substitution
p=u^2, q=2uv, t=v^2 its binary form has coefficient 3sqrt5/16 on u^12, checked exactly
in verify.py. This is an identity of full binary forms, not evaluation at
one chosen square.

The restriction is an invariant binary form of degree twelve. By the
invariant-space and orbit facts in PR1033, it spans the unique f12 line
and has precisely the reduced divisor E12. Hence (delta,f6) defines the
reduced twelve-point orbit as a plane complete intersection. In particular
its local ideal at every orbit point is the plane maximal ideal. We use
the associated projective ideal sheaf; no claim about a nonsaturated raw
ideal is needed for any local argument.

For a reproducible whole-orbit affine presentation, put p=1. In exact
lexicographic order q>t the reduced Groebner basis is

    q^2-4t,
    t^6+(22sqrt5/5)t^5-33t^4-(44sqrt5/5)t^3-33t^2
        +(22sqrt5/5)t+1.

It has twelve standard monomials. On the boundary p=0 the equation delta=0
forces q=0, so t is nonzero; in the full chart t=1 the ideal becomes (1).
Thus all twelve points are in the chart p=1. The check covers the entire
boundary and does not assume that a sampled point represents its orbit.

## 3. Exact bounded first-order certificate

Use I for the displayed affine ideal. Exact reduction gives, component by
component,

    F1 subset I,
    (components of F1)+I^2 = I.                         (*)

The second equality is certified by constructing all pairwise products
of the Groebner generators of I and comparing the complete reduced
Groebner basis after adjoining the four components of F1. The quotient
by I has dimension twelve and that by I^2 has dimension thirty-six.
This is a bounded whole-orbit first-order calculation, not unbounded
elimination of the global nonic base scheme.

At each of the twelve points, write A for the nonic component ideal and
m for the plane maximal ideal. The first inclusion gives A subset m;
the second gives A+m^2=m. Applying Nakayama's lemma to the finitely generated
module m/A gives m/A=0, so A=m. All twelve AMBIENT basepoints are therefore
reduced. In particular the earlier allowed curvilinear lengths two and
three do not occur on the conic gate line.

## 4. The complete base scheme on the conic line

PR1036 places R1 off both prescribed-axis jet lines. Thus its component
ideal at all sixteen prescribed axes is exactly I_Z, of total length 34.
PR1040 proves that the fifteen-axis orbit occurs only on span(R1-R2),
so it is absent on span(R1). PR1042 excludes every
thirty-point orbit for every nonzero member of the pencil. PR1035 excludes
all remaining possible extra support except the conic orbit already
classified by PR1033. Consequently the complete base scheme on this line is

    B_conic = Z disjoint-union E12,
    length(B_conic) = 34+12 = 46.

All the exclusions are scheme-support statements; the exact local ideals
come from the pinned prescribed-axis theorem and the new ambient Nakayama
certificate. No hidden thickness remains at any support point.
