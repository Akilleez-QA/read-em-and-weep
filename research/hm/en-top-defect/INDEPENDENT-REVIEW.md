# Independent review of the top Eagon–Northcott defect

## Verdict

The identification of the actual top positive homology is correct, conditional on the stated finite-length presentation and the pinned symmetric-power inputs. It does not use generic maximal-minor acyclicity. This is a mathematical review, not a formal proof-assistant check.

## Complex and endpoint

Put F=S(-2) tensor U, dim U=10, G=S tensor W, dim W=2. The ordinary Eagon–Northcott complex has E_0=S and

E_i=Lambda^(i+1)F tensor D_(i-1)(G*) tensor det(G*) for 1<=i<=9.

Consequently E_9=9S(-20) and E_8=80S(-18). The natural identification (Lambda^9 F)*=F tensor det(F)* identifies the transpose of the last differential with

F tensor Sym^7 G tensor det(F)* tensor det(G) -> Sym^8 G tensor det(F)* tensor det(G).

Its image is generated by phi(F) times Sym^7 G. The cokernel is therefore Sym^8_S(H)(20) tensor det(U)* tensor det(W). This step is valid for an arbitrary presentation; no exactness of E is used.

## Support and indexing

At every prime other than the homogeneous maximal ideal m, H localizes to zero. The localized presentation is then a split surjection, whose Eagon–Northcott complex is split exact. Thus every homology module of E is finite length, including H_0(E)=S/I_2(phi).

Place E_i in cochain degree -i before applying derived Hom. A homology module H_i(E) then contributes Ext^p(H_i(E),S) in total cohomological degree p+i, not p-i. Since finite-length modules over this five-variable regular ring have Ext only in degree 5, the hyper-Ext spectral sequence has a single nonzero row. It gives the canonical identification

H^(5+i)(Hom_S(E,S)) = Ext^5_S(H_i(E),S).

The dual complex stops in degree 9. Hence i>4 gives zero Ext^5 and therefore H_i(E)=0, by finite-length duality. For i=4 its degree-nine cohomology is exactly the endpoint cokernel computed above. No assertion of vanishing for H_1,H_2,H_3 follows.

## Grading and determinant orientation

For S=Sym(T), dim T=5, the graded duality formula is

Ext^5_S(M,S)=M^vee(5) tensor det(T)*.

The determinant sign is also seen directly in the last term of the Koszul resolution of the residue field. Substituting the endpoint gives

H_4(E)^vee = Sym^8 H(15) tensor det(U)* tensor det(W) tensor det(T).

Dualizing yields

H_4(E)=(Sym^8 H)^vee(-15) tensor det(U) tensor det(W)* tensor det(T)*.

Thus the determinant-line formula in the proof is correct. An identification omitting the one-dimensional factors requires chosen orientations, rather than an unqualified equivariant trivialization.

## Independent numerical consequences

The established Hilbert function of Sym^8 H is (9,45,55) in degrees 0,1,2. Reflection about degree 15 gives (55,45,9) in degrees 13,14,15 for H_4(E). Finite-dimensional duality preserves the annihilator, so its annihilator is m^3. The level degree-two socle of Sym^8 H gives exactly 55 minimal degree-13 generators of H_4(E). Its degree-zero generators give exactly nine socle elements in degree 15.

The number of minimal degree-14 relations can also be checked without a Betti-table duality convention: 55 generators yield 5*55=275 free elements in degree 14, whereas H_4(E)_14 has dimension 45. The kernel has dimension 230. There are no earlier relations, so all 230 are minimal. This makes no claim that all first relations have degree 14.
