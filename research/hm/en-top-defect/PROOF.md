# The top Eagon–Northcott defect of the quadratic two-generator presentation

## Statement and scope

Let S be a polynomial ring on five degree-one variables over a field of characteristic zero. Let phi:F=S(-2)^10 -> G=S^2 present the established finite-length module H. Write D=I_2(phi), Q=S/D, and let E be the ordinary Eagon–Northcott complex, indexed E_0=S through E_9, with H_0(E)=Q. Forgetting one-dimensional orientation representations, there is a canonical graded identification

H_4(E) = (Sym^8_S H)^vee(-15).

All H_i(E) for i>4 vanish. Here M^vee_j=Hom_k(M_{-j},k), and M(a)_j=M_{a+j}. Canonical means after choosing the determinant orientations of the free generator spaces and the variable space; the determinant-line version is recorded below. This is an identification of S-modules, not merely Hilbert functions.

Using the previously established higher-symmetric-power and socle results, H_4(E) has Hilbert function (55,45,9) in degrees (13,14,15), annihilator m^3, is generated by its 55-dimensional degree-13 component, and is level with 9-dimensional degree-15 socle. Thus the top determinantal defect is nonzero and the grade-sensitive acyclic tail is sharp. This does not determine the remaining connecting-map parameter k in the minimal resolution of Q.

## Proof without a generic-exactness hypothesis

For i>=1,

E_i = Lambda^(i+1) F tensor D_(i-1)(G*) tensor det(G*),

where D denotes divided powers. In particular E_9=S(-20)^9 and E_8=S(-18)^80. Away from m, H vanishes, so phi is a split surjection. The Eagon–Northcott complex of a split surjection is split exact. Consequently every H_i(E), including Q, is supported at m and has finite length.

For any finite-length S-module M, Ext^p_S(M,S)=0 unless p=5. The hyper-Ext spectral sequence for the finite free complex E therefore has only the p=5 row and collapses, giving

H^n(Hom_S(E,S)) = Ext^5_S(H_(n-5)(E),S).

This also proves H_i(E)=0 for i>4: the left side is zero for n=5+i>9 and finite-length graded duality is faithful.

At n=9 the left side is the cokernel of E_8* -> E_9*. The transpose of the last Eagon–Northcott differential is, up to determinant orientations, the ordinary symmetric-power presentation map

F tensor Sym^7 G -> Sym^8 G,

shifted by 20. Indeed, dualizing the divided-power contraction turns it into symmetric multiplication by phi(f). Its cokernel is Sym^8 H(20). Thus

Ext^5_S(H_4(E),S) = Sym^8 H(20).

Graded local duality gives Ext^5_S(M,S)=M^vee(5), after choosing the variable orientation. Hence H_4(E)=(Sym^8 H)^vee(-15), as asserted.

## Consequences from pinned existing inputs

PR494 establishes Sym^8 H has Hilbert function (9,45,55), is generated in degree zero, and has annihilator m^3. PR496 establishes that it is level in degree two, with socle dimension 55. Graded duality reverses generator and socle degrees, yielding all claims in the statement. None of these implications uses coefficients of the selected HM/source instance or a matrix-rank evaluation.

One further presentation consequence follows from PR496: beta_(4,6)(Sym^8 H)=20*8+70=230. Duality of minimal free resolutions over S then gives beta_(1,14)(H_4(E))=230. The assertion is only about the degree-14 first relations; possible additional first-relation degrees are not excluded here.

## Determinant lines

Write F=S(-2) tensor U and G=S tensor W. Without choosing orientations, the identification is

H_4(E) = (Sym^8 H)^vee(-15) tensor det(U) tensor det(W)* tensor det(T)*,

where S=Sym(T). Indeed coker(E_8* -> E_9*)=Sym^8 H(20) tensor det(U)* tensor det(W), whereas Ext^5_S(M,S)=M^vee(5) tensor det(T)*. No equivariant trivialization of these lines is presumed.
