## 1. Actual equivariance and the choice of quadratic lifts

The degree-five-invariants input establishes an action on the actual normalization and its section module, not merely on the node configuration. The unique lift of each automorphism to normalization respects composition; pulling back the ambient linearization supplies compatible actions on every degree and on the unit map. Consequently B, N, M, their graded pieces, and all multiplication maps used here are genuine representations or equivariant modules for the same finite normalizer.

The identification Q₂=M₂ is valid: Q₂ was defined upstream as the image of the normalization-to-conductor map, and the kernel of that map on sections is B₂. By low-degree ambient equality, B₂=P₂. Therefore the short exact sequence split in Section 2 is the correct one. Averaging over the finite normalizer gives an equivariant splitting without changing any of the actual module maps.

The resulting map F₀=P⊕P(−2)⊗Q₂→N is a minimal graded presentation. Its degree-zero generator is the unit; its degree-two generators map isomorphically to N₂/P₂. The actual normalization resolution states that these are all minimal generators. Graded Nakayama, or comparison with that minimal resolution, therefore proves surjectivity. The ten degree-three minimal first syzygies and generation of its kernel L in degree three follow from the same actual resolution. This use of uniqueness of minimal presentations is legitimate even though no equivariant matrices were originally specified.

## 2. The actual cubic relation module

The projection L₃→P₁⊗Q₂ is injective because its kernel is I₃=0. For an element q in the kernel of the conductor multiplication, π(0,q) lies in B₃. Since P₃→B₃ is an isomorphism, there is a unique p making (p,q) a relation. This proves equivariantly that

L₃ ≅ ker(P₁⊗Q₂→M₃).

Thus the upstream conductor calculation identifies the actual relation representation as V₃⊗W, with W two-dimensional and central-involution odd. Nothing here assumes an ambient ideal resolution, identifies L with I, or replaces the actual multiplication map by an unspecified map with the same dimensions.

Since L is generated by L₃, P₂⊗L₃→L₅ is surjective. Taking Heisenberg invariants preserves this surjection over C. Both the map and its invariant restriction are normalizer-equivariant; the latter is therefore a genuine K-equivariant map.

## 3. Duality and parity

The original HM source confirms the full-normalizer duality in Section 1, printed p65; the symmetric-square identity in Lemma 3.3(v), printed pp70–71; the tensor identity used by the lower-conductor input in Lemma 3.3(ix); and the six-dimensional invariant-quintic representation in Theorem 3.5, printed p71. The additive-weight notation is handled correctly: weights 2 and 3 are opposite modulo five, so V₃≅V₂* as full normalizer representations. It would not be enough to have this only after restriction to the Heisenberg subgroup, but the cited full-group duality gives exactly what is required. Galois conjugation or dualization can permute labels without changing the relevant signs.

There is also a useful independent parity check. The standard involution lift acts on every standard five-dimensional representation with eigenvalue multiplicities three positive and two negative, hence trace one. Its trace on the symmetric square is (1²+5)/2=3. The three-dimensional multiplicity space of P₂ therefore has trace three, so the involution acts identically there. Likewise the five-dimensional multiplicity space in P₁⊗Q₂ has trace one. Its actual three-dimensional image is even by the lower-conductor target calculation, leaving an entirely odd two-dimensional kernel. This confirms that the sign comparison is not sensitive to the naming convention for irreducible representations.

The invariant line in V₂⊗V₂* is the identity in End(V₂), fixed by conjugation under the whole normalizer. Thus it carries the trivial K-character, not a hidden one-dimensional twist. It follows that (P₂⊗L₃)ᴴ is U₂⊗W and entirely odd; its quotient L₅ᴴ is also entirely odd. Meanwhile P₅ᴴ is entirely even. The first-summand projection L₅ᴴ→P₅ᴴ must vanish.

Finally, for an invariant ideal element f, (f,0) is an invariant relation and projects to f. Hence I₅ᴴ=0. This last inference uses only that inclusion and projection, and does not identify all degree-five relations with ideal equations.

## 4. The geometric dimension bound

A general hyperplane section of this integral surface can be chosen integral and to avoid the finite node set. No normality or Cohen–Macaulayness of the original surface is needed in the argument. The hyperplane equation is a nonzerodivisor on O_S, so tensoring the ideal sequence with O_H gives the exact restriction sequence

0→I_S(d−1)→I_S(d)→I_(D/H)(d)→0.

In degree four, I₄=0 and H¹(I_S(3))=0 imply J₄=0. In degree five, I₄=0 and H¹(I_S(4))=0 imply I₅≅J₅. Saturation identifies these with the global-section spaces of the corresponding ideal sheaves. There is no assumption here that the unsaturated quotient by a linear form already equals the saturated coordinate ring of the section.

Because D is integral, its saturated homogeneous coordinate ring Q/J is a domain. A linear form not vanishing identically on D is nonzero there, and multiplication by it is injective from degree four to degree five. Consequently

35=dim(Q/J)₄≤dim(Q/J)₅=56−dim I₅,

which gives dim I₅≤21. This is enough for the subsequent orbit argument. The numerical values are the polynomial dimensions in four variables, not in five variables, as required.

## 5. Orbit contradiction, cokernel, and scope

The scalar center acts trivially in degree five. Heisenberg semisimplicity decomposes I₅ into characters of its order-25 abelian quotient. Conjugation by the normalizer permutes the twenty-four nontrivial characters transitively, so their multiplicities are all equal. With the invariant part already zero,

dim I₅=24m.

The bound dim I₅≤21 forces m=0 and hence I₅=0. Only after proving this injectivity does the proof use dim B₅=dim P₅=126 to obtain surjectivity and C₅=0. This correctly avoids the earlier dimension-only pitfall.

The normalization-resolution input, the lower-conductor ranks, and the low-equation exclusion do not rely on the new quintic conclusion, so the displayed local dependency chain is noncircular. The result closes actual ambient restriction through degree five. It does not establish higher-degree ambient surjectivity, a full resolution of the primary ideal, source lifting, full-adjoint vanishing, or algebraicity.

**Conclusion:** the proof establishes I₅=C₅=0 under its explicitly declared inputs. Its new mechanism, actual cubic-syzygy parity followed by the integral-hyperplane bound, is valid.
