1. A plane containing no component meets the disjoint lines in distinct reduced points. A proper hyperplane through it exists because each component excludes at most one member of the pencil. Sextic interpolation passes from the twenty-five-point section to its subset and then to plane forms. Restricting the sheaf generators and taking their image correctly gives the scheme-theoretic plane ideal; no flatness or absence of Tor is presumed.
2. A finite base locus permits a coprime pair of restricted quintics. Their complete intersection has length twenty-five and degree-six Hilbert function twenty-two, yielding the preliminary incidence bound. Generation of each reduced point's maximal ideal also makes independent first jets a nonempty open condition. The finite intersection of these open conditions and the coprime locus is nonempty. Thus the residual scheme is disjoint from E, without requiring it to be reduced.
3. Independently checked Gold–Little–Schenck, Theorem 1.2, at https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0311129, pages 3–4. It states the residual cohomology identity for mutually residual subschemes, not only reduced point partitions. With degrees five and five, s=7 and a=6, it gives 22-n=(25-n)-h_R(1), hence h_R(1)=3. A further quintic avoiding the finite support of R is a unit there. Its products with the three-dimensional linear-form space are independent modulo I_Z, giving 22-n>=3 and n<=19.
4. For a proper hyperplane section, the saturated reduced-point coordinate ring admits an Artinian reduction with Hilbert function equal to its first difference. The backwards Macaulay bounds from a_6=5 have minimum sum twenty through degree five. Equality forces precisely (1,2,3,4,5,5,5), and therefore a planar section. The incidence bound excludes it. Thus every proper lower Rao map has rank at least twenty-six, kernel at most twenty-nine and cokernel at most four.
