## 1. Local-ring argument

Because U is an open neighborhood of the origin in a 27-dimensional complex vector space, its local ring R at the origin is the Noetherian ring C[x_1,...,x_27]_(x_1,...,x_27), up to abstract coordinates, and has dimension 27. Regularity of Phi puts its m component germs in R; Phi(0)=0 puts their ideal J inside the maximal ideal. In particular R/J is a nonzero Noetherian local ring of finite dimension d.

A parameter ideal in R/J can be generated by d elements. Lifting those generators gives elements y_1,...,y_d in R such that the radical of J+(y_1,...,y_d) is the maximal ideal of R. This ideal has at most m+d generators. The minimum generator count for an ideal of definition is dim R, so 27 <= m+d. This is exactly the needed inequality d >= 27-m.

The cited [Stacks Proposition 10.60.9](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00KD) supplies both the existence and the lower generator bound. The alternative [Krull height theorem](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BBZ) is consistent with this deduction. No unproved assertion that a singular fiber has its expected dimension is used. The argument permits dependent, singular, or identically zero component functions, and does not require surjectivity of Phi.

The statement is genuinely local, so allowing regular functions with denominators invertible at 0 causes no problem. An assertion about arbitrary rational maps undefined at 0 would not follow.

## 2. From local dimension to nonzero complex defects

Write Z=Phi^(-1)(0). Removing nilpotents does not change its topological space or its Krull dimension. Since 0 is a closed complex point, its local-ring dimension agrees with its local geometric dimension. If d>0, some irreducible component C of Z_red through 0 has positive dimension. For every Zariski-open neighborhood N of 0, C intersect N is a nonempty open subset of C and cannot consist of the single closed point 0. Thus (C intersect N) minus {0} is nonempty and locally closed in Z_red.

Schemes of finite type over C are Jacobson and their closed points have residue field C. Consequently this punctured locally closed subset contains a nonzero complex closed point of Z. Relevant standard facts are [Stacks Lemma 33.14.1](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0478) and [Lemma 33.20.3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/06LF).

This confirms the precise Zariski-neighborhood conclusion. It is not merely a statement about formal tangent vectors or nilpotent thickenings, and the proof does not need an analytic-neighborhood claim.
