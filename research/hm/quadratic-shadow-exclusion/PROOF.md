# Excluding the twenty-five-dimensional cubic ghost

Work over C with the normalized Horrocks–Mumford bundle F on P(V)=P^4, B=End_0(F), T=V*, and S=Sym(T). Put K_j=H^1(B(j-1)). All module structures below are actual cohomological multiplication, not arbitrary maps between isomorphic representations.

PR478 proves that the cubic ghost dimension r=dim Tor_2^S(K,C)_3=dim Tor_1^S(K,C)_3 is one of 0,15,25. Its possible channels are A* tensor U' and A* tensor J_5, of dimensions fifteen and twenty-five. We exclude the second: r belongs to {0,15}. Equivalently, the J_5 Schur scalar of the actual Tor map in PR478 is nonzero. The U' scalar remains undetermined.

## 1. A two-generator module through degree three

Define H_j=H^2(B(j-5)). Its Hilbert function in degrees zero through five is (2,10,20,20,10,2), and it vanishes outside these degrees. Serre duality and self-duality of B give a perfect S-compatible pairing H_j tensor H_(5-j) -> C, or H is isomorphic to its graded dual shifted by five.

The module H is generated by its two degree-zero elements through degree three. Here are the actual multiplication inputs, with the direction of duality explicit.

- PR476 identifies T tensor H^2(B(-1)) -> H^2(B) with evaluation T tensor V tensor W' -> W'. Its dual says the adjoint of T tensor H^2(B(-5)) -> H^2(B(-4)) is an isomorphism H^2(B(-1)) -> T* tensor H^2(B). Therefore the first multiplication H_0 tensor T -> H_1 is an isomorphism.
- PR467 proves T tensor H^2(B(-4)) -> H^2(B(-3)) nonzero by a universal Euler-complex comparison. Its target A* tensor Q_4 is irreducible, so this multiplication H_1 tensor T -> H_2 is onto.
- PR469 proves T tensor H^2(B(-3)) -> H^2(B(-2)) onto by the universal wedge comparison and the nonzero SL_2 action on Sym^3 W. This is H_2 tensor T -> H_3.

Thus the natural cover S tensor H_0 -> H is onto in degrees zero through three. Write R_j for its kernel in degree j. Since dim H_0=2, we have R_1=0, dim R_2=2 binom(6,4)-20=10, and dim R_3=2 binom(7,4)-20=50. Consequently

Tor_1^S(H,C)_3 = R_3/(T R_2).

No conclusion about a selected quadratic or cubic is used here.

## 2. The cubic ghost is dual to these cubic relations

Tensor the exact coordinate Koszul complex on P^4 by B(2). In its hypercohomology spectral sequence the term at p=-i is Lambda^i T tensor B(2-i), and d_a has bidegree (a,1-a). The q=1 row at p=-2 has homology Tor_2^S(K,C)_3. The q=2 row at p=-4 has homology Tor_4^S(H,C)_7, since its terms are Lambda^i T tensor H_(7-i).

The d_2 map between these two positions is an isomorphism. Indeed, its source has no incoming d_2: the complex ends at p=-5. Its possible outgoing d_3 lands in H^0(B(1)) at p=-1, which is zero, and all subsequent outgoing targets have negative q. The target has no outgoing d_2 because H^0(B(2))=0. Its possible incoming d_3 comes from p=-5,q=3, which vanishes since H^3(B(-3))=H^1(B(-2))*=0; larger incoming differentials start outside the complex. There are no other possible differentials affecting these two E_2 terms. The spectral sequence abuts zero, so d_2 is both injective and surjective.

For an explicit audit of the positive-twist H^0 vanishings, ch(B)=3-15h^2+(75/4)h^4, so Riemann–Roch gives chi(B(1))=-55 and chi(B(2))=-85. The established h^1 values are 55 and 85. H^2 vanishes at twist one by PR455 and at twist two by the structural bound PR167; H^3 vanishes by PR476 section 3, and H^4 vanishes by Serre duality and stability at the corresponding negative twists. Therefore H^0(B(1))=H^0(B(2))=0. Stability alone is not being used to assert a positive-twist vanishing. The H^1(B(-2)) vanishing is PR466. Thus

Tor_2^S(K,C)_3 is isomorphic to Tor_4^S(H,C)_7.

The perfect pairing on H pairs the Koszul complexes in complementary exterior degrees. Since dim T=5 and H has socle shift five, it gives

Tor_4^S(H,C)_7 is isomorphic to Tor_1^S(H,C)_3* tensor det T.

The normalizer lies in SL(V), so det T is trivial; for the dimension argument even this equivariant normalization is unnecessary. In particular

r = dim Tor_1^S(H,C)_3 = 50 - dim(T R_2).

This identifies actual Koszul homology; it does not infer a map from dimensions alone.

## 3. The quadratic shadow excludes r=25

The lex-module Hilbert-function comparison for a submodule of a free module with equal generator degrees says that the cubic shadow of a ten-dimensional subspace R_2 of (S tensor C^2)_2 has dimension at least that of the first ten lexicographic quadratic monomials in the free module. All ten lie in its first component. In coordinates x_0,...,x_4 their list is

x_0 S_1, x_1^2, x_1 x_2, x_1 x_3, x_1 x_4, x_2^2.

Their cubic shadow consists of fifteen monomials divisible by x_0, ten additional monomials divisible by x_1 but not x_0, and three additional monomials x_2^3,x_2^2 x_3,x_2^2 x_4. Its dimension is therefore 15+10+3=28. Hence

dim(T R_2)>=28, and r<=22.

Combined with PR478's exact alternatives {0,15,25}, this excludes 25 and leaves precisely {0,15}. The J_5 scalar is nonzero, and the only possible cubic ghost is the fifteen-dimensional A* tensor U' channel. This is stronger than the prior statement that at least one of two scalars is nonzero.

The lex comparison used here is the standard lex-submodule theorem, not a rank-adjusted Gotzmann assertion. It concerns only a ten-dimensional quadratic subspace of a two-generator free module; no selected HM coefficients or numerical matrices are evaluated.

## 4. Independent character-theoretic exclusion

There is also a representation-theoretic reason for the same exclusion, which checks the shadow argument independently. In the normalizer notation of PR478, H_0=W', H_1=T tensor W', and H_2=A* tensor Q_4, where Q_4 is the four-dimensional spin representation, not the ordinary four-dimensional representation T_4. The actual surjection S_2 tensor H_0 -> H_2 and the identities

S_2 T=A* tensor U',  U' tensor W'=W' direct-sum Q_4

therefore identify the ten quadratic relations as R_2=A* tensor W'. Consequently

T tensor R_2=A tensor (R_6 direct-sum 1 direct-sum U'),

using T tensor A*=A tensor (U direct-sum W'), U tensor W'=R_6, and W' tensor W'=1 direct-sum U'. In particular this space contains no A tensor J_5 summand.

For completeness use the adjacent positions in the same Koszul spectral sequence for B(2). They are E_2^(-3,2)=Tor_3(H,C)_7 and E_2^(-1,1)=Tor_1(K,C)_3. The d_2 between them is an isomorphism: the source's incoming d_2 starts in H^3(B(-3))=0, and its outgoing d_3 ends in H^0(B(2))=0. The target's other possible incoming differentials start in H^3(B(-2))=0 and H^4(B(-3))=H^0(B(-2))*=0. All remaining sources or targets are outside the complex. The H^3(B(-2)) vanishing is PR466, and negative-twist H^0 vanishing follows from stability. Zero abutment proves the asserted isomorphism.

Serre–Koszul duality now identifies Tor_1(K,C)_3 with Tor_2(H,C)_3* (up to the trivial determinant factor). Since H has no linear relations, Tor_2(H,C)_3 is the kernel of T tensor R_2 -> S_3 tensor H_0 and embeds into T tensor R_2. It has no A tensor J_5 channel. Therefore the cubic relation ghost of K has no A* tensor J_5 channel. PR478 identifies the cubic relation and second-syzygy ghosts with the same zero-scalar channels, so this also forces its J_5 scalar nonzero.

This second proof does not assume that a representation-compatible map is nonzero. It excludes the candidate kernel because its dual cannot occur in the actual space containing that kernel. It leaves the U' channel unresolved, exactly as the shadow bound does.
