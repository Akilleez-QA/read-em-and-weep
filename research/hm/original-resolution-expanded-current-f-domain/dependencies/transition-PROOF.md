# Actual transitions have legal-factor bound one

## Result and scope

For the exact product-root model and frozen transition formula already authenticated by the accepted product-root bridge, the literal entries of g and its rank-two adjugate have conservative carrier/legal bound **4/1**, per actual owner occurrence. The former legal allowance two is unnecessary for these undifferentiated transitions. The same actual invariant derivation has bounds **5/2** after one derivative and **6/3** after two distinct derivatives, provided its established exact-derivation semantics are retained.

Separately, the literal resolution inclusion

    j_m(R) = I D_m R^t D_m^-1 P

adds carrier/legal at most **4/1**, rather than 4/2, when R is expressed in the same physical cohomology frame. This is a formula-level bound for every coefficient of R; it does not require R to be closed.

Carrier bounds remain conservatively unchanged. The mathematical weights and partition conditions are unchanged. This note does not evaluate an actual source, matrix product, residual, coefficient detector, or jet. It does not prove an obstruction value. Its finite checks use only inert source syntax and integer frame-exponent metadata.

## 1. Accepted data and exact occurrence convention

The accepted product-root proof fixes C: y²=x(x²−1), X=C⁴, N=O_X(sum pr_r^*O), and N²=f*O(1). On one curve, its actual legal frames are

    e_0=1, e_1=y/x.

The old standard squared-line frame ell is 1 on the finite x-chart and x=1/t on the infinity chart. There t=1/x, u=y/x², and u²=t(1−t²). These are actual divisor-sheaf frames. No square root of t is adjoined.

The legal clearing sections in one coordinate are

    finite:   L_0=1,  L_1=x(x²−1),
    infinity: L_0=t,  L_1=1−t².                              (1)

They are local representatives of Y and X(X²−Y²), of degrees one and three on P¹. In particular the infinity formula for L_1 includes its entire degree-three frame change. Its finite representative cannot be transported as an unweighted function.

A vertex occurrence v includes its standard tag A_v, carrier c_v, and four legal bits m_v. Two occurrences remain distinct even when all three labels agree. Let H_v be its own homogeneous carrier section, of degree five for a primary and four for the auxiliary. A bound (h,l) on an expression with owner list T means clearing by

    product_(v in T) H_v^h product_(r=1)^4 L_(m_vr)^l,

with multiplicity. Addition takes maxima only after the actual occurrence lists are aligned. No factor may be taken from an absent occurrence. The proof below initially uses precisely the two endpoint occurrences of g_ij.

The accepted homogeneous primitive theorem gives own-carrier power two for every I and P entry, including the auxiliary's genuine v0² denominator, and weights

    I column a: w_a=a;       P row a: −w_a;       a=0,1.

It supplies homogeneous polynomial extensions after clearing on every standard product chart. There is no legal denominator in these unweighted primitive entries.

## 2. The four actual transition multipliers

The frozen source defines

    T_ij=P_i I_j,
    g_ij=D_j T_ij^t D_i^-1,
    D_i=diag(nu_i,nu_i^-1),   nu_i=product_r e_(m_ir).

This is the accepted actual row-transition convention. No matrix product is calculated here. Each formal summand of (T_ij)_(a,b) has homogeneous weight b−a, since it is an entry of P row a times I column b.

Fix any common standard product chart A and use ell_A=product_r ell_(A_r). Its coefficient vector is v_A=tau_A v_ref, with tau_A=product_(r in A)t_r. Thus

    (T_ij)_(a,b)(v_ref)=tau_A^(a−b)(T_ij)_(a,b)(v_A).

Consequently g_(b,a) is (T_ij)_(a,b)(v_A) times the product of the following one-curve scalar factors:

| (a,b) | Factor |
|---|---|
| (0,0) | e_j/e_i |
| (1,1) | e_i/e_j |
| (1,0) | e_i e_j/ell |
| (0,1) | ell/(e_i e_j) |

For example the off-diagonal (a,b)=(0,1) factor is ell/(e_i e_j), not 1/(e_i e_j) on an infinity chart. This ell is forced by the homogeneous weight +1 of T_(0,1). Omitting it would confuse the actual physical transition with a finite-chart expression.

This derivation uses the same actual e_i,e_j throughout. The two endpoint standard tags are not used to replace these frames by square roots of their standard ell frames.

## 3. Exact curve-basis tables

Write h=x²−1 on the finite chart and s=1−t² on the infinity chart. The following are identities in the function fields, reduced using y²=xh and u²=ts. The four columns are denoted r,r^-1,p,p^-1 only for readability; there is no independent inversion operation to certify.

### Finite chart, basis (1,y)

| (m_i,m_j) | r=e_j/e_i | r^-1=e_i/e_j | p=e_i e_j/ell | p^-1=ell/(e_i e_j) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 00 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 01 | y/x | y/h | y/x | y/h |
| 10 | y/h | y/x | y/x | y/h |
| 11 | 1 | 1 | h/x | x/h |

The denominator h in 1/e_1 comes from x/y=y/h. It must not be replaced by x alone.

### Infinity chart, basis (1,u)

| (m_i,m_j) | r=e_j/e_i | r^-1=e_i/e_j | p=e_i e_j/ell | p^-1=ell/(e_i e_j) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 00 | 1 | 1 | t | 1/t |
| 01 | u/t | u/s | u | u/(ts) |
| 10 | u/s | u/t | u | u/(ts) |
| 11 | 1 | 1 | s | 1/s |

In particular the mixed reciprocal is 1/u=u/(ts). It uses one legal factor t from the actual bit-zero endpoint and one factor s from the actual bit-one endpoint. Treating it as needing two copies of one endpoint's legal factor is both weaker and the wrong allocation when the factors differ.

Multiply each entire row by L_(m_i)L_(m_j). The resulting four entries are:

| Chart | Bits | Cleared r, r^-1, p, p^-1 |
|---|---|---|
| finite | 00 | 1, 1, 1, 1 |
| finite | 01 | hy, xy, hy, xy |
| finite | 10 | xy, hy, hy, xy |
| finite | 11 | x²h², x²h², xh³, x³h |
| infinity | 00 | t², t², t³, t |
| infinity | 01 | su, tu, tsu, u |
| infinity | 10 | tu, su, tsu, u |
| infinity | 11 | s², s², s³, s |

Every coefficient in (1,y) or (1,u) is polynomial. This is an explicit clearance statement, stronger than regularity only on the legal overlap. The corresponding quadratic coordinate rings are free of rank two over Q[x] and Q[t], so coefficientwise polynomiality is unambiguous. The four-curve coordinate rings are their tensor products and have the corresponding 16-element product bases. Reduction of any further product of basis elements only multiplies by xh or ts and introduces no new denominators.

The use of t as a clearing factor is a power bound in the named section. On C, t has valuation two at O. We make no assertion that it is a uniformizer or that the bound is an order-one valuation at O.

## 4. Global carrier clearance, ownership, and all standard tags

On the common chart A, each term of T_(a,b) is cleared by H_i²H_j², by the homogeneous primitive theorem. Its cleared local coefficient is polynomial in the base coordinates, with the prescribed weight b−a already included. Applying the tables coordinate by coordinate therefore proves that

    H_i² H_j² product_r L_(m_ir)L_(m_jr) · g_(b,a)

has polynomial coefficients in the product curve basis on A. We retain the source ledger's conservative carrier allowance four for both occurrences; multiplying by additional regular carrier sections preserves polynomiality. This yields 4/1 without changing any carrier premise.

The proof treats each one of the 256 ordered four-bit legal-mask pairs and each of the 16 standard product charts. It is not restricted to a selected finite chart. Repeated endpoint labels retain their two occurrence factors. If later the same selected occurrence is deliberately identified in an arithmetic word, the allowances add there, as usual; label equality by itself is not occurrence identification.

The frozen g leaf uses the carrier and legal-mask data; its owner standard tags select domains and coefficient trivializations, not an extra scalar in that leaf. To compare owners with distinct standard tags A_i,A_j, represent their GLOBAL homogeneous carrier sections and primitive maps in the same chart A on the relevant intersection. The primitive's coefficient change is tau^(weight), precisely the change used in section 2. Legal section changes use their fixed degrees one or three. These are ordinary changes of trivialization, and all standard equations inverted on an owner's own standard intersection are units there. No new standard denominator remains in the displayed cleared coefficient, because its construction is polynomial on every A independently.

Thus no standard equation is charged to a third vertex or borrowed from a missing standard tag. When this bound is later used in an outer suffix argument, that argument must still retain the original full target intersection and its original target-tag correspondence. The improvement proved here neither replaces nor relaxes that requirement.

A two-by-two adjugate only permutes and negates four entries. Hence it has exactly the same 4/1 bound with exactly the same endpoint occurrence list. No determinant identity is required for this bound. The accepted determinant-one theorem separately identifies it with the genuine inverse.

## 5. Derivative consequence and its limit

The actual invariant field has polynomial coordinate expressions

    V(x)=y,             V(y)=(3x²−1)/2,
    V(t)=−u,            V(u)=(3t²−1)/2.

It preserves each of the displayed local curve coordinate rings. For a regular numerator divided by positive powers of owned carrier/legal sections, the quotient rule increases each participating denominator power by at most one. Absent factors need not be introduced, though padding the same occurrence list is harmless. The same is true in the tensor-product coordinate rings.

Therefore the exact derivative of the actual g, or its adjugate, is bounded by 5/2. A second derivative in a distinct coordinate is bounded by 6/3. This includes derivatives of the table factors and the homogeneous coefficient frame changes; it is not differentiation of a fixed pointwise frame constant. These consequences are mathematical derivation bounds. They do not prove that an arbitrary numerical jet engine implements that derivation, and they do not certify any stripped or projected derivative operation whose own line convention has not been established.

## 6. Separate inclusion improvement

Use the same physical frame at its selected owner m. The accepted common-frame inclusion and projection are I D_m and D_m^-1 P. In a fixed matrix entry of

    I D_m R^t D_m^-1 P,

the term containing I column a and P row b gains nu_m^(2(b−a)). The product of the homogeneous I and P coefficients has weight a−b. Expressing them on a standard chart therefore changes the scalar factor to

    c_(m,A)^(b−a),        c_(m,A)=e_m²/ell_A.

Only 1,c,c^-1 occur. In one coordinate their actual values are

| Chart | Legal bit | c | c^-1 | L c | L c^-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| finite | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| finite | 1 | h/x | x/h | h² | x² |
| infinity | 0 | t | 1/t | t² | 1 |
| infinity | 1 | s | 1/s | s² | 1 |

Thus one additional legal factor at the SELECTED inclusion owner clears every multiplier. I and P occur at that same owner, so their conservative carrier powers add to four. For R having an already established bound on its occurrence list, align that list with the selected owner and add 4/1 there. If the selected owner is already in the list, this is an addition at that occurrence, not a new copy of its clearance allowance.

This proof is coefficientwise and works for an arbitrary R expressed in the physical cohomology frame used in the formula. It does not require closedness, determinant one, or a curvature identity. It also does not remove legal factors already present in R or prove that a particular source's R has the correct sheaf membership. No new conclusion about End homotopies is asserted here; that is a separate argument.

## 7. Static verification and retained frontier

check_frame_metadata.py authenticates six inert inputs, verifies the exact raw-engine nu, Dframe, g, and I/P leaf source contracts without importing them, and checks the source-bound weights. Its scalar checks operate on integer exponent triples for x,h,y or t,s,u, with y²=xh or u²=ts represented by the exponent rule. They construct no source polynomial, primitive matrix, or residual.

The checker covers all 256 ordered mask pairs on all 16 common standard charts, all 256 single-owner legal/standard combinations for inclusion, and the standard-tag conversion identities. Active negative controls detect a missing ell factor, a missing mixed reciprocal endpoint charge, the false infinity choice e_1=1/sqrt(t), and legal-zero clearance. Normal and optimized Python runs must agree.

This removes the imported undifferentiated transition legal-2 premise in favor of a source-bound legal-1 theorem, and separately improves the inclusion's extra legal allowance. It does not alter any source, weight, partition, old-connection definition decision. Full-source and endpoint ledgers must be recomputed explicitly before claiming new final exponents. Source semantics, actual residual attribution, higher derivative/normalization operations, class comparison, outer-domain target conditions, and obstruction values remain distinct.
