# No quintic relations in the vector-valued HM middle module

## Result

Let S=Sym X with dim X=5, and let L be the actual reindexed HM middle module with Hilbert function (2,10,20,20,10,2), generated by U=L₀=W′. Then its minimal relations occur only in degrees two, three, and four:

    Tor₁^S(L,C)₅=0.

Consequently the parameter c in the audited self-dual Betti shape is zero, without assuming that the cubic obstruction a vanishes. By duality,

    Tor₄^S(L,C)₅=0.

This is a genuine higher-syzygy exclusion in the previously unresolved a=15 case. It does not determine a, b, d, or e. In particular, it does not turn the Hilbert numerator into a pure resolution.

## 1. Audited inputs

Use the conventions and proofs in ../middle-module-relations/PROOF.md and AUDIT.md, ../cubic-relation-resolution/PROOF.md and AUDIT.md, ../apolar-middle-module/PROOF.md and AUDIT.md, and ../scalar-apolar-middle/PROOF.md. Write R=ker(S⊗U→L). The imported data are

    dim R₄=130,   dim R₅=250,
    L₁=X⊗U,      L₄=L₁*,      L₅=U*,
    X=V*,        U=W′=U*.

All dualities are N-equivariant with the conventions already audited there. In particular

    L₄=V⊗W′.

The group is N=H₅⋊SL₂(F₅); the center μ₅ acts trivially on U and primitively on X. In total degree five the center therefore acts trivially. The quotient SL₂(F₅) acts transitively on the 24 nontrivial characters of H₅/μ₅.

The quintic invariant and finite-character formulas are

    S₅^{H₅}=χ₃⊕χ₃′,
    χ₃⊗W′=χ₆,
    χ₃′⊗W′=W′⊕Q,

where Q is the irreducible spinorial four-dimensional representation. These are imported from the same audited primary-source representation calculations used in the vector cubic and scalar quintic arguments. The primary source is Horrocks–Mumford, Topology 12 (1973), 63–81:
https://www.dam.brown.edu/people/mumford/alg_geom/papers/1973a--Rk2BdleP%5E4-NC.pdf

The scalar annihilator ideal and the vector relation module R are different objects. The proof below concerns R throughout; the scalar proof contributes only the already established representation formulas and character-orbit observation.

## 2. A universal bound: at most sixteen quintic relations

Let C=R₅/(X R₄). This is exactly the minimal quintic relation space: every product of a lower-degree relation to degree five lies in X R₄, since R is an S-submodule. Thus c=dim C.

The universal module polynomial-shadow inequality proved in ../middle-module-relations/PROOF.md states that for T⊂S_d⊗E,

    dim(XT)/dim S_(d+1) ≥ dim T/dim S_d.

For T=R₄, dim S₄=70 and dim S₅=126. Therefore

    dim(XR₄) ≥ 126·130/70 = 234,
    c = 250−dim(XR₄) ≤ 16.

Only ordinary polynomial multiplication and the dimension of a subspace are used here. No selected HM or quintic coefficient map is evaluated.

## 3. The minimal quintic quotient has no Heisenberg invariants

Finite-group invariants are exact over C. Taking H₅-invariants of

    0→R₅→S₅⊗U→L₅→0

and using the formulas above yields

    (S₅⊗U)^{H₅}=χ₆⊕W′⊕Q,
    R₅^{H₅}=χ₆⊕Q.

Indeed L₅=U*=W′ and the sole W′ constituent maps onto it.

Choose an N-equivariant splitting S₄⊗U=R₄⊕L₄ as representations. Full polynomial multiplication

    X⊗(S₄⊗U)→S₅⊗U

is surjective. The H₅-invariants of its complementary source summand are

    (X⊗L₄)^{H₅}
      =(V*⊗V⊗W′)^{H₅}
      =W′.

The last equality follows from irreducibility of V over H₅: the invariant endomorphisms of V form the one-dimensional trivial N-representation, spanned by the identity. As W′, χ₆, and Q are pairwise nonisomorphic irreducibles, this complementary source cannot reach either χ₆ or Q in the invariant target. Surjectivity therefore forces both of these constituents to be reached from (X⊗R₄)^{H₅}.

Its image is exactly (XR₄)^{H₅}, again by exactness of invariants. Since XR₄⊂R₅, this proves

    (XR₄)^{H₅}=R₅^{H₅},    C^{H₅}=0.

Notice the use of the full vector relation space R₄, not the scalar ideal I₄ or the submodule generated by quadratic relations.

## 4. The orbit-size contradiction

The center acts trivially on C, so its restriction to H₅ is a sum of one-dimensional characters of H₅/μ₅. Its trivial-character multiplicity is zero by Section 3. Since C is an N-module, conjugation by SL₂(F₅) preserves character multiplicities. Transitivity on the 24 nontrivial characters means all those multiplicities are equal. Hence

    dim C is a nonnegative multiple of 24.

But Section 2 gives dim C≤16. Thus C=0, as claimed.

## 5. Updated necessary Betti shape

The audited self-duality is β_(i,j)=β_(5−i,10−j). Its degree bounds already exclude minimal relations of degree six or larger. Substituting c=0, the remaining possible form is

    F₀=S²;
    F₁=S(−2)¹⁰ ⊕ S(−3)^a ⊕ S(−4)^b;
    F₂=S(−3)^a ⊕ S(−4)^(20+b+d) ⊕ S(−5)^e ⊕ S(−6)^d;
    F₃=S(−4)^d ⊕ S(−5)^e ⊕ S(−6)^(20+b+d) ⊕ S(−7)^a;
    F₄=S(−6)^b ⊕ S(−7)^a ⊕ S(−8)¹⁰;
    F₅=S(−10)².

Here a∈{0,15}, b∈{0,5,10,15,20}, d is a nonnegative multiple of five, and e is a nonnegative integer. If a=0, minimality still forces d=0, as in the previous proof. No assertion is made that every displayed parameter choice occurs.

## 6. Literature search and evidence boundary

A targeted public literature search for the minimal resolution of H²(End₀F(*)) found primary literature on the cohomology modules of F itself and on restrictions of F to planes. Those are different modules and do not justify a resolution here. No published minimal resolution of the specified vector module was established by this search, and no claim of nonexistence is made. The remaining cubic fifteen-dimensional scalar was not resolved.

This proof instead closes an independent higher-relation gap by universal shadow growth, exact finite-group invariants, and orbit size. There was no selected actual HM, Ψ, source, cofactor, or rank-instance evaluation; no external write; and no large computation.
