## 2. The minimal quotient and universal upper bound

For the minimal degree-zero presentation S⊗U→L, the first Betti space is R/(S₊R). Since S is generated by X=S₁ and R is an S-submodule,

    (S₊R)₅ = XR₄.

Indeed every product of a relation of degree k<5 with a polynomial of degree 5−k factors through multiplication of a relation in R₄ by a linear form. Hence C=R₅/XR₄ is exactly Tor₁^S(L,C)₅, regardless of any lower-degree minimal relation counts.

The normalized shadow inequality in the cited proof applies to arbitrary subspaces of a free module, so it applies to the full R₄⊂S₄⊗U. Its leading-monomial argument does not require scalar ideals, genericity, or any special multiplication coefficients. For each free-module component, a uniform degree-d exponent vector a followed by incrementing coordinate i with probability (a_i+1)/(n+d) yields a uniform degree-(d+1) exponent vector. A monomial set maps into its shadow, giving the normalized bound; summing over the free-module components preserves it.

Consequently

    dim(XR₄) ≥ (126/70)·130 = 234,
    0 ≤ dim C = 250−dim(XR₄) ≤ 16.

The arithmetic is integral already; no rounding or strict inequality is needed. In contrast to the scalar quintic argument, the non-strict module inequality is sufficient. This step uses the entire degree-four relation space, not the submodule generated by quadrics or cubics.

## 3. Exact exclusion of the invariant sector

Over C, invariants under finite H₅ are exact. The generated degree-five presentation therefore gives an exact sequence on invariants. Its target is L₅=W′. The unique W′ constituent in χ₆⊕W′⊕Q must map isomorphically onto it, while the inequivalent constituents map to zero. Thus

    R₅^H₅ = χ₆⊕Q.

Choose an N-equivariant splitting of the degree-four presentation. Finite-group semisimplicity supplies such a splitting, and tensoring by X gives the source decomposition

    X⊗(S₄⊗U) ≅ (X⊗R₄) ⊕ (X⊗L₄).

The ordinary polynomial multiplication from this source onto S₅⊗U is surjective and N-equivariant. It remains surjective after taking H₅-invariants. The complementary invariant source is

    (X⊗L₄)^H₅
      ≅ (V*⊗V)^H₅ ⊗ W′
      ≅ W′.

The first factor is the invariant endomorphism line of the H₅-irreducible V. It is spanned by the identity, and the entire normalizer acts trivially on it by conjugation. Thus there is no hidden one-dimensional quotient-group twist in this calculation.

An equivariant map from this complementary W′ cannot reach χ₆ or Q. Surjectivity on invariants forces both target constituents χ₆ and Q to be reached from (X⊗R₄)^H₅. Multiplication of a relation is a relation, so this source has image contained in R₅. Furthermore exactness of invariants applied to the surjection X⊗R₄→XR₄ identifies that invariant image with (XR₄)^H₅. It follows that

    (XR₄)^H₅ = R₅^H₅,
    C^H₅ = 0.

This proves absence of all H₅-invariants, not merely absence of an N-trivial line. That stronger conclusion is exactly what the orbit argument needs. No identification between the vector relation module R and the scalar annihilator ideal I occurs.

## 4. Orbit-size contradiction

The center μ₅ acts trivially on U and by a primitive fifth-root character on X. Hence it acts trivially in degree five on S₅⊗U and on its subquotient C. The restriction of C to H₅ therefore factors through the finite abelian quotient H₅/μ₅, so it decomposes into its one-dimensional character spaces.

The zero/trivial character has multiplicity zero because C^H₅=0. Normalizer equivariance transports each character space isomorphically to the character space obtained by conjugation, preserving multiplicities. The quotient SL₂(F₅) is transitive on the 24 nonzero characters, as on the nonzero vectors of the dual two-dimensional F₅-space. Thus

    dim C = 24m

for an integer m≥0. Combining this with dim C≤16 forces m=0 and C=0.

Both ingredients are essential: the dimension bound alone allows nonzero quintic relations, and the orbit bound alone would not exclude small representations factoring through N/H₅. Section 3 eliminates precisely that latter possibility. The proof establishes c=0 without knowing whether a is 0 or 15, or knowing b,d,e.

### Identification of the linear second-syzygy space

Write E=R₂ and Z₁=ker(F₁→F₀)=im(F₂→F₁). Since F₁ has its lowest generator degree two, its degree-three part is

    (X⊗E) ⊕ G₃,

where G₃ is the chosen minimal cubic relation generator space. The images of G₃ are independent modulo XR₂ in R₃. Hence a relation among these degree-three elements has zero G₃ component and lies in ker(X⊗E→R₃). There are no degree-two syzygies among the basis E. Consequently

    Z₁,₃ = A = Tor₂^S(L,C)₃ ⊂ X⊗E.

This identification holds even when G₃ and A both have dimension fifteen. It does not discard the cubic generators or assume the quadratic presentation is complete.

### Why degree-four generators of F₂ cannot enter the kernel

Let B be the degree-four minimal generator space of Z₁. In degree four,

    F₂,₄ = (X⊗A) ⊕ B.

The images of a basis of B are independent modulo (S₊Z₁)₄. Because Z₁ has no components below degree three, this latter space is exactly XZ₁,₃. The image of X⊗A lies in XZ₁,₃. If an element v+b of the displayed direct sum maps to zero in F₁, projecting its image to Z₁,₄/XZ₁,₃ forces b=0. Thus every degree-four kernel element lies in X⊗A.

The differential of a degree-three generator A has no constant entries in the degree-three summand of F₁ by minimality. Its only possible entries are linear forms in the degree-two summand S(−2)⊗E, and these give the inclusion A⊂X⊗E just established. By S-linearity, the differential on X⊗A is therefore exactly polynomial multiplication into S₂⊗E. Hence

    ker(F₂,₄→F₁,₄) = ker[X⊗A→S₂⊗E].

This argument allows arbitrary degree-four syzygy generators and all their possible components in F₁. Their minimal independence, rather than a presumed special form of their differential, excludes them from this kernel.

### Identification with Tor₃ and the shadow bound

The minimum-degree bound for the minimal resolution gives no generators of F₃ below degree four and no elements of F₄ in degree four. Therefore exactness identifies F₃,₄ injectively with ker(F₂,₄→F₁,₄). Also F₃,₄ is precisely its degree-four minimal generator space. Thus

    Tor₃^S(L,C)₄ ≅ ker[X⊗A→S₂⊗E],
    d = 5a−dim(XA).

If a=0 this is zero. If a=15, the same universal free-module shadow inequality, now with degree one and underlying coefficient space E, gives

    dim(XA) ≥ [dim S₂/dim S₁]·dim A = (15/5)·15 = 45.

The source has dimension 5·15=75, so d≤30. The previously checked nontrivial central character in internal degree four makes d divisible by five. The asserted finite list follows.

No use of c=0 is needed for this identification or bound: the minimum degree of F₄ is already at least five in the earlier general shape.
