# Linear presentation excludes divisorial quadratic failure

## Statement and boundary

Let S=Sym(V) over C, with dim V=m>=2. Let M be a nonzero finite-length graded S-module with a linear presentation

    S(-1)^b -> S^a -> M -> 0.

Thus every defining relation is generated by relations of degree one; generation in degree zero alone is not enough. Assume dim M_3=2. For q in S_2 write T_q:M_1->M_3 for multiplication. Let D be the closed cone where rank T_q<2, defined by the two-by-two minors.

Then:

1. For every nonzero lambda in M_3^vee, the map

       C_lambda:S_2 -> M_1^vee,  q |-> [w |-> lambda(qw)]

   has rank at least three.
2. D has codimension at least two in S_2.
3. At every q_0 with rank T_(q_0)=1, at least one minor has nonzero first derivative at q_0. Consequently a nonempty open set of directions q_1 makes the local cokernel of T_(q_0+tq_1) at t=0 isomorphic to C[t]_(t)/(t). If rank T_(q_0)=0 and T_(q_1) is surjective, that local cokernel is two copies of C[t]_(t)/(t). If rank T_(q_0)=2 it is zero.
4. In the HM setting of PR440--443, q_1 can be chosen to be a good square ell^2, also nonisotropic for u_0. With that choice the actual algebraic translated-root curve of PR443 has its localized endpoint quotient Q_A killed by the parameter s at zero, not merely by s^2. Its fiber is the original unresolved Q_0.

The last statement is an existence theorem for a more carefully chosen direction. It does not retroactively assert that every good direction selected in PR442 or PR443 has this stronger property. It does not evaluate the rank at the original quadratic or the value of Q_0.

Combining with PR445, after localization at s=0, for this choice of curve the torsion in relative degree-three paired coefficient cohomology is killed by s. Every single restriction between successive parameter thickenings on degree-two cohomology is zero. A nonzero exceptional closed-fiber coefficient class cannot lift even to C[s]/(s^2) in this curve. This is an auxiliary coefficient-class nonlifting statement, not an actual-source, module, or quantization obstruction.

## 1. Linear relations and inverse cubics

Choose degree-zero generators e_1,...,e_a of M. For lambda in M_3^vee set

    P_i(y)=lambda((y_1 x_1+...+y_m x_m)^3 e_i).

These are homogeneous cubic polynomials in dual variables y. Since M is generated in degree zero, the map lambda |-> (P_i) is injective by polarization. In particular some P_i is nonzero when lambda is nonzero.

Put W=span{partial P_i/partial y_j: all i,j}, a space of quadratic polynomials. The derivatives are

    partial_j P_i=3 lambda((sum y_k x_k)^2 x_j e_i).

The elements x_j e_i span M_1. Under the perfect polarization pairing between S_2 and quadratic polynomials in y, the displayed derivatives are the functionals q |-> 3 lambda(q x_j e_i). Therefore

    dim W=rank C_lambda.                              (1)

This is the rank of the vector-cubic catalecticant, not the Hessian rank of one scalar cubic.

Every linear relation r=sum_i l_i(x)e_i in the presentation gives

    sum_i l_i(partial_y) P_i=0.                        (2)

If a nonzero gamma=(gamma_i) and nonzero point v in V^vee satisfy

    sum_i gamma_i l_i(v)=0

for every defining linear relation, there is a surjective graded S-module map

    M -> C[t],   e_i |-> gamma_i,   x_j |-> v_j t.     (3)

Indeed the defining relations map to zero, and they generate the whole relation module. At least one generator maps to a nonzero constant, so the image is all C[t]. This contradicts finite length. We will extract (3) from the assumption dim W<=2.

## 2. Elementary classification when dim W<=2

Let U be the span of the cubics P_i.

### Case A: dim U=1

Write P_i=gamma_i P for one nonzero cubic P and a nonzero vector gamma. For every relation, (2) says that the directional derivative of P along sum_i gamma_i l_i is zero. Let

    Ann_1(P)={l in V: l(partial_y)P=0}.

This is a proper subspace: a positive-degree nonzero polynomial has some nonzero derivative in characteristic zero. Choose a nonzero v annihilating Ann_1(P). Then sum_i gamma_i l_i(v)=0 for all relations, giving the forbidden quotient (3). This case is impossible, without even using dim W<=2.

### Case B: dim U>=2

We first show that some P in U has a two-dimensional first-derivative span. Every P has derivative span contained in W. A nonzero homogeneous cubic whose derivative span has dimension one is a scalar cube of a linear form: after a linear change of coordinates its directional derivative kernel has codimension one, so it depends on just one variable. If every nonzero P in U had derivative span one, two independent members would be cubes of independent linear forms; their sum has derivative span two, a contradiction. Thus such a P exists.

After choosing coordinates, P depends on exactly two variables x,y. To justify this, its directional derivative map has kernel of codimension two; choose complementary coordinates and integrate the vanishing derivatives in all other directions. Its derivative span is W, so W consists of binary quadratic polynomials.

Every Q in U is also binary. In fact each derivative of Q is in W. For an outside variable z_j, partial_(z_j)Q is binary; hence all second derivatives in outside variables vanish and

    Q=R(x,y)+sum_j z_j q_j(x,y)

with q_j homogeneous quadratic. The x and y derivatives of Q must be binary too, so partial_x q_j=partial_y q_j=0. Homogeneity gives q_j=0.

Now dim W=2 inside the three-dimensional space of binary quadratics. Choose a nonzero constant-coefficient quadratic differential operator L which annihilates W under the perfect degree-two differential pairing. For each Q in U,

    partial_x(LQ)=L(partial_x Q)=0,
    partial_y(LQ)=L(partial_y Q)=0.

The polynomial LQ has degree one and hence is zero. Over C a nonzero binary quadratic differential operator is, after an invertible linear coordinate change and scaling, either partial_x partial_y or partial_y^2. Their cubic kernels are respectively

    span{x^3,y^3},              span{x^3,x^2y}.        (4)

These kernels have dimension two. Since dim U>=2 and U lies in one of them, U equals that kernel.

In the first case write P_i=gamma_i x^3+delta_i y^3. Equation (2) gives separately

    sum_i gamma_i l_i(v_x)=0,
    sum_i delta_i l_i(v_y)=0,

where v_x and v_y are the coefficient-evaluation points in the x and y directions. The vectors gamma and delta are nonzero, indeed independent because dim U=2. The first identity already gives the forbidden line quotient (3).

In the second case write P_i=gamma_i x^3+delta_i x^2y. The coefficient of xy in (2) is

    2 sum_i delta_i l_i(v_x)=0.

Again delta is nonzero, so this gives (3) with gamma replaced by delta and v=v_x. The tangent case has therefore not been omitted.

Both cases contradict finite length. Thus dim W>=3. By (1), this proves statement 1.

## 3. The bad locus has no divisor

Let N=dim S_2. Consider the incidence subvariety

    Z={(q,[lambda]) in S_2 x P(M_3^vee): C_lambda(q)=0}.

Its fiber over each [lambda] is a linear space of dimension at most N-3 by statement 1. Since P(M_3^vee)=P^1,

    dim Z <= (N-3)+1=N-2.

Projection to S_2 is closed because the other factor is projective. Its image is exactly D: a map to the two-dimensional target fails surjectivity precisely when a nonzero target-dual functional kills its image. Hence dim D<=N-2. This is a dimension statement for its support; no reducedness or smoothness of the whole determinantal scheme is asserted.

For completeness, a general affine line through a prescribed q_0 avoids D away from q_0. The bad direction classes lie in the closure of the radial projection of D minus {q_0} into P(S_2), which has dimension at most N-2, less than dim P(S_2)=N-1. This statement concerns unrestricted quadratic directions; it is not asserted for the smaller Veronese family of square directions.

## 4. First-order transversality at rank one

Suppose T_0=T_(q_0) has rank one. Split M_1=C e_0 direct sum K and choose target basis so T_0(e_0)=(1,0) and T_0(K)=0.

Assume, for contradiction, that every two-by-two minor has zero differential at q_0 in every quadratic direction. The differential of the minor on columns e_0,k is the second component of T_q(k), for each k in K. Thus every T_q has the block form

    T_q = [ a(q)   b(q) ]
          [ c(q)     0  ],

where a,c are scalar linear functions and b is a K-dual-valued linear function on S_2.

The function c cannot vanish identically: otherwise every T_q has image in the first target line, contradicting D having codimension at least two. But for every q in the hyperplane c(q)=0, the displayed matrix has rank at most one. Thus D contains a hyperplane, again contradicting Section 3.

Some minor therefore has a nonzero linear differential at q_0. For every direction q_1 outside the kernel of that nonzero functional, the minor of T_0+tT_(q_1) has order exactly one in t. Over A=C[t]_(t), one matrix entry is a unit because T_0 has rank one. Elementary invertible row and column operations put that unit in a separate one-by-one block. The remaining cokernel is a cyclic module whose defining ideal is generated by the residual second-row entries, equivalently by the maximal minors up to units. All vanish at t=0 and at least one has order one. That ideal is therefore (t), proving

    coker_A(T_0+tT_(q_1)) = A/(t).                    (5)

The equality denotes isomorphism of modules, not a chosen canonical generator.

If rank T_0=0, the pencil is tT_(q_1). A surjective T_(q_1) splits as a map of vector spaces, giving cokernel (A/(t))^2. If rank T_0=2, a unit minor gives zero local cokernel.

These statements do not claim that every rank-one point of D is smooth. They prove that its determinantal equations have some nonzero differential, enough for (5).

## 5. Good-square directions and the actual coefficient curve

The HM cohomology module M=H^1_*(F) satisfies exactly the hypotheses: PR167 gives a presentation ending

    15S(-1) -> 5S -> M -> 0

and the finite Hilbert series 5+10z+10z^2+2z^3. Thus it is finite length, linearly presented, and dim M_3=2.

PR443 proves that the set of ell in S_1 for which T_(ell^2) is surjective is nonempty Zariski open. The condition alpha=ell^T U_0^(-1)ell !=0 is also nonempty open, since U_0 is nondegenerate. In the rank-one case Section 4 gives a nonzero linear functional d on S_2 whose nonvanishing ensures (5). The polynomial d(ell^2) is not identically zero: squares span S_2 by polarization. Therefore these three nonempty opens intersect in the irreducible affine space S_1.

Choose ell in that intersection. In the rank-zero and rank-two cases only the first two open conditions are needed. No case is asserted to be the actual rank of the fixed quadratic.

For the corresponding PR443 curve, t=2s+alpha s^2 is s times a unit at s=0. Its primary cokernel over the local s-ring is consequently killed by s in all three cases. The natural surjection from fifteen copies of that primary cokernel onto the endpoint quotient, proved in PR440/442 and valid for the moving cubic of PR443, gives

    s Q_A=0.

Right-exact cokernel base change identifies Q_A/sQ_A with the original Q_0. Since sQ_A=0, the quotient map identifies Q_A itself with Q_0 as a module on which s acts by zero. This statement is canonical for the specified chosen family, not a canonical choice of ell. It does not identify Q_A for arbitrary earlier choices of ell.

The rank-one case implies dim Q_0<=15; the rank-zero case retains <=30; rank two implies Q_0=0. Those fiber bounds were already implicit in PR442. The new consequence is that an allowed existential choice of square direction removes all higher local parameter torsion, using linear presentation rather than a fixed-map evaluation.

PR445 now identifies torsion in relative degree-three cohomology with Ext^1_A(Q_A,A) twice. It is killed by s as well. Its formula for fat-fiber degree two and its transition maps shows that every restriction from thickness n+1 to n is zero. In particular the map from the dual-number thickening to the original closed fiber is zero. A nonzero closed-fiber class cannot be the restriction of a class on that thickening.

This is stronger than PR445's direction-independent third-thickening exclusion, but applies to the more carefully chosen nonempty open set of square directions above. It still does not determine whether any such exceptional closed-fiber class exists.
