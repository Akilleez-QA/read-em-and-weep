## 1. Low-rank vector cubics and the linear-presentation hypothesis

The argument correctly treats the span of **all** first derivatives of all component cubics, rather than the Hessian or derivative rank of an arbitrarily chosen scalar component.

Degree-zero generation makes the inverse-cubic construction injective: polarization recovers every value of a functional on `S_3 M_0=M_3`. Since the elements `x_j e_i` span `M_1`, the span of the quadrics obtained by differentiating the components is the image of the transpose of `C_lambda`, up to a harmless common scalar. Thus equation (1) has the correct rank interpretation, even when the chosen degree-one spanning set has relations.

Every defining linear relation gives equation (2). Conversely, the quotient construction (3) uses the **entire kernel being generated by these linear relations**. With the indicated `S`-action on `C[t]`, each such relation maps to zero. A nonzero generator image supplies a constant, and the nonzero point `v` makes the image of `S` contain `t`; the resulting module map is therefore surjective. This is an infinite-length quotient, impossible for finite-length `M`. No analogous factorization is justified from degree-zero generation alone, and the proof does not make that error.

The classification is complete:

1. If the scalar component span `U` has dimension one, the relation directions annihilate a nonzero cubic. The first-order annihilator is proper. Any nonzero point in its annihilator gives the forbidden line quotient. This case is excluded without the small-derivative-rank assumption, as claimed.
2. If `dim U>=2` and the total derivative span has dimension at most two, some member has derivative rank two. Otherwise two independent members would be cubes of independent linear forms, and their sum would have derivative rank two.
3. That member depends on two variables after a linear coordinate change, and its derivative span equals the whole two-dimensional space `W`. Every other member is binary as well. The argument eliminating terms `z_j q_j(x,y)` is valid: its `x` and `y` derivatives would otherwise retain outside variables, whereas all derivatives lie in the binary space `W`.
4. A nonzero quadratic differential operator annihilating `W` also annihilates every member of `U`. Its output on a cubic is homogeneous linear, and both derivatives vanish. Over the complex numbers the operator has either distinct linear factors or a repeated factor. The two cubic kernels are exactly `span{x^3,y^3}` and `span{x^3,x^2 y}`.
5. In the first kernel, the coefficient of `x^2` in the relation equation supplies the required evaluation identity. In the second, the coefficient of `xy` supplies it using the nonzero coefficient vector of `x^2 y`. This explicitly includes the repeated-factor/tangent case; it does not mistakenly classify every pencil as a span of two distinct cubes.

These checks establish `rank C_lambda>=3` for every nonzero `lambda`. There is no omitted case `dim W=0`, because a nonzero homogeneous cubic in characteristic zero has a nonzero derivative.

## 2. Incidence codimension and the rank-one differential

The incidence argument is valid without assuming constant rank or flatness. Every fiber over the projective line of target-dual functionals has dimension at most `N-3`; hence the incidence space has dimension at most `N-2`. Properness of projection along the projective factor makes its image closed. Its image is precisely the failure-of-surjectivity locus because the target has dimension two. This proves codimension at least two for the support, and does not assert that the determinantal scheme is reduced.

The supplementary unrestricted-line statement is also correct: radial projection centered at `q_0` of `D` minus `q_0` has closure of dimension at most `N-2` in the `(N-1)`-dimensional projective direction space. It is appropriately not used to deduce the square-direction assertion.

At a rank-one point, vanishing of every minor differential forces the component of every `T_q(k)` transverse to the original image to vanish for every `k` in `ker T_0`. This yields exactly the displayed block form. If `c` were identically zero, all maps would be nonsurjective. Otherwise its zero hyperplane consists entirely of nonsurjective maps. Both alternatives contradict codimension at least two. Thus a maximal minor has a nonzero differential.

Along a direction where that differential is nonzero, one original matrix entry is a unit in the local parameter ring. Splitting off that unit reduces the cokernel to a cyclic module. Its defining ideal is the ideal of maximal minors, up to units. All those minors vanish at the origin, and one has valuation exactly one, so the ideal is `(t)` and the cokernel is `A/(t)`. The rank-zero and rank-two alternatives are likewise correct. This does not overstate the conclusion as smoothness of the whole determinantal locus.

## 3. The three open conditions on square directions

The imported good-square result was checked in the pinned PR443 text. Its proof uses a single rational proportionality factor for the flattened derivative rows of two vector-valued polynomials. Euler's identity and differentiation make that factor constant, contradicting the independent target-dual functionals. It proves nonemptiness on the space of linear forms itself; it does not merely intersect an arbitrary open set with the Veronese variety.

For the additional transverse condition, a nonzero functional `d` on `S_2` cannot vanish on every square, since squares span `S_2` in characteristic zero. Thus `d(ell^2)!=0` is a nonempty open condition. Nonisotropy is another nonempty open condition because `U_0` is nondegenerate. Irreducibility of `S_1` makes all three opens intersect. Only the first two are needed in the rank-zero and rank-two cases.

The argument is existential and applies whichever of the three fixed ranks actually occurs. It neither selects a numerical form nor determines the fixed HM multiplication rank.

## 4. Endpoint quotient and dual-number nonlifting

The endpoint comparison is used correctly as a **surjection**, not an identification with fifteen copies of the primary quotient. PR440 supplies multiplication-compatible graded comparison maps; PR442 and PR443 expressly allow taking the further quotient by a moving cubic. All additional relations remain present.

At zero the substitution `t=2s+alpha s^2` identifies the ideal generated by `t` with `(s)` in the local `s`-ring. Cokernel base change therefore takes each of the three primary-cokernel alternatives to an `s`-annihilated module. The surjection to the endpoint gives `s Q_A=0`. Right-exact base change identifies its residue quotient with the **original** `Q_0`, and since that quotient map now has zero kernel, it canonically identifies this specified local family module with `Q_0` equipped with zero `s`-action.

The PR445 consequence is also correct. Its relative degree-two module vanishes, while the torsion in relative degree three is canonically the doubled `Ext^1_A(Q_A,A)`. Over the DVR, that Ext module has the same elementary-divisor exponents as `Q_A`, so it is killed by `s`. The PR445 fat-fiber identification is natural for restriction: under the parameter-kernel descriptions, the map from thickness `n+1` to thickness `n` is multiplication by `s`, not an inclusion or an isomorphism. Every such map is consequently zero here. In particular a nonzero closed-fiber coefficient class cannot lift to the dual-number thickening of this chosen curve.

This conclusion neither asserts the existence of a nonzero closed-fiber class nor produces an actual-source, module-lifting, or quantization obstruction. The draft preserves those distinctions.

## 5. Precision note resolved

The degree-three torsion annihilation and all the thickening assertions are **local at `s=0`**. The final proof explicitly inserts “after localization at s=0” into the introductory sentence beginning “Combining with PR445.” This resolves the sole optional precision note. It does not claim that multiplication by the global function `s` kills torsion supported at other parameters of the algebraic curve. No such global strengthening is proved or needed.

Likewise, simple primary cokernel does not require a chosen two-column controller polynomial from PR442 to have a simple root: extra columns may improve that controller. The draft correctly argues with the full primary cokernel and makes no claim about the multiplicity of that auxiliary polynomial.
