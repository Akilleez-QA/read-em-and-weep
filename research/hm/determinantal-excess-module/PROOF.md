# The indecomposable factor of the determinantal excess

Work over C with S=Sym(T), dim T=5, and m=S_+. Let H be the dual-cohomology module, I=Ann_S(H), D=Fitt_0^S(H), and Z=I/D. PR495 proves that Z is generated in degree three, with

Z_3=A⊗W′,  Z_4=V⊗T_4,  Z_j=0 otherwise,

and that multiplication T⊗Z_3→Z_4 is onto. The notation distinguishes the ordinary four-dimensional T_4=W⊗W′ from the spin representation Qspin. All tensor decompositions below are for the pinned finite normalizer over C.

We prove that, as a graded S-module, Z is the direct sum of two isomorphic indecomposable modules of length fifteen. We also determine its full S-linear endomorphism algebra. This gives an actual module structure for the thirty-dimensional square-zero excess, not just its Hilbert function.

## 1. The unique multiplication factor

The normalizer identities used in PR489 and PR495 give

T⊗A=V⊗(U′⊕W),
U′⊗W′=W′⊕Qspin,
W⊗W′=T_4.

Consequently

T⊗A⊗W′=V⊗(W′⊕Qspin⊕T_4).

The target V⊗T_4 is irreducible and appears exactly once. Define a graded module C by

C_0=A,  C_1=V⊗W,  C_j=0 for j≠0,1,

with multiplication T⊗C_0→C_1 the nonzero projection onto the V⊗W summand of T⊗A. Multiplication out of C_1 is zero. These prescriptions define an S-module: every product of two positive-degree variables acts as zero, so the commuting-variable relations are satisfied.

The multiplication of C⊗W′ is a nonzero equivariant map to V⊗T_4. The actual multiplication of Z is also nonzero by its proved surjectivity. Multiplicity one makes the two maps scalar multiples. Rescaling the target gives a normalizer-equivariant graded S-module isomorphism

Z≅C(-3)⊗W′.

Here C(-3)_j=C_(j−3), and W′ has degree zero. Thus, after forgetting the normalizer action and choosing a basis of W′, Z≅C(-3)⊕C(-3). The decomposition into two chosen copies is not asserted to be normalizer-invariant.

For completeness, C has the exact presentation

C=(S⊗A)/(S·(V⊗U′)+m²(S⊗A)),

where V⊗U′ is embedded as the kernel of the displayed projection in degree one. This is a complete presentation, not an assertion that all the displayed quadratic relations are minimal.

## 2. Its exact annihilator

By construction m² annihilates C. The space of linear scalars annihilating C is a normalizer-stable subspace of the irreducible T. It is not all of T, because T C_0=C_1≠0. It is therefore zero. No nonzero scalar constant annihilates C. Since the annihilator is homogeneous,

Ann_S(C)=m²,  Ann_S(Z)=m².

The latter equality also follows from the two-copy decomposition. These are annihilators of the excess module, not I=Ann_S(H).

## 3. Degree-zero endomorphism rigidity

Let E_d=Hom_S(C,C)_d. Since C is generated in degree zero, restriction injects E_0 into End_C(A). The scalar maps give a copy of C in E_0. Under normalizer conjugation, End_C(A) is the direct sum of that scalar line and an irreducible twenty-four-dimensional representation. Indeed, the finite Heisenberg group modulo its center has twenty-five characters on End_C(A), each once; the trivial character gives the identity, and the quotient normalizer acts transitively on the twenty-four nontrivial characters. A stable subspace of their sum must contain either none or all of those one-dimensional character spaces. This proves the claimed irreducibility directly.

Finite-group semisimplicity now implies that E_0 is either just the scalar line or all of End_C(A). The second alternative is impossible. Let L_1=ker(T⊗A→C_1)=V⊗U′, which is nonzero. If every linear map on A extended to C, L_1 would be stable under id_T⊗B for every B∈End_C(A). Write a nonzero relation as r=Σ_i t_i⊗a_i using a basis a_i of A. Applying the matrix unit that takes a_i to any chosen a_j and kills the other basis vectors shows t_i a_j=0 in C_1 for every j. Hence every t_i is a linear scalar annihilator of C. Section 2 forces t_i=0 for all i, contradicting r≠0. Therefore

End_S(C)_0=C.

This argument concerns the normalizer-stable space of all S-linear endomorphisms. It does not assume that each individual endomorphism is equivariant.

## 4. Full endomorphism algebra and indecomposability

There are no negative-degree endomorphisms, since C is generated by C_0 and has no negative component. There are no endomorphisms of degree greater than one, by support. Every linear map A→V⊗W extends uniquely to an S-linear degree-one endomorphism, acting as zero on C_1: for a generator a and a variable t, both images of t a are zero in degree two. Thus

End_S(C)=C⊕U_C,
U_C=Hom_C(A,V⊗W),  dim U_C=50,

where U_C is in degree one and U_C²=0. Its multiplication is (λ,u)(μ,v)=(λμ,λv+μu). This algebra has no idempotents except zero and one: an idempotent has λ²=λ and (2λ−1)u=0, hence u=0. Therefore C is indecomposable even as an ungraded S-module. Every ungraded S-linear map on these finite graded modules decomposes into its homogeneous components, so the displayed algebra accounts for all such maps.

The two-copy decomposition consequently gives

End_S(Z)≅Mat_2(C⊕U_C).

It has a four-dimensional degree-zero part and a two-hundred-dimensional degree-one part, with no other degrees; its total dimension is 204. The latter degree-one part is also directly Hom_C(Z_3,Z_4), confirming its dimension independently. It is square-zero, while the degree-zero matrix algebra acts on it on the left and right. In particular Z itself is not indecomposable, despite its irreducible normalizer representation in generating degree three.
