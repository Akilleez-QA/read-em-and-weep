# Exact quartic generators of the scalar annihilator

Work over C with the notation and actual scalar annihilator I=Ann_S(H) of PR488 at cecc51819d529e270afc9aac73a5715c3beee54c. Thus S=Sym(T), dim T=5, H_j=H^2(End_0(F)(j-5)), and R=S/I has Hilbert function (1,5,15,25,15,3). In particular I has no elements below degree three, I_3=A tensor W', and R_4=V tensor U'.

We prove that I has exactly fifteen minimal quartic generators, with normalizer representation V tensor U. The cubic generators have exactly ten linear syzygies, with representation V tensor W'. No choice of the remaining parameters in the presentation of H is made.

## 1. Universal normalizer decompositions

Write W,W' for the two-dimensional quotient representations; U=Sym^2 W and U'=Sym^2 W'; Q_4 for the four-dimensional spin representation; and T_4=W tensor W' for the ordinary four-dimensional representation. They are distinct labels. The relevant five-dimensional representations are T=V*, A=V_1.

Horrocks--Mumford Lemma 3.3(vii), dualized, gives

Sym^4 T = V tensor (T_4 + Q_4 + U + U').

All quotient representations in this identity are self-dual, so dualizing changes V* to V but not the displayed quotient labels. The tensor identities used in the pinned preceding proofs give

T tensor A = V tensor (U' + W),
U' tensor W' = W' + Q_4,
W tensor W' = T_4.

Consequently

T tensor I_3 = V tensor (W' + Q_4 + T_4).

Each displayed summand is irreducible for the normalizer, and different labels give inequivalent representations at the same nontrivial Heisenberg weight. Each appears with multiplicity one. The actual multiplication map

mu: T tensor I_3 -> Sym^4 T

is equivariant. Its V tensor W' summand must vanish, since that summand is absent in the target. On each of the other two summands it is either zero or an isomorphism onto the corresponding target summand. Therefore its image dimension belongs to {0,20,40}. This assertion does not assume either scalar is nonzero.

## 2. Hilbert growth forces both remaining channels

Let J be the homogeneous ideal generated by I_3. Then J_3=I_3 and J_4=T I_3. The standard graded algebra S/J has degree-three dimension 35-10=25. Macaulay's universal Hilbert-function growth bound applies regardless of whether S/J has finite length.

The degree-three binomial expansion is

25 = binom(6,3) + binom(3,2) + binom(2,1).

Thus

dim(S/J)_4 <= 25^{<3>} = binom(7,4)+binom(4,3)+binom(3,2)=42.

Since dim S_4=70, it follows that dim(T I_3)>=28. Combined with the three possibilities in section 1, this forces dim(T I_3)=40. Hence both V tensor Q_4 and V tensor T_4 multiply nontrivially, and

ker(mu)=V tensor W',
T I_3=V tensor (Q_4 + T_4).

The kernel is precisely the space of linear syzygies among the ten cubic generators. It has dimension ten. This proves a nonvanishing statement for the actual multiplication using a universal growth inequality, not evaluation of an actual multiplication matrix.

## 3. The exact minimal quartic generator representation

From PR488, the degree-four quotient is R_4=V tensor U'. The multiplicity-free decomposition of Sym^4 T therefore gives

I_4=V tensor (T_4 + Q_4 + U).

There are no lower-degree generators except I_3, so the minimal quartic generator space is I_4/(T I_3). Sections 1 and 2 yield

(I/S_+I)_4 = V tensor U,
dim (I/S_+I)_4=15.

Thus the lower bound of five in PR488 sharpens to exactly fifteen. Together with the ten cubic generators, the ideal needs at least twenty-five generators in total. The following sections exclude all quintic and higher generators. The complete minimal resolution of I is not determined.

## 4. No generators in degree six or above

There is also an exact upper degree bound. Define the reversed graded dual N_j=R_(5-j)*, with multiplication dual to that of R. Because R is level with socle precisely R_5, N is generated in degree zero. This standard duality follows directly by dualizing the quotient N/S_+N: its dual is the socle of R, with reversed degrees.

We have dim N_0=3 and dim N_1=15. Generation therefore makes

T tensor N_0 -> N_1

surjective between spaces of equal dimension, hence an isomorphism. The degree-four component at homological position four of the Koszul complex of N is Lambda^4 T tensor N_0. Its outgoing differential to Lambda^3 T tensor N_1 becomes, after this isomorphism, the exterior comultiplication

Lambda^4 T tensor N_0 -> Lambda^3 T tensor T tensor N_0.

This is injective: composing with exterior multiplication gives plus or minus four times the identity, and the field has characteristic zero. Thus Tor_4^S(N,C)_4=0.

Dualizing the finite-dimensional degree components of the Koszul complexes, and using the perfect pairings R_j tensor N_(5-j) -> C, gives

Tor_i^S(R,C)_d* tensor det(T) = Tor_(5-i)^S(N,C)_(10-d).

Only vanishing is needed here; the determinant twist is a line and does not affect it. The case i=1,d=6 now gives Tor_1^S(R,C)_6=0. For d>6, the homological-position-one Koszul term T tensor R_(d-1) is already zero, so Tor_1^S(R,C)_d=0 as well. Since Tor_1^S(R,C)=I/S_+I, I is generated in degrees three, four and possibly five only.

Together with section 3, this proves that the ideal has exactly ten cubic and fifteen quartic minimal generators, and that every further minimal generator, if any, is quintic. The next section bounds this remaining generator space structurally.

## 5. Reduction of quintic generators to one channel

Let J=(I_3,I_4). Then J_4=I_4, so dim(S/J)_4=15. The degree-four Macaulay expansion is simply 15=binom(6,4). Hence

dim(S/J)_5 <= binom(7,5)=21.

Write C=I_5/J_5 for the minimal quintic generator space. The exact quotient sequence at degree five gives

dim C=dim(S/J)_5-dim R_5 <=21-3=18.

The center of the finite Heisenberg group acts trivially in polynomial degree five. Thus C, being a normalizer-stable quotient in that degree, decomposes as characters of the abelian group Gamma/Z(Gamma), of order twenty-five. Here Gamma denotes the finite Heisenberg group. The quotient normalizer SL_2(F_5) acts transitively on its twenty-four nontrivial characters. Consequently any normalizer representation containing one such character contains all twenty-four with equal positive multiplicity. The dimension bound eighteen excludes this possibility. Therefore the full Heisenberg group acts trivially on C.

Taking invariants for a finite group over C is exact. The surjection I_5 -> C consequently induces a surjection I_5^{Gamma} -> C. Horrocks--Mumford Theorem 3.5 gives S_5^{Gamma}=U+U'. Dualizing the theorem to the convention S=Sym(T) leaves these self-dual labels unchanged. Since R_5=U', exactness of invariants gives I_5^{Gamma}=U. This is irreducible for the quotient normalizer. Hence

C=0 or C is isomorphic to U.

## 6. Euler differentiation eliminates the last channel

Consider the invariant quintic space U=I_5^{Gamma} inside S_5. Directional differentiation defines the equivariant map

V tensor U -> S_4,

since V=T* is the space of directional derivatives on Sym(T). This map is nonzero: a nonzero homogeneous quintic over C cannot have all its first derivatives zero. Its source V tensor U is irreducible, and section 1 shows that S_4 contains precisely one copy of this representation. Its image must therefore be that copy. Section 3 places this entire copy inside I_4.

Consequently every first derivative of every q in U belongs to I_4. Euler's homogeneous identity, for any dual coordinate bases, gives

5q = sum_i x_i (partial q / partial x_i).

It follows that U is contained in T I_4, hence in J_5. The surjection I_5^{Gamma}=U -> C from section 5 is therefore zero; since it is surjective, C=0.

The scalar annihilator ideal is thus generated exactly by ten cubics A tensor W' and fifteen quartics V tensor U, and has no minimal generators in any other degree. Its cubic generators have exactly ten linear syzygies V tensor W'. The differentiation argument is universal and equivariant; it does not select or evaluate any actual quintic formula. The complete syzygy resolution, selected-form membership and the separate parameters r and s of H are not determined here.
