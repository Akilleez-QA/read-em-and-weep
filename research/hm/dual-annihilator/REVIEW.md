## Pairing symmetry and degree-four action

The trace pairing B tensor B to O is symmetric. Serre duality pairs two cohomological degree-two classes, so interchanging them introduces sign (-1)^(2*2)=+1. Thus the resulting pairing between H_j and H_(5-j) is symmetric under exchanging the factors, and scalar polynomial multiplication is self-adjoint for it. No alternating sign from a chosen identification W' with its dual changes this intrinsic symmetry.

For q in S_4 define

Phi(q)(x)(w,w') = <q w, x w'>,

where x is in T and w,w' are in H_0. Self-adjointness, commutativity, and symmetry give

<q w,x w'> = <x q w,w'> = <w',x q w> = <q w',x w>.

Consequently Phi(q) belongs to T* tensor Sym^2(H_0*), the irreducible normalizer representation V tensor U'. Its kernel is precisely Ann(H)_4: the elements x w' span all of H_1, and H_4 pairs perfectly with H_1, so Phi(q)=0 if and only if q annihilates H_0. Generation by H_0 then makes this equivalent to annihilating all of H. The map is nonzero because S_4 H_0=H_4 is nonzero. Equivariance and irreducibility therefore prove that its image has dimension fifteen.

For degree five the same reasoning without x gives a nonzero equivariant map S_5 to Sym^2(H_0*)=U', whose kernel is Ann(H)_5. Its image has dimension three. Neither argument requires a decomposition of S_4 or S_5.

## Degrees zero through three

The annihilator has no constants and no linear terms: the latter follows directly from the isomorphism T tensor H_0 to H_1. In degree two its intersection with the irreducible S_2 is either zero or all of S_2. The action is nonzero by generation and H_2 nonzero, so the intersection is zero.

For degree three the action target is Hom(H_0,H_3)=A tensor (U'+J_5), whereas S_3=A tensor (J_5+W'). The inequivalent A tensor W' summand maps to zero. The A tensor J_5 summand must map nontrivially because S_3 H_0=H_3 is nonzero; irreducibility makes its kernel zero. This proves the claimed ten-dimensional annihilator and quotient dimension twenty-five.

Every homogeneous polynomial of degree greater than five annihilates H by its support and generation. This completes the Hilbert-function computation.

## Inverse system and levelness

Let U be the span in S_5* of the functionals

lambda_(w,w')(p)=<p w,w'>,

for w,w' in H_0. The degree-five calculation proves dim U=3. For homogeneous q of degree d between zero and five,

q annihilates U if and only if lambda_(w,w')(q p)=0

for all p in S_(5-d) and w,w' in H_0. By self-adjointness this is equivalent to <q w,p w'>=0 for all such elements. Actual generation says that the p w' span H_(5-d); the perfect pairing with H_d then says q w=0 for every w. Since H_0 generates H, this is equivalent to q in Ann_S(H). Degrees greater than five satisfy both conditions automatically. Thus Ann_S(H) is exactly the apolar annihilator of U.

For a direct levelness verification, let a nonzero class of q have degree d<5. The preceding equivalence supplies lambda in U and p in S_(5-d) with lambda(qp) nonzero. Express p as a sum of products x p' with x in T. At least one product has lambda(qx p') nonzero, so qx is nonzero modulo the annihilator. Therefore q is not in the socle. Degree-five classes are all in the socle and form a three-dimensional space. This avoids invoking any unproved inverse-system theorem.

## Quartic minimal generators and scope

The dimensions are dim Ann(H)_3=10 and dim Ann(H)_4=70-15=55. Since Ann(H) has no terms below degree three, its minimal quartic-generator space is Ann(H)_4 divided by T Ann(H)_3. The latter subspace has dimension at most 5*10=50. Hence the minimal quartic-generator space has dimension at least five. This is a lower bound, not an assertion that the ten cubic annihilators have no linear syzygies.
