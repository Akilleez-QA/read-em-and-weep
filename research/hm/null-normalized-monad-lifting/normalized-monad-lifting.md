# A normalized Moyal lifting criterion with the actual HM line factors

20 October 2026. Formal result and existing review record; actual HM/source values are not evaluated here.

## Results

There is a canonical quantization of every line summand in the actual normalized four-term HM resolution, and an explicit quantization of every morphism between them. However, the strict global-differential route has a stronger first-order limitation than one might expect: its symmetry-required first correction space is zero. Consequently a strict first lift exists in the pure P direction if and only if its literal first twisted curvature C_P(d,d) vanishes as a global section. This is a criterion, not an evaluation of that section.

The full Čech resolution route does not have that limitation. Its first and second lifting equations below are necessary and sufficient in the perfect-complex deformation model. The supplied first-lift existence and symmetry-replacement theorem turn a solution of the symmetry-homogeneous second equation into a valid construction for the actual prescribed boundary. The second equation has not been solved here.

## 1. Actual root and line complex

Set X=(P¹_t)^4, N=O(1,1,1,1), f=ρ∘d⁴ with d(t)=t². The normalized bundle is E=f*H⊗N. The pinned carrier identifies the normalized HM monad as

5O(-1) → 2Ω²(2) → 5O

on P⁴. The pinned Euler coresolution and cancellation give a quasi-isomorphic line complex, whose actual pullback and root twist are

L⁻¹=5N⁻¹ → L⁰=15N → L¹=10N³ → L²=2N⁵.              (1)

Its sole classical cohomology is E in degree zero. In particular the root cannot be dropped when constructing the modules: the exponents are -1,1,3,5, not 0,2,4,6. It cancels only in Hom. We import the exactness and source identification of (1), not any quantization of it. Pullback preserves this locally split bundle resolution.

Write d for its differential, and n_r=2r+1. Then

End^q(L)=O(2q,2q,2q,2q)^{m_q},
(m_0,m_1,m_2,m_3)=(354,245,80,10), m_-q=m_q.

Factor permutations act trivially on the constant multiplicity spaces: (1) is pulled back from P⁴, on which permuting the four roots induces the identity. The line factors have their ordinary tensor-product permutation linearizations.

## 2. Canonical line modules and actual quantized morphisms

The global vector fields are V_i=½t_i²∂t_i. Give O(n) its standard SL₂ linearization and restrict to the unipotent subgroup generated by V. In the finite frame e_0 of O(n), and the infinity frame e_∞=t^n e_0, the induced operator is

D^(n)(p e_0)=(½t² p' - ½nt p)e_0,
D^(n)(q(u)e_∞)= -½q'(u)e_∞, u=1/t.                (2)

The formulas agree on overlaps: differentiating t^n cancels the weight term. They work for negative as well as positive n. On N^n use D_i^(n) in factor i. These four operators commute, are globally regular, and satisfy D_i(ma)=D_i(m)a+m V_i(a). This is canonical relative to the stated coordinates and standard line linearization; it is not an assertion of an additive action on E.

Use right modules and ordinary composition F G=F∘G. Put

P=½(D_1⊗D_2-D_2⊗D_1),
Q=½(D_3⊗D_4-D_4⊗D_3).

Each D acts in the appropriate object or Hom representation. Define the module action m⋆a by applying exp(sP+tQ) to m⊗a and then ordinary multiplication. On functions this is exactly the stipulated global Moyal product. Commutativity of the D_i and the Leibniz identity prove associativity; no new ambient product is introduced.

For an ordinary local bundle morphism F:N^m→N^n, the induced Hom operator is

D_i^Hom F=D_i^(n)∘F-F∘D_i^(m).

If F is multiplication by p in finite frames, it is multiplication by

½t_i²∂_i p - ½(n-m)t_i p.                          (3)

The quantized morphism is the differential operator

Q_{n,m}(F)(z)= composition(exp(sP+tQ)(F⊗z)),       (4)

where the second tensor factor uses D^(m). Formula (4), not ordinary multiplication by the same coefficient, represents the module map. The twist identities give

Q(F)(z⋆a)=Q(F)(z)⋆a,
Q(F)Q(G)=Q(F⋆G),
F⋆G=composition(exp(sP+tQ)(F⊗G)).                 (5)

To verify (5) directly, expand the exponentials and use D(Fz)=(D^Hom F)z+F Dz. All three-factor expressions become the same exponential of the sum of the pairwise commuting contraction operators. This also proves that these formulas glue across the genuine line transitions.

Locally a line module is free of rank one over the formal star algebra: a classical local frame gives a right-module map with invertible reduction, hence an inverse recursively modulo (s,t)^3. Thus (4) is an isomorphism from formal sections of the ordinary Hom sheaf to star-module morphisms, by reduction and induction. In particular the global Hom spaces are the classical global Hom spaces tensored with the parameter ring. Non-equivariant F are allowed precisely because of (4); they are not asserted to be unchanged module maps.

The twist composition is associative and respects the grading. The internal commutator actions D^Hom give the required Leibniz rule for compositions. All statements hold to any formal order for the individual line modules; only order two is used below.

## 3. Exact strict global-complex criterion

Let C^q=H⁰(X,End^q L), with ordinary differential ∂=[d,-]. This finite graded algebra has dimensions

C^0:354; C^1:19,845; C^2:50,000; C^3:24,010;
C^q=0 for q<0 or q>3.

These counts use h⁰(O(2q)^⊠4)=(2q+1)^4; they are not new HM rank computations. Define C_P(F,G)=composition(P(F⊗G)), C_PP(F,G)=composition(P²(F⊗G)/2), and similarly Q, QQ, PQ, with C_PQ=composition(PQ(-⊗-)).

For d_s=d+s a+s² b, the strict complex condition d_s⋆d_s=0 modulo s³ is exactly

∂a=-C_P(d,d),                                    (6)
∂b=-[a²+C_P(d,a)+C_P(a,d)+C_PP(d,d)].             (7)

These are necessary and sufficient for a strict deformation with the fixed quantized terms (1). Local splitting of the classical resolution and nilpotent lifting then give a locally free degree-zero cohomology module, reducing to E. Conversely a general bundle lift is NOT asserted to admit this strict presentation.

### A new first-order restriction

The required a has deck character χ12 and signs (-,+) under the internal swaps (12),(34). In a finite frame, a section of each End¹ summand is a polynomial of degree at most two in each t_i. Its χ12 part is exactly

t₁t₂ · span{1,t₃²,t₄²,t₃²t₄²}.

Every such section is (12)-even. Thus

(C¹)_(χ12,(12)=-)=0.                              (8)

The root linearization cancels in Hom; its transition exponent is even. There is no hidden alternating permutation sign. As d is invariant, ∂ is equivariant. The source C_P(d,d) has exactly the requested χ12,(-,+) signs. If any strict solution a to (6) exists, project it to that sector by the finite-group idempotent. Its projection is zero by (8), so C_P(d,d)=0 as a section. Conversely if that section is zero, a=0 is a strict homogeneous first lift. Hence

strict first lift in the P direction exists ⇔ C_P(d,d)=0 literally.   (9)

The same statement holds for Q. This applies even if the initially proposed strict lift was not homogeneous, since projection produces a homogeneous solution. We have NOT evaluated either curvature or inferred that either is nonzero.

If both literal first curvatures vanish, use a_P=a_Q=0. The strict two-parameter second criterion becomes

∂b_PP=-C_PP(d,d),
∂b_PQ=-C_PQ(d,d),
∂b_QQ=-C_QQ(d,d),                                (10)

with the corresponding symmetry sectors. These equations constitute a concrete sufficient construction route for the actual boundary, but their solvability is not proved. In particular neither d²=0 nor commuting vector fields implies d⋆d=0.

## 4. Why global H¹,H²,H³ vanishing does not make the strict route exhaustive

Every diagonal Hom line has no intermediate sheaf cohomology. Nevertheless the negative internal degrees have nonzero H⁴:

H⁴(End⁻³ L): dimension 6,250, total degree 1;
H⁴(End⁻² L): dimension 6,480, total degree 2;
H⁴(End⁻¹ L): dimension 245, total degree 3.          (11)

The lower cohomology row is the global C complex. The upper row has the differential induced by [d,-]. After those row differentials, the only possible cross-row differential is d₅, with bidegree (internal +5, sheaf -4):

upper internal -3 → lower internal 2,
upper internal -2 → lower internal 3.              (12)

Thus the global degree-two obstruction quotient can shrink in full Ext², while total degree-one data can come from the upper row. These are exactly the positions relevant to first lift and second obstruction. Vanishing of intermediate sheaf cohomology is insufficient to identify H²(C) with Ext²(E,E).

There is a concrete nonzero upper-row cochain space in precisely the missing symmetry sector. On H¹(P¹,O(-6)), the finite-frame Laurent basis is t^-5,t^-4,t^-3,t^-2,t^-1. Its deck-odd part has dimension three and its deck-even part dimension two. For χ12 in H⁴End⁻³, the raw dimension is 10·3²·2²=360. A swap of two H¹ factors introduces the Čech/Künneth Koszul minus sign. Thus active-swap-odd means Sym²(C³), while passive-swap-even means Λ²(C²), giving

(H⁴End⁻³)_(χ12,(-,+)) ≅ C¹⁰⊗Sym²(C³)⊗Λ²(C²), dimension 60.   (13a)

This is an E₁/cohomology-row space, NOT 60 Ext¹ classes or 60 survivors. Its row differential must first vanish; the surviving row classes can then map by d₅. In this symmetry sector the lower-row C¹ is zero, so H²(C) is simply the kernel of C²→C³. The exact first-order criterion in the full model is that the class of C_P(d,d) lie in the image of d₅ from the corresponding upper-row cohomology: the kernel of H⁴End⁻³→H⁴End⁻² within the displayed 60-dimensional space (there is no preceding End⁻⁴ term). This does not calculate d₅; it identifies its exact domain, target, symmetry, and required value. Imported first-order existence ensures this image condition, not literal curvature vanishing.

In particular if C_P(d,d) is nonzero, the imported existence of the actual first-order bundle lift would not contradict (9). It forces that curvature to be killed in the full model, potentially through the upper-row/d₅ mechanism. This is a structural explanation, not a computation of d₅ or of its rank.

## 5. Full symmetry-homogeneous lifting equations

Choose the standard product affine cover, its functorial Čech nerve, and totalize the strict Čech End complex with the usual Koszul signs. A refining trivializing affine cover is also allowed. All Hom modules, their products and their restriction maps are already quantized by (2)-(5). Let A=Tot Čech(End L), δ its Čech differential, and D=δ+[d,-] its classical total differential. For cochains of Čech/internal bidegrees (p,r) and (q,s), the product is (-1)^(rq) times Čech cup followed by ordinary composition. Thus [d,-] in the total algebra acts as (-1)^p times the internal graded commutator. C_P,C_PP, etc. are the same twist corrections with these totalization signs. The vector fields commute with restrictions. Consequently δ remains a derivation of the twisted product.

The total Maurer-Cartan equation is

δ α + (d+α)⋆(d+α)=0,

where α has total degree one and positive parameter order. At first order α=s a_P+t a_Q, it becomes

D a_P=-C_P(d,d),   D a_Q=-C_Q(d,d).                (13)

Each a may have Čech/internal bidegrees (0,1),(1,0),(2,-1),(3,-2),(4,-3). These encode differential correction, gluing, and higher homotopy data; suppressing the last four components recovers the restrictive strict route.

For any first solution define

R_PP=a_P a_P+C_P(d,a_P)+C_P(a_P,d)+C_PP(d,d),
R_QQ=a_Q a_Q+C_Q(d,a_Q)+C_Q(a_Q,d)+C_QQ(d,d),
R_PQ=a_P a_Q+a_Q a_P
       +C_P(d,a_Q)+C_P(a_Q,d)
       +C_Q(d,a_P)+C_Q(a_P,d)+C_PQ(d,d).             (14)

All products and signs are those of the total algebra. Associativity and (13) imply D R=0. The exact second-order criterion is

D b_PP=-R_PP,  D b_PQ=-R_PQ,  D b_QQ=-R_QQ.        (15)

Equivalently their classes in H²(A,D)=Ext²(E,E) must vanish. Equations (13)-(15) specify an actual construction, rather than a generic instruction to compute an unspecified obstruction.

The equivalence to lifting the perfect object uses the standard Čech/twisted-complex deformation description: locally the classical resolution splits into E and contractible pairs; nilpotent perturbation cancels the contractible pairs, while the total equation gives their compatible descent. Conversely a bundle lift can be locally compared to these split resolutions, supplying the differential, transition, and homotopy components. Thus this full criterion is not restricted to strict global matrix deformations. Reduction to a bundle in degree zero makes the resulting perfect lift locally a bundle, not an unrelated derived object.

One can project first solutions into χ12,(-,+) and χ34,(+,-) by finite-group averaging. A first solution exists here under the imported actual first-order existence premise. The audited symmetry-replacement theorem then identifies the resulting first bundle boundary with the actual transported boundary, up to reduction-preserving gauge, with the induced order-two terms retained. No arbitrary normalized Ext¹ class is substituted.

The PP and QQ second sources have trivial deck character and both swaps even; the mixed source has χ1234 and both swaps odd. Solutions to (15), when they exist, can be projected to the same sectors. Solving all three equations (15) constructs a full two-parameter lift and is sufficient but potentially stronger than the requested diagonal lift. For the diagonal s=t=h, set a_diag=a_P+a_Q. The exact criterion is instead

D b_diag=-(R_PP+R_PQ+R_QQ).                         (16)

Under the imported mixed-vanishing premise, this tests [R_PP+R_QQ]=0. It need not test each pure class separately: those classes can contain components odd under interchange of the two blocks, which cancel in the sum. Only this diagonal sum, under the imported faithful normalization/compact-target reduction, is exactly the original six-condition problem. No equivalence between six-condition vanishing and a full two-parameter lift is asserted.

## 6. Established versus conditional conclusions

Established here by universal algebra and line cohomology: explicit canonical line actions including the true root; quantized right-module morphisms (4); finite strict equations and dimensions; zero strict homogeneous first-correction sector; criterion (9); upper-row positions and possible d₅; full second-order equations (13)-(15).

Imported: the classical HM line resolution and its quasi-isomorphism; global-Moyal normalization of the actual algebroid; actual first-order existence; boundary symmetry comparison, including cusp uniqueness and accepted HM ranks; and the separately audited mixed/compact-target reduction.

Not established: a literal curvature vanishing, a first strict lift, a second strict or full primitive, a rank/value of a differential on physical HM data, or vanishing of any remaining obstruction coordinate. No new smoothness premise is used.
