# A four-step derived excess behind the determinantal algebra

Work over C. This is a structural route exclusion, not a determination of the parameter k in PR503. It uses the actual quadratic binary presentation of H from PR490, its purely quadratic presentation from PR499, and the determinantal Hilbert function from PR495. All polynomial rings and modules in this note are regraded so that an original quadratic in S has degree one. Our shift convention is M(s)_d=M_(d+s), and (M^vee)_d=Hom_C(M_(-d),C).

Put S=Sym(T), dim T=5, dim E=5, dim W=2, and use the actual identification S_2=E tensor Sym^2(W). Let

P=Sym(E tensor Sym^2(W)),
A=S^(2)=direct sum over d>=0 of S_(2d),
B=direct sum over d>=0 of Sym^d(E) tensor Sym^(2d)(W).

P has fifteen degree-one variables. The map P -> A is induced by the actual identification of its degree-one vector space with S_2. The map P -> B is ordinary multiplication in the E and W factors.

## 1. The ordinary intersection is exactly Q^(2)

Choose a binary basis x,y, and write a_i,b_i,c_i for the three variables associated to an E basis element and x^2,xy,y^2. The universal quadratic relation columns are (-b_i,a_i) and (-c_i,b_i). Their two-by-two minors generate the kernel of P -> B.

Here is a direct scheme-theoretic verification, avoiding any set-theoretic rank argument. The minors give the quadratic moves

a_i b_j = a_j b_i,
b_i c_j = b_j c_i,
a_i c_j = b_i b_j,

and also a_i c_j = a_j c_i by the third relation with i,j interchanged. Every monomial records an E-index multiset and a total binary exponent, assigning an exponent 0,1,2 to each occurrence of an index. The displayed moves connect all assignments with the same total: a pair with exponents 0,2 can be balanced to 1,1; exponent 0 can be exchanged with 1 and exponent 1 with 2. Repeated balancing followed by exchanges gives a common assignment for each multiset and total. Thus the quotient has at most one monomial basis element per such pair of data. Their images in Sym^d(E) tensor Sym^(2d)(W) are distinct standard monomials and span it. The quotient is therefore B, not merely a ring with the same closed points.

Since H has only quadratic presentation columns, its Fitting ideal D is generated by these minors after P -> S. Their images lie in the even Veronese, and generation by quartics gives

A tensor_P B = A/(D intersect A) = (S/D)^(2) = Q^(2).

The Hilbert function of Q is (1,5,15,35,35,3). Hence this intersection has Hilbert function (1,15,35), length51, and is supported only at the homogeneous maximal ideal of P. In particular every higher Tor_i^P(A,B) is supported there as well and has finite length.

## 2. Cohen--Macaulay dimensions and canonical modules

A is the section ring of (P^4,O(2)); B is the section ring of (P^4 times P^1,O(1,2)). Thus dim A=5 and dim B=6. Both are Cohen--Macaulay. For A this follows from the vanishing of intermediate cohomology of line bundles on P^4. For B, Kunneth and the same projective-space calculation give no intermediate cohomology: when d>=0 only H^0 of O(d,2d) survives; when -4<=d<0 the P^4 factor has no cohomology; and when d<=-5 the only possible cohomology is H^5, the top degree. The complete section-ring descriptions identify H^0 and also exclude the low local-cohomology defect.

Their standard graded canonical modules have pieces

(omega_A)_d = S_(2d-5),
(omega_B)_d = Sym^(d-5)(E) tensor Sym^(2d-2)(W),

with terms interpreted as zero below their indicated nonnegative exponents, up to harmless one-dimensional determinant factors in equivariant formulations. The first is generated in degree3 with dimension5, and the second in degree5 with dimension9. Generation follows from the surjectivity of ordinary symmetric multiplication. All numerical assertions here forget the determinant linearizations.

## 3. Exact derived amplitude four

Let F be a minimal P-free resolution of B, in homological degrees0 through9; its length is 15-depth(B)=9. Form C=F tensor_P A in cohomological degrees -9 through0. Its cohomology is Tor_i^P(A,B) in degree -i.

Compute the local cohomology of C using a Cech complex for the homogeneous maximal ideal. Since every Tor module has finite length, the cohomology-first spectral sequence collapses and H^(-i)(R Gamma_m C)=Tor_i^P(A,B).

On the other hand A is Cohen--Macaulay of dimension5, so applying local cohomology to each term leaves only H^5_m(A). Therefore R Gamma_m C is represented by the finite complex F tensor_P H^5_m(A), shifted so that its first possible cohomological degree is -9+5=-4. This proves

Tor_i^P(A,B)=0 for i>4.

At its left endpoint, Tor_4 is the kernel of

F_9 tensor H^5_m(A) -> F_8 tensor H^5_m(A).

Take graded C-duals. Graded local duality identifies (H^5_m(A))^vee with omega_A. Consequently

(Tor_4^P(A,B))^vee
 = coker(F_8^* tensor omega_A -> F_9^* tensor omega_A)
 = Ext_P^9(B,P) tensor_P omega_A
 = (omega_B tensor_P omega_A)(15).

The middle equality uses right exactness of tensor applied to the last cokernel of the dual free resolution, not a flatness claim. The final shift uses omega_P=P(-15).

The tensor product omega_A tensor_P omega_B is nonzero: modulo the homogeneous maximal ideal it is the tensor product of their nonzero minimal-generator spaces. More precisely it is generated in degree8, with exactly45 independent minimal generators there and no generators elsewhere. There are no relations in that lowest degree, since both modules start in their generating degree. Hence Tor_4 is nonzero and has socle exactly45-dimensional in degree7 (because the shift (15) takes degree8 to degree -7 before graded duality).

Thus the actual derived intersection has exact homological amplitude four, and its top homology is a level finite-length module of socle degree7 and type45, in the Veronese grading.

The same argument also gives the full duality, for 0<=i<=4:

(Tor_i^P(A,B))^vee = Ext_P^(5+i)(B,omega_A)
 = Tor_(4-i)^P(omega_A,omega_B)(15).

Indeed dualizing the entire Cech-reduced complex gives Hom_P(F,omega_A) with the degree-five translation. Since B is Cohen--Macaulay, the dual free complex Hom_P(F,P) resolves omega_B(15) placed in cohomological degree9. Tensoring that dual free complex with omega_A computes its derived tensor product, yielding the second equality. This is an equality of graded modules, with the standard canonical-module normalization above.

## 4. Consequence and boundary

A Tor-independent pullback of a universal resolution of B along P -> A cannot resolve Q^(2): its fourth homology is necessarily nonzero, and has the canonical-module description above. This is a proved structural obstruction to treating the universal Segre--Veronese determinantal model as a transverse specialization. It explains a concrete missing dependency in that approach: the positive Tor homology, necessarily nonzero in degree4, must be controlled or removed before one can infer a resolution of Q. No nonvanishing of each intermediate Tor1, Tor2, or Tor3 is inferred.

This argument does not identify Tor_4^P(A,B) with either of the two unresolved S-linear connecting-map channels in PR503. It does not select k from {15,25,35,45}, and the type45 occurring here is not asserted to equal any k-value. No selected HM/source-instance evaluation, numerical multiplication matrix, coefficient search, or large computation is used.
