# Independent audit: degree-thirty exclusion

**PASS.** Independently reviewed ADDENDUM_DEGREE_30.md, Sections A–E, with the hypotheses of the previously audited main proof. No outstanding correction.

- The genus-three A5 Riemann–Hurwitz equation is 15a+20b+24c=62; reduction modulo five rules it out. The central quotient of a faithful SL2(F5) action in genus at most six consequently has genus zero and exactly twelve branch points. Its involutions have their fixed points outside that branch orbit. In coordinates fixing those two points, the squarefree degree-twelve equation is even, and both lifts of x -> -x have order two. This contradicts the order-four preimages in SL2(F5). This also covers the unramified possibilities, which were excluded by the positive-genus quotient analysis.
- Castelnuovo gives genus at most 126 in degree thirty; the degree-twenty-five quotient gives genus at most six. Faithfulness of the induced quotient action follows from the Galois deck-kernel argument, so the integral N-stable case is excluded.
- An axis-stable projective line is the span of two distinct Schrödinger eigenlines. The axis normalizer supplies every affine multiplier and translation on their five labels, hence all ten unordered pairs. Six axes give sixty distinct lines: a line counted for two axes would be H-stable, contradicting irreducibility.
- An H-stable curve of degree five or six has normalization genus at most two. The displayed Riemann–Hurwitz divisibility forces genus one. H acts by translations, and conjugation on H factors through the cyclic elliptic linear automorphism group of order at most six. Faithful prescribed conjugation therefore forces its N-orbit to have at least twenty components.
- The component-orbit enumeration includes multiplicities. A free H-orbit forces an N-orbit of twenty-five lines and a forbidden degree-five remainder. An orbit of size five forces thirty lines and contradicts the sixty-line bound. Otherwise there are at most six H-stable components; perfection excludes nontrivial orbits of size two through four, and the remaining sizes five and six contradict the elliptic argument.

Thus an actual invariant effective curve cycle of degree thirty is impossible. Together with the audited bounds c3(B) in {0,10,20,30} and exclusion of degrees ten and twenty, this proves c3(B)=0. It does not eliminate the finite singularity defect or decide the generic quadratic rank. No operation-instance evaluation was used.
