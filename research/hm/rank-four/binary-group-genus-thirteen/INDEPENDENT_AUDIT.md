## 1. Complete Riemann–Hurwitz enumeration

For a faithful order-120 action and 2≤h≤13, the normalized RH value is (h−1)/60, in (0,1/5]. The element-order list {2,3,4,5,6,10} is correct: semisimple elements split over F5 or its quadratic extension, while repeated-eigenvalue elements are scalar or signed unipotents. The unique involution is −I.

Quotient genus at least two contributes at least 2. Quotient genus one contributes either 0 or at least 1/2. Thus the quotient is the sphere. At most two branch points give a nonpositive value; at least five give a value at least 1/2. Only triples and quadruples remain.

For triples, 4/5≤1/a+1/b+1/c<1 with a≤b≤c. The proof's case split covers every a and b in the element-order list, including the equality at the upper-genus boundary. Direct substitution gives precisely:

- (2,3,10): h=5
- (2,4,5), (2,4,6), (2,4,10): h=4,6,10
- (2,5,5), (2,5,6), (2,5,10): h=7,9,13
- (2,6,6): h=11
- (3,3,4), (3,3,5), (3,3,6): h=6,9,11
- (3,4,4): h=11

For quadruples, start from (2,2,2,2), of value zero. One replacement by 3 raises the value to 1/6; one replacement by 4 already gives 1/4, and two replacements by 3 give 1/3. Thus (2,2,2,3), with h=11, is the only quadruple. This proves completeness of all thirteen candidates without using an empirical group search.

Genus zero and one are correctly imported from ADDENDUM_DEGREE_30.md §A of normalizer-small-curve-exclusion, ultimately its PROOF.md §4. Those arguments include the low-genus central quotient and branch-orbit obstruction; they do not assume h≥2.

## 2. Generating tuples, central involutions, and quotient cases

Branch monodromy supplies an exact-order product-one tuple generating the full group, because the cover is connected. For a triple containing an order-two entry, that entry must be the central z. The other two entries are consequently generated by z and a single element, an abelian subgroup. Cyclically rotating a product-one tuple permits the order-two entry to be placed first without changing this conclusion. The quadruple with three involutions forces its last entry to be z, contradicting exact order three.

The projections to A5 of elements of orders four and six have exact orders two and three, respectively: their cyclic subgroups contain z. Orders three remain three. A generating tuple in G projects to a generating tuple in A5. Therefore the three remaining nonexceptional signatures really would give generating triples of types (3,3,2), (3,3,3), and (3,2,2).

For two three-cycles in A5, supports meeting in exactly one letter give a five-cycle product; otherwise their support union has at most four letters and their generated subgroup fixes a letter. This directly excludes generation with product of order two or three, including the (3,3,3) case. No unproved assertion about Euclidean triangle-group solvability is needed. For (3,2,2), the two involutions have product of order three and generate a dihedral group of order at most six. Quotient generation therefore fails in every case.

## 3. Exceptional (3,3,5) finite-field argument

This calculation uses the defining faithful representation of SL2(F5), not a hypothetical complex two-dimensional curve representation. An exact-order-three element has distinct eigenvalues satisfying λ²+λ+1=0 and trace −1. An exact-order-five element in characteristic five is nonidentity unipotent and has trace 2, as does its inverse. Thus a=b=−1 and c=2 are correct for x,y,xy.

The Cayley–Hamilton trace identity gives a²+b²+c²−abc−2=2. After diagonalizing x over the algebraic closure of F5, direct multiplication gives

    tr([x,y]) = 2 − (λ−λ⁻¹)²vw.

The multiplier is nonzero since λ≠λ⁻¹. Hence vw=0, so x and y have a common invariant eigenline. The argument uses only this implication for a matrix with distinct eigenvalues; it does not invoke a trace criterion without its characteristic hypotheses. Triangular matrices form a solvable group in this characteristic as well: both the diagonal quotient and unipotent kernel are abelian. Since x,y generate G, their solvability contradicts the nonsolvable quotient A5. This disposes of the thirteenth signature.

## 4. Normalization, faithful quotient action, and degree 45

An A-stable integral curve spans P4 by Schrödinger irreducibility. Any projective transformation fixing it pointwise has the curve inside its finite union of projectivized eigenspaces. Integrality puts it in one eigenspace; nondegeneracy forces that eigenspace to be all V. Thus the stabilizer acts faithfully on the curve and its normalization, even when the embedded curve is singular.

Castelnuovo's geometric-genus bound is applicable to the normalization's birational linear series. At degree 45, 44=3·14+2 and

    π(45,4)=3·14·13/2+14·2=273+28=301.

Monotonicity gives the same upper bound for every smaller admissible degree. For the faithful A-action, RH yields 25(g(Y)−1)≤g(X)−1, so g(Y)≤floor((g(X)+24)/25)≤13.

The action of N/A on Y=X/A is genuinely faithful: k(X)/k(X)^A is a degree-25 Galois extension with deck group exactly A. Any element of N trivial on the invariant field is in that deck group. This is stronger than merely observing that A acts trivially on Y, and it supplies exactly the faithfulness needed for the genus contradiction.

## 5. All reduced-curve component possibilities through degree 45

The A-orbit sizes are 1,5,25. With no line or conic components, the last two sizes cost respectively at least 75 and 90 in total degree; the axis case uses all six conjugate stabilizer axes and distinct A-orbits. Thus every component in a hypothetical degree-≤45 curve is A-stable.

The Hilbert-polynomial proof in rank-three-curve-constraints/PROOF.md §2 applies separately to each A-stable integral component and to the entire reduced curve. It makes each component degree a positive multiple of five. There are consequently at most nine components. The permutation action factors through the perfect G; an orbit of size 2,3,4 is impossible since S2,S3,S4 are solvable. Singletons are excluded by the integral-curve theorem. For each remaining orbit m≥5, the inequality md≤45 and 5|d imply d=5. No unsupported classification of all indices at most nine is used.

The imported small-quintic argument is valid: its normalization has genus at most one, and the faithful A-action excludes genus zero. On the elliptic normalization A acts by translations since its image in the cyclic origin-preserving automorphism group (order dividing 2,4,6) is trivial. Conjugation on A thus factors through a group of order at most six. The faithful prescribed conjugation N/A→Aut(A) gives |N_C/A|≤6, hence at least twenty components in the orbit. Twenty quintics cost at least 100, a contradiction.

The contradiction proves degree >45; the global Heisenberg divisibility then upgrades this to degree ≥50. The result is about reduced support and does not assert existence, multiplicities, or isolated-point bounds.
