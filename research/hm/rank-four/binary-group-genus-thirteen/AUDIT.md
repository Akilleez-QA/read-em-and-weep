# Author's verification audit

This is a verification by the proof's author, not an independent review.

- The RH normalization is (2h−2)/120=(h−1)/60. At h=13 it is 1/5. Quotient genus one is excluded because any positive ramification contribution is at least 1/2; unbranched covers give zero.
- The branch-count bounds are 3≤r≤4. The thirteen signatures were derived by ordered reciprocal inequalities in the proof and independently cross-checked with a tiny exact-rational enumeration of multisets from {2,3,4,5,6,10}. No group-element or source-map computation was performed.
- The four-branch candidate (2,2,2,3) is included. It cannot be discarded merely by a triangle argument; the three involutions all equal the central z and force the fourth branch cycle to have order two.
- For triangles containing an order-two cycle, the centrality argument excludes generation directly. No assertion that a sphere cover has an arbitrary chosen generating triple is made: branch monodromy supplies the generating tuple with exact inertia orders.
- The quotient signatures are correct: orders four and six project to two and three. Two three-cycles with union of supports equal to five have product a five-cycle; otherwise their subgroup fixes a letter. Two involutions with product of order three generate at most six elements. This avoids relying on Euclidean or spherical triangle-group classifications.
- In the exceptional characteristic-five calculation, exact order three forces trace −1 and exact order five forces trace 2. The product xy is the inverse of the third branch cycle, retaining trace 2. The Fricke expression is 1+1+4−2−2=2. The diagonal multiplier (λ−λ⁻¹)² is nonzero in characteristic five. Thus one off-diagonal entry vanishes; no converse trace criterion or characteristic-zero trace assertion is assumed.
- Simultaneous triangularizability is used over the algebraic closure of F₅ only to imply solvability of the finite generated subgroup. It does not assert that the original complex curve representation is two-dimensional. The defining finite-field representation is faithful by definition of G.
- Genus zero and one are imported from the previously proved genus-at-most-six theorem; the new RH enumeration covers all genera two through thirteen. The proof does not overlook genus-one actions or apply hyperbolic formulas to genus zero.
- Castelnuovo at d=45 is 273+28=301. The quotient bound is (301+24)/25=13 exactly. Both actions are faithful by the nondegenerate-integral-curve and function-field arguments; singular curves are handled by normalization.
- The reduced-cycle consequence uses component degrees ≥3 only after importing both the no-line and no-conic results. Free A-orbits cost ≥75 and axis orbits cost ≥90. When all components are A-stable, every component degree is a multiple of five, every nonsingleton G-orbit has size ≥5, and total degree ≤45 forces quintics. Their existing orbit bound ≥20 is stronger than needed. No assertion that every small G-orbit has size five or six is required.
- Final conclusion is only Γ₄=0 or degree in {50,55,…,125}. It does not establish nonemptiness, finiteness of Z₄, or a bound for isolated rank-four points.
