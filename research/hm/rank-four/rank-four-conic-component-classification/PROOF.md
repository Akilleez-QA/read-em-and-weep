# Conic components of the rank-four curve: one remaining orbit

## Statement and dependencies

Use the hypotheses and notation of `../rank-four-line-component-exclusion/PROOF.md`: N=A⋊G, A=F5², G=SL2(F5), the Schrödinger space V, dim Z5≤1, deg Γ4≤125, and no line in Z4. A conic here means an irreducible reduced degree-two projective curve over C; it is a smooth plane conic.

**Theorem.** If Γ4 contains a conic, it contains the canonical orbit of exactly twenty-five invariant conics

    C_a={q_a=0} ⊂ P(T_a),  a∈A,

and there are no other conic components. This orbit has total degree 50. Thus any remaining components have degree at least three, and their total degree is at most75.

This classifies the conic possibility, but does not exclude it. We also prove necessary coefficient restrictions if it occurs.

## 1. Stabilizers under A

Let C be a conic and U its three-dimensional vector span. The A-stabilizer of U is proper, because the Schrödinger representation is irreducible. Consequently A_C⊂A_U has order one or five.

If A_C is trivial and K=N_C projects to H⊂G, then K≅H and

    |N·C|=25[G:H].

The degree budget gives 2|N·C|≤125, so [G:H]≤2. Perfectness of G excludes index two. Thus H=G, and K is a complement to A. The central-involution cocycle argument of the line-exclusion proof conjugates K to G by an element of A.

After that conjugation U is G-invariant. Since V=T⊕W is the sum of inequivalent irreducibles of dimensions three and two, U=T. The conic equation spans a G-invariant line in Sym²(T*). Because G has no characters and Sym²T=1⊕P5, its equation is the unique invariant quadratic q. Thus C is canonical.

The canonical conic has trivial A-stabilizer: its A-stabilizer is G-invariant, hence zero or all A, and the latter would make T an A-invariant proper subspace. Its stabilizer contains G, so its N-orbit has exactly25 members.

## 2. Axis-stable conics have at least300 translates

Suppose |A_C|=5. Conjugate its axis to the diagonal Weyl subgroup generated by Z, with Z e_j=ζ^j e_j. The span U is a sum of three distinct eigenlines, indexed by a three-element subset S⊂F5.

Every such S is uniquely of the form {c,c−d,c+d}, where d≠0 is identified with −d. Indeed there are five choices of c and two choices of ±d, and no triple can have two centers: if both a,b are centers of the same triple {a,b,r}, then 2a=b+r and 2b=a+r give 3(a−b)=0. These ten triples exhaust the ten subsets.

On this plane a Z-invariant smooth conic necessarily has equation

    x_c² + λ x_{c−d} x_{c+d}=0,  λ∈C*.

For a direct check, its equation must be an eigenvector for Z acting on quadratic forms. A nondegenerate three-variable quadratic eigenform needs one square and the cross term in the other two variables with equal weight. Three squares cannot have equal weights; three cross terms cannot either; and all other single-weight possibilities are degenerate. The equality of the two surviving weights is exactly 2c=(c−d)+(c+d). Smoothness requires both coefficients nonzero.

There are exactly60 distinct span planes in the N-orbit of U. For one axis, translations in A and the diagonal torus in G act on indices by all affine transformations j↦aj+b. These act transitively on the ten triples. G is transitive on the six axes. Planes belonging to distinct axes are distinct: invariance under two distinct axes implies invariance under their generated group A, which contradicts irreducibility. This proves the count 6·10=60.

The normalizer element D e_j=ζ^(j²)e_j fixes each coordinate span plane. It changes the coefficient ratio in the displayed conic equation by ζ^(±2d²), a primitive fifth root. Thus C,D C,…,D⁴ C are five distinct conics with the same span. Over each of the60 orbit planes there are at least five orbit conics. Therefore

    |N·C|≥300,   total orbit degree≥600.

This contradicts the125 degree budget. No axis-stable conic can occur in Γ4.

## 3. Apply the classification to Γ4

Every translate of a conic in Z4 is again a conic in Z4. Since Z4 has dimension at most one, these are distinct irreducible components and their degrees add. Sections1–2 exhaust the A-stabilizers. Consequently all conic components, if any, comprise exactly the canonical25-conic orbit. No second conic orbit exists under the same budget.

## 4. Necessary parity ranks and coefficient restrictions

We use `../rank-three-250-plane-exclusion/PROOF.md` and its ADDENDUM. On the canonical conic C⊂P(T), these give even rank≥5 and odd rank≥3. Therefore C⊂Z4 forces the ranks to be identically

    rank b_even=5,  rank b_odd=3.

Write T'=Sym²W, and let H≅P5 be the surviving pure-T coefficient image, as in those proofs. We prove two further necessary restrictions:

1. The T constituent of the pure-T Schur module S_(2,1)T=T⊕P5 is killed.
2. The mixed even coefficient map g:T'⊗T→J_even has its entire image in the one specific H.

### 4.1 The T constituent cannot survive

If it survives, both inequivalent constituents survive injectively, so the pure-T map identifies with the universal map to sl(T). For fixed u its kernel on Sym²T is precisely C u². To check this, identify T with its dual via q and write C_u(v)=u×v. The universal map on a symmetric matrix S is a nonzero scalar multiple of S C_u, up to transpose/sign conventions. Since C_u has rank two for every nonzero u and kernel Cu, symmetry implies S C_u=0 exactly when S is proportional to u u^t. This remains valid for isotropic u.

On C≅P1, the pure-T image I therefore has the equivariant resolution

    0 → O(-6) → O(-2)⊗Sym²T → I → 0.

It has rank five, so the full even image, also of rank five, equals I fiberwise. In particular g induces an equivariant map O(-2)⊗T'→I.

Let U0 be the two-dimensional binary-icosahedral representation parametrizing C, so Sym²U0≅T and det U0=1. The O(-2) in the resolution is the restriction of the tautological line on P(T); the kernel is its third tensor power. Twisting by O(2), taking cohomology and using complete reducibility gives

    H⁰(I(2)) ≅ Sym²T ⊕ H¹(O(-4))
                ≅ 1 ⊕ P5 ⊕ T.

The last isomorphism follows from equivariant Serre duality on P(U0). There is no T' constituent. Thus the displayed mixed map is zero, i.e. g(s,u)=0 for all u∈C. Since C spans T, the linear coefficient map g is zero on all T. Cyclic zero then gives f(w,w,t)=0 for w∈W and t∈T, contradicting repeated-input injectivity. Hence the T constituent is killed.

### 4.2 All mixed even coefficients lie in H

The pure-T image now has rank four and lies in H everywhere on C. Project g to J_even/H. Since the full even rank is five, this projected family has rank at most one on C. If it is nonzero, its saturated image is an equivariant line subbundle L⊂(J_even/H)⊗O_C. Write L=O(-d). The nonzero map O(-2)⊗T'→L and the inclusion into a trivial bundle imply 0≤d≤2. The span of its image in the fixed target therefore has dimension at most h⁰(O(d))≤3.

That span is a nonzero G-subrepresentation and a quotient of T'⊗T=P4⊕P5: the conic spans T, so the coefficient span on C is the entire coefficient image. Every nonzero such quotient has dimension at least four, a contradiction. Thus the projection vanishes and im g⊂H.

In particular the even image on C is the fixed five-dimensional H. These are necessary restrictions only. No contradiction to odd rank three or to repeated-input injectivity in the remaining configuration has been proved here.

## Scope

Only the abstract representation, orbit, degree, and vector-bundle hypotheses imported above are used. No actual source tensor or selected source input was evaluated, and  This does not prove Γ4 empty, does not rule out higher-degree components, and does not improve the general rank-four compatible-fiber bound.
