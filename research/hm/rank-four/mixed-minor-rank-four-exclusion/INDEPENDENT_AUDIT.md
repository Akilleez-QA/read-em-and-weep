# Independent audit of the mixed-minor exclusion

## Verdict

The proof in PROOF.md is valid, conditional on its imported geometric hypotheses. Both the stronger degree-(10,14,14) construction and the degree-(10,11,11) variant pass independently. No new gap was found in component avoidance, regularity, the local-order argument, or the multiplicity comparison. In particular, the stronger construction gives deg Γ4≤27, contradicting the independently established nonempty degree100 possibility. Since Z4⊂Z5 and dim Z5≤1, Z4 is finite as a support.

## Global construction

All degree14 minors are sections of the same O(14), and their common zero support is {rank b≤13}=Z9. The generic rank14 hypothesis guarantees that this system is nonzero. Similarly, the degree10 system has base support Z5.

There are finitely many surface components of Z9. No such component is contained in Z5 because dim Z5≤1. For each one, the decics vanishing identically on it form a proper vector subspace of L10. Avoiding the finite union, and zero, produces f with dim(V(f)∩Z9)≤1. This conclusion also handles curve and point components of Z9; nonreduced base schemes have no effect on this support argument.

Every component of V(f) is a hypersurface of dimension three and is outside the base support of L14. One can therefore choose g avoiding all of them. In the polynomial ring the pair f,g is regular, and its quotient is Cohen–Macaulay and unmixed. A surface component of V(f,g) contained in Z9 would contradict dim(V(f)∩Z9)≤1. A further member h of L14 avoids all those surface components and is a nonzerodivisor modulo (f,g). There is no hidden irrelevant embedded associated prime: the quotient is Cohen–Macaulay with positive dimension. The resulting projective scheme W is pure of dimension one and has degree1960.

The same argument works with L11 and Z6. In particular, it is incorrect to insist that the later systems themselves must have one-dimensional base support: only their base support after restriction to V(f) needs that bound.

## Local orders and singular curves

For an integral curve C⊂Z4, its generic point has codimension three in the smooth ambient P4, so R=O_(P4,η_C) is a regular local ring of dimension three regardless of singularities of C at closed points. Its residue field is C(C), which causes no change to length or Hilbert–Samuel calculations.

Modulo the maximal ideal, b has rank s≤8. An invertible s-block permits invertible row and column operations to split off I_s, leaving a matrix with entries in the maximal ideal. Each k-minor has order at least k−s. Preservation of determinantal ideals under these operations then gives orders at least2 for f and6 for g,h. Every curve component of Z4 lies in W; because W is pure one-dimensional, it is a component of W and (f,g,h) is maximal-ideal-primary in R. Its colength is exactly the coefficient of C in the fundamental cycle of W.

## Unequal-order length bound without local Bézout

Let q'=(f³,g,h). Its radical is the maximal ideal, so it is a parameter ideal in the regular, hence Cohen–Macaulay, ring R. Its generators form a regular sequence and lie in m6. For a parameter ideal a generated by a regular sequence, the associated graded ring is (R/a)[T1,T2,T3]. Thus length(R/a^n)=length(R/a)·binom(n+2,3). Regularity of R gives length(R/m^(6n))=binom(6n+2,3). Since (q')^n⊂m^(6n), leading coefficient comparison gives length(R/q')≥216.

The parameter sequence f,g,h can be permuted. In the one-dimensional Cohen–Macaulay ring R/(g,h), f is a nonzerodivisor, so the three successive f-power quotients of R/(f³,g,h) each have length length(R/(f,g,h)). Therefore length(R/q')=3 length(R/q), and length(R/q)≥72. This explicitly proves the required unequal-order estimate; individual ideal containments are not being mistaken for an unproved product estimate.

For the degree-(10,11,11) variant, use (f³,g²,h²)⊂m6. Successively taking parameter powers multiplies colength by3·2·2=12, proving length(R/q)≥18. The numerical bound is floor1210/18=67.
