# Rank-five curve support: empty or the canonical conic orbit

## Theorem and hypotheses

Retain the standing complex Schrödinger-normalizer setting N=A⋊G, A=F5², G=SL2(F5), and the residual loci Z_j. Import the established statements

- dim Z5≤1;
- the reduced union Γ5 of its curve components has degree at most75;
- every canonical negative reflection line is disjoint from Z5.

The degree bound is proved in ../mixed-minor-rank-four-exclusion/RANK_FIVE_DEGREE.md. The last assertion is precisely the pointwise rank Q≥6 theorem in ../rank-four-canonical-conic-exclusion/LINE_ADDENDUM.md and uses the actual twenty-dimensional target representation. Under these hypotheses,

    Γ5=∅, or Γ5=⋃_{a∈A} C_a,

where the C_a are the twenty-five canonical smooth conics in the positive reflection planes. In the second case deg Γ5=50. Thus Z5 has no other positive-dimensional support.

If we also import ../mixed-minor-rank-four-exclusion/GLOBAL_EMPTINESS.md, then every point of Z5 has residual rank exactly five. The classification itself does not require global emptiness of Z4; it requires only the three displayed hypotheses and the universal curve theorems below.

## 1. Lines: transfer only the orbit lemma

Sections1–5 of ../rank-four-line-component-exclusion/PROOF.md prove the following statement solely about the projective normalizer representation: a line with at most125 translates under N either is a canonical negative line or meets one. Its proof includes axis-stable lines and free A-orbits, and does not use a rank locus.

If L⊂Z5 were a line, every N-translate would be a distinct integral component of Γ5, because dim Z5≤1. The degree bound gives |N·L|≤75≤125. The orbit lemma would force L to be canonical or to meet a canonical negative line. Either alternative contradicts disjointness of Z5 from those lines. Hence Γ5 has no line components.

We do not transfer the old theorem's rank-four hypothesis; we apply its explicitly proved orbit lemma to the new disjointness and degree bound.

## 2. Conics: transfer only Sections1–3 of the orbit classification

Let C be an integral conic component of Γ5. Every translate is a component, so 2|N·C|≤75. Its three-dimensional vector span is a proper subspace of V, so its A-stabilizer has order one or five.

If A_C=1, write K=N_C and H for its image in G. Then |N·C|=25[G:H], whence [G:H]≤1. Thus K is a complement to A. The central-involution cocycle argument conjugates K to G by an element of A. Its span is then the unique three-dimensional G-subrepresentation T of V=T⊕W. Since G is perfect, its invariant conic equation is G-fixed, and (Sym²T*)^G is generated by the nondegenerate canonical quadratic q. Thus C is canonical.

If |A_C|=5, Section2 of ../rank-four-conic-component-classification/PROOF.md gives at least300 distinct conics in its N-orbit: there are60 distinct span planes and at least five conics over each plane, obtained by the diagonal order-five shear. This is a purely projective orbit calculation, contradicting the degree bound.

The canonical conics form one orbit of exactly25 members, as proved in Section1 of that file: their A-stabilizer is trivial and their stabilizer contains G. Hence all conic components, if any, comprise precisely this orbit, of total degree50.

No conclusion from Section4 of that file is imported. Its coefficient restrictions use total rank b≤8 and are specific to Z4; they do not apply here.

## 3. Cubics and quartics: the theorem is explicitly rank-independent

Sections1–6 of ../rank-four-degree-three-four-exclusion/PROOF.md prove that any N-invariant reduced curve of total degree at most125 has no integral components of degree three or four. The proof exhausts A-stabilizers and spans, using only normalizer representation theory, low-genus automorphism constraints, and the total degree budget.

Apply that theorem directly to Γ5, whose degree is at most75. Its reference in the concluding paragraph to the rank-four no-conic theorem is not used: the degree-three/four exclusion has already been proved independently in its preceding sections. Thus Γ5 has no cubic or quartic components.

## 4. The residual higher-degree union vanishes

Let D be the reduced union of all components of Γ5 other than its conic components. The degree of a component is preserved by N, so D is N-invariant. Sections1–3 show that each component of D has degree at least five. Also deg D≤75, and if conics occur then deg D≤25.

The general theorem in ../rank-four-degree-hundred-lower-bound/PROOF.md states that a nonempty N-invariant reduced curve whose integral components all have degree at least five has degree at least100. This theorem is not tied to any rank locus. For clarity, its proof in the only range needed here is as follows.

Suppose 0<deg D≤75. A free A-orbit of components costs at least25·5=125. An A-orbit of size five has an axis stabilizer; the six conjugate axes give at least30 components in its N-orbit, costing at least150. Therefore every component of D is A-stable and spans P4. The disjoint-normalization polarization lemma (Section2 of that file and ../rank-four-degree-hundred-audit/AUDIT.md, Section2) gives50|deg D. Thus deg D=50.

Finally ../rank-four-degree-sixty-lower-bound/PROOF.md, Section5, excludes every nonempty N-invariant reduced curve of degree at most55 without line or conic components. This is again a general curve theorem. In brief, its component-orbit argument reduces to N-stable integral curves: an A-stable component of degree five or ten has at least20 N-translates; any other nonsingleton orbit has at least five components and costs at least75. The integral case is excluded by the genus lower bound326 and the refined Hilbert-function upper bound306 through degree55. No rank-specific input is used in that argument.

This contradiction proves D=∅. If conics occur, their full canonical orbit is all of Γ5; if they do not, Γ5 is empty. This proves the theorem.

## 5. Exact remaining question and next structural step

There remains a single possible positive-dimensional configuration: residual rank exactly five along every canonical conic. The canonical conic pointwise bounds in ../rank-four-canonical-conic-exclusion/POINTWISE_ADDENDUM.md are

    rank b_even≥5,   rank b_odd≥4.

Since rank Q=rank b−4, containment C⊂Z5 would force both blocks to have constant ranks exactly(5,4) along C. Conversely those ranks on one canonical conic imply its containment in Z5, hence containment of the complete25-conic orbit by equivariance.

The next bounded structural target is therefore to exclude, or characterize, the simultaneous constant-rank configuration(5,4) on C≅P1 with its binary-icosahedral linearization and actual target parity dimensions(8,12). This is a new rank-five problem. The earlier contradiction for constant odd rank three and the earlier simultaneous(5,3) conic coefficient restrictions do not by themselves exclude it.

If that configuration is ruled out, dim Z5≤0 follows. Isolated rank-five points are not excluded by this paper, even when Γ5 is empty. No assertion about the scheme multiplicity or purity of Z5 is made.
