# Birational exclusion of the invariant quintic, including nonnormal surfaces

## Status and precise hypotheses

Work over C. Let Y be an integral nondegenerate surface in P(V)=P4 of degree 5, stable under the standard projective normalizer

    Gamma = A semidirect SL2(F5),  A = (F5)^2,

with the indicated standard action on A. Assume a general hyperplane section C of Y is integral and has arithmetic genus at most 1. Then **no such Y exists**.

In particular, this closes the integral-quintic branch of the audited rank-one-middle-quintic reduction when combined with its separate SECTIONAL_GENUS.md, now independently checked in SECTIONAL_GENUS_INDEPENDENT_AUDIT.md (PASS). This argument uses neither normality nor Cohen–Macaulayness of Y, and does not use chi(O_Y). It bypasses classification of embedded quintic surfaces.

## 1. Faithfulness and the projective group

The scalar center Z(H5) acts trivially on P(V). Its quotient A acts faithfully projectively in the standard Heisenberg representation. No additional element of H5 semidirect SL2(F5) acts as a scalar: a scalar would conjugate A trivially, whereas the standard SL2(F5) action on A is faithful; its SL2 component is therefore the identity, leaving only Z(H5). Thus the projective group is exactly Gamma as displayed above.

The induced Gamma action on the function field C(Y) is faithful. Indeed, an element acting trivially on that field fixes Y pointwise, since it is a regular automorphism of Y. A finite-order projective transformation has fixed locus equal to the union of the projectivized eigenspaces of a diagonalizable lift. An integral Y contained in that finite union is contained in one eigenspace. Because Y spans P4, that eigenspace is all V, and the projective transformation is the identity.

Consequently Gamma embeds in Bir(S) for any smooth projective resolution S of the normalization of Y.

## 2. A resolution is rational or elliptic ruled

Let f:S -> Y be the normalization followed by a smooth projective resolution, and put L=f*O_Y(1). This line bundle is nef, globally generated, and has L^2=5. Consider the basepoint-free subsystem pulled back from ambient hyperplanes. Its morphism has two-dimensional image and is birational onto Y. By Bertini, its general member D is smooth and irreducible.

For completeness, the finite normalization has no contracted curves. The resolution contracts finitely many curves to finitely many points of the normalization; a general ambient hyperplane avoids their finitely many image points in Y. Thus a general pullback D contains no contracted curve, and D -> C is a proper birational map of integral curves, hence the normalization map. In particular,

    0 <= g(D) <= p_a(C) <= 1.

Adjunction on the smooth surface S gives

    K_S.L = 2g(D)-2-L^2 = 2g(D)-7 <= -5.

If H^0(S,mK_S) were nonzero for an integer m>0, an effective divisor linearly equivalent to mK_S would have negative intersection with the nef class L. This is impossible, including the possibility of a zero divisor (whose intersection is zero). Hence every plurigenus vanishes.

Enriques' ruledness theorem now says that S is birational to B x P1 for a smooth projective curve B. This precise classical implication is stated in Beauville's own algebraic-surfaces lecture notes, PDF page 26 (zero-based page 25), and again on PDF page 57:

https://math.univ-cotedazur.fr/~beauvill/conf/Shanghai.pdf

The ruled base satisfies g(B)<=g(D)<=1. Here is a direct justification that avoids hidden assumptions on exceptional curves. If g(B)>0, the rational map S --> B extends to a morphism: resolve its indeterminacy, and each exceptional P1 must map constantly to B, so the map descends. Its general fiber F has positive L-degree, since f contracts only finitely many curves and F moves in a covering family. Therefore D.F=L.F>0, proving that D maps nonconstantly onto B. Riemann–Hurwitz gives g(B)<=g(D) when g(D)<=1. If g(B)=0 the desired bound already holds.

There are only two possibilities: Y is rational, or Y is birationally ruled over an elliptic curve.

## 3. The rational case is impossible by Serre's 5-subgroup theorem

Choose the order-five matrix

    u = [[1,1],[0,1]] in SL2(F5).

The subgroup P=A semidirect <u> of Gamma has order 125 and is nonabelian: u does not centralize A. Explicitly, for v=(0,1), uv-v=(1,0) is nonzero, so the commutator of u with translation by v is nontrivial.

If Y were rational, faithfulness would embed P into Cr2(C). This contradicts the following exact published theorem:

J.-P. Serre, "A Minkowski-style bound for the orders of the finite subgroups of the Cremona group of rank 2 over an arbitrary field", Theorem 5.1(i), printed page 204, PDF page 12: a finite ell-subgroup of Cr2(k), with ell>3 and ell different from the characteristic, is abelian and generated by at most two elements.

Primary author/institution-hosted PDF:
https://www.college-de-france.fr/media/jean-pierre-serre/UPL151543545980010914_9___A_Minkowski_style_bound.pdf

Also the author's preprint record:
https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.0514

Only abelianness for ell=5 is needed. This is a theorem about all finite 5-subgroups, not merely elementary abelian groups; confusing these two versions would invalidate this step.

## 4. The elliptic-ruled case is impossible

Suppose C(Y) is isomorphic to C(E)(t), where E is a smooth genus-one curve. Every birational automorphism of E x P1 descends to an automorphism of E. Indeed, composing it with projection to E gives a rational map constant on general P1 fibers, because every rational map P1 --> E extends and is constant. Applying the same argument to its inverse shows that the induced map on E is an automorphism. Thus there is an exact sequence

    1 -> PGL2(C(E)) -> Bir(E x P1) -> Aut(E),

where exactness is asserted at the first two terms and the image in Aut(E).

After choosing an origin, Aut(E) is the semidirect product of its abelian translation group with the cyclic origin-fixing group (order 2, 4, or 6 over C). It is therefore solvable.

SL2(F5) is perfect. One elementary verification: the upper unipotents x(t) generate together with lower unipotents, and with h=diag(2,3), the commutator h x(s) h^(-1) x(s)^(-1) equals x(3s). Thus every upper unipotent lies in the derived group; the lower ones do as well, and these generate SL2(F5). Any homomorphism from SL2(F5) to the solvable group Aut(E) is trivial.

Moreover [SL2(F5),A]=A: already the element -I has commutator image (-I-I)A=-2A=A. Since SL2(F5) acts trivially on E, its commutators with A do also, so all A acts trivially on E.

The faithful Gamma action therefore restricts to an injection

    A -> PGL2(C(E)) -> PGL2(overline(C(E))).

But there is no subgroup C5 x C5 in the latter group. To see this directly, diagonalize a nonidentity order-five element a, which fixes exactly two points of P1. Every element commuting with a preserves those points individually (interchanging them conjugates a to a^(-1), which is different from a). Its centralizer is the one-dimensional diagonal torus. Any finite subgroup of that torus is cyclic. Since A is abelian of exponent five and rank two, this is a contradiction.

Both birational possibilities are excluded, proving the theorem.

## Boundaries and audit points

- The sectional input, integral general hyperplane section of arithmetic genus one, is separately proved and independently audited in ../rank-one-middle-quintic-reduction/SECTIONAL_GENUS.md and SECTIONAL_GENUS_INDEPENDENT_AUDIT.md. The standalone theorem above also works with sectional geometric genus at most one directly.
- The full projective normalizer and its standard faithful SL2 action on A are essential. An H5-invariant elliptic quintic scroll by itself is not excluded.
- Normality, projective normality, smoothness, chi invariance under normalization, and a del Pezzo/scroll embedded classification are never assumed.
- The field assumption here is C; do not export the proof unchanged to positive characteristic.
- No selected matrix, physical input, rank evaluation, numerical geometry computation, or source-app mutation was performed. Only this local proof file was written.
