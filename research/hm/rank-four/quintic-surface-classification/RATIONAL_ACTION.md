# Rational-action obstruction for the Heisenberg normalizer

## Result

Let A = (F_5)^2 and G = A ⋊ SL_2(F_5), with the standard action. Then G has no faithful birational action on a rational complex surface. Consequently it has no faithful regular action on a smooth rational complex surface.

**Order correction:** |SL_2(F_5)| = 120, so |G| = 25·120 = 3000. For the order-125 Heisenberg group H_5, the semidirect product N = H_5 ⋊ SL_2(F_5) has order 15000; quotienting its order-5 scalar center gives G.

## Primary-source theorem and hypotheses

Jean-Pierre Serre, “A Minkowski-style bound for the orders of the finite subgroups of the Cremona group of rank 2 over an arbitrary field,” Moscow Mathematical Journal 9 (2009), 193–208, Theorem 5.1(i), printed p. 204 (PDF page 12, zero-based page 11): a finite ℓ-subgroup of Cr(k), for ℓ > 3 and ℓ different from char(k), is abelian and generated by at most two elements.

Source: https://www.college-de-france.fr/media/jean-pierre-serre/UPL151543545980010914_9___A_Minkowski_style_bound.pdf

The field is arbitrary. The preceding §5.1 explicitly recalls only ℓ ≠ char(k); the finite-generation/small-field hypotheses elsewhere in the paper are NOT hypotheses of Theorem 5.1. Thus it applies to k = C and ℓ = 5. The theorem is structural, not the numerical Minkowski bound (which is vacuous over C). Serre cites the finite rational-surface classification in the proof. This precise theorem removes the need for a separate degree-by-degree del Pezzo analysis here.

## Proof

Take u = [[1,1],[0,1]] in SL_2(F_5). It has order 5 and acts nontrivially on A: for the standard basis e_1,e_2, u(e_2) = e_1 + e_2. Thus

P = A ⋊ ⟨u⟩

has order 125 and is nonabelian; indeed [u,e_2] = e_1 ≠ 0, with the convention [u,a] = u a u^{-1} a^{-1} and additive notation on A. It is a Sylow 5-subgroup of G. If G embedded in Cr_2(C), P would also embed, contradicting Serre's theorem.

More generally, the splitting is unnecessary: if 1 → A → Γ → SL_2(F_5) → 1 has the indicated conjugation action, the inverse image of ⟨u⟩ has order 125 and is nonabelian for the same conjugation reason. Such a Γ likewise cannot embed in Cr_2(C).

## Projective kernel, including the central involution

Assume N = H_5 ⋊ SL_2(F_5) acts linearly on V = C^5 extending the faithful Schrödinger representation of H_5, and that the induced action of SL_2(F_5) on H_5/Z(H_5) = A is standard. Then the kernel of N → PGL(V) is exactly Z(H_5).

Indeed, a scalar element centralizes the represented H_5 pointwise. Its induced conjugation on A is therefore trivial. Conjugation by its H_5 component is already trivial on A, whereas the SL_2 component acts by the faithful standard representation; hence the latter component is the identity. The element then lies in H_5, where the scalar elements are precisely Z(H_5). Conversely Z(H_5) acts by scalars in the Schrödinger representation.

In particular, the central involution −I of SL_2(F_5) does NOT disappear projectively: it acts on A as inversion, which is nontrivial in characteristic 5. It is central only in the SL_2 factor, not in G. No extra quotient by this involution is legitimate.

## Faithful restriction to an integral invariant surface

Let Y ⊂ P(V) be an integral, nondegenerate projective variety, invariant under a subgroup Γ ⊂ PGL(V). Then Γ acts faithfully on Y. For if g fixes Y pointwise, choose a linear representative T. The projective fixed locus is the finite union of the projectivizations of the eigenspaces of T. Irreducibility forces Y into one eigenspace. Nondegeneracy forces that eigenspace to be V, so T is scalar and g is the identity.

Here nondegeneracy is automatic for any nonempty N-invariant projective subvariety Y when the H_5 representation V is irreducible: the vector space over the projective linear span of Y is a nonzero H_5-invariant subspace, hence equals V. Integrality/irreducibility is essential for the fixed-locus argument; a reducible invariant union alone does not give faithful action on each component.

Consequently, an integral N-invariant surface Y in P(V), under the preceding representation hypotheses, cannot be rational. A faithful projective action on Y induces a faithful action on its function field C(Y). If Y were rational, C(Y) ≅ C(x,y), giving the forbidden embedding of G into Cr_2(C). This argument works even if Y is singular or nonnormal: normalization and resolution preserve the function field, and no assertion about lifting to a chosen resolution is needed.

## Scope

This obstruction excludes rational integral invariant surfaces. It does not by itself classify nonrational surfaces, rule out reducible invariant unions, or establish the geometry of a proposed normalization. Those steps require their own hypotheses and arguments.
