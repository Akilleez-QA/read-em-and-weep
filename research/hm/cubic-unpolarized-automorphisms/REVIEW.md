## Checks

1. An embedded smooth prime divisor F isomorphic to P3 in the smooth blowup is Cartier. Global generation of H forces its restriction to be O(a), with a nonnegative integer. When a=0, the coordinate sections are constant, so the image is a point and the only possible three-dimensional fiber is an exceptional divisor.

2. When a>0, a positive-dimensional projective fiber would contain a curve on which the ample restriction of H has degree zero. Thus the restriction of the proper blowdown is quasi-finite and finite. For F distinct from E_z, a nonempty intersection is the zero divisor of a nonzero restricted defining section. On integral smooth F this is an effective Cartier divisor of dimension two, which cannot lie in a finite fiber. Hence F avoids every E_z.

3. Away from the exceptional divisors the blowdown is an isomorphism. Properness ensures that the resulting embedded copy Y of P3 is closed in P4, not merely locally closed in its complement of Z. It is a smooth hypersurface. Degree gives d=a^3, and adjunction gives -4=a(d-5). The only positive integral solution is a=d=1. Its normal bundle is O(1), whereas each exceptional divisor has normal O(-1). The claimed intrinsic characterization is therefore valid.

4. An automorphism consequently preserves the sum E. The canonical class formula K=-5H+3E and the torsion-free Picard group imply preservation of H. The complete system |H| recovers the original blowdown. This proves descent to a projective automorphism preserving Z, with the inverse supplied by the functorial blowup construction. Uniqueness follows on the common dense open set.

5. Every automorphism of the integral cubic image lifts uniquely to its normalization. The resulting injection into Aut(M) has image all of Gamma because the known complete-cubic action supplies a polarized automorphism for each Gamma element with the same normalization lift. This comparison proves automatic polarization preservation without assuming that Pic(V51) injects into Pic(M).

6. The result is correctly limited to groups of complex algebraic automorphisms. It makes no additional claim about automorphism group schemes, abstract rigidity, or the other source constructions. The retained abstract deformation tangent dimension is not contradicted by the finite automorphism group.
