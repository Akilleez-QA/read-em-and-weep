# Two cubic channels and exclusion of the maximal ghost

Work over C, with the actual HM modules and maps. Put S=Sym(T), T=V*, dim V=5, K_j=H^1(End_0(F)(j-1)), P_j=H^1(I_C(6-j))*, and N_0=SP_0. Here C is the disjoint union of the twenty-five transverse HM evaluation-degeneracy lines. The group denoted G below is the HM normalizer; N_0 is a module, not that group.

The new exclusion is that K cannot have forty minimal cubic relations. In fact its cubic relation space is either zero, a specific irreducible of dimension fifteen, or a different irreducible of dimension twenty-five. This also forces at least one of two actual Tor-map scalars to be nonzero and canonically isolates the twenty primary cubic relations of N_0.

## 2. The representation of the forty octic syzygies

The tensor rules needed here are

Sym^2 T=A* tensor U', Lambda^2 T=A* tensor W,
T tensor A*=A tensor (U+W'),
U tensor W'=R_6, U tensor Q_4=W+Q_4+R_6,
W' tensor W'=1+U', W' tensor Q_4=U'+J_5,
W tensor R_6=U'+T_4+J_5.

These follow from the HM normalizer rules and the SL_2(F_5) character table. For example U tensor Q_4 follows by restricting Sym^2 W tensor Sym^3 W=Sym^5 W+Sym^3 W+W, and W' tensor Q_4 follows by restricting W' tensor Sym^3 W'=Sym^4 W'+Sym^2 W'. The remaining last identity is checked against the same character table. All tensor products with nontrivial Heisenberg central weight used below are inequivalent irreducibles when their quotient labels differ.

Let H=I_5 and U_6=T tensor W' be the canonical ten-dimensional new sextic generators. PR468 proves S_3 H -> I_8 is injective. The degree-eight Eagon--Northcott cohomology gives an exact sequence for I_8/(S_3 H), with primary term T_4 tensor H^1(F(1))=A tensor (W+R_6), and quotient H^2(B(-2))=A tensor Q_4. Thus, as a G-representation,

I_8/(S_3 H)=A tensor (W+R_6+Q_4).

Semisimplicity is used only for characters here; no splitting of graded modules is asserted. The sextic part of the free cover in degree eight is

S_2 tensor U_6=A tensor (1+2U'+W+Q_4+2R_6+J_5).

Consequently its kernel onto I_8/(S_3 H), equivalently the degree-eight relation space Z_8 of the full minimal cover, is A tensor (1+2U'+R_6+J_5). PR470 proves multiplication T tensor Z_7 -> Z_8 is injective, with Z_7=A* tensor W'. Its image therefore has character A tensor (1+U'+R_6). Subtracting this actual subrepresentation gives the minimal octic first syzygy space

Z_8/(T Z_7)=A tensor (U'+J_5).                              (3)

## 3. Why dualization computes Tor in degree three

Dualize the full minimal resolution of S/I_C into S(-5), then normalize degrees so that its final cokernel is P. In homological order toward P the free modules are

C_0=S tensor V + S(-1) tensor R_6,
C_1=S(-1)+45S(-2),
C_2=40S(-3)+10S(-4),
C_3=10S(-5)+6S(-6), C_4=S(-11).

The possible intervening cohomology at C_1 is the normalized canonical module. Since C is a disjoint union of lines, its canonical sheaf is the direct sum of O_line(-2), whose section module first occurs in degree two. Graded local duality identifies Ext^3(S/I_C,S(-5)) with this canonical section module; the normalization above shifts its first degree to eight. Explicitly, Ext^i(S/I_C,S(-5))=0 for i<3 because local cohomology H_m^(5-i)(S/I_C) vanishes above dimension two. The canonical Ext^3 is the graded dual of H_m^2(S/I_C), whose degree-d piece is H^1(O_C(d)); Serre duality on each line gives exactly the canonical section module described above. In particular this dual complex is exact in internal degrees at most seven apart from its final cokernel P.

Therefore it computes the beginning of a minimal resolution of P in internal degree three. This is a low-degree exactness assertion, not an assumption that dualizing any resolution preserves exactness everywhere. By (3),

Tor_2^S(P,k)_3=A* tensor (U'+J_5),
Tor_1^S(P,k)_3=0.                                          (4)

The normalized shift can also be checked directly: the last original free terms dualize to 6S(5)+5S(6) and 45S(4)+S(5); shifting degrees by six produces C_0,C_1 as displayed. The original canonical section module begins in degree two and hence in normalized degree eight.

## 4. The two actual scalars and the ghost alternatives

Apply Tor(-,k) to 0 -> N_0 -> P -> R_6 tensor k(-1) -> 0. In internal degree three it gives

Tor_2(P,k)_3 --f--> Lambda^2 T tensor R_6
   -> Rel(N_0)_3 -> 0.                                    (5)

There is also a canonical identification of the kernel. The preceding term Tor_3(R_6 tensor k(-1),k)_3 vanishes, because it is concentrated in degree four. Thus Tor_2(N_0,k)_3 injects into Tor_2(P,k)_3 with image ker f. In the PR477 extension, the primary module starts in degree three, so its Tor_1 and Tor_2 have no degree-three part. Its Tor sequence therefore identifies Tor_2(K,k)_3 with Tor_2(N_0,k)_3. Consequently ker f is canonically Tor_2(K,k)_3, not just an equal-dimensional representation.

The map f is induced by the actual module quotient, not an arbitrarily selected equivariant map. Equations (3)--(4) and the last tensor identity give its source and target as

A* tensor (U'+J_5) --f--> A* tensor (U'+T_4+J_5).

Schur's lemma makes f scalar on each of the U' and J_5 channels and zero into the absent T_4 channel. Thus Rel(N_0)_3 is the direct sum of the mandatory A* tensor T_4 and whichever of the U',J_5 channels have zero scalar.

The map in (2) onto A* tensor T_4 is necessarily an isomorphism on its unique T_4 isotypic summand, and zero on the other summands. Hence Rel(K)_3 is precisely the sum of those zero-scalar channels. This gives the preliminary possibilities of dimensions 0,15,25,40.

For clarity this is a genuine cubic ghost pair. The first relation module of K has Euler degree one and fifteen minimal generators degree two. Its free cover contributes 15+75=90 vectors in degree three, while the full degree-three relation space has dimension 5 dim S_3-dim K_3=175-85=90. If r=dim Rel(K)_3, the span of products of lower relations has dimension 90-r, so the kernel of their degree-three presentation has dimension r. There are no lower syzygies among these relations. Thus beta_{1,3}(K)=beta_{2,3}(K)=r.

## 5. A rank obstruction from the Euler quotient

Let R_2 be the twenty-dimensional degree-two kernel of S tensor V -> K: its five Euler products and fifteen new quadratic relations. Let D=S R_2, generated in degree two only. It has the same generic rank as the submodule generated by epsilon and the quadratic relations, since every x_i epsilon belongs to D.

We prove rank D>=3. The fifteen-dimensional quotient of R_2 by T epsilon embeds in H^0(Q(2)) by (1), since line bundles on P^4 have no intermediate cohomology. Its image sheaf cannot have rank zero. Suppose it has rank one. Take its saturation in Q and then its reflexive hull. This hull is a line bundle O(a): on smooth P^4 a rank-one reflexive sheaf is invertible. The inclusion extends to Q across the codimension-at-least-two locus where the saturation differs from its hull, since Q is locally free. It remains injective, as it is nonzero generically.

A nonzero inclusion O(a) -> Q implies H^0(Q(-a))!=0. Twisting (1), H^0(O(-a))=0 and H^1(O(-a-1))=0 for a>=1, so a<=0. On the other hand all fifteen independent quadratic sections factor through O(a+2), giving h^0(O(a+2))>=15. Since h^0(O(2))=15 and h^0(O(1))=5, this forces a=0.

The saturated image line is intrinsic and G-invariant. Its extension O -> Q therefore gives a G-invariant one-dimensional subspace in H^0(Q)=V. This contradicts irreducibility of V. Consequently the quadratic image in Q has rank at least two. Adding the Euler line gives rank D>=3.

## 6. The extremal shadow would instead force rank two

Suppose r=40. Then dim D_2=20 and dim D_3=90-r=50. Consider the quotient Q'=S^5/D, with no degree-one relations; deliberately use D generated in degree two, not the module with epsilon as a degree-one generator. Its Hilbert function in degrees one through three is 25,55,125.

Take the lexicographic submodule E of S^5 with the same full Hilbert function as D. Use Dellaca, Lemmas 6.3, 6.6, 6.7 and the consecutive-cancellation argument in the proof of Theorem 6.1. Lemma 6.6 supplies the Betti-preserving polarization/specialization step, not a separate formula for lex-module Betti numbers. In degree two, E has all fifteen quadratic monomials in its first component and the five monomials x_0 x_i in its second. Its degree-three shadow has exactly 35+15=50 monomials, so E has no degree-three generator. It has no generators of degree below two either.

If E had a later generator, let e>=4 be its first such degree. The degree-two-generated part is m^2 in the first component plus x_0 m in the second. Both have two-linear resolutions, so this part has no first syzygy of degree e. Newly added degree-e minimal generators cannot enter degree-e first syzygies with nonzero constant coefficients, by minimality. Therefore beta_{1,e}(E)=0 while beta_{0,e}(E)>0.

Equivalently for S^5/E, beta_{2,e}=0 and beta_{1,e}>0. Its degree-e first syzygy entry cannot be removed by consecutive cancellations to obtain the Betti table of S^5/D: the neighboring beta_{2,e} and beta_{0,e} are both zero. But D is generated in degree two, so beta_{1,e}(S^5/D)=0. This contradiction proves that E has no later generators.

Thus E=m^2 e_1 + x_0 m e_2 has rank two. Equality of full Hilbert functions implies equality of ranks, and hence rank D=2, contradicting section 5. This excludes r=40 without presupposing the quotient rank in a rank-adjusted Gotzmann theorem.

## 7. Consequences and the remaining dependency

At least one of the two actual scalar channels of f is nonzero. The exact alternatives are

Rel(K)_3=0, A* tensor U', or A* tensor J_5;
r=0,15,25.

Consequently rank f is respectively40,25,15, and Rel(N_0)_3 has dimension20,35,45. More precisely its twenty-dimensional primary channel A* tensor T_4 is canonically its unique T_4 isotypic summand and maps isomorphically to the primary generators in (2). Any remaining cubic relation is in one, not both, of the inequivalent U' and J_5 channels. Thus the next unresolved task is to determine at most one surviving irreducible ghost channel, rather than an arbitrary forty-dimensional cancellation space. This is a structural exclusion for the actual modules; it does not claim both scalars are nonzero.
