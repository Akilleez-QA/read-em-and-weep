# Good square directions and an algebraic translated-root curve

## Statement and scope

Work over C. Let S=Sym(V), with V finite-dimensional and in degree
one, and let M be a
finitely generated graded S-module generated by M_0. The first new
statement is the following strengthening of PR441:

> If dim M_d=2 for an integer d>=2, then a general linear form
> ell in V satisfies ell^(d-1) M_1=M_d.

In particular, when dim M_3=2, a good quadratic can be chosen to
be a square ell^2. This is not a consequence of intersecting the
good-quadratic open set with the Veronese variety: such an
intersection need not be nonempty for an arbitrary open set.
Section 1 proves the assertion directly by differentiation,
homogeneity, and degree-zero generation.

Apply it to the fixed normalized Horrocks--Mumford bundle F on
Y=P^4, its primary module M=H^1_*(F), and B=End_0(F). Retain the
original invariant forms u_0=t_2 and v_0=t_3. The exact fixed
endpoint remains

    Q_0 = H^1(B(6)) /
          [u_0 H^1(B(4)) + v_0 H^1(B(3))].

The pinned inputs prove that M is generated in degree zero,
dim M_3=2, M_4=0, and u_0 is nondegenerate. Choose existentially
a good square ell^2 with the additional open condition

    alpha = l^T U_0^(-1) l != 0,

where u_0(x)=x^T U_0 x and ell(x)=l^T x. Define

    D = U_0^(-1) l l^T,          H(s)=I+sD,
    tau(s)=2s+alpha s^2,         v(s,x)=v_0(H(s)x).

Here U_0 and D describe quadratic forms and changes of coefficient
coordinates, not evaluated HM cohomology multiplication matrices.
The new algebraic construction has the following properties:

1. On T=Spec C[s,(1+alpha s)^(-1)], g(s)=H(s)^(-1) is an actual
   algebraic target automorphism through g(0)=identity, and

       (g(s)^(-1))^*u_0 = u_0+tau(s)ell^2,
       (g(s)^(-1))^*v_0 = v(s).

   Thus both transformed forms are polynomial in s; no merely
   formal square root is required.

2. There is a monic quadratic f(t), inherited from the two-column
   pencil with direction ell^2, such that the explicit polynomial
   quotient module

       Q_s = coker[(u_0+tau(s)ell^2,v(s)):
                (H^1(B(4)) direct sum H^1(B(3))) tensor C[s]
                    -> H^1(B(6)) tensor C[s]]

   has length at most 60 and is annihilated by f(tau(s)).
   It therefore has at most four nonzero fibers on the entire
   s-line. These module assertions include the point omitted from T.

3. For b in T(C), put a=tau(b) and e_a=ord_(t=a) f, with e_a=0
   when f(a)!=0. Then

       length (Q_s)_(s-b) <=15 e_a <=30,
       (s-b)^(e_a) (Q_s)_(s-b)=0.

   The exponent at every allowed geometric parameter is at most
   two. At s=0 the fiber is exactly Q_0, and the completed quotient
   is reconstructed from its first auxiliary-parameter jet.

4. With q_0:Z=(P^1)^4 -> Y the original ordered-root map, the
   algebraic family q_g=g(s) o q_0 on T has the exact original
   special coefficient geometry. The whole paired-character
   order-six ideal cohomology vanishes whenever f(tau(s))!=0.
   In particular it vanishes over C(s).

The contribution is an algebraic coefficient/root-map family
through the original geometry, obtained from a structural
good-square theorem. It does not evaluate Q_0, construct an actual
source primitive or a quantized module, or prove algebraicity of
a formal quantization. The coefficient-family algebraicity proved
here must not be conflated with those separate algebraicity goals.

## 1. A two-dimensional target admits a good pure power

Let d>=2 and dim M_d=2. Choose independent lambda,mu in M_d^vee.
For ell in V define the M_0^vee-valued homogeneous polynomials
of degree d

    Phi(ell)(m) = lambda(ell^d m),
    Psi(ell)(m) = mu(ell^d m),             m in M_0.

The map M_d^vee -> Sym^d(V^vee) tensor M_0^vee sending lambda
to Phi is injective. Indeed degree-zero generation gives
M_d=S_d M_0. Pure powers ell^d span S_d over C, by polarization.
Explicitly, for x_1,...,x_d in V,

    d! x_1...x_d =
      sum_(J subset {1,...,d})
        (-1)^(d-|J|) (sum_(j in J) x_j)^d.

Consequently a functional vanishing on every ell^d m vanishes
on M_d. In particular Phi and Psi are nonzero and C-linearly
independent as vector-valued polynomials.

Let T_ell:M_1 -> M_d be multiplication by ell^(d-1). Its
composition with the surjection

    V tensor M_0 ->> M_1,       x tensor m |-> xm

has, after applying lambda and mu, the two linear functionals

    (x,m) |-> lambda(ell^(d-1)xm),
    (x,m) |-> mu(ell^(d-1)xm).

Differentiation gives

    D Phi(ell)(x)(m) = d lambda(ell^(d-1)xm),
    D Psi(ell)(x)(m) = d mu(ell^(d-1)xm).              (1)

Thus, when derivatives are flattened into rows indexed by a
basis of V and a basis of M_0, the rank of the two derivative
rows is exactly the rank of T_ell. The same scalar must relate
all entries, including entries belonging to different M_0
components. No componentwise proportionality assumption is used.

Suppose, for contradiction, that T_ell is never surjective.
Every such map has rank at most one. Therefore all 2-by-2
minors of these two polynomial derivative rows vanish at every
complex ell, and hence vanish identically.

Choose coordinates z_1,...,z_n on V and let K=C(z_1,...,z_n).
The derivative row D Phi is nonzero over K: a nonzero homogeneous
polynomial of positive degree in characteristic zero cannot
have all derivatives zero. Choose a nonzero entry of this row.
The vanishing minors then imply that there is a single a in K
such that

    D Psi = a D Phi                                      (2)

for every coordinate and every M_0 component. For example, take
a to be the ratio of the corresponding entries of D Psi and
D Phi; the minors prove the remaining identities.

Euler's identity, applied componentwise and then using (2), gives

    d Psi = sum_i z_i partial_i Psi
          = a sum_i z_i partial_i Phi = a d Phi.

Since d is nonzero in C, Psi=a Phi in K tensor M_0^vee. Differentiate
this equality in each coordinate and compare it with (2):

    (partial_i a) Phi = 0            for every i.

At least one component of Phi is a nonzero element of K, so
partial_i a=0 for every i. The simultaneous constants of these
derivations in K are exactly C. For completeness, write a=p/q
with coprime polynomials. If a!=0, each derivative identity gives
q partial_i p=p partial_i q. Unique factorization implies that
p divides partial_i p. A nonzero derivative has smaller total
degree than p, so partial_i p=0, and then partial_i q=0. In
characteristic zero all partials zero means both polynomials
are constant. The case a=0 is immediate.

It follows that Psi is a constant multiple of Phi. Injectivity
of the construction gives the same dependence between mu and
lambda, a contradiction. Some T_ell is therefore surjective.

The entries of T_ell in any bases are homogeneous polynomials
of degree d-1 in ell. Surjectivity is the nonvanishing of at
least one 2-by-2 minor, so the good linear forms constitute a
nonempty Zariski-open subset of V. The property is invariant
under nonzero rescaling and hence also defines a nonempty
open subset of P(V).

This is a two-dimensional-target statement, not a strong
Lefschetz theorem in all degrees or for arbitrary target
dimensions. Only the displayed multiplication map is proved
surjective. The proof uses no selected module-instance entries,
rank calculation, or test linear form.

## 2. Choose a square direction transverse to the quadratic form

PR441 Section 3 imports the exact primary resolution from PR167:

    0 -> 2S(-8) -> 20S(-6) -> 35S(-5) direct sum 2S(-3)
      -> 15S(-4) direct sum 4S(-3) direct sum 10S(-2)
      -> 15S(-1) -> 5S -> M -> 0.

The degree-zero surjection 5S -> M proves generation by M_0.
The same input gives

    Hilb(M,z)=5+10z+10z^2+2z^3.

Thus Section 1 with d=3 applies to this fixed primary module.
It supplies a nonempty open subset of linear forms ell with

    ell^2 M_1=M_3.                                      (3)

PR441 Section 4 also proves the nondegeneracy of u_0. In ordinary
binary-quartic coefficient coordinates its fixed normalization is
u_0=12(c^2-3bd+12ae). The present argument uses only its
nondegeneracy, not a selected evaluation of that expression.

Choose coefficient coordinates x and write u_0=x^T U_0 x for an
invertible symmetric matrix U_0. The polynomial
l |-> l^T U_0^(-1)l is nonzero, since U_0^(-1) is a nondegenerate
symmetric matrix. Hence alpha!=0 defines another nonempty open
subset of the space of linear forms. Affine space is irreducible,
so its intersection with the open set in (3) is nonempty.

Choose ell in that intersection. This is an existential choice
from two specified nonempty opens. No numerical linear form,
cohomology multiplication matrix, or value of a fixed rank is
selected or computed.

## 3. An explicit algebraic rank-one transport

Put D=U_0^(-1)ll^T and alpha=l^T U_0^(-1)l. Direct symbolic
multiplication, valid for every such U_0 and l, gives

    D^2=alpha D,
    U_0D=D^T U_0=ll^T.

For H(s)=I+sD this implies

    H(s)^T U_0 H(s)
       = U_0 + (2s+alpha s^2)ll^T.                     (4)

Because alpha!=0, the coefficient space splits as
ker(l^T) direct sum C U_0^(-1)l. The endomorphism D acts as zero
on the first summand and as alpha on the second. Therefore

    det H(s)=1+alpha s,
    H(s)^(-1)=I-[s/(1+alpha s)]D.                       (5)

Consequently H(s) and its inverse define algebraic GL_5-valued
maps on T=Spec C[s,(1+alpha s)^(-1)]. Use this specified lift to
define g(s)=H(s)^(-1) in PGL_5. It has g(0)=identity.

Equation (4) is the literal polynomial identity

    u_0(H(s)x)=u_0(x)+tau(s)ell(x)^2,
    tau(s)=2s+alpha s^2.                                (6)

The cubic v(s,x)=v_0(H(s)x) is polynomial in both s and x,
homogeneous of degree three in x and of degree at most three
in s. Its constant term is v_0. Thus (6) and this cubic
agree exactly with the transported-pair convention

    u_g=(g^(-1))^*u_0,       v_g=(g^(-1))^*v_0.

The pair is a transported regular sequence at every point of T,
because g is an automorphism there. At s=-1/alpha, H is singular;
only the polynomial formulas and module constructions below
are asserted, not an automorphism or a transported root geometry.

The parameter map satisfies

    tau'(s)=2(1+alpha s),
    1+alpha tau(s)=(1+alpha s)^2.                       (7)

In particular it is unramified at s=0 and at every point of T.
More precisely, after putting A=C[t] and C_s=C[s] with t acting
as tau(s), C_s is a free A-module of rank two. Indeed

    C_s = A[s]/(alpha s^2+2s-t),

and division by this monic quadratic after multiplying by
alpha^(-1) gives the A-basis 1,s. Localizing gives a finite
etale degree-two map

    T -> Spec C[t,(1+alpha t)^(-1)].

This last assertion uses the displayed localization of the target;
the unlocalized polynomial map has one ramification point.

## 4. Pull back the length-two primary control

Use the good square q_1=ell^2 in PR442's two-column argument.
For A=C[t], set

    R_t=coker[u_0+t ell^2:
                M_1 tensor A -> M_3 tensor A].

Choose a two-dimensional subspace L of M_1 on which ell^2 is
an isomorphism onto M_3. Under this identification, the restricted
map is tI+C for a constant endomorphism C of M_3. Put

    E_t=coker(tI+C),        f(t)=det(tI+C).

The elementary polynomial-reduction proof in PR442 Section 1
shows

    dim_C E_t=2,           f E_t=0,           E_t ->> R_t.

The polynomial f is monic of degree two. The proof does not
evaluate C or f. If e_a=ord_(t=a)f, the same argument gives

    length (E_t)_(t-a)=e_a,
    (t-a)^(e_a)(E_t)_(t-a)=0.                           (8)

When e_a=0, the corresponding localization is zero.

Tensor with C_s along t=tau(s), and write E_s=E_t tensor_A C_s
and R_s=R_t tensor_A C_s. Cokernel base change identifies

    R_s=coker[u_0+tau(s)ell^2:
                M_1 tensor C_s -> M_3 tensor C_s].

There is still a surjection E_s ->> R_s, and f(tau(s)) kills
both modules. Because C_s is free of rank two over A, its
A-module decomposition with basis 1,s gives, as C-vector spaces,

    E_s = E_t direct sum E_t,
    R_s = R_t direct sum R_t.

These are vector-space statements, not decompositions compatible
with multiplication by s. In particular

    length_(C_s) E_s=4,
    length_(C_s) R_s=2 length_A R_t <=4.                (9)

For these finite-length modules, length equals C-dimension.

The same natural section-resolution comparison as in PR440 and
PR442 gives, over C_s,

    R_s^(direct sum 15) ->> Q_s.                       (10)

Indeed the natural surjections

    15M_1 direct sum 4M_2 ->> H^1(B(4)),
    15M_3                 ->> H^1(B(6))

commute with polynomial multiplication, and M_4=0. Passing to
the quadratic cokernel first yields the surjection from 15R_s;
quotienting further by v(s)H^1(B(3)) yields (10). Its validity
does not require v(s) to be pulled back from a cubic polynomial
in t. In fact Q_s is not asserted to be the base change of
PR442's fixed-cubic quotient: the cubic here changes with s.

All additional resolution relations and the cubic image are
retained as quotient relations. The source 15R_s is not
identified with Q_s. Combining (9) and (10) gives

    length_(C_s) Q_s <=60,       f(tau(s)) Q_s=0.       (11)

Since alpha!=0 and f is monic quadratic, f(tau(s)) is a nonzero
degree-four polynomial. Thus the support of Q_s is contained
in at most four complex parameters. The number four and the
length bound 60 apply before restricting to T, not merely to
the algebraic automorphism locus.

Right-exact cokernel base change, without a flatness assumption
on Q_s, gives at every b in C

    Q_s/(s-b)Q_s =
      Q(u_0+tau(b)ell^2,v(b)),
    Q_s/sQ_s=Q_0.                                     (12)

Nakayama's lemma equates nonzero fiber with nonzero localization
at b. This proves the stated bound on nonzero fibers. It does
not assert that any of the four possible parameters is bad.
Other primary columns, the cubic image, and resolution relations
can make a root of f(tau(s)) harmless.

## 5. Local length, etale exponent, and the exact first jet

Let b be in T(C) and a=tau(b). Then

    tau(s)-a=(s-b)[2+alpha(s+b)].

The second factor is a unit in (C_s)_(s-b), because its value
at b is 2(1+alpha b)!=0. Thus the ideals (t-a) and (s-b)
become equal in that local ring.

For a direct length justification, take a composition series of
the A_(t-a)-module (E_t)_(t-a), whose length is e_a by (8).
Every factor is C=A_(t-a)/(t-a). Flat base change to
(C_s)_(s-b) preserves exactness, and each factor becomes

    (C_s)_(s-b)/(tau(s)-a) = C,

of length one. Consequently

    length (E_s)_(s-b)=e_a,
    (s-b)^(e_a)(E_s)_(s-b)=0.

Use the localized quotients E_s ->> R_s and 15R_s ->> Q_s:

    length (R_s)_(s-b)<=e_a,
    length (Q_s)_(s-b)<=15e_a,
    (s-b)^(e_a)(Q_s)_(s-b)=0.                          (13)

Since e_a<=2, the local endpoint exponent is at most two
everywhere on T. If e_a=1, the endpoint localization is killed
by s-b and its dimension is at most 15.

The omitted point b=-1/alpha is different: there

    tau(s)+1/alpha=alpha(s+1/alpha)^2.

If f vanishes at a=-1/alpha, pullback can double its root
multiplicity. The module bound there is exponent at most
2e_a<=4 and length at most 30e_a<=60, not the etale bound (13).
No translated-root geometric assertion is made at that point.

At b=0, a=0 and tau'(0)=2. Put e_0=ord_(t=0)f. After
completion at zero define

    Qhat_s = Q_s tensor_(C_s) C[[s]].

It satisfies

    length Qhat_s<=15e_0<=30,
    s^(e_0)Qhat_s=0,        Qhat_s/sQhat_s=Q_0.         (14)

For e_0=0 these statements mean Qhat_s=0. In every case
s^2Qhat_s=0, so Qhat_s -> Qhat_s/s^2Qhat_s is an isomorphism.
Let

    w(x)=sum_i (Dx)_i (partial v_0/partial x_i)(x),
    J=C[epsilon]/(epsilon^2).

The polynomial formulas imply

    u_0+tau(s)ell^2 = u_0+2s ell^2 mod s^2,
    v(s)=v_0+s w mod s^2.

Right-exact cokernel base change therefore identifies the
entire completed module, with s acting as epsilon, as

    Qhat_s =
      coker[(u_0+2epsilon ell^2,v_0+epsilon w):
          (H^1(B(4)) direct sum H^1(B(3))) tensor J
              -> H^1(B(6)) tensor J].                 (15)

The identification is the canonical reduction for the specified
choices; it does not make ell or L canonical. It also does not
evaluate the maps in (15). This is the first jet in the
auxiliary target-translation parameter, not a first-order
quantization result, a source jet, or a collision-ideal order.

The relation to PR442's formal square root is exact. From D^2=alpha D,

    H(s)^2=I+tau(s)D.

The map C[[t]] -> C[[s]], t |-> tau(s), is an isomorphism of
complete local C-algebras because its linear coefficient is 2.
Under this change of parameter, PR442's formal series
(I+tD)^(1/2), which has constant term I, equals H(s).
For example, its scalar rank-one expression is

    (I+tD)^(1/2)
      = I + [(sqrt(1+alpha t)-1)/alpha]D,

and sqrt(1+alpha tau(s))=1+alpha s for the branch with
constant term 1. The transformed cubics therefore also agree
after completion. The new construction is an algebraic
realization after this etale local reparametrization, not a
claim that the original square-root series is polynomial in t.

## 6. Actual algebraic root geometry and fiberwise vanishing

Keep Z=(P^1)^4, the collision ideal I=I_Delta, the paired
permutation group

    G_pair=< (12),(34),(13)(24) >,
    chi=sgn restricted to G_pair,

and the original ordered-root map q_0:Z -> Y fixed. Over T set

    q_g=g(s) o q_0,
    A_g=q_g^*O_Y(1),
    E_g=q_g^*B tensor A_g^(-2).

These are actual algebraic morphisms and locally free sheaves
over T. At s=0, g is the identity, so the fiber is precisely
the original coefficient geometry. The target bundle F, and
hence B, is held fixed; its pullback by q_g varies. There is no
assumption that F is invariant or equivariant under g(s).

At every b in T(C), the transported pair is regular and the
full-character filtration and adjacent-syzygy endpoint from
PR437/439 apply exactly as in PR441 Section 5. They give

    H^2(Z,I^6(E_g)_b)_chi^vee =
      Q(u_0+tau(b)ell^2,v(b))
        direct sum Q(u_0+tau(b)ell^2,v(b)).             (16)

All collision strata, including quadruple collisions, are
retained; only a singular parameter of the target-coordinate
matrix has been omitted from the base.

If f(tau(b))!=0, the selected primary restriction
tau(b)I+C is invertible. Thus the full primary multiplication
by u_0+tau(b)ell^2 is surjective. Naturality gives the same
surjectivity on H^1(B(4)) -> H^1(B(6)), so the quotient in
(16) vanishes. Therefore

    H^2(Z,I^6(E_g)_b)_chi=0

at all such parameters. Since f(tau(s)) is not the zero
polynomial, the same proof over the field C(s) gives generic
whole-coefficient order-six vanishing. Fixed projective
cohomology commutes with this field extension.

The special fiber of (16) is exactly

    H^2(Z,I^6(E_g)_0)_chi^vee = Q_0 direct sum Q_0.

The module Q_s, its localizations, and its completion have
been defined by explicit positive-cohomology cokernels.
They are not identified with a relative R^2 or with the
ordinary module dual of a relative R^2. Fiberwise Serre
duality does not justify such an identification across a
torsion specialization. No relative cohomology/base-change
claim beyond the explicit cokernel base changes is used.

## 7. What is new, and what remains open

PR441 gives a good quadratic direction; it does not prove
that a square direction is good. Section 1 supplies that
strictly stronger existence statement by an argument that
works in every degree with a two-dimensional target.

PR442 gives a length-two pencil and a formal square-root
transport. Sections 2--6 choose a good square, exploit its
rank-one algebra to replace that transport by an explicit
algebraic curve through the identity, and preserve the exact
original fiber. The degree-two pullback has global primary
length at most four and endpoint length at most 60, while
the etale local length and exponent bounds at the original
parameter remain at most 30 and two, respectively.

The larger global bound 60 is not an improvement over PR442's
bound 30 on the t-line. It records the two-sheeted algebraic
parameterization needed here and controls the polynomial
family before the singular parameter is removed.

Generic vanishing, bounded torsion length, and algebraicity
of this coefficient family do not imply Q_0=0. The elementary
module C[s]/(s) already has zero generic fiber and a nonzero
fiber at zero. No flatness near zero is established for Q_s.
Nor does a zero of f(tau(s)) imply an actual nonzero endpoint.

No selected actual HM/source-instance evaluation, numerical
cohomology multiplication matrix, random sample, checker, or
large computation is used. In particular the original fixed
Q_0, fixed map ranks, actual high-order source membership,
the six pure conditions, module lifting, and algebraicity of
a formal quantization/module remain unresolved. A nonzero
ideal-cohomology endpoint would not itself prove nonzero
unrestricted image or nonzero actual source class.
