# The determinantal thickening of the scalar annihilator

Work over C, with S=Sym(T), dim T=5, H as in PR482, and I=Ann_S(H). Put D=Fitt_0^S(H), the ideal of maximal (two-by-two) minors in a two-generator presentation of H. This is distinct from I. Write W=W′ for the two-dimensional generator representation and E=A* for the five-dimensional factor of the quadratic relation space.

The pinned inputs are: H has two degree-zero generators, no linear relations, quadratic relation space L_2=E⊗W, and optional cubic relations only; S_2=E⊗Sym²W and the inclusion L_2→S_2⊗W is the canonical lower Clebsch–Gordan inclusion, up to nonzero scalar. Further, I is generated by I_3=A⊗W′ and fifteen quartics V⊗U, with

T I_3=V⊗(Qspin⊕T_4),  I_4=V⊗(Qspin⊕T_4⊕U).

The representation labels Qspin (spin four-dimensional) and T_4 (ordinary four-dimensional) are different. The scalar quotient R=S/I has Hilbert function (1,5,15,25,15,3) and is level of degree five, type three.

## 1. Two universal determinant facts

Let L=ker(S⊗W→H). The ideal generated by the entries of L is m², where m=S_+: the quadratic relation entries span all of S_2, and every relation has degree at least two. This can also be read from Λ²_S H=S/m².

Every two-by-two determinant of elements of L annihilates both generators of H, by the two-column adjugate identity. Thus D⊂I. Conversely, if a∈I then ae_1 and ae_2 belong to L. For any relation be_1+ce_2, determinants with these two elements give ac and −ab. Since all relation entries generate m², this proves

I m² ⊂ D ⊂ I.

Here arbitrary elements of L may be used: their determinants are polynomial combinations of minors of any generating presentation. Neither assertion assumes that a cubic annihilator is a minimal relation of H.

## 2. A rank-one exclusion for quadratic arrays

A five-by-three matrix of homogeneous quadrics whose fifteen entries span S_2 cannot have rank at most one over the fraction field K of S.

Indeed, a nonzero rank-one matrix over the polynomial UFD factors as a column f times a row g with polynomial entries, with both factors homogeneous. To see the polynomial assertion, divide a nonzero row by the gcd of its entries to obtain a primitive row g. Every other row is a K-multiple of g; primitivity forces that multiplier to be polynomial, because a prime in its denominator would have to divide every entry of g. The gcd of all fifteen entries is one, since they span S_2. Thus the homogeneous factor degrees are d and 2−d with d∈{0,1,2}. If d=0 the entries span at most three quadrics; if d=2 they span at most five. If d=1, the three linear entries of g have a common nonzero zero in the five-dimensional affine space. All fifteen products vanish there, whereas the full space S_2 does not. Each case is impossible.

## 3. Both quartic determinant channels survive

Use a basis x,y of W. For each a∈E, denote by α_a,β_a,γ_a the quadrics corresponding respectively to a⊗x²,a⊗xy,a⊗y² under S_2=E⊗Sym²W. The two universal quadratic relation columns are

r_a(x)=−β_a e_1+α_a e_2,  r_a(y)=−γ_a e_1+β_a e_2.

Equivalently, these are the columns of the traceless matrix M_a with rows (−β_a,−γ_a) and (α_a,β_a). The fifteen coefficients for a basis of E form a basis of S_2. Section 2 therefore excludes rank one of their five-by-three coefficient array over K.

The determinant map on quadratic relations has source

Λ²(E⊗W) = (Λ²E⊗Sym²W) ⊕ (Sym²E⊗Λ²W).

It is nonzero on each of these two summands. If it vanished on the second, det M_a=0 for every a, including every linear combination. The K-span of the M_a would be a totally isotropic subspace for the nondegenerate determinant quadratic form on the three-dimensional space of traceless matrices. Such a subspace has dimension at most one: polarization gives U⊂U^perp, hence 2 dim U≤3. This contradicts section 2.

If it vanished on the first summand, det(M_a w,M_b w)=0 for every a,b and w∈W_K. Here a and b may range over E_K. If some M_a in this K-span is invertible, M_a^{-1}M_b preserves every line in K² and hence is scalar, so all M_b are proportional. If no M_a is invertible, the previous totally-isotropic argument applies. In either case the coefficient array has rank at most one, again impossible. This proves nonvanishing on both summands without evaluating any selected HM coefficients.

The normalizer identities are

Λ²E=V⊗W′,  Sym²E=V⊗U,
W′⊗Sym²W′=W′⊕Qspin,
S_4=V⊗(T_4⊕Qspin⊕U⊕U′).

The first two follow by applying the Galois twist and duality to the usual identities Λ²V=A⊗W and Sym²V=A⊗U′; the twist exchanges W,W′ and U,U′. Thus the first determinant summand is V⊗(W′⊕Qspin), and the second is the irreducible V⊗U. The target has no V⊗W′. Irreducibility and the two nonvanishing statements consequently give exactly

D_4 = V⊗(Qspin⊕U),  dim D_4=35.

There are no elements of D below degree four, and cubic relation columns cannot contribute in degree four. In particular the V⊗T_4 part of I_4 is not determinantal in degree four.

## 4. Equality in every degree at least five

Since I has generators only in degrees three and four,

I_5=T I_4=T² I_3+T(V⊗U).

The first summand lies in D by I m²⊂D. The second lies in D because V⊗U⊂D_4. Hence I_5⊂D_5; the reverse inclusion was proved in section 1. All higher components of I are generated by I_5, so

D_d=I_d for every d≥5.

This does not claim that D is generated by D_4 alone. Its generators have degrees four and possibly five; no generator degree above five is needed. No remaining parameter r,s,q,u,v is selected by this argument.

## 5. The exact nilpotent excess and socle

Put Z=I/D and A_det=S/D. The preceding results give

Z_3=A⊗W′,  Z_4=V⊗T_4,  Z_d=0 otherwise.

The multiplication T⊗Z_3→Z_4 is onto, because T I_3=V⊗(Qspin⊕T_4) and its Qspin summand is precisely the part removed by D_4. Thus Z is generated in degree three and m²Z=0. It has no degree-three socle: the common kernel of multiplication by T is a normalizer-stable subspace of the irreducible Z_3, and cannot be all of Z_3 because multiplication is onto a nonzero target. Hence Soc(Z)=Z_4.

The exact sequence 0→Z→A_det→R→0 is a square-zero extension, since I²⊂D by degree (I starts in degree three and D agrees with I from degree five on). Its Hilbert function is

HF(A_det)=(1,5,15,35,35,3),  length(A_det)=94.

If an element of degree below five is in Soc(A_det), its image in the level algebra R is zero, so it lies in Z. Therefore the entire socle is

Soc(A_det)_4=V⊗T_4 (dimension20),  Soc(A_det)_5=R_5 (dimension3),

with no other degrees. In particular this determinantal algebra has type23 and is not level. Its final free-resolution term over S is 20S(−9)⊕3S(−10). The extension does not split as graded C-algebras: any section R→A_det must be the identity on degree-one generators (the map on that degree is an isomorphism), which would force the nonzero cubic space I_3 to vanish in A_det, contrary to D_3=0.

This identifies the precise, thirty-dimensional discrepancy between the scalar annihilator and the presentation-minor ideal, rather than conflating them. It is independent of the unresolved cubic and middle Betti choices. It does not evaluate the original fixed pair or a source class, or resolve higher-order lifting or algebraicity.

## 6. The remaining quintic generator count

There are exactly thirty-five minimal quartic generators of D, and only degree five can contain further generators. Let J=(D_4) and let c=dim(D_5/J_5). Since dim(S/J)_4=70−35=35=binom(7,4), Macaulay's Hilbert-function growth theorem gives dim(S/J)_5≤binom(8,5)=56. Because D_5=I_5 and dim(S/I)_5=3, this yields

c=dim(S/J)_5−3=123−dim(T D_4)≤53.

Let Γ be the finite Heisenberg group. The invariant part I_5^Γ is the irreducible quotient-normalizer representation U. As proved in PR489, the first derivatives of these invariant quintics span V⊗U. This lies in D_4, so Euler's homogeneous identity puts I_5^Γ inside T D_4=J_5. Taking Γ-invariants is exact over C; consequently (D_5/J_5)^Γ=0. In degree five the center acts trivially, while the quotient normalizer acts transitively on the twenty-four nontrivial characters of Γ/Z(Γ). Their multiplicities in D_5/J_5 are therefore equal. Thus c is a multiple of twenty-four, and the bound above gives exactly the necessary possibilities

c∈{0,24,48}.

If the cubic relation parameter r of H is zero, H has a presentation entirely by quadratic columns; all maximal minors are then quartic, so c=0. No converse and no choice of c for r=15 are asserted.
