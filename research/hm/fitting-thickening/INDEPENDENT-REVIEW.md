# Independent mathematical review of the determinantal thickening

The proof was independently checked conditional on the pinned quadratic presentation, scalar ideal generators and levelness inputs. The following points were verified separately.

## Universal determinant inclusions

For a two-generator module, the adjugate identity shows that each maximal presentation minor annihilates both generators. Conversely, an annihilator a supplies the relations ae_1 and ae_2. Taking their determinants with a relation be_1+ce_2 gives ac and -ab. Thus Ann(H) times the entry ideal lies in Fitt_0(H). The quadratic relation entries span S_2 by the pinned Clebsch–Gordan inclusion, so the entry ideal is exactly m². This proves I m² ⊂ D ⊂ I without assuming that the annihilator cubics are minimal module relations.

## Rank-one exclusion and both nonzero channels

The rank-one factorization over the polynomial UFD is valid. A primitive row has no common irreducible divisor; therefore every rational multiplier giving another polynomial row must be polynomial. Homogeneity gives factor degrees d and 2-d. When d is zero or two, the span of entries has dimension at most three or five. When d=1, the three linear forms in the row factor have a common nonzero zero in five variables, whereas a basis of S_2 has none. This excludes rank one for the coefficient array without computing any actual coefficients or ranks.

For the symmetric E-factor of the determinant source, vanishing implies that every element of the K-span of the traceless matrices M_a is singular. Polarization of the nondegenerate three-dimensional determinant quadratic form makes that span totally isotropic. Since U is contained in its orthogonal complement, its dimension is at most one. For the alternating E-factor, vanishing says det(M_a w,M_b w)=0 for every w. If an invertible matrix exists in the K-span, its inverse reduces the condition to an endomorphism preserving every line, hence a scalar. If none exists, the previous isotropic argument applies. Thus either channel vanishing contradicts the rank-one exclusion. All these are universal polynomial identities, not selected HM evaluations.

## Representation and degree audit

The representation identities follow from HM Lemma 3.3(ii),(v), followed by the stated Galois twist and duality: Λ²A*=V tensor W′ and Sym²A*=V tensor U. Therefore Λ²(E tensor W′) has constituents V tensor (W′ plus Qspin plus U). The target S_4 has no V tensor W′, and has one copy each of V tensor Qspin and V tensor U. Both remaining channels have now been proved nonzero, so D_4 is exactly their sum of dimension35. Optional cubic relation columns cannot enter degree four. The ordinary representation T_4 is not the spin representation Qspin, and is absent from D_4.

Because I is generated by its cubic space and V tensor U in degree four, I_5=T²I_3+T(V tensor U). The first term is in I m² and the second in T D_4. Hence D_5=I_5, and multiplication gives equality in every higher degree. This does not assert that quartic minors alone generate D.

## Quotient, socle and nonsplitting

The resulting quotient I/D has dimensions10 and20 in degrees three and four. Multiplication from degree three onto degree four follows from the previously proved decomposition of T I_3. Its common linear annihilator in degree three is zero by irreducibility and the nonzero multiplication. Thus its socle is precisely its degree-four term. In S/D every socle element below degree five maps to zero in the level algebra S/I, so it belongs to I/D. This proves socle dimensions20 in degree four and3 in degree five, and the final resolution term 20S(-9) plus 3S(-10).

The extension is square-zero because I² begins in degree six, where D agrees with I. A graded C-algebra section would be forced to be the identity on degree-one generators, but would then kill I_3 in S/D, contradicting D_3=0. Hence the claimed graded nonsplitting is valid.

The only invariant part of D_5=I_5 is U; its first derivatives lie in V tensor U inside D_4, so Euler's identity places it in T D_4. Thus the quotient has no Heisenberg-invariant part.
