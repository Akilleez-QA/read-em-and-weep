# Independent mathematical review

I independently checked the higher symmetric-power derivation against the pinned quadratic presentation and scalar-endomorphism rigidity inputs. The conclusions are conditional on those inputs, rather than a fresh verification of their entire dependency chain. No formal proof-assistant checking or selected HM/source-instance evaluation was performed.

The polynomial grading and symmetric-power grading are separate. The symmetric powers here are ordinary symmetric powers of an S-module, not super-symmetric powers. Their generation in polynomial degree zero makes degree greater than five vanish: all polynomial coefficients in a tensor can be moved onto one H factor.

In polynomial degree two, the relation map has coefficient tuple (ya, yb−xa, −xb) in the first binary quadratic factor. If it vanishes, injectivity of multiplication by x and y forces a=b=0. Its image is the kernel of ordinary binary multiplication by the dimension comparison 3(p+1)−2p=p+3. This proves the degree-two dimension and the actual multiplication model, not merely a numerical coincidence.

I checked both directions of the higher-degree functional correspondence. Fixing p−2 generators converts a balanced symmetric p-linear functional of total degree n into a self-adjoint endomorphism of degree 5−n. For n=3,4,5 the pinned endomorphism rigidity makes it a unique scalar. Conversely, the compatibility q(...,a)b=q(...,b)a ensures that every relation gives a scalar c annihilating both generators. Since R=S/Ann(H), c=0 in R. Thus q extends through H in each argument, and the perfect pairing constructs the desired balanced functional. Symmetry follows from the same compatibility and the symmetry of the pairing. This relation check is necessary; generation and a dimension count alone would not justify the converse.

The degree-four and degree-five exclusions use only the absence of degree-zero and degree-one relations. For degree three, put m=p−2 and let q_k be the binary quadratic associated to inputs with k copies of y. Compatibility is yq_k=xq_(k+1). Coprimality in C[x,y] forces q_k=x^(m−k)y^k c, with c of degree 2−m. Hence the solution dimensions per A* coefficient are two for m=1, one for m=2, and zero for m≥3. This independently confirms the cubic dimensions ten, five, and zero. The full Hilbert functions follow as stated.

The exact annihilator assertion for p≥5 is also valid. Support gives inclusion of the entire cube of the maximal ideal. No quadratic scalar can annihilate all generators, since its nonzero A* coefficient multiplies a nonzero binary form to a nonzero polynomial in the proved degree-two multiplication model. The first two degrees have no relations, and the annihilator is homogeneous. Thus no additional lower-degree element is possible.

The same checks establish the sharp maximal-ideal nilpotence exponents: generation in degree zero and the nonzero last component imply that the preceding power of the maximal ideal does not annihilate the module. None of these conclusions selects H's remaining Betti parameters or determines the original fixed-pair source class, higher-order lifting, or algebraicity.

I also checked the final exact-annihilator addition for the third and fourth powers. The pinned scalar quotient R_3=A⊗J_5 is irreducible. The scalar action on each symmetric power is normalizer-equivariant and nonzero in degree three, because that component is nonzero and generated from degree zero. Its kernel in R_3 is therefore zero. Since I_3 annihilates H and hence all its symmetric powers, this proves the exact degree-three annihilator I_3. Combined with the low-degree injectivity and degree-four support cutoff, the asserted annihilator I_3+m⁴ follows. This is an invariant-kernel argument, not an assertion that every individual map is equivariant.
