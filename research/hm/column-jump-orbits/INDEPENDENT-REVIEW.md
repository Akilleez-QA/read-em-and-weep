## Checks

1. **Koszul reduction.** In internal degree four the only relevant terms are A_0 tensor exterior^4(T), A_1 tensor exterior^3(T), and A_2 tensor exterior^2(T). Their dimensions are 5, 50, and 50. The first differential is the exterior coproduct and is injective in characteristic zero: exterior multiplication composed with it is multiplication by four, up to a harmless sign convention. Its constant image can therefore be quotiented before taking fibers. The resulting kernel has dimension beta_{3,4}, with no missing adjacent boundary.

2. **Heisenberg factor and normalizer.** The common central character is the fourth power of that on T and is nontrivial. Semisimplicity and the unique five-dimensional irreducible for this character give multiplicity bundles 9O and 10O(2). The proof does not require an unjustified normalizer-equivariant splitting into ordinary multiplicity representations. Normalizer transport preserves the irreducible isomorphism class and induces invertible changes of reduced source and target, possibly after scalar choices of intertwiners. Hence the ideal of maximal generic-rank minors, including its multiplicities and not just its support, is invariant. The projective A5 action is used as a pinned input, not inferred from the dimension of W alone.

3. **Determinantal degree.** A nonzero generic-rank r minor is a section of O(2r). The local ideal generated by all such minors on P1 is invertible and gives an effective divisor of degree at most 2r. The input u in {0,5,10,15,20} gives 5 <= r <= 9, so the degree is at most eighteen. This argument remains valid when r is smaller than either matrix dimension.

4. **Orbit restriction.** The proof of cyclic finite point stabilizers in characteristic zero is valid. The possible A5 stabilizer orders give orbit sizes 60, 30, 20, and 12. The six order-five subgroups have disjoint pairs of fixed points. Their dihedral normalizers interchange the pairs, so these twelve points form the unique size-twelve orbit. Consequently every nonzero invariant effective divisor of degree at most eighteen is exactly that reduced orbit.

5. **Jump size.** Smith normal form at a point makes the valuation of the r-minor ideal the sum of the r nonzero invariant-factor valuations. Divisor coefficient one therefore forces precisely one positive valuation, equal to one. Fiber corank rises by one, so u rises by five. For r=5, the stronger degree bound ten excludes any jump. No conclusion choosing the generic value of u is being smuggled into this argument.

6. **Equality-case saturation.** With J the image sheaf and I its saturation, both are vector bundles, J is globally generated, and length(I/J) is the determinantal degree. The splitting summands of J have nonnegative degree; those of I have degree at most two because I embeds into 10O(2). If r=6 and jumps occur, deg(I/J)=12 forces J=6O and I=6O(2). The outer maps in the displayed factorization are constant of full rank. The middle determinant cuts out the twelve points simply. This is a necessary factorization, not a contradiction. The proof correctly explains why a constant rank-three kernel sheaf does not imply constant fiber kernel dimension at torsion-cokernel points.
