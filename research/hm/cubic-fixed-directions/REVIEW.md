## Verdict

The theorem is valid under its stated actual-primary/modular identification and equivariance assumptions. In particular, the fixed-direction argument really excludes r=2; it does not merely prove a conditional smoothness statement for the restricted surface. If r=1, all sixty ambient bad values have both reduced normalization preimages on Y, so the ambient and surface singular/nonnormal loci coincide as the same sixty-point reduced subset. The value r=0 versus r=1 remains undecided.

The review compared the proof with the local texts of no-node-trisecants, cubic-embedding-locus, cubic-exceptional-fibers, and cubic-surface-immersion. It also checked the locally available text of Barth–Hulek–Moore, Sections 4.2–4.5, particularly printed pp.57–58 and Propositions 9–10 on pp.63–64. Those passages give the extension of translations to S(5), the section/component incidences, the equivariant embedded pentagons, their paired vertices, and transverse surface branches. The review does not independently re-establish the actual-primary/modular dictionary or the older cubic-map construction.

## Checked arguments

1. **The component stabilizer is the required subgroup.** The action of U=(F5)^2 on a five-cycle has image in its cyclic rotation subgroup: a group of exponent five has no nontrivial image in the order-two quotient of the dihedral group. Transitivity makes the image have order five, so its kernel G has order five. It fixes every component, and hence every vertex, of that pentagon. This is a subgroup of the projective Heisenberg translations, so a generator lifts to a noncentral Heisenberg element. No arbitrary order-five subgroup of the larger normalizer is substituted.

2. **The opposite branch is genuinely covered.** Fixing an ambient node preserves its two-point normalization fiber. An order-five group acts trivially on this fiber. Independently, the four incident pentagon components form an invariant set: the predecessor's complete line classification and node avoidance of the distinguished section lines ensure that there are exactly these four lines through the node. An order-five group cannot permute a four-element set nontrivially. The two directions on each smooth branch are distinct, and transversality makes directions on different branches distinct as well. All four marked exceptional points are therefore fixed, including the two belonging to the opposite normalization branch. No unsupported agreement of local multipliers on paired pentagons is needed.

3. **The exceptional fixed-point list is exhaustive.** The nondegenerate Heisenberg commutator supplies a conjugation multiplying a noncentral generator by a central element of primitive fifth-root character. Its spectrum is invariant, with multiplicities, under multiplication by that root. Finite order gives diagonalizability. Dimension five then forces precisely five distinct one-dimensional eigenspaces. At a fixed projective eigenline L_z, the tangent representation Hom(L_z,A/L_z) has four distinct eigenvalues, obtained by taking ratios. Consequently its projectivization E_z has precisely four fixed points. The four already identified marked directions exhaust them. Rescaling a lift affects none of these projective conclusions.

4. **The optional-intersection exclusion is valid.** Equivariance preserves Sigma_ell, its exceptional lift A_z, and each of R_z^+ and R_z^-. If A_z meets R_z^- in its possible single point, that point must be G-fixed. The fixed-point list makes it marked. The predecessor's disjointness of different bad lines excludes either marked point on R_z^-, while disjointness of R_z^+ and R_z^- excludes the other two. This proves the conditional exclusion in Section 4. The ensuing conditional smoothness statement follows from the predecessor's complete multiple-fiber classification and finite unramified singleton-fiber criterion.

5. **The endpoint argument decisively excludes r=2.** The point p_z is fixed, so alpha=Phi(p_z) is fixed. In the r=2 alternative alpha belongs to Sigma_ell and thus has a unique exceptional preimage e_w in E_w. Because E_w is invariant and its restriction is an embedding, this preimage is fixed by G. It is therefore marked, but cannot be p_w because alpha and beta are distinct on the embedded component curve. The remaining marked points belong to components ell' different from ell. For r=2 the image of each such component is its own bad line Sigma_ell'. Thus alpha lies in two distinct bad lines, contradicting their pairwise disjointness. This argument needs neither a formula for e_w nor any choice of a numerical direction.

6. **The r=1 membership argument is complete, including scheme fibers.** The singleton Sigma_ell is fixed by G. Each of its unique exceptional preimages is fixed and hence marked, so both lie on Y. They are distinct because the exceptional divisors at distinct nodes are disjoint. The ambient fiber is already proved to be the disjoint union of two reduced points. Its scheme-theoretic intersection with Y contains both points and therefore is that same reduced fiber; restricting the map introduces no hidden nilpotents. The two marked directions of ell itself cannot be these points, because Sigma_ell is disjoint from Phi(C_ell) in this case.

7. **Counting and smoothness conclusions follow without genericity.** There are sixty pairwise disjoint bad ambient values for r=1, and each now gives a two-point surface normalization fiber. The predecessor's everywhere-unramified finite normalization criterion identifies the surface singular and nonnormal loci with its multiple-fiber values and excludes any further singleton-fiber singularities. The same predecessor results identify the ambient singular and nonnormal loci. For r=0 the ambient map, and hence its restriction, is a closed immersion. With r=2 excluded, this proves all the asserted equivalences and the equality of the two sixty-point reduced loci in the remaining nonsmooth alternative.

## Independent second check of the r=2 contradiction

There is also an invariant-line verification. Under the simple-spectrum tangent representation, any invariant two-dimensional vector subspace is a sum of two eigenlines. Thus the invariant exceptional projective line A_z must contain two of the four marked fixed points. It already contains p_z. It cannot contain the other marked point of R_z^+, since that would force A_z=R_z^+, excluded in cubic-surface-immersion. Its second fixed point must therefore lie on R_z^-, contradicting Section 4's exclusion. This independently confirms that the conditional smoothness case cannot actually occur. The endpoint proof in the reviewed text is shorter and does not depend on Section 4.
