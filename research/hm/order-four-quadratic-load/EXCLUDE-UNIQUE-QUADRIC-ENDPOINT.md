# The endpoint a=1, b=6 is impossible

## Result

For a general proper hyperplane section Z of the 25 HM lines, the endpoint a=h^0(I_Z(2))=1, b=h^0(I_Z(3))=6 cannot occur. Thus the surviving generic possibilities are

    a=0, 0<=b<=5; or a=1, 4<=b<=5.

In particular b<=5 in every case. This does not eliminate a=1, establish residual trace transversality, or settle Q4.

## Inputs

Write E=F(3) and U=H^0(E), dim U=4. Use the actual evaluation U tensor O -> E, whose six exterior minors generate the reduced ideal sheaf of the smooth union C of 25 disjoint lines. Also use I_C(4)=0, stability of F, H^0(F(t))=0 for t<=2, and H^1(F(-2))=0. The latter follows directly from the HM monad: at twist -2 the only cohomology of its middle term 2Omega^2 is in degree two, and the two line-bundle terms are acyclic.

The endpoint geometry already proved supplies, for each general H, an integral degree-five curve D_H spanning H, containing Z=C intersect H, and a two-dimensional subspace K_H of U whose sections vanish identically on D_H. This K_H is the constant kernel of evaluation along D_H minus Z. It is intrinsic and varies rationally with H. No genus assertion is needed here.

## 1. The map to section pencils has image dimension at most three

Evaluation has rank exactly one at every point p of C. It cannot have rank zero: at a rank-zero point all two-by-two minors would belong to the square of the maximal ideal, whereas the reduced smooth line ideal has a nonzero linear conormal space and is generated by these minors. Rank two is excluded on C by definition.

Consider the projective incidence

    J={(p,K): p in C, K in Gr(2,U), K contained in ker(ev_p)}.

Its fibers over C are Gr(2,3), of dimension two; hence dim J=3. Since D_H contains Z, its section pencil K_H belongs to the image of J. Therefore the rational map

    H |-> K_H,  from the four-dimensional dual projective space

has image dimension at most three. Its general fiber is positive-dimensional.

## 2. A positive-dimensional fiber produces a fixed surface

Fix a general image point K and an irreducible positive-dimensional component T of its fiber, inside the open where the endpoint construction is defined. Let W_K be the common zero scheme of the two independent sections spanning K. Each D_H for H in T lies in W_K.

The zero scheme of any nonzero section of E has no divisorial component, because a divisorial factor of degree d>0 would give H^0(E(-d))=H^0(F(3-d)) nonzero. Its ideal is locally generated by two functions of height two, so it is a pure two-dimensional local complete intersection. In particular W_K has dimension at most two and finitely many irreducible components.

A curve component of W_K can contain at most one of the curves D_H with distinct H: integrality forces equality with that component, and D_H spans the unique hyperplane H. Consequently finitely many curve components cannot account for a dense subset of T. One integral surface component S of W_K contains D_H for a dense subset of T. Formally, the finitely many component-containment conditions are constructible in this algebraic family; one has dense closure, and restricting T to its dense constructible open gives the stated fixed component.

A general H in this family does not contain S. Otherwise the positive-dimensional family would consist of hyperplanes containing a surface; a surface in P4 spans at least a plane, and if it spans only a plane then D_H would be planar, impossible, while a span of dimension at least three is contained in at most one hyperplane. Thus S intersect H is a proper degree-deg(S) curve containing D_H, and deg(S)>=5.

## 3. Heisenberg invariance forces S to contain all 25 lines

The projective Heisenberg group acts trivially on U. Indeed the center must act trivially on a four-dimensional representation, since nontrivial central characters have irreducible dimension five. After killing the center, a nontrivial character of the Heisenberg quotient would bring its full 24-element normalizer orbit into U, which is impossible in dimension four. Hence all global sections of E are Heisenberg-invariant.

The zero scheme of any nonzero s in K is therefore Heisenberg-invariant and has degree c2(E)=10. The orbit of its surface component S under the projective Heisenberg group has size 1,5,or25. Since every orbit component has degree at least five, the orbit must have size one. Thus S is invariant.

At least one component line of C lies in S. Otherwise each intersection S intersect L_i is finite. On the irreducible parameter family T, the section point p_i(H)=H intersect L_i lies in that finite set for every i, so it is constant. All 25 section points would be fixed. Since they span H, this forces H to be constant, contradicting dim T>0. The projective Heisenberg group acts transitively on the 25 lines, so invariance of S implies C is contained in S.

## 4. The degree of S is five or ten

Its degree lies between five and ten. An invariant integral surface in this representation has degree divisible by five. For clarity, its Hilbert polynomial P(m) satisfies P(m)=h^0(O_S(m)) for large m. When m is not divisible by five, the center has nontrivial weight on O_S(m), so that representation has dimension divisible by five. The polynomial 2P(m) has integral coefficients and degree two. Modulo five it vanishes at all four nonzero residue classes. Hence it is the zero polynomial modulo five, and its leading coefficient deg(S) is divisible by five.

Thus deg(S)=5 or 10.

## 5. Both possible degrees contradict established geometry

If deg(S)=10, choose nonzero s in K. Its zero scheme has total degree ten and contains the integral component S of degree ten. Purity and the degree count force multiplicity one on S and no other components. A generically reduced Cohen–Macaulay scheme has no remaining nilpotent structure supported in smaller dimension, so the zero scheme equals S scheme-theoretically.

The section Koszul sequence, tensored with E, is

    0 -> E(-5) -> End(E) -> I_S tensor E -> 0.

Stability gives H^0(End(E))=C, and H^1(E(-5))=H^1(F(-2))=0. Therefore h^0(I_S tensor E)=1. But both independent sections in K vanish on S, a contradiction.

If deg(S)=5, take a general integral hyperplane-section curve Gamma of S. Such a section is integral by Bertini; the general hyperplane also avoids the isolated non-Cohen–Macaulay locus of the integral surface. For every i>=1, a line bundle of degree 5i on an integral curve has at most 5i+1 sections (for instance, pull back to its normalization). The restriction sequences and h^0(O_S)=1 give

    h^0(O_S(4)) <= 1+6+11+16+21=55.

Since h^0(O_P4(4))=70, there is a nonzero quartic vanishing on S. Because C is contained in S, this contradicts I_C(4)=0.

Both alternatives are impossible. Hence the endpoint a=1,b=6 never occurs generically.

## Boundary

This exclusion uses the actual evaluation geometry, Heisenberg action, degree-ten section zero schemes, and the independently established quartic-ideal vanishing. It is not a selected coefficient or rank evaluation. The remaining a=1 branch is not excluded; when a=0, the now numerically allowable cubic trace systems still need actual intersection control. No original source subspace is identified and no full Q4 claim is made.
