# Primary-bad quadratics have codimension at least three

## Statement and established inputs

Let A0=M0=V3, X=P(A0), and M2=V tensor W'. Let S2=A0 tensor sl(W') (dimension 15) be the quadratic parameter representation. For x in X, write

    e_x : S2 -> M2,   q |-> qx.

The already established exterior-plus-sl2 multiplication model supplies the actual dual bundle map

    beta : M2* tensor O_X -> sl(W')* tensor Omega_X^1(2).

Its fiber rank equals rank(e_x). We use the established pointwise bound rank(e_x)>=5 and generic bound rank(e_x)>=7. The former comes from the Galois-conjugated HM Lemma 2.5; the latter follows from the prior rank exclusions. Neither is inferred from dimension or generation alone.

Then the closed primary-bad locus

    B = {[q] in P(S2) : q:M0 -> M2 is not injective}

has dimension at most 11. In particular, its codimension in P(S2)=P14 is at least three.

## 1. The actual linear matrix, without evaluating it

The equivariant Euler sequence on X is

    0 -> Omega_X^1(2) -> A0* tensor O_X(1) -> O_X(2) -> 0.

Compose beta with the first inclusion, tensored with sl(W')*. This gives

    b : M2* tensor O_X -> (sl(W')* tensor A0*) tensor O_X(1).

Thus b is a 15-by-10 matrix of linear forms, intrinsically an equivariant bundle map. Euler is an inclusion of vector bundles, hence b and beta have the same rank at every point. No entry or minor is evaluated in this argument.

For t=6,7, let J_t be the homogeneous ideal generated by all t-minors of b in R=Sym(A0*). The degree-t span of these minors is N-stable: this follows directly by taking the t-th exterior power of the equivariant map. Since the generic rank is at least seven, J_6 and J_7 are nonzero.

## 2. No divisorial common factor

For any nonzero N-stable collection of homogeneous polynomials of degree t, let g be the homogeneous gcd of its nonzero members. Unique factorization shows that n(g) is a nonzero scalar multiple of g for every n in N: applying n preserves the ideal and therefore its common divisor with multiplicities. Consequently the line Cg is an N-character. Since N is perfect, that character is trivial. In particular g is an N-invariant polynomial, and deg(g)<=t.

There are no N-invariant polynomials on A0 of positive degree at most seven. Indeed, A0 has central weight 3 modulo 5, so Sym^d(A0*) has central weight -3d. A necessary condition for an invariant is 5 dividing d. The only remaining degree in this range is five. HM Theorem 3.5, or Lemma 3.3(viii), gives

    (Sym^5(A0*))^H = U direct-sum U',

by Galois conjugation of the original five-dimensional representation. Conjugation can interchange U and U' but preserves their sum. Neither irreducible three-dimensional summand has an N/H-invariant vector. Thus degree five supplies no N-invariant either.

Apply this to the minors for t=6 and t=7. Their gcd is constant. A codimension-one irreducible component of their common zero locus in P4 would have an irreducible homogeneous defining polynomial dividing every minor, contradicting the constant gcd. Therefore

    dim {x : rank(e_x)<=5} <= 2,
    dim {x : rank(e_x)<=6} <= 2.

The empty-locus cases are included. The conclusion concerns the absence of divisorial components, not expected codimension, smoothness, reducedness, or a chosen minor.

## 3. Incidence dimension

Consider the closed incidence

    Z = {([x],[q]) in P(A0) x P(S2) : qx=0}.

Over a point of evaluation rank r, its fiber is P(ker e_x), of dimension 14-r. The pointwise rank bound excludes ranks below five. Stratify the base by rank:

- Rank five: base dimension at most 2, fiber dimension 9, total at most 11.
- Rank six: base dimension at most 2, fiber dimension 8, total at most 10.
- Rank at least seven: base dimension at most 4, fiber dimension at most 7, total at most 11.

The standard fiber-dimension inequality on these finite locally closed strata gives dim Z<=11. Its projection to P(S2) is closed, since the other factor is projective, and its image is exactly B. Hence dim B<=11 and codim(B,P14)>=3.

## 4. Uniform nets and punctured planes

A general projective plane P2 in P14 is disjoint from B: the dimension sum is at most 2+11=13<14. Thus a general three-dimensional vector subspace of S2 is a net in which every nonzero quadratic induces an injective primary map M0 -> M2.

There is also a statement through every prescribed parameter. Fix any nonzero q0, without assuming whether [q0] belongs to B. Project from p=[q0] to P13. The closure Y of the image of B minus {p} has dimension at most 11. A general projective line in P13 misses Y, because 1+11<13. The projective plane through p determined by that line therefore meets B only possibly at p. Consequently, for every prescribed q0 there is a net containing it in which every parameter other than [q0] has injective primary multiplication.

This last assertion is an existence theorem about other quadratics in a plane, not a decision about q0 itself. It does not identify a deformation with the original source pair or transfer a full adjoint vanishing statement back to q0.

## Scope

This improves the previous codimension-at-least-two bound for failure of primary injectivity. It neither proves generic evaluation rank ten nor resolves the actual rank-seven alternative. It does not prove invertibility of the full adjoint quadratic map, residual surjectivity, or nondegeneracy of the 20-by-20 lift boundary. It makes no assertion about the original selected quadratic.
