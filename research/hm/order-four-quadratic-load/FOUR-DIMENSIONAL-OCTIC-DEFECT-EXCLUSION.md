# A four-dimensional octic defect is excluded by the quadratic congruence base

## Statement

Let D⊂P3 be an integral degree-eight curve, with its ideal sheaf generated by forms of degrees at most four. Let M be a line bundle of degree seven. Suppose six antisymmetric sections F_ij∈H0(M) span a four-dimensional space W and satisfy

    x_i F_jk−x_j F_ik+x_k F_ij=0.

Then this configuration is impossible.

The proof is a classification of universal pencils of alternating forms followed by a degree comparison of rank-one sheaves. It does not choose or evaluate any actual HM coefficient, matrix, or minor. In the active octic branch, the resolution 0→2O(-5)→2O(-3)⊕O(-4)→I_D→0 supplies precisely the needed quartic generation.

## 1. The coefficient space and its annihilator pencil

The four independent sections define an injective coefficient map W*→Λ²V, with image a four-dimensional subspace A. At the generic point x of D, its value c(x) lies in A and satisfies x∧c(x)=0. Thus c(x)=x∧y is decomposable and represents a line through x.

The rational image in P(A)=P3 spans that P3, because the four sections are independent. It lies on the Klein quadric. Let A^perp⊂Λ²V* be the two-dimensional annihilator, a pencil of alternating forms. The condition c=x∧y∈A is exactly

    ω(x,y)=η(x,y)=0,

for a basis ω,η of the pencil. We classify the pencil by its quadratic Pfaffian, equivalently the scalar coefficient of (sω+tη)∧(sω+tη).

All coordinate changes below are universal changes of basis in a four-dimensional vector space. They are not source-instance substitutions.

## 2. An identically singular pencil is impossible

If its Pfaffian is identically zero, every nonzero pencil member is a decomposable two-form and ω∧η=0. The two corresponding two-dimensional subspaces of V* intersect in a line, so choose a common factor α and write ω=α∧β, η=α∧γ with α,β,γ independent.

Complete these to a basis and take ω=e0*∧e1*, η=e0*∧e2*. The coefficient space is defined by c01=c02=0. The Klein equation restricts there to c03 c12=0. Hence its intersection with P(A) is the union of two planes.

An integral curve contained in this union must lie in one plane. This contradicts the spanning of P(A). Thus the Pfaffian is not identically zero.

## 3. Distinct roots: the base is two skew lines

If the Pfaffian has distinct roots, choose its two independent rank-two root forms ω,η. Their wedge is nonzero; otherwise every pencil member would have zero Pfaffian. Consequently their factor planes in V* are complementary, and a basis gives

    ω=e0*∧e1*,    η=e2*∧e3*.

For x=(a,b,c,d), outside the two lines a=b=0 and c=d=0, the unique line through x satisfying both alternating equations is spanned by (a,b,0,0) and (0,0,c,d). Its Plücker coordinates in A are, up to fixed signs,

    ac, ad, bc, bd.

Their base ideal is (a,b)(c,d)=(a,b)∩(c,d), the reduced union B of two skew lines. It is locally Cohen–Macaulay and has degree two.

The uniqueness follows directly: modulo x, a vector y satisfying the two equations is determined up to a scalar, since its first coordinate pair is proportional to (a,b) and its last pair proportional to (c,d).

## 4. A double root: the base is a locally CM double line

Suppose the Pfaffian has one double root. Choose its rank-two root form η and an independent nonsingular form ω. Then η∧η=0, ω∧η=0, and ω∧ω≠0.

Write η=α∧β and extend α,β to a basis. The condition ω∧η=0 says that ω has no term involving only the two complementary basis elements. Therefore it can be written

    ω=λ α∧β+α∧u+β∧v,

where u,v are linear combinations of the complementary basis elements, after absorbing terms in span{α,β} into λ. Nonsingularity of ω forces the classes of u,v modulo span{α,β} to be independent. Subtract λη from ω and use α,β,u,v as a basis. Thus every such pencil has normal form

    η=e0*∧e1*,    ω=e0*∧e2*+e1*∧e3*.

For x=(a,b,c,d), the vector y=(0,0,b,−a) satisfies η(x,y)=ω(x,y)=0. Outside a=b=0, it determines the unique allowable line through x modulo x. Its independent Plücker coordinates span the quadrics

    a², ab, b², ac+bd,

up to signs and permutation. Thus the base scheme B has ideal

    J=(a²,ab,b²,ac+bd).

Its support is L={a=b=0}. This scheme has no embedded points and is a degree-two locally Cohen–Macaulay curve. Indeed on the chart c≠0, eliminate a=−bd/c; the remaining equations reduce to b²=0. On d≠0 eliminate b and obtain a²=0. These charts cover L. Locally it is a square-zero double structure on a smooth line, hence pure and Cohen–Macaulay, with generic length two. In particular deg B=2. Equivalently its nilpotent ideal is the cokernel of O_L(-2)→2O_L(-1) given by (c,d), hence O_L.

This proves the double-root normal form directly; no unproved Jordan-form classification is needed.

## 5. The base-ideal injection on D

In both cases the universal line assignment is represented by four independent quadrics Q0,...,Q3 with base scheme B locally CM of degree two. The generic point of integral D is not on B, since B is supported on one or two lines and D has degree eight. There the compatible coefficient vector (the four sections spanning W) and the vector (Q0,...,Q3) represent the same unique line through x. Hence they differ by a common nonzero rational factor λ.

Let I_{B∩D,D} be the image of I_B in O_D. The quadrics generate I_B, so their restrictions generate I_{B∩D,D}⊗O_D(2). Sending Qi to its corresponding M-section constructs a map

    I_{B∩D,D}⊗O_D(2) → M.                      (1)

It is well-defined: any relation among the restricted Qi holds at the generic point and remains a relation among the λQi; its resulting section of torsion-free M is therefore zero everywhere. This is a sheaf-theoretic argument and makes no assertion that the rational assignment extends as a map at its base points.

Map (1) is injective, since it is nonzero generically and its source is torsion-free on integral D. Its cokernel is zero-dimensional. Consequently

    deg M ≥ 16−length(B∩D).                    (2)

This formula remains valid when B is the nonreduced double line or when D is singular.

## 6. Quartic generation forces the contradictory degree bound

Because I_D is generated in degrees at most four, its degree-four global sections have base scheme D. No component line of B is contained in D. Therefore one can choose a quartic h∈H0(I_D(4)) which vanishes identically on no component of B. With two components, avoid the union of the two proper linear subspaces of such quartics; with one component there is just one condition.

Since B is locally Cohen–Macaulay and pure, h is a nonzerodivisor on O_B. Its Cartier cut has length 4 deg B=8. The intersection B∩D is a closed subscheme of this cut, because h vanishes on D. Hence

    length(B∩D)≤8.

Combining with (2) yields deg M≥16−8=8, contradicting deg M=7. This excludes every four-dimensional spanning compatible defect.

## Consequence for the active octic

For the actual octic M=O_D(4)(−Z) has degree seven. Compatible fixed-point differential quartics therefore cannot span four dimensions. Spans at most two vanish by the small-defect theorem. A nonzero three-dimensional span forces the separately proved alpha alternative M≅O_D(1)(−p) for a unique smooth point p, with h0(M)=3.

Thus only zero compatible derivatives or that precise alpha-type projection configuration remain. In particular if h0(M)=4, all compatible fixed-point derivatives must vanish: a span three would force h0(M)=3, while spans one/two and four have been excluded. Differential descent then applies once generic tangency of the cubic-base carrier is known.

No conclusion excluding the remaining h0(M)=3 alpha case is asserted here. No external writes, selected HM/source evaluations, numerical ranks, scans, or experiments were used.
