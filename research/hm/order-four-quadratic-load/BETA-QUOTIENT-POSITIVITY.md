# Positivity of the beta quotient excludes degree-ten components off the rank-zero parameter curve

## Inputs and scope

Work over C. Use the setup of BETA-DEGENERACY-SURFACE.md at commit 26240e70230aef0de1f1b8714a06bd644adeec69 and BETA-IMAGE-DIMENSION-DROP.md at f5b50172bd65d1772d1af3d4a511decc8363183e in Akilleez-QA/read-em-and-weep PR543. In particular E has rank two and determinant O(5); U=H0(E) has dimension four; the evaluation minors generate the ideal of C, a disjoint union of 25 lines; and X_B is pure locally CM of dimension two and degree 15. The rank-one evaluation on C defines κ:C→P(U*). Its image is closed of dimension at most one.

Assume the residual beta family supplies an integral component S of X_B containing all of C and the moving integral sextics D_H. The established degree alternatives are deg S=10 or 15. The latter implies X_B=S reduced and integral. This note adds a new positivity exclusion, not a classification of all singular X_B.

## 1. Global quotient outside κ(C)

Fix B outside κ(C). At every point of X=X_B the map B⊗O→E has rank exactly one: outside C the kernel of U→E has dimension two, so cannot contain B; on C the rank-zero possibility is precisely B=κ(p).

Therefore on X there are vector bundles A,Q of rank one and K of rank two with exact sequences

0→K→B⊗O_X→A→0,
0→A→E|X→Q→0.

A is globally generated by B. Locally the rank-one determinantal matrix is [1 0 0; 0 a b], so X is the regular codimension-two embedding (a,b), though X need not be smooth. The remaining section in U/B cuts C scheme-theoretically on X, by generation of I_C by all six minors. Since X is CM and C has dimension one, this section of Q is a nonzerodivisor. Thus Q=O_X(C), and

A=O_X(5H−C).

The normal bundle is Hom(K,Q); its determinant is A⊗Q², up to a constant one-dimensional vector space. Adjunction gives ω_X=Q=O_X(C). These formulas require only the regular embedding, not smoothness of X.

X is smooth along C by the previously proved local argument: quotienting the two-dimensional CM local ring by the equation of C gives a regular one-dimensional ring, hence the original ring has embedding dimension two and is regular.

## 2. Intersection calculation on the integral component S

Since S contains C and X is smooth at every point of C, a neighborhood of C in S agrees with X. Each of the 25 disjoint lines L_i is Cartier in S and has the same self-intersection as in the smooth neighborhood in X. Adjunction there gives

−2 = (K_X+L_i)·L_i = 2 L_i²,

because K_X=C and the other lines are disjoint. Therefore L_i²=−1 and C² on S is −25. Also H·C=25 and H² on S is d=deg S. Restriction of the globally generated line bundle A to S is still globally generated, and

(A|S)² = (5H−C)² = 25d−250−25 = 25d−275.

This calculation remains valid if S is singular or nonnormal away from C: H and C are Cartier divisors on S, and C is contained in its smooth locus.

A globally generated line bundle on an integral projective surface has nonnegative self-intersection. Indeed its defining morphism has image dimension at most one, in which case the square is zero, or image dimension two, in which case the square is the positive degree of the generically finite map times the degree of the image. This argument does not need normality or smoothness.

For d=10, the displayed square is −25, a contradiction. Consequently:

**A sextic-containing degree-ten component can occur only when B belongs to κ(C).**

Equivalently, every such X_B with B outside κ(C) is reduced integral of degree 15.

## 3. Consequences for the residual beta parameter map

The beta image already has dimension at most two. If its general surface has a degree-ten sextic-containing component, its parameter image is contained in κ(C), hence has dimension at most one, and general fixed-B fibers have dimension at least three.

If the beta image has dimension two, its general point avoids κ(C). Its general X_B must then be integral of degree 15. It remains singular by the earlier smooth-surface exclusion, but is a global local complete intersection, with invertible dualizing sheaf O_X(C), and is smooth along all of C, not just the active marked points.

For such a degree-fifteen X_B, A²=100. The globally generated quotient defines a morphism

h_B:X_B→P(B*)=P².

Its positive square forces a two-dimensional image, hence all of P², and its generic degree is 100. This is a generically finite morphism; no finiteness assertion is made, since it could contract curves.

On each active residual sextic D, A|D is the degree-five beta image line bundle L, and the image spans P². Therefore h_B|D has degree one onto a plane quintic Γ: degree(L)=degree(h_B|D)·degree(Γ)=5 and Γ is not a line. Since a nonconstant morphism of integral projective curves is finite, the inclusion O_Γ→(h_B|D)_*O_D has a finite-length cokernel of length

p_a(Γ)−p_a(D)=6−3=3.

Thus these sextics are partial normalizations, of total defect exactly three, of plane quintics in the intrinsic degree-100 quotient. This statement does not assume D is smooth, normal, or Gorenstein.

## Boundary and reproducibility

The new exclusion is degree ten away from κ(C); it does not exclude integral singular degree-fifteen surfaces, or degree-ten cases over κ(C). No claim identifies this rank-zero curve with any different HM discriminant. No numerical HM matrices, coefficients, evaluations, scans, local-model spike, or one-shot experiment were used. The argument is symbolic and uses only the two pinned input proofs and elementary intersection theory for globally generated line bundles.
