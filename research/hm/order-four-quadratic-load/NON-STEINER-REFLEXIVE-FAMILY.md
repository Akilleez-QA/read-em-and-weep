# A non-Steiner family of stable reflexive rank-three sheaves on P^4

## Construction

Work over C and write h=c_1(O_{P^4}(1)). Choose a homogeneous regular sequence

l,q_1,q_2,q_3

of degrees (1,2,2,2). Let Z be its zero scheme in P^4. This is a zero-dimensional complete intersection of length 1·2·2·2=8. Define G and F by

0 → O(−2) → O(−1)⊕O^3 → G → 0,

where the first map has entries l,q_1,q_2,q_3, and set F=G^∨.

Then F is a slope-stable reflexive sheaf of rank three with

c_1(F)=−h,   c_2(F)=2h^2,   c_3(F)=−4h^3,   c_4(F)=−40h^4.

It is not a cokernel of a map 2O(−2)→5O(−1).

## Reflexivity and local projective dimension

The displayed map is injective because its source is a line bundle and it is nonzero at the generic point. Off Z one entry is a unit locally, so G is locally free of rank three.

At every point of Z the presentation is minimal over the regular local ring of dimension four. Hence G has projective dimension one and depth three. It is locally free elsewhere. It follows that G is torsion-free and satisfies Serre's S_2 condition, and therefore is reflexive on P^4. Its dual F is reflexive, and F^∨=G.

For completeness, dualizing the presentation gives

0 → F → O(1)⊕O^3 → O(2) → O_Z(2) → 0.

Thus F is locally free off Z. At a point of Z, its stalk is the second syzygy of the nonzero finite-length module O_Z(2). Since this module has projective dimension four, F has projective dimension two and depth two there. In particular, these sheaves are genuinely non-locally-free reflexive sheaves.

## Stability

For a rank-three reflexive sheaf F with c_1(F)=−h, the vanishings

H^0(F)=0,   H^0(F^∨(−1))=0

exclude every destabilizing subsheaf. Indeed, a saturated destabilizing rank-one subsheaf has first Chern class dh with d≥0 and gives a nonzero section of F(−d), hence of F after multiplication by a suitable nonzero degree-d polynomial. A destabilizing rank-two subsheaf likewise has determinant O(d), d≥0, and gives a nonzero map O(d)→(∧^2F)^{**}=F^∨(−1), contradicting the second vanishing after the same multiplication argument. These determinant maps extend across codimension-two subsets by reflexivity.

The second vanishing follows directly by twisting G's presentation by O(−1): neither O(−2)⊕O(−1)^3 nor H^1(O(−3)) contributes a section, so H^0(G(−1))=0.

For the first, the dual sequence identifies a section of F with a relation

lL+a_1q_1+a_2q_2+a_3q_3=0,

where L is linear and the a_i are constants. Modulo l, the quadrics q_i form a regular sequence. In particular, they are linearly independent over C: a nontrivial constant relation would make their ideal generated by at most two elements, contradicting its height three. Hence all a_i vanish. Then lL=0 in the polynomial ring, so L=0. This proves H^0(F)=0 and establishes slope stability.

## Chern classes, including the point-supported correction

G's presentation gives

c(G)=(1−h)/(1−2h)=1+h+2h^2+4h^3+8h^4.

It is important not to obtain F's fourth Chern class merely by alternating signs in c(G). Dualization contributes a point-supported Ext sheaf.

From the dual exact sequence,

[F]=[O(1)]+3[O]−[O(2)]+[O_Z(2)]

in K_0(P^4). Since length(Z)=8, ch(O_Z(2))=8h^4. Therefore

ch(F)=3−h−(3/2)h^2−(7/6)h^3+(177/24)h^4.

The identities

ch_2=(c_1^2−2c_2)/2,
ch_3=(c_1^3−3c_1c_2+3c_3)/6,
ch_4=(c_1^4−4c_1^2c_2+2c_2^2+4c_1c_3−4c_4)/24

give successively c_2=2h^2, c_3=−4h^3, and c_4=−40h^4.

## Explicit failure of an unconditional Steiner classification

Any sheaf admitting an exact sequence

0 → 2O(−2) → 5O(−1) → E → 0

has

c(E)=(1−h)^5/(1−2h)^2
     =1−h+2h^2+2h^3+5h^4.

Our F has c_3=−4h^3, rather than 2h^3, and has local projective dimension two at Z, rather than at most one. Either distinction rules out such a resolution.

Thus rank three, reflexivity, slope stability, c_1=−h, and c_2=2h^2 do not force the two-relation linear resolution. This construction does not classify all sheaves with those invariants.

## Boundary for the intended Horrocks–Mumford application

For the actual normalized kernel F=K(1), the required H_5-linearization has central weight 1 on P(V_3). Its relative central weight is

1/3 = 2 modulo 5.

Apply the universal Heisenberg Chern congruence established in PR #240: for rank r and relative weight w, the coefficient c_i is congruent to binom(r,i)(−w)^i modulo 5. In the present rank-three case this requires

c_3(F) ≡ binom(3,3)(−2)^3 ≡ 2 modulo 5.

The constructed family has c_3=−4≡1 modulo 5. It therefore cannot carry the required H_5-linearization, and a fortiori cannot carry the required normalizer linearization.

The family is consequently a counterexample to an abstract classification shortcut, not a surviving equivariant kernel candidate. The application of the congruence is credited to PR #240; its verified source is https://github.com/Akilleez-QA/read-em-and-weep/blob/448020d24955b3a545452a8ce7f10612f3df9f6d/research/hm/local-defect/full-support-equivariant-chern-congruences/PROOF.md .

## Related primary literature and its scope

Bertone and Roggero, “Positivity of Chern classes for reflexive sheaves on P^N,” Geometriae Dedicata 142 (2009), 121–138, provides resolution criteria under additional global-section hypotheses; see in particular Corollary 3.6 and Theorem 4.1:

Those results are not an unconditional classification of the stable reflexive sheaves considered here. The construction and verification above are self-contained and do not invoke hyperplane-restriction stability or a vector-bundle-only classification.
