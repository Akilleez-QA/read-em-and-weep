# The spectral eigenmodule at a transverse zero

## Conditional local theorem

Let V be a rank-two bundle on a smooth complex threefold and phi a trace-free twisted endomorphism. Suppose phi vanishes at p and its three independent local matrix entries have independent differentials there. This transversality is an explicit hypothesis, not a claim about the actual HM endomorphism. Trivialize both bundles near p and write

    phi = [[a,b],[c,-a]].

Then a,b,c are regular parameters, and the completed spectral local ring is

    A = C[[a,b,c,lambda]]/(lambda^2-a^2-bc).

With x=lambda-a, y=lambda+a, it is the ordinary threefold node

    A = C[[x,y,b,c]]/(xy-bc).

The spectral module associated to V and phi is rank one, maximal Cohen--Macaulay and reflexive on A, but not locally free. It represents a nontorsion local divisor class. Thus transverse zeros are compatible with a singular spectral module; they do not by themselves exclude phi.

## 1. Exact presentation and matrix factorization

The spectral action is lambda acting by phi on the rank-two module over the smooth base. Its presentation is

    A^2 --P--> A^2 -> M -> 0,
    P = [[x,-b],[-c,y]].

Over the regular ambient power-series ring S, let

    P' = [[y,b],[c,x]].

Then PP'=P'P=(xy-bc) I_2. Since det(P)=xy-bc is nonzero in S, P is injective over S, giving a free S-resolution of length one. Hence depth(M)=3 by the depth lemma (equivalently Auslander--Buchsbaum), so M is maximal Cohen--Macaulay over the three-dimensional hypersurface A. Modulo its equation, P and P' give the usual two-periodic resolution.

There is an explicit identification

    M = (c,x) subset A,

sending the two standard cokernel generators to c and x. The relations map to xc-cx=0 and -bc+yx=0. This is a surjection to the ideal. Both modules have generic rank one; M is torsion-free, as can also be seen from its free rank-two description over C[[a,b,c]] and the norm of any nonzero element of the spectral quadratic field. Therefore the kernel is zero.

The node is normal: it is a hypersurface, hence S2, and its only singular point has codimension three. Its MCM ideal M is consequently reflexive. At the closed point, c and x are independent minimal generators, so M is not invertible. Equivalently all entries of P lie in the maximal ideal, and M/mM has dimension two rather than one.

## 2. Small resolution and nontorsion divisor class

For completeness the non-Cartier defect is not merely torsion. Use the small resolution in A's local spectrum times P1 with homogeneous coordinates [u:v], defined by

    x u = c v,    b u = y v.

On u=1 it has coordinates c,y,v with x=cv and b=yv. On v=1 it has coordinates x,b,u with c=xu and y=bu. Thus the resolution is smooth; its exceptional set is a single P1, and it is an isomorphism away from the node. These equations and charts can equally be read in the corresponding completed local charts.

The pullback ideal (c,x), modulo torsion, is invertible: generated by c on u=1 and by x on v=1. Their transition is x=v c. Restricted to the exceptional P1, this line bundle has degree +1 or -1 according to the convention identifying the ideal with its divisor; in particular its degree has absolute value one.

If a positive reflexive power M^[n] were locally free downstairs, the n-th power of this line bundle would agree with its pullback away from the exceptional P1. Since the exceptional set has codimension two and the resolution is smooth, an isomorphism between line bundles extends across it: its divisor of zeros or poles cannot be supported in codimension two. But a line bundle pulled back from the local base restricts trivially to the exceptional fiber, while the displayed n-th power has degree +n or -n. Contradiction. Thus the local rank-one reflexive class of M is nontorsion.

This establishes the needed divisor consequence without requiring a full computation of the local class group.

## 3. Consequences and exact boundary of the argument

At a transverse zero, the spectral discriminant a^2+bc has an ordinary quadratic singularity on the base, and the spectral cover has the ordinary node above it. The eigenmodule is the non-Cartier MCM ideal just exhibited, not a line bundle. Any argument requiring a spectral line bundle must therefore exclude these zeros or account for this module.

If the actual global spectral cover is normal, its spectral module is a rank-one MCM sheaf and hence reflexive. If it also has a transverse zero, the global divisor class represented by this sheaf is not Q-Cartier at that point. Consequently such a spectral cover cannot be Q-factorial. This is a conditional global restriction, not a proof that the actual cover is Q-factorial or that actual zeros are transverse.

There is no unconditional contradiction here: the node explicitly admits this MCM module, and the universal matrix above supplies it. The missing implication for an exclusion would be an independently established global factoriality/Q-factoriality theorem for the actual spectral cover, or another condition incompatible with this non-Cartier class. Smooth-cover Picard arguments alone do not supply that implication at a nodal cover.
