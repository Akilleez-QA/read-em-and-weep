# The sextic plane-restriction obstruction has two independent stages

## Result and boundary

Let C be the actual 25 HM lines, F the normalized HM bundle, B=End_0(F), and U=H0(F(3)). For an avoiding Boralevi-good plane P, the six quintics restrict independently by QUINTIC-RANK-FIVE-TANGENCY.md. Nevertheless their linear multiples need not restrict independently. The exact remaining sextic test is:

1. The six restricted quintics have no linear syzygy on P.
2. The ten-dimensional new sextic channel maps isomorphically to the quotient of plane sextics by those linear multiples.

Both are necessary and together sufficient for sextic restriction surjectivity. Neither follows merely from the good-plane resolution. We give the exact cohomological map controlling stage 1 and the canonical map controlling stage 2. Thus there is no proved sextic-surjectivity or b<=6 conclusion here.

## 1. Short foundations: R6=5 and I6=40

This recovers the known calculation of PR464, not a new numerical claim. The graded symmetric square of the normalized equivariant monad computes Sym²F=B(-1). Its only nonzero term cohomology is:

- H1(Lambda²W tensor S_(2,1,1)Q*), dimension five, in complex degree zero;
- H0(Lambda²A* tensor O), dimension ten, in complex degree two.

Indeed O(-2), Omega²(1), O(-1), Omega²(2), and Lambda⁴Q*=O(-1) are acyclic. The shifted Bott weights for S_(2,2)Q* and S_(2,1,1)Q* are respectively (4,5,4,1,0), singular, and (4,5,3,2,0), with one inversion and dominant weight (1,1,1,1,0). The only possible spectral differential goes from dimension five to Lambda²A*, an irreducible ten-dimensional normalizer representation. Equivariance forces it to be zero. Consequently

    h1 B(-1)=5, h2 B(-1)=10,
    Hi B(-1)=0 otherwise.

The actual degree-six Eagon--Northcott complex is

    0→D tensor B(-4)→Lambda³U tensor F(-1)
      →Lambda²U tensor O(1)→I_C(6)→0,

where D=det U. The normalized primary monad makes F(-1) acyclic. Splitting the EN complex and using Serre duality gives

    0→S1 tensor I5→I6→X→0,
    X=D tensor H2 B(-4),  dim X=10.

The left injection uses H1 B(-4)=H3 B(-1)*=0. Thus the thirty linear multiples of the six global quintics are independent, h0 I_C(6)=40, and

    R6=h1 I_C(6)=25·7−(210−40)=5.

This does NOT make I6 generated by the quintics: its quotient by their linear multiples has dimension ten.

## 2. The canonical new sextic map

Write V_P=H0(O_P(1)), dim V_P=3. Set

    A_P=image[V_P tensor I5→H0(O_P(6))],
    L_P=kernel[V_P tensor I5→H0(O_P(6))],
    s=dim L_P,
    T_P=H0(O_P(6))/A_P.

Then dim A_P=18−s and dim T_P=10+s. Restriction of a representative of a class in X defines a canonical map

    eta_P:X→T_P.

It is well-defined because changing that representative by a global linear multiple changes its restriction by A_P. Its cokernel is canonically the full sextic restriction cokernel:

    coker eta_P = coker[I6→H0(O_P(6))].

Thus, putting t equal to its dimension,

    t=10+s−rank eta_P >= s.

Sextic restriction is onto if and only if s=0 and eta_P is an isomorphism of ten-dimensional spaces. Six independent restricted quintics alone do not imply s=0.

There is also a directly square formulation. Since quintic restriction is injective, H0(I_(P union C)(5))=0. The twelve-dimensional subspace I_P(1) tensor I5 injects into I6 and is annihilated by restriction. Hence restriction factors as a square map

    I6 / [I_P(1) tensor I5] → H0(O_P(6)),
                        28 → 28.

Both its kernel and its cokernel have dimension t. This square map is induced by actual restriction; dimension equality is not a rank argument.

## 3. The exact good-plane cohomological map

On P, put Z_P=ker[Lambda²U tensor O_P(1)→O_P(6)]. EN restricts exactly, since P avoids C, to

    0→D tensor B_P(-4)→Lambda³U tensor F_P(-1)→Z_P→0.

On a good plane, the resolution 0→3O(-3)→5O(-2)→F_P→0 gives

    H0 F_P(-1)=H2 F_P(-1)=0,  h1 F_P(-1)=4.

For H2, use F_P*=F_P(1) and surface Serre duality; for H1, twist the resolution by -1 and use the resulting 9-to-5 map on H2. The same resolution gives H0 F_P=0, which implies stability of the normalized rank-two restricted bundle. Therefore H2 B_P(1)=H0 B_P(-4)*=0. With c2(B_P)=15 and rank three, Riemann--Roch gives chi(B_P(1))=-6. If h=h0 B_P(1), then h1 B_P(1)=6+h. Surface duality gives

    h1 B_P(-4)=6+h,  h2 B_P(-4)=h.

The two restricted EN short exact sequences yield the exact sequence

    0→L_P→D tensor H1 B_P(-4)
      →Lambda³U tensor H1 F_P(-1)→T_P
      →D tensor H2 B_P(-4)→0.

The middle arrow has source dimension 6+h and target dimension 16. In particular L_P is its KERNEL, not its whole source. A lower bound h1 B_P(-4)>=6 gives no positive lower bound on s. Omitting this sixteen-dimensional target would produce an invalid sextic rank bound.

Naturality also makes the square formed by eta_P and restriction X→D tensor H2 B_P(-4) commute with the last map above. The latter restriction being onto is one necessary condition for eta_P to be onto; it is not sufficient.

## 4. Binary syzygies and a weaker sufficient target for b<=6

Let P=V(x,y), and regard R=H1_*(I_C) as a module over C[x,y]. The Koszul splitting, with H2 I_C(4)=0, identifies

    Tor1(R,C)_6 = coker[I6→H0(O_P(6))],
    Tor2(R,C)_6 = H0(I_(P union C)(6)) / [x I5+y I5].

Thus both have dimension t. The second formula follows because I4=0, so xI5+yI5 has dimension twelve. The first is middle Koszul homology, not the first kernel. Independent quintic restriction already gives Tor2(R,C)_5=0.

For the cubic truncated relation bundle K3 from RAO-BINARY-PENCIL-BOUNDS.md, every splitting summand is consequently at most O(-6). At most t summands are O(-6), since H0(K3(6)) injects into the full degree-six second-syzygy space of dimension t. All remaining summands are at most O(-7). If b=rank K3, its inclusion via the relation bundle of degree -45 gives

    7b <= 45+t.

In particular t<=3, weaker than sextic surjectivity, would already force b<=6. No such bound on t has been proved here. The established b<=7 is retained.

## 5. Comparison with the existing adjoint and primary obstructions

Use the shifted actual extension notation of PR538:

    K_j=H1 B(j-1),  P_j=D tensor R_(6-j)*,
    0→L→K→N→0,  N⊂P,
    L_j=U tensor H1 F(j-3),  P/N supported in degree one.

Let E_exc=P1/N1, of dimension six. Since L1=L2=0 and N2=P2, one has K1=N1 and K2=P2. Dualizing the degree-six binary Koszul complex identifies the dual sextic defect canonically, up to the fixed determinant line, as

    (coker eta_P)* tensor D
      ≅ ker[P1² --(x,y)→P2] / image[P0 --(-y,x)→P1²].

The cokernel of P1²→P2 is instead dual to Tor2(R,C)_6. It has the SAME dimension t, but equality of dimensions supplies no canonical identification with the displayed middle homology.

This is a LOW-DEGREE full-Rao multiplication test. Its actual-adjoint comparison retains the exceptional image correction. Put

    C_K=coker[K1² --(x,y)→K2].

Then the comparison of the two source sequences gives the exact sequence

    ker[K1²→K2]→ker[P1²→P2]→E_exc²
      →C_K→D tensor Tor2(R,C)_6*→0.

In particular the twelve exceptional source dimensions cannot simply be discarded. There is no primary summand in either K1 or K2, so this map is not the twenty-dimensional primary lift boundary for a quadratic K3→K5. Nor is it the rank-seven/rank-ten primary evaluation map on P(H1 F(0)), whose source is H1 F(2)* and whose intrinsic target is sl(W')* tensor Omega¹(2). The cohomological degrees, parameter spaces and exceptional correction are different.

This does not prove that the various unanswered conditions are logically independent. It proves that no direct identification or duality follows from the actual extension: the established canonical dual is the middle homology of the low-degree full-Rao complex displayed here. Any proposed equivalence with the twenty-dimensional lift or the rank-seven alternative needs an additional natural construction and proof.
