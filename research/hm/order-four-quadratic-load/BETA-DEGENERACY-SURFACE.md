# Fixed-beta degeneracy surfaces and the residual degree dichotomy

## Hypotheses and public dependencies

Over C, let E be the rank-two HM bundle with c1=5,c2=10, and U=H0(E) of dimension four. Assume the actual wedge map identifies Λ²U with H0(I_C(5)), where C is the union of 25 skew lines. The Heisenberg group H5 acts trivially on U and transitively on those lines; its center acts on degree-m homogeneous coordinates with weight m modulo five, up to sign. Every one-dimensional character of H5 is trivial on the center.

Assume a rational, intrinsic family of integral nonplanar sextics D_H⊂H, indexed by general hyperplanes H⊂P4, with arithmetic genus three and containing the 25 reduced spanning points Z_H=C∩H. In the beta case the evaluation kernel map on D_H spans a beta plane, so determines a unique B_H∈Gr(3,U). These inputs are recorded in SEXTIC-PLUCKER-FLAGS.md at a82e9aeaad55a4e58a0fea12581bab1dcf42e4b0 and the integral-sextic notes it cites. The conclusion is conditional on generic beta occurrence. It does not require re-proving the separate generic alpha/line exclusions.

## 1. A fixed B produces a degree-15 locally CM surface

Fix any three-space B⊂U and let X_B be the rank-at-most-one degeneracy scheme of B tensor O_P4→E. Its ideal is generated by the three minors corresponding to Λ²B.

There is no common divisorial factor. Indeed the three quintics are independent, by injectivity of Λ²U→H0(I_C(5)). Their span is H5-stable because B is fixed and the evaluation map is equivariant. Consequently their greatest common divisor g, if nonconstant, spans a one-dimensional H5 representation. The central character forces deg g≡0 mod5. Since 0<deg g≤5, its degree must be five; then all three quintics are scalar multiples of g, contradicting independence. Thus their common zero scheme has no divisorial component. It is nonempty since it contains C. The determinantal height bound for maximal minors of a two-by-three matrix is two; combined with absence of a divisor, the ideal has height exactly two.

Hilbert–Burch, locally in bundle trivializations, gives the global resolution

0 → E(−10) → 3O_P4(−5) → I_X_B → 0.

Here the kernel is E* tensor det(E)^−1=E(−10). The height-two maximal-minor ideal is perfect. Hence X_B is pure of dimension two and locally Cohen–Macaulay. This does NOT assert that X_B is arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay.

The resolution yields

ch(O_X_B)=15h²−50h³+(325/4)h⁴,
χ(O_X_B(n))=(15n²−25n)/2.

In particular deg X_B=15, χ(O_X_B)=0, and a general hyperplane section has arithmetic genus 21. The exact-rational arithmetic is independently reproduced by verify_surface_rr.py, published in PR543 at commit 5e9cc67035b0256c3566daace2173e7c13ad9d6f. The geometric inputs to this calculation are the resolution and c1(E)=5,c2(E)=10; numerical agreement does not establish those inputs by itself.

## 2. Positive-dimensional beta fibers force a large invariant component

The intrinsic beta assignment is a rational map from an open subset of dual P4 to Gr(3,U)≅P3. Its general nonempty fiber has positive dimension. Choose an irreducible positive-dimensional subfamily T with fixed B. For every H∈T, D_H⊂X_B. Since X_B has finitely many irreducible components, one integral component S contains D_H for a dense subfamily, after restricting T.

A general H in that subfamily does not contain S: if S spans P4, none does; if it spans P3, at most one does; and S cannot span a plane because D_H is nonplanar. Thus S∩H is a proper curve section containing the degree-six D_H, and deg S≥6.

X_B is H5-invariant. The projective Heisenberg quotient has order 25, so the orbit of S has size 1, 5, or 25. An orbit of size at least five would contribute at least 5·6=30 to the degree-15 fundamental cycle. Therefore S is invariant.

Moreover C⊂S. Otherwise, if no component line L_i of C were contained in S, every intersection S∩L_i would be finite. The varying point p_i(H)=H∩L_i must then be constant on the irreducible subfamily T, for each i. The 25 points Z_H span H, forcing H itself to be constant, a contradiction. Hence at least one L_i lies in S; invariance and transitivity imply all 25 do.

## 3. Degree divisibility and the remaining alternatives

Any invariant integral surface S⊂P4 has degree divisible by five. To see this, for large m not divisible by five the H5 representation H0(O_S(m)) has nontrivial central character. Its dimension is divisible by five: for a nontrivial central character every irreducible representation of the order-125 Heisenberg group has dimension five. The Hilbert polynomial P_S(m) thus vanishes modulo five at all four nonzero residue classes. Twice the quadratic Hilbert polynomial has integer coefficients; as a polynomial over F5 with four roots and degree at most two it is identically zero. Its leading coefficient is deg S, proving 5 divides deg S.

Combining 6≤deg S≤15 gives precisely

- deg S=10, with multiplicity one in X_B and residual fundamental cycle of degree five; or
- deg S=15, in which case X_B=S as a reduced integral scheme.

For the second assertion, degree 15 leaves no other component and forces generic multiplicity one. Local Cohen–Macaulayness excludes embedded associated primes. A generically reduced Noetherian scheme without embedded associated primes is reduced, so the whole scheme is its sole reduced integral component.

For degree ten, the residual cycle can be narrowed further. An invariant irreducible residual component has degree divisible by five by the same argument. A nontrivial orbit has length five or 25; the total residual degree is five. Therefore the residual cycle is either one invariant integral degree-five surface with multiplicity one, or a single orbit of five distinct planes, each with multiplicity one. This is a statement about the fundamental cycle, not a claim of disjointness, normality, or reducedness of every possible residual subscheme at intersections.

## Boundary

These conclusions construct and constrain an actual geometric receptacle for the surviving beta case: a fixed degree-15 locally CM determinantal surface, with an invariant sextic-containing component of degree ten or fifteen that contains all of C. They do not exclude either alternative, construct the beta family, or give a complete proof repair. In particular locally CM is not ACM, and the degree-ten residual cycle need not be geometrically disjoint. No selected coefficients, matrix ranks, or prohibited experiments are used.
