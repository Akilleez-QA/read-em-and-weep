# Good binary products do not force good squares: a synthetic example

This example is abstract algebra constructed for this boundary argument. It is not an HM multiplication map, selected source, monad, coefficient experiment, or model asserted to arise geometrically. Work over C.

Let U have basis x,y and T have basis e1,...,e5. Inside Sym^2(U) tensor T put

    n1=x^2 e1,
    n2=x^2 e2+2xy e1,
    n3=2xy e2+y^2 e1,
    n4=y^2 e2,
    n5=x^2 e3,
    N=span(n1,n2,n3,n4,n5).

These tensors are independent, by their x^2 and y^2 coefficients. Define the graded module in degrees zero through two by

    A0=T,  A1=U tensor T,  A2=(Sym^2 U tensor T)/N,

with the natural multiplication. Its dimensions are 5,10,10. It can be made a graded module in all degrees by setting higher degrees to zero.

## Every square is bad

For every nonzero ell=sx+ty,

    ell^2(s e1+t e2)
      =s^3 n1+s^2 t n2+s t^2 n3+t^3 n4.

The vector s e1+t e2 is nonzero. Thus ell^2:A0->A2 is noninjective for every nonzero ell.

## There is a good product

Suppose a linear combination of the n_i lies in xy tensor T. Its x^2 coefficients force the coefficients of n1,n2,n5 to be zero. Its y^2 coefficients then force those of n3,n4 to be zero. Therefore

    N intersect (xy tensor T)=0.

Hence xy:A0->A2 is injective. Openness implies a general binary quadratic is also injective in this example.

## There is no common degree-one annihilator

Suppose z in U tensor T satisfies xz,yz in N. Components along e4,e5 vanish, since no relation has those components. Write the remaining part as

    z=x(a e1+b e2+c e3)+y(d e1+e e2+f e3).

The condition xz in N gives

    d=2b,  e=0,  f=0.

The condition yz in N gives

    a=0,  b=2d,  c=0,  f=0.

Combining d=2b and b=2d gives 3b=3d=0, so every coefficient is zero. Thus the example satisfies exactly the no-common-degree-one-annihilator hypothesis used in the binary-quadratic lemma.

## Generic middle corank two

Evaluate the five vector-valued quadratic relations at a point of P(U*). Where x is nonzero, their values span precisely the three-dimensional space generated by e1,e2,e3: n1 and n2 already span the first two directions and n5 supplies the third. Thus generic evaluation rank is three and generic evaluation kernel dimension is two.

As in the universal pencil construction, that kernel is identified with the kernel of multiplication ell:A1->A2 for the linear form vanishing at the evaluation point. The generic middle corank is therefore two, despite the absence of a common degree-one annihilator and the existence of good products.

## Exact boundary established

The assumptions dim N=5, A1=U tensor A0, absence of a common degree-one annihilator, and generic middle corank at most two cannot imply that a good square exists. The binary-product theorem is genuinely stronger off the diagonal than on it. Further HM-specific input would be needed to settle actual squares. No claim that actual HM squares fail is made.
