# Base-ideal powers constrain both auxiliary jet kernels by the actual adjoint group

## 1. Complete-intersection powers, not independent interpolation problems

On the smooth fourfold X=P_Q(E), the four actual evaluation sections U of O_X(L) generate the ideal I of the fifty simple quotient base points. They form a regular sequence locally at the base scheme; away from it the sequence is unimodular. The universal resolutions of powers of a length-four regular-sequence ideal therefore sheafify globally. For I² the shifts and multiplicities are

    0 -> O(-5L)^4 -> O(-4L)^15 -> O(-3L)^20 -> O(-2L)^10 -> I² -> 0.

For I³ they are

    0 -> O(-6L)^10 -> O(-5L)^36 -> O(-4L)^45 -> O(-3L)^20 -> I³ -> 0.

These are the Schur/Koszul resolutions of powers of the actual evaluation ideal. Their exactness can be checked after choosing local regular parameters for the four generators, where they are the standard resolutions of powers of the maximal ideal in four variables; a flat regular-sequence substitution preserves exactness. Their maps retain the actual four sections. Only their universal multiplicity spaces are described below, not any instance coefficients.

Let P=H1(Q,F_Q(2)), R=H1(Q,B_Q(4)), K=H0(Q,B_Q(4)), with B=End0(F). The pinned fifty-point square boundary gives dim P=5, dim R=dim K=r, and H2(B_Q(4))=H3(B_Q(4))=0. Retain R and K as distinct vector spaces even though their dimensions agree.

## 2. Exact cohomology of the two power resolutions

Twist the I² resolution by L+3H. Its first two terms, in degrees0 and-1, are acyclic. The degree-2 term has only H3 equal to fifteen copies of P*, because

    R1 pi_*O_X(-3L+3H)=F_Q(-4),

and Serre duality identifies its H2 with P*. The degree-3 term has H3 equal to four copies of R* and H4 equal to four copies of K*, because

    R1 pi_*O_X(-4L+3H)=B_Q(-7).

All other cohomology vanishes. Therefore, for the first-order jet map e5 and its kernel J5 and cokernel C5 defined in AMBIENT-GAMMA-EXTENSION-JET-COMPLEX.md, there is an actual map

    d5:(R*)^4 -> (P*)^15,

with

    J5=ker d5,
    0 -> coker d5 -> C5 -> (K*)^4 -> 0.

There are no possible higher differentials, since the other two columns are acyclic. In particular dim C5=75+dim J5, as independently required by the 175-to-250 jet map.

Twisting the I³ resolution by 2L+3H gives the same two nonzero cohomology types, now with multiplicities36 and10. Thus

    d6:(R*)^10 -> (P*)^36,
    J6=ker d6,
    0 -> coker d6 -> C6 -> (K*)^10 -> 0.

This gives dim C6=180+dim J6. No vanishing of the original adjoint group is assumed.

## 3. The actual primary map is surjective onto R

The cohomological multiplication map

    mu: U tensor P -> R

is surjective on the established Rao-good open. Indeed the pinned fifty-point construction has an intermediate H1 group extending U tensor P by the thirty-dimensional cubic group. The boundary from the fifty-point sheaf surjects onto that thirty-dimensional quotient on the Rao-good open. Its quotient R is consequently generated by U tensor P.

Thus mu* embeds R* as a specified subspace

    W subset U* tensor P*,    dim W=r.

This is the actual multiplication image, not a generic r-plane.

## 4. J5 is the largest full U*-block inside W

The last two multiplicity spaces of the I² resolution are, with their natural determinant factor,

    det(U) tensor U,    det(U) tensor End0(U).

The last differential is the canonical projection obtained by insertion of the evaluation covector and removal of the scalar endomorphism. After cohomology and duality, d5 is therefore induced by

    W tensor U -> P* tensor End0(U),

the restriction of the trace-free projection U* tensor U -> End0(U). Its kernel inside the full space is P* tensor the identity endomorphism. Consequently, suppressing only the common fixed determinant line,

    J5 = P0,
    P0 = {p in P*: U* tensor p is contained in W}.

In particular

    4 dim J5 <= r.

This is substantially stronger than the crude bound dim J5<=4r. A nonzero first-order jet kernel forces r>=4. The assertion uses the actual primary map and the universal resolution differential, not a maximal-rank assumption.

## 5. J6 is a symmetric prolongation with explicit rank thresholds

The last two multiplicity spaces for I³ are

    det(U) tensor Sym²U,
    det(U) tensor [(U* tensor Sym²U)/j(U)],

where j(u)=sum_i e_i* tensor (u e_i), independent of the basis. The quotient has dimension36. The same naturality gives

    J6 = {a in P* tensor U: j(a) belongs to W tensor Sym²U},

again with the common determinant line retained if desired.

Write a=sum_i p_i tensor e_i. The quadratic coefficients required to lie in W are

    e_i* tensor p_i,
    e_i* tensor p_j + e_j* tensor p_i  (i<j),

up to nonzero conventional factors on the diagonal. If a has tensor rank s, choose bases making p_1,...,p_s independent and p_i=0 for i>s. The independent coefficient space then has dimension

    s(s+1)/2 + s(4-s) = s(9-s)/2.

For s=1,2,3,4 these dimensions are4,7,9,10. Hence any such a in J6 forces r to be at least the corresponding number. In particular J6 nonzero forces r>=4.

Rank-one elements p tensor u satisfy the condition exactly when p belongs to P0, since their coefficient space is the entire U* tensor p. Therefore

    J5 tensor U is contained in J6,

and these are precisely its decomposable elements. If r<=3, both actual jet maps e5 and e6 are injective. This is conditional on a bound on the original adjoint group; no such bound r<=3 is proved here.

The auxiliary jet kernels are thus constrained by actual adjoint multiplication. Their possible nonvanishing is not a new independent obstruction, but the argument does not show that r itself vanishes or finish the primitive twist-four repair.
