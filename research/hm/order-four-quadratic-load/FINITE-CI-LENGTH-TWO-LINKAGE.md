# Finite cubic base: length-two linkage fixes the entire section Hilbert function

Let Z be25 reduced points in P3 contained in a finite complete intersection X of three cubics, and assume Z consists of simple components of X. The generic transitive-label argument in FINITE-CI-DIRECTIONAL-JET-SURJECTIVITY.md gives exactly this conclusion in the actual generic finite-CI branch. Let W be the complementary length-two scheme. It is disjoint from Z but need not be reduced.

## New conclusion

The Hilbert function of Z is forced:

h_Z(d)=1,4,10,17,23,25,25,... for d=0,1,2,3,4,5,6,... .

Its h-vector is (1,3,6,7,6,2). In particular, every quartic vanishing on Z is a linear combination of the three cubic generators with linear coefficients, and h0(I_Z(4))=12. The saturated homogeneous ideal I_Z is generated by these three cubics and exactly one additional quintic. This is a conclusion about the finite-CI branch, not an assertion that every b=3 section has finite base.

## Proof by the residual canonical module

Every length-two subscheme W of projective3-space spans a line and is a complete intersection of degrees(1,1,2). For two distinct points, take their joining line and the binary quadratic cutting them. For a double point, its tangent direction gives the line and a double-root binary quadratic. These exhaust length-two schemes over C. Thus its homogeneous coordinate ring R_W is a one-dimensional graded complete intersection with canonical module

omega_(R_W)=R_W(1+1+2-4)=R_W.

The coordinate ring R_X is Gorenstein with canonical module R_X(3+3+3-4)=R_X(5). Graded canonical-module duality for the quotient R_X->R_W gives

Hom_(R_X)(R_W,R_X)=omega_(R_W)(-5)=R_W(-5).

The left module is the annihilator of the ideal of W in R_X. Because W and Z are linked complementary components of the Gorenstein complete intersection X, this is I_Z/I_X. Equivalently, the double-annihilator identity for unmixed linked ideals identifies the residual ideal. Hence there is an exact sequence of graded modules

0 -> I_X -> I_Z -> R_W(-5) -> 0.                     (1)

Here these are graded modules, not a replacement of their degree-zero pieces by global sections of their sheafifications. In particular the degree-five quotient has dimension1, even though the sheaf O_W has two global sections. This distinction is important.

Since R_W(-5) is cyclic with its generator in degree5, lifting that generator through (1) proves I_Z=I_X+(q5) for a quintic q5 not in I_X. There is exactly one new generator. In degree4 the quotient vanishes, so I_Z(4)=I_X(4). The complete-intersection Koszul resolution has its first syzygies in degree6; therefore multiplication S1 tensor (I_X)_3 -> (I_X)_4 is injective and has dimension4*3=12.

Finally,

Hilb(R_X,t)=(1-t^3)^3/(1-t)^4,
Hilb(R_W,t)=(1-t)^2(1-t^2)/(1-t)^4=(1+t)/(1-t).

Sequence(1) gives Hilb(R_Z)=Hilb(R_X)-t^5 Hilb(R_W). The complete-intersection Hilbert values are1,4,10,17,23,26,27,27,...; subtracting zero through degree4, one at degree5 and two in each degree>=6 gives the stated Hilbert function. This also verifies total length25 and the h-vector sum25.

## Scheme-theoretic contraction of the residual pair by evaluation

Retain the actual HM input that the six quintic minors of U tensor O -> E sheaf-generate I_C(5), and that evaluation has rank two outside C. On H, each such minor m_i lies in (I_Z)_5. The quotient (I_Z/I_X)_5 is one-dimensional, generated by the quintic q5 above. Hence

m_i = c_i q5 modulo (I_X)_5,

with scalar constants c_i. In particular on W one has m_i|W=c_i q5|W.

The restriction q5|W is nowhere vanishing as a section of O_W(5), including when W is nonreduced. Indeed q5 generates the sheafification of I_Z/I_X. On the open-and-closed component W of X, this sheaf is O_W, because Z and W are disjoint; a generator restricts to a unit at every local Artin ring. This argument uses the module-generator assertion, not just its one-dimensional degree-five count.

The constants c_i are not all zero. Otherwise all six minors would vanish on W, contradicting their sheaf generation of I_C(5) at a point of W: W is disjoint from Z=C intersect H and hence from C. Therefore the projective minor map restricted to W is the constant point [c_1:...:c_6]. Its point lies on the Plucker Grassmannian since the minors arise from rank-two evaluation. The kernel-plane map consequently factors scheme-theoretically

W -> Spec(C) -> Gr(2,U).

If W consists of two reduced points, the two evaluation kernels are the same two-dimensional subspace of U. If W is a length-two point, the differential of the kernel-plane map annihilates its embedded tangent direction. Equivalently the corresponding fixed two-dimensional space of global sections vanishes on W as a scheme, not only at its reduced support. This is a new necessary condition on the actual finite-CI branch; it neither excludes such fibers nor proves the square-jet map injective.
