# The resolved eigenquotient projection and its base-point budget

## Hypotheses

Let Q be a general smooth quadric in the simultaneous-good-flanks open. Let E=F|Q(3), so c1(E)=5H, c2(E)=10H^2, and let U be the four actual HM sections. The evaluation degeneracy scheme on Q is the fifty reduced points Z=C∩Q, with transverse rank drop inherited from the smooth determinantal curve C. Let X=P_Q(E) parameterize one-dimensional quotients, with L=O_X(1), and also denote the pullback of H by H.

Let psi be a primitive trace-free map E->E(k): primitive means its zero scheme has no divisorial component. Assume its characteristic polynomial is irreducible. Its eigenquotient divisor T in X then has class 2L+(k-5)H and is integral. Indeed its generic fiber over Q is the irreducible quadratic spectral point; primitiveness prevents a vertical divisor over a divisorial zero. Zeros in codimension at least two produce no additional three-dimensional component. In applications k=2,3,4 are the remaining primitive twists. No zero of psi is assumed transverse.

## 1. All fifty evaluation base points are reduced simple points

Near z in Z, row and column operations put evaluation in the form

    [[1,0,0,0],[0,a1,a2,a3]],

where a1,a2,a3 are regular parameters on Q at z. The projective quotient annihilating the first row is the unique base point above z. In its affine chart, with quotient [u:1], the section ideal is

    (u,a1,a2,a3),

the maximal ideal of a smooth point of the fourfold X. This describes the evaluation base locus, NOT the multiplicity of T there.

Blow up these fifty points, with exceptional divisors E_i, obtaining rho:Xhat->X. Then

    A=rho*L-sum E_i

is globally generated by U and defines the resolved morphism f:Xhat->P(U*). The bundle relation L^2-5HL+10H^2=0 and integral_Q H^3=2 give

    L^4=50, H L^3=30, H^2 L^2=10, H^3 L=2.

As E_i^4=-1 and mixed positive-degree pullback intersections at E_i vanish,

    A^4=0, H A^3=30.

The latter positivity shows the image is all P3, and a general fiber has H-degree thirty.

## 2. Actual eigenquotient multiplicities and projection degree

Let m_i>=0 be the actual multiplicity of T at the i-th evaluation base point, with zero when the point is not on T. Its strict transform is

    That=rho*T-sum m_i E_i.

Then the exact intersection calculation gives

    A^3 That = 30k-50-sum m_i.

Since A is globally generated, this number is nonnegative. If f|That is dominant, it is generically finite and this number is its degree. If it is not dominant, the number is zero. Thus

    sum m_i <= 30k-50.

For k=2,3,4 the respective bounds are 10,40,70. In the nondominant case equality holds.

The membership condition has an intrinsic interpretation. At z, the base quotient annihilates the evaluation-image line in E_z. This quotient is an eigenquotient exactly when psi_z preserves that image line. This determines whether m_i is zero, but supplies no automatic positive multiplicity or transversality claim.

## 3. A stronger bound if the eigenquotient projection is dominant

The canonical class of X is -2L+2H. Blowing up smooth points of a fourfold adds 3E_i, so adjunction on the integral Cartier divisor That gives

    K_That=(k-3)H+sum (3-m_i)E_i restricted to That.

This remains the dualizing line bundle even if That is singular. If f|That is dominant, characteristic zero makes its generic differential nonzero. Composing its top exterior differential with the fundamental-class map from top differentials to the dualizing sheaf of the local complete intersection That gives a nonzero section of

    omega_That tensor f*O_P3(4).

It defines an effective Cartier divisor. This Jacobian construction does not presume normality or smoothness of That. Intersect with the nef class A^2. The other needed intersections are

    H A^2 That=10k+10,
    E_i A^2 That=m_i.

Consequently

    0 <= 10k^2+100k-230 - sum m_i(m_i+1).

For k=2 this is particularly restrictive:

    sum m_i(m_i+1)<=10.

Thus in the dominant primitive-twist-two case at most five evaluation base points lie on T, every such multiplicity is at most two, and the degree of f|That is between five and ten. A multiplicity-two point consumes six of this budget; there can be at most one, with at most two additional simple base points.

In contrast, if the twist-two projection is nondominant, its exact degree equation forces sum m_i=10. The differential argument then gives no inequality. This dominant/nondominant dichotomy is the conclusion, not a proof selecting either branch.

## Boundaries

The fifty simple evaluation base points are proved from actual transverse evaluation geometry; the m_i remain unknown multiplicities of a different divisor. No selected coefficients, matrix ranks, numerical scans, or experiments were evaluated. These budgets provide a new necessary restriction, not a contradiction: the actual multiplicities and dominance of the eigenquotient projection have not been determined. No external write was made.
