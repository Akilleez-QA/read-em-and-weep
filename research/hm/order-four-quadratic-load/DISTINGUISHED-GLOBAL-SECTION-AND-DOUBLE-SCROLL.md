# The distinguished global section, exact division, and its double-scroll cycle

Write R=P(V) for the positive fixed plane, L=P(W) for the negative fixed line, F for the normalized actual HM bundle, and B=End_0 F. Let

    p:X=Bl_R(P4)->P4,       pi:X->T=P(W*)

be the actual negative hyperplane pencil. The symplectic identification identifies T with its negative vertex line L. Put S_k=H0(T,O_T(k)), with S_k=0 for k<0.

## Theorem

There is, up to scalar, a unique invariant section

    t6 in H0(P4,I_R^4 F(6)).

It is the unique section in this four-dimensional space vanishing on L. In fact it vanishes to exactly second normal order along L, with everywhere nonzero quadratic normal term equal to the invariant positive quadratic q0, in the lower summand of F_L(6).

For every integer n>=0, the actual global section spaces and their L-adic filtrations are

    H0(I_R^n F(n+2)) = W tensor S_(n-3),
    H0(I_R^n I_L F(n+2))
      =H0(I_R^n I_L^2 F(n+2)) = t6 S_(n-4),
    H0(I_R^n I_L^3 F(n+2))=0.                       (1)

For adjoints,

    H0(I_R^n B(n+3)) = A tensor S_(n-6),
    H0(I_R^n I_L B(n+3))
      =H0(I_R^n I_L^2 B(n+3))
      =t6 H0(I_R^(n-4) F(n-2)),
    H0(I_R^n I_L^3 B(n+3))
      =H0(I_R^n I_L^4 B(n+3)) = t6^2 S_(n-8),
    H0(I_R^n I_L^5 B(n+3))=0,                       (2)

where A=Sym^2 W. In (2) the product term is defined to be zero when n<7; its displayed ideal expression is used only when n>=7, when its second factor is W tensor S_(n-7) by (1). No negative ideal power is being defined. All powers of ideals are ordinary sheaf powers. These are exact global division identities, not conclusions from leading-jet divisibility alone.

The zero scheme Z=Z(t6) is a pure locally complete-intersection surface of degree 34. Let B_can be the actual smooth canonical genus-four (3,3) factor curve, with degree-three rulings M,N and incidence

    B_can subset L x C0,       C0=(q0=0) subset R.

Let Sigma be the surface swept out by the joining lines between the two coordinates of B_can. Its normalization is the ruled surface

    P_(B_can)(O(M) directsum O(2N)),

and deg(Sigma)=9. The exact fundamental cycle is

    [Z]=16[R]+2[Sigma].                              (3)

Along L the projectivized normal cone of Z is exactly the doubled incidence curve

    P(C_L Z)=2 B_can subset C0 x L.                  (4)

Statements (3)-(4) do not assert that Z is a split double of Sigma, a union of chosen thickened components, or a scheme-theoretic cone.

## 1. The actual blowup dictionary and the invariant section

The established section bundle and vertex evaluation are

    Y=pi_*p*F(2)=W O_T(-3),
    0 -> O_T(-4) -> Y -> O_T(-2) -> 0.              (5)

The evaluation quotient is everywhere onto, including all twelve exceptional parameters. The kernel is the actual distinguished vertex-vanishing section line, not an arbitrarily chosen line in a two-space.

The blowup identity

    pi*O_T(1)=p*O_P4(1) tensor O_X(-E_R)

and p_*O_X(-nE_R)=I_R^n for n>=0 give

    H0(I_R^n F(n+2))=H0(T,Y(n))=W tensor S_(n-3).   (6)

Under the same dictionary, restriction to L is precisely the global-section map in (5), twisted by n. It is ordinary binary multiplication

    W tensor S_(n-3) -> S_(n-2),                    (7)

up to the already proved nonzero vertex-evaluation scalar. It lands in the lower summand O_L(n-2) of F_L(n+2); the other summand does not occur in the vertex evaluation. The kernel is S_(n-4). This proves the first L-vanishing statement in (1), including its actual multiplication interpretation.

At n=4, the space in (6) is W tensor W=1 directsum A and the kernel of (7) is its invariant line. Its generator is t6. In a matched symbolic basis u,v of negative linear forms and g_u,g_v of the known W=H0(I_R^3 F(5)), one can write

    t6=u g_v-v g_u,                                 (8)

up to a nonzero scalar. This is an invariant alternating construction, not a choice of actual coefficient values. Formula (5) also proves that the entire kernel for every n is generated by t6 times the indicated negative forms.

## 2. Exact second normal order along L

Fix a vertex lambda. In its hyperplane H_lambda, the section t6 restricts, away from R, to the distinguished section s_lambda of F_H(2), multiplied by a nonvanishing fourth power of the negative normal coordinate. Thus their local zero ideals agree at the vertex.

NONZERO-VERTEX-EVALUATION.md makes the prior rank-one vertex-curve theorem unconditional for every lambda. Its degree-six/local-Bezout argument proves that the distinguished section has exact local initial ideal

    (f2,c_lambda),

where f2 is a nonzero quadratic, c_lambda is the actual determinant cubic, and the two initial forms are relatively prime. The three affine tangent coordinates in H_lambda at its vertex are precisely the three normal coordinates to L in P4. Consequently t6 lies in I_L^2 F(6), and its second normal jet is nonzero at every point of L.

This also identifies the quadratic intrinsically. The actual split and normal bundle are

    F_L(6)=O_L(9) directsum O_L(2),
    N*_(L/P4)=V* tensor O_L(-1).

The second-normal section space is therefore

    Sym^2 V* tensor [H0(O_L(7)) directsum H0(O_L)].

The first summand has odd binary central character, hence no invariant; the second has exactly one invariant, the quadratic q0 in Sym^2 V*. Since t6 is invariant and its second jet is everywhere nonzero, that jet is a nonzero scalar multiple of q0 in the lower line. In particular no nonzero multiple t6 h with h a negative form can vanish to third order along all of L. This completes (1).

No second-normal scalar is inferred merely from the existence of t6: its nonvanishing uses the previously proved exact local multiplicity six.

## 3. Exact global adjoint division

The actual all-parameter square theorem gives

    pi_*p*B(3)=Sym^2 Y=A O_T(-6).

Squaring the vertex quotient in (5) gives an exact sequence of bundles

    0 -> O_T(-4) tensor Y
      -> Sym^2 Y -> O_T(-4) -> 0,

or

    0 -> W O_T(-7) -> A O_T(-6) -> O_T(-4) -> 0.     (9)

The first map is multiplication by the actual distinguished section line. This description holds at every parameter: for a two-space mapping onto a line, the kernel of its symmetric-square quotient is the product of its kernel line with the full two-space.

Twisting (9) by n and taking H0 proves the first division identity in (2), with the actual product t6. Every such product has order at least two along L. If t6 sigma has order at least three, its quadratic normal term is the product of the nonzero q0 jet with sigma|L. Multiplication by a nonzero vector in the symmetric algebra of a rank-two bundle is injective, so this term vanishes identically exactly when sigma|L=0. Formula (1) then forces sigma=t6 h. Conversely t6^2 h vanishes to fourth order, and its fourth normal term is the nonzero q0^2 term times h. A nonzero negative form is not identically zero on L. Therefore no nonzero such product has fifth normal order. This proves all of (2).

In particular,

    H0(I_R^7 I_L B(10))=t6 H0(I_R^3 F(5))=t6 W,
    H0(I_R^8 I_L^4 B(11))=C t6^2.                  (10)

These equalities package the vertex-factor part of the hyperplane squares by one genuine global section. They do not determine the residual smoothing operator.

## 4. The normal cone along L

On a small parameter neighborhood choose a second hyperplane section nonzero at the vertex. Using it as one local frame vector puts the distinguished-section ideal in the form

    (h,c_lambda),

where h is inversion-even of order two and its quadratic initial form is a unit multiple of q0. The other generator is the exact determinant cone, up to a unit. Its cubic initial form is c_lambda. The initial forms q0,c_lambda form a regular sequence, uniformly in lambda: q0 is an irreducible smooth conic and c_lambda is a nonzero product of line equations, never divisible by q0.

The relative initial-regular-sequence criterion therefore identifies the projectivized normal cone with

    (q0,c_lambda) subset P(N_(L/P4))=R x L.

The actual Cartan/residual-chord theorem proves

    c_lambda|C0 = nonzero scalar times Phi(lambda,-)^2,

where Phi is the actual (3,3) equation of B_can. Hence the displayed scheme is exactly 2 B_can. This proof retains the doubled scheme structure, including the simple ramification fibers, rather than only its reduced support.

## 5. Regularity and the ruled support

Outside R, the blowup is an isomorphism and projection to T is defined. The zero fiber in each H_lambda minus R is the established pure one-dimensional vertex-section curve. Hence the zero locus outside R has dimension two. Inside R the section vanishes identically, and R itself has dimension two. Thus Z has no divisorial component anywhere in P4.

The two local components of a section of a rank-two bundle on the smooth fourfold P4 consequently form a regular sequence at their zero points. Thus Z is a pure locally complete-intersection surface, with no embedded components, and

    [Z]=c2(F(6))=4+6(-1)+6^2=34

in degree.

The reduced support of every distinguished vertex curve is a union of lines through its vertex. Its projective tangent directions are exactly the support of (q0,c_lambda), hence the fiber of B_can over lambda. The normal-cone description therefore proves

    Z_red=R union Sigma,

where Sigma is the family of joining lines indexed by B_can.

For the normalization, use the quotient convention for

    P_(B_can)(O(M) directsum O(2N)).

The two negative coordinates generate O(M), and the three positive coordinates generate O(2N). They define the morphism whose ruling lines join the indicated point of L to the indicated point of C0. Off its two distinguished sections both coordinates are nonzero, so the point determines its pair in L x C0 and therefore its unique point of B_can. Over either distinguished section the fibers are finite, being fibers of one of the degree-three rulings of B_can. The map is thus finite and birational onto Sigma. Its smooth source is the normalization.

If xi is the tautological class, then

    deg(Sigma)=xi^2=deg(M)+deg(2N)=3+6=9.             (11)

## 6. The exact cycle multiplicities

At a point of R, the initial fourth-normal term of t6 is the binary quartic pair obtained by restricting the actual family ell^4 of distinguished sections to that point. This is immediate from the blowup description: on H_lambda the fourth-normal coefficient is s_lambda|R.

If the point lies outside C0, no distinguished section vanishes there, because all supporting lines of its vertex curve have directions on C0. Thus the two leading binary quartics have no common projective zero. They form a regular sequence in the two normal variables. At the generic point of R, the local complete-intersection length is therefore 4*4=16.

The surface Sigma is irreducible, and there are no other support components. Combining its degree nine, the total degree34, and the multiplicity16 of R gives

    34=16+9 mult_Sigma(Z),

so mult_Sigma(Z)=2. This proves (3). It does not identify the complete scheme structure at R intersect Sigma=C0, or a global Ferrand structure on the singular surface Sigma.

## 7. A cycle consequence on smooth invariant-block quadrics

Let q_- be a nondegenerate binary negative quadratic, let s!=0, and set

    Q_s=(q_-+s q0=0).

This is a smooth quadric. The surface Sigma lies in q0=0, since its positive coordinates are proportional to points of C0. On its normalization, let C_+ be the distinguished section mapping three-to-one onto C0. The negative-coordinate factor vanishes simply along C_+, so the pullback divisor of Q_s is

    2 C_+ + pi_B* [pi_M* div(q_-)].

If pi_M*div(q_-)=sum_b m_b b is the degree-six divisor on B_can, pushing forward gives

    Sigma . Q_s = 6[C0] + sum_b m_b [line_b].         (12)

The two negative roots are distinct because q_- is nondegenerate. A root outside the twelve branch parameters contributes three distinct lines, each with m_b=1. A branch root contributes two distinct lines with multiplicities 2 and 1. Thus (12) retains all exceptional multiplicities. Neither R nor Sigma is contained in Q_s, so the restricted section t6|Q_s is regular. Intersecting (3) with Q_s yields its exact fundamental cycle

    [Z(t6|Q_s)] = 28[C0] + 2 sum_b m_b [line_b].      (13)

Its degree is 28*2+2*6=68, as required by c2(F_Qs(6)). Formula (13) is only a cycle statement; it does not assert equality with a union of chosen thickened curves.
