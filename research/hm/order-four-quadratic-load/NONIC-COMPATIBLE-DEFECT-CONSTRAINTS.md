# Nonic compatible defects: four-span exclusion and at most four alpha centers

## Setting

Let D be an integral complete intersection of two cubics in P3. Write H=O_D(1); then deg H=9, p_a(D)=10 and omega_D=2H, including when D is singular. Let Z be25 reduced smooth marked points and assume

R=3H-Z has degree2 and h0(R)=0.

In the active b=2 nonic branch these facts come from ACM restriction and the two-dimensional cubic ideal space. Put M=4H-Z=H+R, of degree11. Consider the linear vector space K of six-tuples of antisymmetric sections F_ij of M satisfying x_i F_jk-x_j F_ik+x_k F_ij=0. The span of a tuple means the subspace of H0(M) generated by its six entries. This is symbolic compatibility, not a selected numerical evaluation.

## 1. A general four-span inequality

For an integral nondegenerate degree-d curve whose ideal sheaf is generated in degrees at most s, any compatible tuple spanning four dimensions must satisfy

deg M >= 2d-2s.                                    (1)

Reason. The four-dimensional coefficient space in the six-dimensional bivector space has an annihilator pencil of alternating forms. An identically singular pencil cuts the Klein quadric in two planes, contradicting an integral coefficient image spanning the whole coefficient P3. For a pencil with two distinct Pfaffian roots, its universal line-through-x assignment has four quadratic coordinates ac,ad,bc,bd, with base the two skew lines (a,b)(c,d). For a double root its four coordinates can be chosen a^2,ab,b^2,ac+bd, with base the locally CM double line (a^2,ab,b^2,ac+bd). In both cases the base B is pure locally CM of degree2. These universal normal forms and the relation argument are proved in FOUR-DIMENSIONAL-OCTIC-DEFECT-EXCLUSION.md; no actual source coefficients are substituted.

The generic compatible tuple is a rational scalar times those quadratic coordinates. Because they generate I_B, this gives an injection of rank-one torsion-free sheaves

I_(B intersect D,D)(2H) -> M.

Consequently deg M>=2d-length(B intersect D). Since the degree-s ideal system has base D and no component line of B lies in D, a degree-s equation of D can be chosen nonzero on every component of B. Local Cohen--Macaulayness makes it a nonzerodivisor, cutting length2s on B. Thus length(B intersect D)<=2s, proving(1). This argument includes nonreduced B and singular D.

For the nonic, d=9 and s=3, so four-span would require deg M>=12, contradicting deg M=11. Thus no compatible tuple has span four. Spans one or two are excluded by the existing small-defect theorem for a nondegenerate curve with no quadric. A nonzero tuple therefore has span3,5 or6, subject to h0(M).

## 2. Three-span must be an alpha projection

The projective coefficient image of a three-span tuple lies in a coefficient plane and on the Klein quadric. If the whole plane is not contained in the Klein quadric, the integral spanning image is a smooth plane conic. The ruled incidence of its lines has hyperplane self-intersection two, so its swept surface has degree one or two. Since x lies on the line represented by the tuple, D would lie on a plane or quadric, impossible for a cubic complete intersection. Hence the coefficient plane lies on the Klein quadric.

A beta plane would put all the represented lines, and therefore D, in a fixed plane. Thus the coefficient plane is alpha: all represented lines pass through a unique point p in P3. Up to constant coordinate changes, the tuple is a common rational scalar times the three linear coordinates vanishing at p. The sheaf relation argument yields

I_(p,D) H -> M,

where I_(p,D)=O_D if p is not on D. This is nonzero and injective. If p were outside D, the map would be a nonzero section of M-H=R, contradicting h0(R)=0. Therefore p belongs to D. No smoothness of p is inferred.

After tensoring by H^-1, such a tuple with fixed center p is exactly a nonzero element of Hom(I_p,R), up to the fixed harmless scalar choices of alpha coordinates. The three entries remain independent because the three linear forms through p have no constant relation on nondegenerate D and the scalar is generically nonzero.

## 3. The alpha centers are precisely basepoints of a seven-section adjoint system

Define N=omega_D R^-1=Z-H, of degree16. Riemann--Roch and h0(R)=0 give h1(R)=7 and h0(N)=7; also h1(N)=0.

For every closed point p of D, including singular points,

Hom(I_p,R) is nonzero iff every section of N vanishes at p.        (2)

Whenever nonzero it has dimension one.

Proof. Apply Hom(-,R) to 0->I_p->O_D->k_p->0. Since D is an integral Gorenstein curve, Hom(k_p,R)=0 and Ext^1(k_p,R) is one-dimensional, including at singular p. The relevant global exact sequence is

0 -> H0(R)=0 -> Hom(I_p,R) -> Ext^1(k_p,R) -> H1(R).

Serre duality identifies the transpose of the last arrow with evaluation H0(N)->N|p, up to the intrinsic one-dimensional identifications. Hence its kernel is nonzero exactly when evaluation is zero, and has dimension one then. This proves(2). The use of the Gorenstein property is explicit; no assertion of smoothness is needed.

## 4. There are at most four candidate centers, scheme-theoretically

Let B be the base scheme of the complete seven-dimensional system H0(N), defined by the image sheaf L=I_B N of its evaluation map. Since D is integral and the system is nonzero, B is finite; let b=length B. Every global section of N factors through L, so h0(L)=7. Its degree is16-b, and Riemann--Roch gives h1(L)=b.

If b>0, Serre duality gives dim Hom(L,omega_D)=b. Multiplication

H0(L) tensor Hom(L,omega_D) -> H0(omega_D)

has no zero divisors, because the curve is integral and both nonzero factors are generically nonzero. The Hopf dimension inequality therefore gives

7+b-1 <= h0(omega_D)=10,

so b<=4. For b=0 the same bound holds trivially. This proves the total scheme length of the possible alpha-center base scheme is at most four, not merely a bound at smooth points.

By(2), each point of its support contributes exactly one one-dimensional line of compatible alpha tuples. Thus all three-span tuples belong to at most four fixed vector lines in K. This does not prove those lines nonempty without the corresponding basepoint condition, nor exclude higher-span tuples.

## 5. Consequence when h0(M)<=4

In this range every compatible tuple has span at most four. The small-defect theorem excludes spans1,2; section1 excludes span4. Every nonzero tuple therefore lies on one of the at-most-four alpha lines just described. A vector space over C cannot be a finite union of proper vector subspaces. Consequently

dim K <= 1.

If h0(M)=2, K=0. If h0(M)=3 or4 and K is nonzero, there is a unique center p supporting all nonzero compatible tuples, and every tuple is a scalar multiple of a single alpha tuple. This uniqueness concerns the tuples that occur; N can in principle have other basepoints whose associated tuple would then force another line and contradict dim K<=1. In fact in this h0(M)<=4 range N therefore has at most one distinct basepoint whenever it has any.

If h0(M)=5 or6, tuples of span5 or6 are not addressed by this argument. Neither the nonic carrier nor these larger-span possibilities are excluded here. No descent, full residual injectivity, source lift, or Q4 conclusion is claimed.

## Actual-family sharpening for h0(M)<=4

In the active family, use the already established differential-descent input: if all fixed-ambient-point derivatives of both cubic generators vanish on the integral carrier, its five-dimensional incidence descends to an invariant degree-nine surface in P4. Such a surface contradicts the Heisenberg degree divisibility by five. The generic complete-intersection carrier is scheme-theoretically generated by those two cubics, so that input applies here.

Therefore the actual two-generator differential-symbol map cannot be zero. Combining with dim K<=1 above gives exact rank one and dim K=1. In particular the case h0(M)=2 is excluded in the actual nonic family. When h0(M)=3 or4 there is a unique common alpha center p, and the two-dimensional cubic generator space has a canonical one-dimensional kernel under its differential symbol.

Canonicity is unchanged by a moving choice of cubic basis: derivative terms from the change-of-basis matrix multiply the cubic generators themselves and vanish on D. Thus the symbol transforms linearly by that basis change, and its kernel line is intrinsic. Its generator need not lift to an ambient cubic equation: vanishing of a symbol on the carrier is not, by itself, the missing global lifting statement.

The canonical center is generically outside Z. Indeed whether it equals each marked line-intersection section is a closed equality condition on a suitable open; the generic selected label set is constant and Heisenberg-invariant. Transitivity on25 labels makes it empty or all25. A unique point cannot equal25 distinct marked points, so it is empty. No smoothness of the center outside Z is asserted.
