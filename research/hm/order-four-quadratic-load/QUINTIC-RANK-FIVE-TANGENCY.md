# Rank-five quintic restriction forces a jumping plane

## The actual geometric setting

Let G=H0(F(3)), dim G=4, and let C be the degeneracy locus of its evaluation. Let P≅P2 avoid C. Put E=F(3)|P. Then

    G tensor O_P → E →0,
    c1(E)=5h,  c2(E)=10h².

The six maximal minors are the actual six saturated quintics. Their restrictions define the classifying morphism f:P→Gr(2,G) of two-dimensional quotients, and E=f*Q for the UNIVERSAL QUOTIENT Q. Its Plücker polarization pulls back to det(E)=O_P(5). Thus f is finite onto its image. The earlier restriction theorem proves the minors span a space of dimension at least five.

We prove the following further result without selected evaluation:

- If their restriction rank is exactly five, the unique Plücker hyperplane is tangent, and the unique relation is a decomposable element s∧t of Lambda²G.
- The saturated image of the two corresponding sections is O_P(3), and the quotient is I_Z(2), where Z is a complete intersection of two quadrics, of length four.
- In particular, rank five is impossible on a Boralevi-good plane. Consequently on every avoiding good plane the restriction rank is six. Such planes form a nonempty open, so the generic restriction rank is six.

The last conclusion uses a complete two-generator quotient argument below, not an inference from tangency alone.

## 1. Why the hyperplane must be tangent

If the rank is five, the Plücker image spans a unique P4 in P5 and lies in its hyperplane section of the smooth Plücker quadric Q4.

Suppose this section were a smooth quadric threefold Q3. Every surface divisor in Q3 has even degree. One can see the required divisor-class statement directly, without a classification: write Q3 as x0x1+x2x3+x4²=0. Its open set x0≠0 is A3, whose divisor class group is zero. The boundary x0=0 is an irreducible hyperplane quadric cone. The localization sequence for Weil divisor classes therefore says that the class group of Q3 is generated by that hyperplane class; smoothness identifies Weil and Cartier divisors. Hence every integral surface has degree a multiple of deg(Q3)=2.

The image Y=f(P) is an integral surface. The degree formula gives

    25 = (f*O_Y(1))² = deg(f:P→Y) deg(Y),

contradicting even deg(Y). Therefore the hyperplane section is singular. A hyperplane section of a smooth nondegenerate quadric is singular exactly when the hyperplane is tangent; here its quadratic form has rank four, so it is the quadric threefold cone with one vertex, not an arbitrary more singular quadric.

## 2. The relation is a dependent pair of actual sections

The quotient convention puts the Plücker image in P((Lambda²G)*). A linear equation of that projective space is an element r∈Lambda²G. The dual quadric consists precisely of the nonzero decomposable elements, characterized by r∧r=0. Thus the tangent relation is r=s∧t for independent vectors s,t∈G. Its vanishing on P says that the two corresponding sections of E have identically zero wedge, hence generically rank-one image.

Their restricted sections are linearly independent. In fact ALL four sections in G remain independent on P. Otherwise at most three sections would generate E. A surjection 3O_P→E has a line-bundle kernel O_P(-5), giving c(E)=1/(1-5h), whose second Chern number is 25 rather than 10. Fewer than three generators are also impossible, since two generators would trivialize E. This proves the asserted independence without a separate general-plane incidence hypothesis.

## 3. Saturation and the exact zero-scheme calculation

Saturate the image of O_P²→E. Its saturation L is a line bundle O_P(a): it is a saturated rank-one subsheaf of a vector bundle on the smooth surface P, hence reflexive by the depth lemma, and every rank-one reflexive sheaf on P2 is a line bundle. Its quotient is rank-one torsion-free, hence

    0→O_P(a)→E→I_Z(5-a)→0,

for a zero-dimensional scheme Z, possibly empty. The two independent restricted sections factor through L, so h0(O_P(a))>=2 and a>=1.

Extend s,t to a basis of G. The other TWO sections generate I_Z(5-a), since the first two map to zero in that quotient. Write d=5-a. Global generation forces d>=0. If d=0, a nonzero ideal I_Z generated by constants must be O_P, so Z is empty; then c2(E)=0, a contradiction. Thus d>0.

The two generating sections are degree-d polynomials with no common divisor: a common divisor would be a divisorial base component, whereas I_Z has only zero-dimensional support. They consequently form a regular sequence, and Z is exactly their scheme-theoretic complete intersection. Therefore length(Z)=d². Chern classes in the preceding exact sequence give

    10 = a d + length(Z) = a d + d² = 5d.

It follows that d=2, a=3, and length(Z)=4. This conclusion includes the zero scheme with its scheme structure; reducedness is not asserted.

The original two sections of O_P(3) are independent cubics. No claim that their common zero scheme equals Z is made: Z is the zero scheme of the saturated inclusion, equivalently of its associated section of E(-3), whereas the original pair can have additional common zeros.

## 4. Good planes exclude this necessary configuration

On a Boralevi-good plane,

    0→3O_P(-3)→5O_P(-2)→F|P→0.

Twisting by one gives H0(F|P(1))=0, equivalently H0(E(-2))=0. But an inclusion O_P(3)→E would give a nonzero section of E(-3), and multiplying it by a nonzero linear form would give a section of E(-2), a contradiction.

Equivalently, the initially suggested good-plane line saturation would have to be O_P(1): independence gives a>=1, while H0(E(-2))=0 gives a<=1. That possibility would give length(Z)=10−1·4=6, but generation of I_Z(4) by the other two sections would make Z a quartic-quartic complete intersection of length16. This is the same contradiction before solving for a.

The inclusion O_P(3)→E is equivalently O_P→F|P, so rank five forces H0(F|P)≠0 and is impossible on any slope-stable restricted plane (its slope is -1/2). In particular rank five is impossible on every good plane avoiding C, and the previously established lower bound five improves to exactly six there. Since both the avoiding-plane condition and the Boralevi resolution condition are nonempty opens of the irreducible Grassmannian, they intersect.

For such a pencil, H0(I_(P union C)(5))=0. In the binary Rao presentation this removes degree-five syzygies; it does not remove the later syzygies or prove quadratic residual injectivity. The previously obtained generic bound b<=7 is unchanged.
