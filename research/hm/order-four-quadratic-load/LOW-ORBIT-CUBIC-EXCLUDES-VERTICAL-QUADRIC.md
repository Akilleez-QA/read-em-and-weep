# A low-orbit cubic excludes the remaining vertical quadric image

## New conclusion

The remaining nondominant primitive twist-two alternative is impossible. Its forced cubic on Q would have exact order two at forty-five marked points and be nonzero at the other five. Injective cubic evaluation forces that cubic to have projective SO(5)-orbit dimension at most four. The classification and invariant calculations below force every such cubic section to be a cube of a linear section. A cube cannot have order exactly two anywhere.

Therefore every primitive twist-two eigenquotient projection is dominant. The earlier projection bounds then apply: degree between five and ten, at most five positive base-point multiplicities, and each multiplicity at most two. This does not exclude the dominant case or prove full Q4.

## 1. The actual cubic evaluation gives a small orbit

Let g be the cubic supplied by TWIST-TWO-VERTICAL-IMAGE-CLASSIFICATION.md after QUADRIC-JETS-EXCLUDE-HIGH-VERTICAL-DEGREES.md has removed image degrees five and ten. Let J be the five marked points where g is nonzero. At each of the other forty-five points, g has order exactly two.

For every infinitesimal automorphism xi of Q, the derivative xi(g) vanishes at those forty-five points. This follows from the vanishing of both g and its first derivatives, and is independent of a local trivialization of O_Q(3). The same holds for g itself. On the simultaneous-good-flanks open,

    H0(O_Q(3)) -> H0(O_Z(3))

is injective, equivalently H0(I_Z,Q(3))=0. Thus the subspace spanned by g and all xi(g) injects into the five coordinates indexed by J and has dimension at most five.

The automorphism Lie algebra is so(5), of dimension ten. Hence the projective orbit of [g] has dimension at most four, and its projective stabilizer Lie algebra has dimension at least six.

## 2. The precise classification input

Use so(5,C)=sp(4,C). Douglas--Repka, Subalgebras of the rank two semisimple Lie algebras, Tables 5--6, printed pages 13--14, list all proper subalgebras of dimension at least six. They are: a Borel algebra; so(4)=sl2 directsum sl2; the derived algebras of the two maximal parabolics; and the two maximal parabolics themselves. The six-dimensional derived parabolics have radicals respectively abelian of dimension three and Heisenberg of dimension three.

## 3. Explicit cubic invariant checks

We identify sections with homogeneous degree-three functions on the affine quadric cone, using the invariant quadratic form to identify the vector representation with its dual. A nilpotent radical acts nilpotently in this finite-dimensional representation, so it acts by zero on any stable one-dimensional subspace. A semisimple algebra likewise acts by zero on a stable line, since it has no nontrivial character.

### Isotropic-line parabolic and its derived algebra

Write the cone equation as 2xy+z1^2+z2^2+z3^2=0. The three commuting radical vector fields are

    x*d/dzi - zi*d/dy, i=1,2,3.

On x!=0 they independently translate the three z coordinates; y is determined by the equation. Their common invariant functions there are C[x,x^-1]. The homogeneous degree-three invariants which extend over the cone are therefore precisely multiples of x^3. This deals with both the parabolic and its derived algebra. It also deals with a Borel, which contains this three-dimensional radical after choosing compatible positive roots.

### Isotropic-two-plane parabolic and its derived algebra

Write the equation as 2x1*y1+2x2*y2+z^2=0. Its Heisenberg radical is generated by

    D1=x1*d/dz-z*d/dy1,
    D2=x2*d/dz-z*d/dy2,
    D3=x1*d/dy2-x2*d/dy1.

On x1!=0, D1 removes z and D3 removes y2, while y1 is determined by the equation. Consequently the common invariant ring on this chart is C[x1,x1^-1,x2]. Its globally regular homogeneous invariants are polynomials in x1,x2: a denominator x1 cannot remain because x1 is prime in the quadric cone ring and its divisor maps dominantly to the x2-line. Thus the cubic invariants form Sym^3(C^2).

The Levi sl2 acts as the usual binary-cubic representation, which has no invariant line. Both the parabolic and its derived algebra contain that sl2. Hence neither stabilizes a cubic line.

### The so(4) algebra

This is the stabilizer of a nonisotropic vector. Write the quadratic form as t^2+q_W on its four-dimensional orthogonal complement. The invariant polynomials under SO(4) are generated by t and q_W. On the quadric cone q_W=-t^2, so its degree-three invariants are exactly multiples of t^3.

The full so(5) stabilizer cannot occur: orthogonal invariant polynomials are polynomials in the quadratic form and give no nonzero odd-degree section on its zero quadric.

These cases prove that every nonzero cubic section whose projective stabilizer has dimension at least six is a cube of a linear section.

## 4. Contradiction and remaining scope

Apply section 3 to g from section 1. It is a nonzero scalar multiple of ell^3. The order of ell^3 at any point where it vanishes is three times the positive integer order of ell, never two. This contradicts the exact double vanishing at forty-five marked points.

Thus the vertical quadric image is excluded; together with the prior quadric-jet exclusions, the entire nondominant primitive twist-two branch is removed. No assumption of normality, reducedness, isolated singularities, or K3 node bounds for the cubic divisor was needed. The dominant twist-two branch remains open.
