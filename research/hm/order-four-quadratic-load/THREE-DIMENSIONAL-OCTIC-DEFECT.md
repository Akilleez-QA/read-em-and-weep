# A three-dimensional octic defect forces projection from one smooth point

## Setup and conclusion

Let D⊂P3 be integral, nondegenerate, of degree eight and arithmetic genus seven, with no quadric equation. Put H=O_D(1). Let M be a line bundle of degree seven, and suppose antisymmetric sections F_ij∈H0(M), indexed by 0≤i,j≤3, span a vector space of dimension at most three and obey

    x_i F_jk−x_j F_ik+x_k F_ij=0.

If some F_ij is nonzero, their span has dimension exactly three and there is a smooth point p∈D such that

    M≅H(−p).

The three-dimensional section space generated by the F_ij is the basepoint-free projection system from p, under this isomorphism. For the ACM octic, h0(H)=4, so it equals all of H0(M).

For M=O_D(4)(−Z), this forces Z∼3H+p. The point p is unique, and if this configuration varies canonically with the HM family it lies outside Z generically. This is a necessary condition, not an exclusion of this alpha-type alternative.

## 1. The coefficient plane lies in the Klein quadric

The two-dimensional-defect lemma shows that a nonzero compatible family cannot span a space of dimension at most two. Choose a basis s1,s2,s3 of its actual span W and express the six F_ij as constant linear combinations of these sections. Equivalently there is an injective linear map W*→Λ²V, where V is the four-dimensional coordinate-vector space with basis indexed by x0,...,x3. Injectivity follows because otherwise the six sections would span a proper subspace of W.

At points where the sections do not all vanish, they define a rational map D⇢P(W*)=P2. Its image spans P2, since s1,s2,s3 are independent. Under the coefficient embedding, its value c(x)∈Λ²V satisfies x∧c(x)=0 by the compatibility identities. For nonzero x, the kernel of c↦x∧c is x∧V. Hence c(x)=x∧y for some y; it is decomposable and its corresponding two-plane in V contains x.

Thus the integral nondegenerate image curve Γ in this coefficient plane lies on the Klein quadric Gr(2,V)⊂P(Λ²V). Either the whole plane lies on the Klein quadric or Γ is an integral plane conic. Indeed restriction of the Klein equation is a homogeneous quadratic; if nonzero, its integral nondegenerate curve component must be a smooth conic. Reducible or doubled conics have no integral nondegenerate reduced curve component.

## 2. A conic of lines would put D on a quadric

Suppose Γ is a smooth plane conic. It parametrizes a nonconstant family of lines in P3. Let S_Γ denote the rank-two tautological subbundle on Γ, and consider the ruled incidence surface P(S_Γ)→P(V). Its tautological hyperplane bundle has self-intersection deg(det S_Γ*)=deg O_Γ(1)=2.

The image of this ruled surface has dimension two: if it were a curve, every line in the varying family would be an irreducible component of that fixed curve, impossible for infinitely many distinct lines. Therefore the map is generically finite onto an integral surface T, and the projection formula gives

    (generic degree)·deg T=2.

In particular T has degree one or two. The relation x∈the line represented by c(x) places D inside T. A plane or quadric surface containing D contradicts the absence of a quadric equation. Hence the conic alternative is impossible.

This argument uses only the degree-two Plücker polarization and includes any degeneracy of the ruled surface's image; it does not assume that the image is a smooth quadric.

## 3. Only an alpha plane remains

Every plane contained in Gr(2,4) is either alpha (all two-planes contain a fixed one-dimensional subspace a⊂V) or beta (all are contained in a fixed three-dimensional subspace B⊂V).

In the beta case, x belongs to B for every general x∈D, so D lies in the plane P(B), contradiction. Therefore the coefficient plane is alpha, with fixed point p=P(a)∈P3.

After a projective coordinate change take p=[1:0:0:0]. The coefficient bivectors then have the form

    c(x)=e0∧(s1(x)e1+s2(x)e2+s3(x)e3),

after a change of basis of W. The relation x∧c(x)=0 means that at the generic point of D the triples (s1,s2,s3) and (x1,x2,x3) are proportional. Write the common rational factor as α. Thus si=α xi generically.

If p∉D, the three xi generate H everywhere. The formula xi↦si defines a nonzero sheaf map H→M: all relations are respected because they hold generically and M is torsion-free. Such a map is injective, with zero-dimensional cokernel, forcing deg M≥deg H. This contradicts 7<8.

Hence p∈D. The three xi now generate the ideal sheaf I_{p,D}⊗H, of degree 8−1=7. The same relation argument constructs an injection

    I_{p,D}⊗H → M.

Its source and target have equal degree. The cokernel therefore has length zero, so this is an isomorphism. Since M and H are line bundles, I_{p,D} is invertible. In the one-dimensional local domain O_D,p its maximal ideal is consequently principal; the local ring is regular. Thus p is smooth and M≅H(−p).

Moreover the si generate M, because the xi generate I_{p,D}⊗H. This proves the basepoint-free projection-system assertion, rather than assuming it.

## 4. Uniqueness and the marked-family interpretation

For the ACM octic, h0(H)=4 and H is very ample, so h0(H(−p))=3. Thus h0(M)=3 whenever this nonzero three-dimensional compatible defect occurs.

The point p is unique. If M≅H(−p') for another smooth point p', then O_D(p)≅O_D(p'). A degree-one line bundle on an integral curve of positive arithmetic genus has at most one section: two sections would give a basepoint-free degree-one map to P1 and hence an isomorphism with P1. Therefore its effective Cartier divisor is unique, giving p=p'.

In the actual family M=O_D(4)(−Z), uniqueness makes p intrinsic. The subset of the 25 normalizer-transitive labels equal to this single point is invariant and cannot contain all 25 distinct points. Hence p∉Z generically. The isomorphism of line bundles gives Z∼3H+p, but does not claim an effective decomposition Z=(cubic cut)+p; indeed p avoids Z.

## Boundary

This theorem handles compatible defects whose actual span is at most three. If h0(M)=4 and the derivative sections span a proper three-space, the same proof would force h0(M)=3 for the ACM octic, a contradiction. Thus in the four-section case any nonzero compatible derivative system must span all four dimensions.

The remaining possibilities are zero compatible derivatives (giving the previously established surface descent), the stated projection-from-a-smooth-point alternative when h0(M)=3, or genuinely four-dimensional compatible defects. No selected coefficient, rank, or source-instance computation and no external write were used.

## Additional consequence: the two-cubic differential symbol has rank one

In the actual b=2 family, consider the linear map sending a cubic f∈V_t=H0(I_Zt(3)) to its compatible six-tuple of fixed-point derivatives, restricted to D_t and divided by Z_t. This map is well-defined independently of how f is extended to a local parameter-dependent basis: changing an extension by parameter-dependent multiples of the cubic equations adds derivative terms that are scalar multiples of those equations, and these vanish on D_t. Thus the resulting map is intrinsic at fixed t.

Assume the only surviving alpha configuration described above. Any nonzero such six-tuple has the same center p, because p is uniquely determined by H and M. At each general x of D, both tuples therefore lie on the same one-dimensional bivector line span{x∧p}. Hence any two nonzero tuples differ by a rational function on D.

The first tuple's six components generate M everywhere, by Section 3. Their pointwise proportionality makes the rational function regular locally wherever one component of the first tuple is nonzero: divide the corresponding component of the second tuple by it. These quotients agree at the generic point, hence glue to a global regular function on integral projective D. It is constant.

Therefore the image of the differential-symbol map from the two-dimensional cubic space has dimension at most one. If all derivative tuples were zero, the established differential descent would give an invariant surface of degree eight, impossible. Consequently its image has dimension exactly one and its kernel is a canonical line in the cubic space.

In particular there is a unique cubic equation h_t up to scalar, varying rationally with the generic parameter, such that every fixed-point differential quartic of h_t vanishes on D_t. This does not assert that h_t is the restriction of an ambient cubic: vanishing has only been established along the carrier D_t, not along the whole cubic hypersurface V(h_t). Any further lifting argument must retain that distinction.
