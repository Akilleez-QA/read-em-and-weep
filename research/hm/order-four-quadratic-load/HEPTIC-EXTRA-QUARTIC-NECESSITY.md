# The marked ACM heptic necessarily has an extra quartic

## Statement and scope

Work over the complex numbers with the actual HM union C of 25 skew lines in P4 and its generic reduced hyperplane section Z. Assume the surviving three-cubic curve-base branch has integral marked carrier D of degree seven. The established normal form gives

0 → O_P3(−4) ⊕ O_P3(−5) → 3O_P3(−3) → I_D → 0,

with D the entire cubic base scheme, p_a(D)=5, and Z contained in the smooth locus of D. Then

1 ≤ h0(O_D(4)(−Z)) ≤ 2,

and consequently

h0(I_Z(4)) = 12 or 13.

In particular, the quartic equations through Z cannot all be linear multiples of the three cubic equations. This is a necessary condition, not an exclusion of the heptic branch or a complete Q4 repair.

## 1. Family and constant ranks

Restrict the dual projective space to a dense open T of dimension four on which Z_t consists of 25 distinct points, the cubic kernel has rank three, and the cubic base scheme D_t is geometrically integral with the displayed resolution. Generic flatness and openness of geometric integrality permit this restriction. The total family D_T is integral after removing components not dominating T and shrinking further; its dimension is five.

The full cubic kernel V_t is intrinsic. Its multiplication map

H0(O_P3(1)) ⊗ V_t → H0(O_P3(4))

has rank eleven: there are twelve products and precisely one linear syzygy. Its image is H0(I_Dt(4)), by the resolution. After shrinking T it is a rank-eleven subbundle. In a local trivialization, eleven independent product columns can be selected algebraically; a nonzero minor is inverted. This is only an abstract constant-rank argument, not a selected source evaluation.

Suppose for contradiction that I_Zt(4)=I_Dt(4) generically. Shrink so this equality holds throughout T. Every algebraic family of quartics vanishing on Z_t is then an algebraic family of linear combinations of the three cubic generators. The one-dimensional syzygy does not obstruct this: the multiplication map onto its rank-eleven image has a local splitting, though not a canonical one.

## 2. Fixed-point differentiation preserves the family ideal

On an affine chart of hyperplanes write

H_t: X4 = t0 X0 + t1 X1 + t2 X2 + t3 X3.

Use homogeneous coordinates x=[X0:X1:X2:X3] on H_t, and choose a local algebraic basis f_a(t,x), a=1,2,3, of its cubic kernel.

At a marked point p(t)=H_t intersect L on a fixed component line L of C, a parameter tangent v satisfying Σ v_j x_j(p)=0 keeps the ambient point fixed to first order. Indeed L meets H_t transversely. Differentiating f_a(t,p(t))=0 in these directions shows that the parameter-gradient vector of f_a evaluated at p is proportional to (x0(p),...,x3(p)). Hence each quartic

x_i ∂_(t_j) f_a − x_j ∂_(t_i) f_a

vanishes on all of Z_t. By the hypothesized equality and the regular subbundle in Section 1, it belongs to the family ideal generated by the f_a, with regular linear-multiple coefficients on a suitable open.

On the chart x0=1, put

δ_j = ∂_(t_j) − x_j ∂_(t_0),  j=1,2,3.

These three independent derivations preserve the ideal (f_1,f_2,f_3). They are therefore tangent to D_T at its general smooth point. They fix the ambient point: its coordinates are x1,x2,x3 and w=t0+Σ_(j=1)^3 t_j x_j, all annihilated by δ_j. The same reasoning on each x_i≠0 chart covers the general point, without assuming a chosen coordinate is nonzero everywhere.

## 3. Differential descent produces an invariant degree-seven surface

Let π:D_T→P4 be ambient evaluation, and let S be its reduced image closure. The three independent tangent directions in Section 2 lie in the kernel of dπ, so characteristic-zero generic differential rank gives dim S≤2. Conversely every fiber of ambient evaluation in full point/hyperplane incidence lies in the P3 of hyperplanes through that point. Thus dim S≥dim D_T−3=2. Consequently S is an integral surface.

The closure of D_T in full projective incidence is contained in the full incidence I_S of points of S and hyperplanes containing them. This incidence is an irreducible projective-three bundle over S, so both spaces have dimension five. The containment is therefore equality as reduced closed sets. It follows that for general H the reduced support of S intersect H is precisely D_H.

A general hyperplane section of an integral surface in characteristic zero is generically reduced, and its degree is the degree of the surface. The integral reduced curve support here is D_H of degree seven, with generic multiplicity one. Hence deg S=7. Possible isolated non-Cohen–Macaulay phenomena of S are irrelevant to this degree calculation; no normality or Cohen–Macaulay assumption on S is used.

The cubic kernel, its entire base D_H, and ambient evaluation are intrinsic to C. They commute with the Heisenberg action after replacing T by an invariant dense open (intersect its finitely many translates). Thus S is invariant under the projective Heisenberg group.

For completeness, the degree of any invariant integral surface S⊂P4 is divisible by five. The linear Heisenberg lift acts on H0(O_S(m)); if m is not divisible by five, its nontrivial central character forces this dimension to be a multiple of five. For sufficiently large m, this is its Hilbert polynomial

P_S(m) = (d/2)m² + (d/2+1−g)m + χ.

The integer-coefficient polynomial 2P_S(m), reduced modulo five, vanishes on each of the four nonzero residue classes, because each class has arbitrarily large representatives. A polynomial of degree at most two cannot have four distinct roots unless zero. Its leading coefficient d is therefore zero modulo five. Applied to d=7 this is a contradiction.

This disproves I_Z(4)=I_D(4).

## 4. Exact dimension and the degree-three residual series

Since Z consists of smooth points of D, it is an effective Cartier divisor of degree 25 on D. The line bundle

M = O_D(4)(−Z)

has degree 28−25=3. The exact sequence

0 → I_D(4) → I_Z(4) → M → 0

is exact on P3, with M extended by zero from D. The ACM resolution gives H1(I_D(4))=0 and h0(I_D(4))=12−1=11. Therefore

h0(I_Z(4)) = 11 + h0(M).

Section 3 proves h0(M)≥1.

Here is an upper bound which does not assume D is Gorenstein. An integral projective curve is Cohen–Macaulay and has a rank-one torsion-free dualizing sheaf ω_D. Write a=h0(M) and b=dim Hom(M,ω_D)=h1(M), by duality. Riemann–Roch gives

a−b=deg M+1−p_a(D)=−1,

so b=a+1. The multiplication/evaluation pairing

H0(M) × Hom(M,ω_D) → H0(ω_D)

has no zero divisors: nonzero factors are nonzero at the generic point of the integral curve. The Hopf dimension inequality gives a+b−1≤h0(ω_D)=5. Hence 2a≤5 and a≤2. Together with a≥1 this yields the assertion.
