# An integral sextic with four cubics and no quadric is ACM

## Result and scope

Let D be an integral curve of degree six in P^3 over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Suppose I_D(2)=0 and h^0(I_D(3))=4. Then

    0 -> O_P3(-4)^3 -> O_P3(-3)^4 -> I_D -> 0.

In particular D is arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay and has arithmetic genus three. Its four cubics generate its entire saturated ideal. Thus its cubic base scheme is exactly D, with no extra isolated or embedded base scheme.

This applies to the actual residual curve already obtained in FOUR-CUBIC-SEXTIC-BASE.md, PR543 commit fd58cd7d25b213841fce0c34e5207bc84e62c42f. No assumption that D is smooth, general in a Hilbert scheme, or linearly normal is made. No selected coefficient, source matrix, or rank is evaluated. No classification of a possibly nonreduced degree-three liaison residual is needed.

## 1. The general plane section has no conic

Take a general plane P and put Gamma=D intersect P. It consists of six reduced points. Since D is integral in characteristic zero, uniform position applies to THIS curve, unlike the disconnected union of 25 HM lines. If Gamma lies on a curve of minimal degree s, a general such minimal curve is integral. If s=1, Laudal's lifting lemma forces a plane containing D; if s=2 it forces a quadric, because

    deg D = 6 > 2^2+1 = 5.

Both contradict I_D(2)=0. Therefore I_Gamma(2)=0.

Verified primary-paper statements: Paola Bonacini, 'On the plane section of an integral curve in positive characteristic', Theorem 2.4 and Corollary 2.5, PDF page 3, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1009.4021 ; and 'Laudal's Lemma in positive characteristic', Theorem 1.1, PDF page 1, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1009.4017 . The former records the characteristic-zero uniform-position theorem and derives the integral-minimal-curve consequence. The latter states the characteristic-zero degree bound used here, attributing it to Laudal and Gruson–Peskine. No positive-characteristic assertion is imported.

## 2. All generators of the plane-section ideal lift

Write S=k[x0,x1,x2,x3], R=S/I_D, choose the equation L of P, and set B=S/(L). The element L is a nonzerodivisor on R. Put J=(I_D+(L))/(L), an ideal of B. Then J is contained in the saturated ideal I_Gamma.

Six points with I_Gamma(2)=0 impose six independent conditions on plane quadrics, so H^1(I_Gamma(2))=0. Also H^2(I_Gamma(1))=H^2(O_P2(1))=0. Consequently I_Gamma is 3-regular and generated by its degree-three piece, which has dimension 10-6=4.

The map (I_D)_3 -> (I_Gamma)_3 is injective: its kernel is L(I_D)_2=0. The source and target both have dimension four. Thus J_3=(I_Gamma)_3. Since the latter generates I_Gamma, the inclusions

    (J_3) = I_Gamma subset J subset I_Gamma

give J=I_Gamma in every degree. In particular R/LR is the saturated homogeneous coordinate ring of Gamma, not merely a ring with the same high-degree Hilbert function.

## 3. Cohen–Macaulayness and the resolution

The coordinate ring of a nonempty finite reduced projective scheme is Cohen–Macaulay of dimension one: a linear form avoiding its points is a nonzerodivisor. Therefore R/LR is Cohen–Macaulay. Since L is R-regular, R is Cohen–Macaulay of dimension two.

The Hilbert function of B/I_Gamma is (1,3,6,6,...), so its Hilbert series is (1+2t+3t^2)/(1-t). Hence

    Hilb_R(t) = (1+2t+3t^2)/(1-t)^2
              = (1-4t^3+3t^4)/(1-t)^4.

The four cubic generators of I_Gamma lift to generators of I_D by graded Nakayama (equivalently, subtract lifted combinations modulo L and induct on degree). The codimension-two Cohen–Macaulay ideal I_D has a Hilbert–Burch resolution with four generators and three syzygies. Its Hilbert numerator forces all three syzygies to have degree four. This is precisely the displayed resolution.

For n sufficiently large, the Hilbert function is

    (n+1)+2n+3(n-1)=6n-2,

so p_a(D)=3.

## Boundary

This establishes the genus and full ideal of the necessary integral-sextic configuration. It does not exclude the configuration, prove Rao trace transversality, or close Q4. The use of integral-curve lifting and uniform position occurs only after the actual residual D has independently been proved integral; neither theorem is applied to the original 25-line union.
