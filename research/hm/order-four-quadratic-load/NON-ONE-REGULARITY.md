# Non-1-regularity of both normalized rank-three kernel branches

## Statement and representation-theoretic hypotheses

Work over the complex numbers. Let H be the order-125 Heisenberg group, with center generated by z of order 5, and fix a primitive fifth root of unity ζ. Write V_a for the five-dimensional irreducible H-representation on which z acts by ζ^a, for a nonzero modulo 5. Let X = P(V_3), using the convention that P parametrizes lines. Consequently S_1 = H^0(X,O_X(1)) = V_3^* has central weight 2.

Let N normalize H. Assume that conjugation by N acts transitively on the 24 nontrivial characters of H/Z(H). These are the group-theoretic hypotheses used below; in the intended application N is the specified Horrocks–Mumford normalizer.

Suppose F is an N-linearized, slope-stable reflexive sheaf on X with

- rank(F) = 3;
- c_1(F) = −h and c_2(F) = 2h^2;
- z acts on F by ζ, i.e. F has central weight 1.

Then F is not Castelnuovo–Mumford 1-regular.

This is a conditional restriction on the indicated equivariant branch, not a classification or nonexistence theorem for all such stable reflexive sheaves. Section 5 below proves non-1-regularity for the alternative c_2(F)=7 branch without using a group action.

## 1. The linear resolution forced by 1-regularity

Assume for contradiction that F is 1-regular. Stability gives H^0(F(t))=0 for every t≤0: a nonzero section would provide a rank-one subsheaf of slope at least 0 in F(t), whose slope is t−1/3<0.

Put S = Sym(V_3^*) and M = ⊕_t H^0(X,F(t)). The saturated section module M has depth at least two at the irrelevant maximal ideal: its zeroth and first local cohomology vanish. Since S has dimension five, Auslander–Buchsbaum gives projective dimension at most three. The usual section-module formulation of Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity gives reg(M)≤1. Thus M is generated in degree one, and its minimal free resolution is linear. Indeed, regularity bounds the degrees in homological degree i above by i+1, while minimality and the degree-one generation bound them below by i+1.

The resulting sheaf resolution has the form

0 → β_3 O(−4) → β_2 O(−3) → β_1 O(−2) → β_0 O(−1) → F → 0.

To compute the Betti numbers, twist by O(1) and put G=F(1). Its rank and first two Chern classes are 3, 2h, 3h^2, respectively, so ch_2(G)=−h^2. Comparing rank, ch_1, and twice ch_2 gives

β_0−β_1+β_2−β_3 = 3,
β_1−2β_2+3β_3 = 2,
−β_1+4β_2−9β_3 = −2.

Writing q=β_3≥0, one obtains

β_0=5+q,   β_1=2+3q,   β_2=3q,   β_3=q.

In particular h^0(F(1))=5+q. All these generator and relation spaces are canonical N-representations. More explicitly,

B = M_1 = H^0(F(1)),
A = ker[B⊗S_1 → M_2],

dim B = 5+q, and dim A = 2+3q.

The equality for dim A holds because the resolution is linear and there are no degree-two contributions from the subsequent term S(−3)^{β_2}.

## 2. A bound on global sections requiring no local-freeness assumption

We claim that every globally generated reflexive rank-three sheaf G on P^4 with determinant O(2) satisfies h^0(G)≤17.

Choose two general global sections. They are independent at the generic point and fail to be independent in codimension at least two. To see the latter assertion on the locally free locus, use global generation and the standard incidence calculation: rank at most one for a map from a two-dimensional space to a three-dimensional fiber has codimension two. The singular locus of the reflexive sheaf has codimension at least three and introduces no divisor. Hence there is an injective sheaf map O^2→G whose quotient Q has rank one and is locally free at every codimension-one point.

The quotient Q is torsion-free. At a point of codimension at least two, reflexivity gives depth(G)≥2 and the free source has depth at least two. The depth lemma applied to 0→O^2→G→Q→0 therefore gives depth(Q)≥1. At codimension-one points Q is locally free. Thus Q has no associated point of positive codimension, as required for torsion-freeness.

Its reflexive hull is a line bundle, and its determinant is det(G)=O(2). Therefore Q embeds in Q^{**}=O(2); equivalently Q=I_Z(2) for a closed subscheme Z of codimension at least two, possibly empty. Since H^1(O)=0,

h^0(G)=2+h^0(I_Z(2))≤2+h^0(O(2))=2+15=17.

Apply this to G=F(1), which is globally generated by 1-regularity. We obtain 5+q≤17, hence q≤12.

## 3. Heisenberg dimensions restrict q

The central weight of B=H^0(F(1)) is 1+2=3 modulo 5. Every irreducible H-representation of nonzero central weight has dimension five, and for each such weight it is unique up to isomorphism. Consequently

B|_H ≅ V_3⊗R,

where H acts trivially on the multiplicity space R. Thus 5 divides dim B=5+q. With 0≤q≤12, this leaves precisely

q∈{0,5,10},   dim R=(5+q)/5∈{1,2,3}.

## 4. The normalizer forbids every remaining relation space

The representation B⊗S_1 has central weight 3+2=0. Its invariant part has dimension

dim(B⊗S_1)^H = dim(V_3⊗V_3^*)^H · dim R = dim R,

by Schur's lemma. Since A embeds in B⊗S_1, necessarily

dim A^H≤dim R.

On the other hand, A is an N-representation with trivial H-center, so its H-action factors through H/Z(H). Over C it decomposes into character spaces. The multiplicities of all 24 nontrivial characters are equal, because N acts transitively on them. Hence, for some integers a,m≥0,

dim A = a+24m,   a=dim A^H.

Using dim A=2+3q:

- If q=0, dim A=2, so m=0 and a=2>1=dim R.
- If q=5, dim A=17, so m=0 and a=17>2=dim R.
- If q=10, dim A=32, so m is 0 or 1 and a is 32 or 8, in either case greater than 3=dim R.

Each possibility contradicts the inclusion A⊂B⊗S_1. Therefore F cannot be 1-regular.

## 5. The second-Chern-class-seven branch is also not 1-regular

Now suppose instead that F is a rank-three reflexive sheaf with c_1(F)=−h and c_2(F)=7h^2. Neither stability nor equivariance is needed for this part.

If F were 1-regular, G=F(1) would be globally generated, with

c_1(G)=2h,   c_2(G)=8h^2.

As in Section 2, two general sections give an exact sequence

0 → O^2 → G → I_Z(2) → 0,

where Z has codimension at least two. Since I_Z(2) is a quotient of the globally generated sheaf G, it is globally generated. Its global sections are quadrics whose common scheme-theoretic zero locus is Z; in particular this linear system has no fixed divisorial component.

There exist two quadrics in this system with no common irreducible factor. Indeed, choose any nonzero quadric q_1. It has finitely many irreducible factors, and for each factor the subspace of quadrics in the system divisible by that factor is proper, because the system has no fixed divisor. Over C a finite union of proper linear subspaces cannot cover the whole system, so one can choose q_2 outside their union.

The quadrics q_1,q_2 define a codimension-two complete intersection of degree four, containing Z scheme-theoretically. Hence the degree of the codimension-two cycle of Z is at most four. On the other hand, the exact sequence gives

c_2(G)=c_2(I_Z(2))=[Z]_2,

so that this degree is eight. This is a contradiction. (If Z has no codimension-two components, the degree is zero, which is equally contradictory.) Therefore F cannot be 1-regular in the c_2(F)=7 branch either.

## Scope and consequence for the proof repair

For c_2(F)=2 the conclusion uses only the stated invariants, reflexivity, stability, the specified central weight, and the 24-character normalizer orbit. For c_2(F)=7, reflexivity and the two Chern classes suffice. No classification of low-Chern-class reflexive sheaves, hyperplane-restriction stability, selected Horrocks–Mumford coefficient evaluation, or matrix experiment is used.

In particular, proving 1-regularity for the actual normalized kernel would itself yield a contradiction. It is not a viable extra hypothesis under which such an N-linearized sheaf could continue to exist. The arguments do not yet prove that a hypothetical kernel is 1-regular, and therefore do not exclude rank seven by themselves. They instead establish a necessary restriction in both branches: for F=K(1), reg(F)≥2 and hence reg(K)≥3.
