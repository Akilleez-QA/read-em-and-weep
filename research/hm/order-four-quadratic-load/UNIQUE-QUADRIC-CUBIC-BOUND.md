# The unique-quadric branch has at most six section cubics

## New conclusion

Assume the surviving alternative a=h^0(I_(Z,H)(2))=1 for a general proper hyperplane section Z of the 25 HM lines. The unique quadric Q through Z is integral by RAO-SECTION-QUADRICS.md. Then

    4 <= b=h^0(I_(Z,H)(3)) <= 6.

This excludes the previously remaining possibility a=1,b=7. If b=6, the extra cubics on Q have a fixed curve of degree five and a residual ruling pencil; all 25 points lie on that degree-five fixed curve.

This is not a lifting theorem and does not eliminate a=1. It uses the specific degree 25 and quintic base-generation together, so it does not apply to the nine-line boundary example.

## 1. Cubics restricted to the integral quadric

Let W be the image of H^0(I_(Z,H)(3)) in H^0(O_Q(3)). Since the ideal of Q is generated by its unique quadratic equation,

    dim W=b-4.

If dim W<=1, then b<=5 and the desired bound is immediate. For the fixed-divisor argument below assume dim W>=2. Every member of W vanishes on Z. The six restricted quintic generators have base scheme exactly Z on H, hence also on Q.

If Q is smooth, work on Q=P1 times P1, with hyperplane class H_Q=(1,1). If Q is a cone, first note that a general Z avoids its vertex. Indeed uniqueness of Q makes its vertex intrinsic and equivariant. The set of labels whose section points equal the vertex is generically constant and invariant under the transitive normalizer action. It is either empty or all 25 labels; the latter is impossible for distinct points. Thus it is empty. Resolve the cone by the Hirzebruch surface F2, with exceptional curve E, fiber f, and hyperplane class H_Q=E+2f. The lifted 25 points avoid E. The quintic generators have no common base point on E either, since the vertex is not in Z.

Use Y for either smooth surface. In both cases H_Q^2=2. Pull back W if needed, and let D be its full fixed divisor. The residual system is a vector space of sections of a divisor M=3H_Q-D with no fixed curve. Put

    d=H_Q·D,  H_Q·M=6-d.

Both quantities are nonnegative integers. Removing the fixed divisor does not change the dimension of W.

## 2. Counting both the fixed and moving parts

Suppose dim W>=2. Two general residual members have no common curve, so their intersection is zero-dimensional of length M^2. Every point of Z outside D belongs to that intersection. Therefore

    |Z outside D| <= M^2.

Choose a linear combination of the six quintic generators whose restriction avoids every component of D. This is possible because their base locus on Y is finite and disjoint from E in the cone case. Its divisor has class 5H_Q, so

    |Z on D| <= 5H_Q·D=5d.

This argument deliberately does not assume that every point of Z lies on the fixed divisor. Combining the two estimates yields

    25 <= 5d+M^2.                                      (1)

The absence of fixed curves implies that M is nef. On a smooth quadric write M=(u,v), with u,v>=0. Then

    M^2=2uv <= (u+v)^2/2=(6-d)^2/2.

On F2 write M=uE+vf. Nefness gives u>=0 and v>=2u; its hyperplane degree is v=6-d and

    M^2=-2u^2+2uv <= v^2/2=(6-d)^2/2.

Thus (1) implies

    25 <= 5d+(6-d)^2/2.

For d=0,1,2,3,4 the right side is respectively 18, 35/2, 18, 39/2, 22, all below 25. Hence d>=5 and the moving divisor has hyperplane degree at most one.

## 3. Bounding the moving system

On the smooth quadric, a nef divisor of degree at most one is zero or one of the ruling classes. On F2, the inequalities u>=0, v>=2u and v<=1 force M=0 or f. Such a line bundle has at most two independent global sections. Hence dim W<=2.

If dim W was initially at most one the same conclusion was already immediate. In all cases

    b=4+dim W<=6.

If b=6, the residual space has dimension two, so it is the complete ruling pencil. Thus M has degree one, D has degree five, and the residual pencil is base-point free. Every point of Z must therefore belong to D.

The same count supplies a further necessary restriction in this endpoint case: D has no line component in its image on Q. Indeed a general quintic avoiding D intersects it in length 25, already exhausted by the 25 distinct points of Z. A line component would contribute five distinct points of that intersection, whereas a general HM section has no three collinear points. Likewise the positive-degree image of D must be reduced: otherwise its reduced support has degree less than five and a quintic could meet it in fewer than 25 distinct points. These endpoint statements concern the fixed curve, not a claimed lifting of it to P4.
