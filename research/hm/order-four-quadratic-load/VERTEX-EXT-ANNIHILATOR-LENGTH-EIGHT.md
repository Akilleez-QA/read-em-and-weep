# The vertex Ext annihilator has generic length eight, not seven

This note combines the actual local spinor filtration with the newly established generic vertex-module Loewy dimensions (2,3,2). It determines the generic transverse Ext module, without choosing any actual coefficient and without asserting global homogeneous generators along L.

## 1. Actual local model and the two different parity quotients

Complete at the generic point of the vertex line L, with residue field K=C(L). The transverse quadric singularity is

R=K[[a^2,ab,b^2]] subset B=K[[a,b]].

Write M=B_odd for the rank-one spinor module and m for the maximal ideal of R. Up to local trivializations the actual sequence is

0 -> M -> R^2 -> J -> 0,
J subset M, with T=M/J of length seven.

The second map has two odd generators f,g in M. Thus J=Rf+Rg. Since M/J has finite length, (f,g)B has finite colength in B: a common nonunit factor would leave odd classes along a curve, contradicting finite length. Hence f,g are a regular sequence in the regular local ring B.

Every B-syzygy on f,g is h(-g,f). For its two entries to be even, h must be odd. Therefore the actual R-syzygy module is M, with inclusion h -> (-gh,fh), up to the isomorphism with the given kernel.

Hom_R(M,R) identifies with M by multiplication inside the fraction field extension: equivalently the dual of the A1 spinor is the same rank-one module. Restricting maps R^2 -> M to the kernel therefore gives the ideal

I_e=(f,g)M subset R.

It follows that the two actual modules are the distinct parity pieces

T=(B/(f,g))_odd,
V_e=R/I_e=(B/(f,g))_even.

Here V_e is the generic stalk of sheaf Ext^1(J,S), as defined in POSITIVE-CONE-EXT-ANNIHILATOR-AND-VERTEX-GATE.md. This proves the bridge to the Ext annihilator directly; it does not identify I_e with Ann(T).

## 2. Loewy dimensions force a regular (3,5) initial pair

The established generic Loewy dimensions are

dim(T/mT)=2, dim(mT/m^2T)=3, dim(m^2T)=2, m^3T=0.

For M, the successive m-adic pieces have dimensions 2,4,6,8,..., represented by binary forms of odd degrees 1,3,5,7,... . The first equality says J has no degree-one initial relation. The next says its degree-three initial relation space has dimension one. Change the two R-generators by an invertible constant operation so f has order three and g has order at least five.

The degree-five quotient has dimension two. Multiples f3 Sym^2 span three independent degree-five relations. Thus g must have order exactly five, and its leading form g5 is independent of f3 Sym^2. There is no lower-order cancellation capable of generating an extra leading degree-five relation: the only degree-three generator is f3 and its multiples are injective in the polynomial domain.

At degree seven, there is likewise no cancellation from earlier degrees, since g5 is not in f3 Sym^2. Hence the degree-seven initial relations are exactly

f3 Sym^4 + g5 Sym^2.

The equality m^3T=0 requires this space to be all Sym^7, of dimension eight. The two displayed summands have dimensions five and three. If f3 and g5 had a common factor of degree at least one, their least common multiple would have degree at most seven and the summands would intersect, contradicting dimension eight. They are therefore coprime.

The initial forms f3,g5 form a regular sequence. The standard filtered regular-sequence argument then identifies the associated graded quotient of B/(f,g) with K[a,b]/(f3,g5). One may verify this directly: any cancellation of leading multiples is generated by the Koszul syzygy (g5,-f3), which lifts to (g,-f), allowing successive removal of all such cancellations. No extra initial relation appears.

## 3. Exact generic Ext length and filtration

The Hilbert series is consequently

(1-z^3)(1-z^5)/(1-z)^2
 =1+2z+3z^2+3z^3+3z^4+2z^5+z^6.

Its odd degrees give (2,3,2), recovering the actual T. Its even degrees give

V_e: (1,3,3,1), length eight.

Thus m^4 V_e=0 and m^3 V_e is nonzero of dimension one. In particular I_e contains m^4 but not m^3. Its degree-two initial relation space is two-dimensional, generated by a f3 and b f3, while additional degree-three relations arise from a g5 and b g5. The assertion I_e=m^3 would have been false: R/m^3 has length 1+3+5=9, not eight.

These are generic transverse statements. They do not assert equality of completed ideals with a homogeneous ideal; only the associated graded is identified. They do not provide a global quintic generator or trivialize a possibly nontrivial generator extension over L. Special fiber jumps, global twists, and any finite defect in V_e remain part of the global vertex gate.

## 4. Consequence for the remaining positive problem

The preserving positive excess is already reduced to the actual restriction K20 -> H0(V_e(4))_+, where K20=A+V+P4+2D5. The present result supplies the correct generic transverse length and four-step filtration of its target module: eight with layers (1,3,3,1), rather than the length-seven spinor defect with layers (2,3,2).

It does not decide the global restriction rank. In particular global sections cannot be counted from generic lengths alone, and a global splitting or global pair f,g is not inferred. The remaining information is the gluing and twists of this Ext module and the actual image of ordinary quartics in it.
