# The normal trace duality retains a section-line residue

## Checked conclusion

Local duality does NOT identify the dual normal trace boundary with the degree-six socle section alone. The actual vertex open has a nonzero invariant section-line cohomology channel which is invisible under the Serre projection and whose dual square residue pairs nontrivially with the vertex component constant. This is a precise mismatch between a Serre quotient class and its adjoint/square dual residue, not a claim that this extra channel extends independently to the whole cone.

Consequently the proposed argument “the socle has degree six, so only the degree-eight nilpotent can pair with it” omits a genuine degree-fourteen residue channel. The actual coefficient beta/alpha is not evaluated here. The fourth Bockstein is not evaluated either.

## 1. Dualizing the actual square sequence

Work on the smooth weighted stack X, with physical twists and omega_X=O(-3). Put E=G, det(E)=O(2), and let sigma be its actual regular section. The sequence is

    0 -> E(-4) --tau--> B(-2) --q--> I_Z^2 -> 0.

Local duality gives a canonical sheaf map

    r:E(-1) -> omega_(2Z),

since E(-4)^* tensor omega_X=E(-1) and
sheaf Ext^1(I_Z^2,omega_X)=omega_(2Z). The dual of the cohomological boundary

    H1(I_Z^2) -> H2(E(-4))

is the induced map

    H1(E(-1)) -> H1(omega_(2Z)).                    (1)

The latter target pairs with H0(O_(2Z)); its image annihilates the global constant. This also follows directly by dualizing the ideal sequence. Thus it is the annihilator of constants, not an arbitrary functional on the whole square algebra, that enters the original boundary.

## 2. Universal local formula and its transition check

Use an oriented actual Serre frame with sigma=(f,g). All determinant and canonical frames remain attached to the displayed coefficients. Set

    tau(w) = [[-f w2-g w1, 2f w1],[-2g w2, f w2+g w1]],
    q([[a,b],[c,-a]]) = f^2 c-2fg a-g^2 b.

Relative to the earlier convention N=[[fg,-f²],[g²,-fg]], the displayed q is -tr(NE), and the displayed tau is the simultaneous opposite of the earlier tau satisfying tau(sigma)=2N. This is an isomorphic exact-sequence convention, not a change of the intrinsic operation. Its common overall residue scalar/sign, and the beta/alpha formula, must be transported consistently when comparing to the preceding trace theorem. None of the nonzero-residue conclusions depends on that common sign.

The square ideal has generators (f^2,fg,g^2), and syzygies

    h1=(-g,f,0),  h2=(0,-g,f).

After the displayed q coordinate convention, tau(w) is
2w2 h1-2w1 h2. Identify the dual of E using the functional
w |-> det(v,w)=v1 w2-v2 w1. In the dual Hilbert--Burch presentation, the two generators therefore have coefficients v1/2,v2/2. Its relations are

    g e1=0,  f e1-g e2=0,  f e2=0.

Inside the complete-intersection duality algebra A/(f^2,g^2), identify e1 with g and e2 with f. This identifies the canonical module of A/(f,g)^2 with the submodule generated by f,g. Hence

    r(v) = (g v1+f v2)/2 modulo (f^2,g^2),          (2)

with the canonical-module frame understood. The Serre projection, however, is

    sigma wedge v = f v2-g v1.                    (3)

These are different maps.

This calculation is compatible with the actual nonsplit quintic transition. Under g'=g+p f, the section and vector frames change by v1'=v1, v2'=v2+p v1. If R=g v1+f v2, then R'=R+2pf v1 and

    R' g^2-R(g+p f)^2
       = -f^2(2p g v2+p^2 R).                     (4)

Thus R/g^2 is invariant on the double cubic f^2=0. Equivalently, the generalized residue R/(f^2 g^2) has the correct triangular change of generators. Diagonal line changes are the intrinsic Ext and determinant changes in (1), not an assertion that these fractions are globally scalar functions. Formula (4) checks the nonsplit transition which a split-quintic argument would miss.

In particular, adding the actual section-line term gives the exact identity

    r(v+sigma t)-r(v)=fg t.                       (5)

The Serre projection (3) does not change. Grothendieck residue transformation gives

    Res_(f^2,g^2)(fg t)=Res_(f,g)(t).              (6)

These identities are uniform in a regular pair; no actual coefficients are selected.

## 3. The missing channel actually exists and has nonzero trace

Let U=X minus C be the negative-projection vertex open. Use the root gerbe over L=P(W) to keep half twists, then descend even cover degrees. Its coordinate-degree-d piece is

    Sym^d(Wprime) O_L(-d/2).

Consequently the degree-six part of O_U(-1) is

    Sym^6(Wprime) O_L(-4).

The established decomposition Sym^6(Wprime)=A+P4 and Serre duality on L give

    H1(Sym^6(Wprime) O_L(-4))^G
      = [(A+P4) tensor A]^G = C.                  (7)

Let t be a nonzero invariant class in this line. Affine projection on coarse charts and exact tame descent realize it as a genuine class of H1(U,O_U(-1)), not an invented local coefficient.

The actual normalized vertex socle functional, with its full line factor, is

    lambda:Sym^6(Wprime) O_L(-3) -> O_L(-1).

It is the nonzero complete quadratic A channel, as proved in ACTUAL-POSITIVE-ATIYAH-MULTIPLICATION-SURJECTS-ONTO-NINE-SPACE.md. Twisting by -1 gives

    lambda(-1):Sym^6(Wprime) O_L(-4) -> O_L(-2).   (8)

The map on invariant H1 in (8) is nonzero: its Serre dual sends 1 in H0(O_L) to the nonzero section lambda in Sym^6(Wprime)^* tensor H0(O_L(2)). Both invariant spaces are lines. Thus the base trace of lambda(t) is NONZERO.

By (5),(6), this is precisely the proper vertex trace of r(sigma t). In particular,

    [sigma t] != 0 in H1(U,E(-1)),
    Tr_vertex(r(sigma t)) != 0.                   (9)

This proves nonzeroness of the actual section-line channel without assuming injectivity from dimensions or guessing its global extension.

The coefficient fg t has cover degree 3+5+6=14. It pairs with the constant in the (f^2,g^2) algebra of socle degree14. It pairs trivially with n8, since its product with n8 has degree22, above that socle. All line factors are accounted for by (8) and the final H1(O_L(-2)) trace. The full block torus has only been used on the homogeneous vertex geometry; no global torus linearization of E is assumed.

## 4. Even the marked vertex Serre residue omits an extension channel

There is a second exact reason not to identify the global bundle with a chosen homogeneous vertex presentation. On U, local-to-global Ext for the regular codimension-two ideal gives

    0 -> H1(U,O_U(-2))
      -> Ext^1_U(I_Z(2),O_U)
      -> H0(Z_v,Lres) -> 0.                         (10)

Indeed the sheaf Hom term is O_U(-2), the sheaf Ext^1 term is Lres, and H2(U,O_U(-2))=0 because the negative projection has cohomological dimension one (after exact tame descent). The injection can also be seen by applying Ext to the ideal sequence; sheaf Ext below codimension two for O_Z vanishes.

The cover-degree-zero term of H1(U,O_U(-2)) is

    H1(L,O_L(-2))=C,

with trivial G representation. Thus even an invariant, normalized, nowhere-zero vertex Serre residue leaves at least this genuine extension direction undetermined on U. Adding it changes no local residue and preserves local freeness, since that property is detected by the same unit residue. This is distinct from the nonsplit cubic/quintic generator extension: the latter is retained in (4), while (10) concerns the Serre middle bundle itself.

Globally H1(X,O_X(-2))=H2(X,O_X(-2))=0, so the two-component residue does determine the global Serre extension. But its restriction to (10) requires the global matching; there is no canonical splitting of (10) supplied by the vertex residue alone. A torus eigencharacter of that residue does not, by itself, prove a chosen full-torus linearization of the restricted middle bundle. In particular the nonzero quintic-generator extension does not already compute the missing scalar in the dual normal trace: one must compare the actual global Serre extension with these local presentations.

## 5. Exact residue formula for the undetermined ratio

Take a nonzero actual theta in H1(X,E(-1))^G, dual to H2(E(-4))^G. Restrict a representing cocycle v to the vertex. Define

    A_theta = Tr_vertex(r(v)),
    B_theta = Tr_vertex(n8 r(v)).                 (11)

Here r is the intrinsic map (1); locally it is (2), and the trace includes base cohomology and the canonical line. Therefore (11) is independent of representative and local frames. The conic trace supplies exactly the opposite of A_theta because the total functional annihilates the global constant. It may not simply be discarded when matching the actual global theta.

If e_v is the idempotent equal to one on the vertex component and zero on the conic, the dual boundary evaluates e_v as A_theta and n8 as B_theta. Consequently the actual kernel line satisfies

    alpha A_theta+beta B_theta=0,
    beta/alpha=-A_theta/B_theta.                  (12)

In fact B_theta is nonzero: the boundary on the two-dimensional invariant shadow is surjective with one-dimensional kernel, and its actual kernel vector has alpha!=0. If B_theta were zero, (12) would force A_theta=0, contradicting surjectivity. This conclusion uses the already-proved exact sequence and alpha!=0, not a selected residue calculation.

The new information is that the numerator in (12) is sensitive to a nonzero, explicitly identified section-line channel: replacing a local Serre lift by sigma t changes A_theta by the nonzero trace (8), while leaving its Serre projection and B_theta unchanged. Such a replacement is NOT asserted to extend globally. Instead it identifies precisely what the global conic/vertex matching has to fix. Knowing only that the Serre quotient of theta is the degree-six socle does not fix that matching and does not imply A_theta=0.
