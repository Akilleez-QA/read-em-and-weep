# Differential descent excludes the finite three-cubic-base branch

## Statement

Let C be the actual reduced invariant union of25 skew HM lines in P4. Assume the projective Heisenberg group G of order25 acts transitively on these lines, and its linear Heisenberg lift acts irreducibly on the five-dimensional ambient coordinate representation. Then the following generic hyperplane-section branch is impossible:

h0(I_Z(2))=0, h0(I_Z(3))=3, and the three section cubics have finite common zero scheme.

This does not exclude a three-dimensional cubic system whose common base contains a curve. No complete residual quadratic or Q4 claim follows.

## 1. Inputs established for the hypothetical branch

Write Z_H=C intersect H. After restricting to a dense open T of dual P4, its25 points are distinct and the three-dimensional cubic kernels form a vector bundle. Their common zero scheme X_H is a finite(3,3,3) complete intersection of length27. The transitive-label/excess-length argument proves that all25 marked points are simple components of X_H, and the complementary residual W_H is length two and disjoint from Z_H. It can be a double point.

The length-two linkage computation gives

(I_Z)_4=(I_X)_4= S1 (I_X)_3.

These inputs are proved in FINITE-CI-DIRECTIONAL-JET-SURJECTIVITY.md and FINITE-CI-LENGTH-TWO-LINKAGE.md. The present proof uses the quartic equality, not the residual-pair Plucker contraction. All these assertions are conditional on the hypothetical generic finite-base branch.

## 2. Differential preservation of the full incidence ideal

Use an affine chart of hyperplanes

H_t: X4=t0 X0+t1 X1+t2 X2+t3 X3.

Choose a local algebraic basis f_a(t,x), a=1,2,3, of section cubics, with x=[X0:X1:X2:X3]. Such a basis exists on a dense open by constant-rank algebraic evaluation; no numerical selection is involved.

At a marked point p_i(t), the derivative in a parameter direction v satisfying sum v_j x_j(p_i)=0 keeps the ambient intersection point fixed to first order. Indeed the point lies on a fixed component line transverse to H_t. Differentiating f_a(t,p_i(t))=0 shows that the vector of four parameter derivatives of f_a at p_i is proportional to x(p_i). Thus every quartic

x_i partial_(t_j)f_a - x_j partial_(t_i)f_a

vanishes on Z_t. By the quartic equality above it belongs to the degree-four part of the ideal generated by f1,f2,f3.

This is an identity of families after shrinking T: the12 products x_i f_a are linearly independent in degree4, since the complete-intersection generators have no syzygy before degree6. Solving for the linear-multiple coefficients therefore gives regular coefficients on a suitable open, rather than only unrelated fiberwise identities.

For each component choose a coordinate x_i nonzero at its general point and relabel it x0. On the affine x0=1 chart, set

delta_j=partial_(t_j)-x_j partial_(t_0), j=1,2,3.

Each derivation preserves the ideal I=(f1,f2,f3) in the incidence chart. These are exactly three independent tangent directions fixing the ambient point: the ambient coordinates are x1,x2,x3 and w=t0+sum_(j=1)^3 t_j x_j, and delta_j annihilates all four.

## 3. Why double residual points cause no gap

In characteristic zero a derivation preserving an ideal also preserves its radical. Here is an algebraic proof. Work in the quotient ring by the ideal and let a^n=0. Applying delta^n to a^n, the sole term containing no undifferentiated factor a is n! (delta a)^n; every other term is divisible by a. Hence (delta a)^n belongs to (a), and therefore (delta a)^(n^2)=0. Thus delta sends nilpotents to nilpotents, proving radical preservation.

Apply this to I. The three delta_j descend to derivations of the reduced incidence scheme D_red. At a general point of any irreducible component, away from the other components, they are tangent to that component. Therefore they remain tangent to reduced residual components even if their unreduced fibers are double points. No assumption of reducedness of W_H has been introduced.

One may shrink T so all components under discussion dominate T and X->T is finite flat. Vertical components disappear after this shrink. The25 marked components and the residual support are disjoint over T. Every dominating reduced residual component D_alpha has dimension4, because it is finite over the four-dimensional T.

## 4. The residual family descends to a curve of degree at most two

Let pi be ambient evaluation from the incidence variety to P4. At a general smooth point of D_alpha, the three independent delta_j lie in the kernel of d(pi|D_alpha). Thus differential rank is at most one. Characteristic-zero generic differential rank gives dim closure pi(D_alpha)<=1.

The image cannot be a point: D_alpha dominates T, whereas one fixed ambient point belongs only to a proper hyperplane in dual P4. Consequently its reduced image closure R_alpha is an integral curve.

Take the closure of D_alpha in full projective incidence. It is contained in the incidence of R_alpha. The latter is irreducible of dimension4: it is a projective3 bundle over the integral curve R_alpha. Both have dimension4. Therefore the two closures are equal. In particular, for a general H, the reduced intersection R_alpha intersect H is exactly the corresponding residual support, with the possible harmless duplication of the same R_alpha removed.

Let R be the reduced union of the distinct image curves. For general H, R intersect H is reduced, and its points occur among the support of the length-two W_H. Therefore

deg R = length(R intersect H) <= 2.

This argument bounds the degree of the reduced image, not the length of an artificially assumed reduced residual cover. It applies equally to one doubled residual point, a connected degree-two reduced cover, or two degree-one components.

## 5. Heisenberg irreducibility gives the contradiction

The families X_H, Z_H and their residual W_H are intrinsic to the cubic ideal spaces and the actual line arrangement. They are equivariant. Hence their reduced ambient residual image R is invariant under G.

There are at most two irreducible components of R. A group of odd order25 cannot act nontrivially on a set of at most two elements, so each component is invariant. An integral degree-one curve is a line. An integral degree-two curve spans a plane (the usual degree bound for a nondegenerate integral curve in its linear span is degree>=dimension of span). Thus each component spans a nonzero proper projective linear subspace of P4.

That span is G-invariant. Equivalently its vector subspace is invariant under the linear Heisenberg lift; central scalars do not affect invariance. This contradicts the irreducibility of the ambient five-dimensional representation. Therefore R cannot exist, and the hypothetical generic finite three-cubic-base branch is excluded.

## Result and boundary

The argument removes the finite complete-intersection alternative from the generic a=0,b=3 branch, including the nonreduced residual length-two case. A surviving generic b=3 branch must have positive-dimensional cubic base. The proof does not settle such a base, the b=1,2 cases, trace transversality, the full source-lift boundary, or Q4.

This is a symbolic proof with an explicit radical-stability argument. No actual HM coefficients, selected matrices, ranks, hyperplanes, parameter scans, local-model spike, or consumed experiment were used. No external write was performed by this worker.
