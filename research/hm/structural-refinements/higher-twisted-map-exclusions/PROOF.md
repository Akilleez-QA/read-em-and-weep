# Higher twisted endomorphisms: forced singular curves and nonfree spectral modules

## Scope and inputs

Work over C. Let F be the normalized Horrocks–Mumford bundle on P4, with c1(F)=−H, c2(F)=4H² and H⁰(F(u))=0 for u<3. The section vanishing is the verified primary input in [sharper-hm-regularity](https://github.com/Akilleez-QA/read-em-and-weep/blob/063da90fc4543304e87e701cdefcc08225412f17/research/conductor-normalization/sharper-hm-regularity/SHARPEST-BOUND.md). Put B=End₀(F). This note concerns k=2,3,4.

The unconditional result is that every nonzero φ∈H⁰(B(k)) has nonzero nonsquare q=−det φ, and q's hypersurface has a positive-dimensional singular locus. Its integral spectral cover carries a rank-one maximal Cohen–Macaulay module whose nonfree locus is exactly the lift of the zero scheme of φ, set-theoretically. If the cover is normal, it must fail local factoriality along a positive-dimensional locus. No vanishing of H⁰B(2), H⁰B(3), or H⁰B(4) is asserted.

## 1. Generic rank one, zero determinant, and square determinant are excluded

For ψ:F→F(k) of generic rank one, its kernel and saturated image are saturated rank-one subsheaves of vector bundles. They are reflexive, hence O(a), O(b). Indeed the torsion-free quotients and the depth lemma give S2 for the kernels; rank-one reflexive sheaves on P4 are line bundles. Their inclusions and the section vanishing imply

    a≤−3, b≤k−3.

The quotient F/O(a) is I_Z(−1−a), where codim Z≥2. The induced nonzero map to O(b) lies in

    Hom(I_Z(−1−a),O(b))=H⁰(O(a+b+1)).

Here Hom(I_Z,O)=O by Hartogs extension on the normal base. But a+b+1≤k−5<0 for k≤4, contradiction.

For trace-free φ, Cayley–Hamilton gives φ(k)φ=q id. If q=0 and φ≠0, φ has generic rank one. If q=s²≠0 with s∈H⁰(O(k)), φ−s id has generic rank one; trace zero in characteristic zero rules out φ=s id. These cases are therefore impossible. A homogeneous polynomial q that is a square in the rational function field after trivializing O(2k) is already the square of a degree-k homogeneous polynomial, up to a nonzero constant, by unique factorization; over C the constant is also a square. Thus the spectral cover below is integral.

## 2. A forced curve in the singular scheme of q

The Chern roots of B are 0, α−β, β−α, where α,β are the roots of F. Consequently

    c1(B)=0, c2(B)=4c2(F)−c1(F)²=15H², c3(B)=0,
    c3(B(k))=(k³+15k)H³.

For k=2,3,4 these nonzero coefficients are 38,72,124. A nowhere-zero section of B(k) would yield an exact sequence 0→O→B(k)→E→0 with E locally free of rank two, forcing c3(B(k))=0. Hence Z(φ) is nonempty. Locally its ideal is generated by three entries: in a frame,

    φ = [[a,b],[c,−a]],     q=a²+bc.

The height theorem gives codimension at most three for every irreducible component of Z(φ); on P4 every such component has dimension at least one. Moreover q∈I_Z² and dq∈I_Z Ω¹ locally, hence

    Z(φ) ⊂ Sing(V(q))

as closed subschemes, using the Jacobian singular scheme. Therefore dim Sing(V(q))≥1. In particular smooth branch divisors and branch divisors with only isolated singularities are excluded, independently of any Picard-group theorem or normality hypothesis.

If, and only when justified separately, Z(φ) has the expected codimension three, its ideal of three local generators is a regular sequence on the smooth base, and its fundamental cycle is c3(B(k)). Its degree is then 38,72,124 respectively. These numbers are NOT asserted to be the degree of the general singular locus or of a zero locus with excess dimension.

## 3. Spectral module and its exact nonfree support

Let A=O⊕O(−k), with multiplication determined by q, and X=Spec_P4 A. It is a finite flat projective integral double cover π:X→P4, locally z²=q, with π_*O_X=A. The map φ gives F an A-module structure, via φ(−k):F(−k)→F; call its associated coherent sheaf M. Then π_*M=F and M has rank one over the degree-two function-field extension.

M is maximal Cohen–Macaulay: at a closed base point a regular system of four base parameters is a regular sequence on the free module F. After passing to A and localizing at a point over the base point it remains a regular sequence, and its ideal is primary to the maximal ideal because the cover is finite. Thus depth M_x=4=dim O_X,x. The assertion localizes. This argument does not assume normality or cyclicity at singular points.

We now identify the nonfree locus set-theoretically. If φ(y)≠0, it is a nonscalar trace-free 2×2 matrix over C, so it has a cyclic vector v: v,φ(y)v are linearly independent. Lift v locally. Their determinant is a unit near y, yielding an isomorphism of A-modules A→F with 1↦v, z↦φv (with local trivializations). Thus M is invertible above y. This covers both nonzero nilpotent matrices at branch points and the distinct-eigenvalue case off the branch.

If φ(y)=0, then q(y)=0 and there is a unique x above y, with maximal ideal generated by the base maximal ideal and z. Its residue module is

    M_x/m_x M_x ≅ F_y/(m_y F_y+φ F_y) ≅ C².

A free rank-one module would have one-dimensional residue fiber, contradiction. Therefore

    NonFree(M) = {x∈X : z(x)=0 and φ(π(x))=0}

as sets. In particular NonFree(M) has dimension at least one. This also supplies a Picard-free exclusion of any spectral scenario in which M would be everywhere invertible.

## 4. Consequence for normal and locally factorial covers

If X is normal, its rank-one MCM module M is reflexive: MCM gives S2, and over the integral cover the localized MCM condition rules out associated primes of positive height, hence gives torsion-freeness. Over a factorial normal local ring a rank-one reflexive module is free. It follows that every point in NonFree(M) is a point where X is not locally factorial. Thus a candidate normal X must have a positive-dimensional non-locally-factorial locus. In particular no locally factorial normal spectral cover can occur.

Normality is a hypothesis here, not an automatic consequence of q being nonsquare. For example repeated factors can make a nonsquare double cover nonnormal. The construction, MCM assertion, forced singular curve, and nonfree-locus computation in Sections 1–3 still apply to such covers; the reflexive/class-group argument in this section does not.

## 5. Exact line-spectral criterion and Chern obstruction

On the full cover, or on a restricted cover over a linear Pn with n≥2, suppose independently that the spectral module is a line bundle of the form π*O(m). Then projection formula gives

    F|Pn ≅ O(m)⊕O(m−k).

Its Chern classes require

    2m−k=−1,     m(m−k)=4.

For k=2,4 the first equality has no integral solution. For k=3 it forces m=1 and the second Chern number would be −2, not 4. More generally substituting m=(k−1)/2 gives c2=(1−k²)/4, incompatible with 4 for every integer k. A sufficient hypothesis for the displayed form is that the spectral module is invertible and Pic(X)=Z[π*O(1)]. Neither normality alone nor rank-one MCM alone permits replacing M by a line bundle.

## 6. Primary-source Picard verification (not needed for Sections 1–4)

In fact Pic(X)=Z[π*O(1)] for the integral covers of P4 above, including singular ones. This does NOT imply that the rank-one MCM module M is invertible.

The primary source is Grothendieck et al., SGA 2, Exposé XII, Corollaries 3.6 and 3.7, revised edition printed page 121, PDF page 129:

https://www.cmls.polytechnique.fr/perso/laszlo/sga2/sga2-smf.pdf#page=129

Here are the hypotheses rather than a blanket smooth-cover citation. The ramification divisor D=(z) is an effective Cartier divisor because X is integral; D≅V(q)⊂P4 as schemes, even if q has repeated factors. Its line bundle is O_X(D)=π*O(k), which is ample. X is locally a hypersurface in the smooth total space of O(k), hence Cohen–Macaulay of dimension four. For every n>0,

    H¹(D,O_D(−nk))=H²(D,O_D(−nk))=0,

by the hypersurface sequence on P4 and the vanishing of intermediate cohomology of projective-space line bundles. Outside D, X→P4 is étale, so its local rings are regular, hence parafactorial in the dimensions used by XII 3.6. Thus XII 3.6 gives Pic(X)≅Pic(D). Corollary XII 3.7 applies to the three-dimensional projective hypersurface scheme D, without a smoothness assumption, giving Pic(D)=Z[O_D(1)]. Restriction of π*O(1) is O_D(1), establishing the asserted generator. The two corollaries' non-strict dimension inequalities must be read from the typeset source, since text extraction sometimes renders ≥ as >.

This verifies the conditional criterion for an invertible M globally, but the stronger actual obstruction is Section 3: M is necessarily nonfree along a positive-dimensional locus.

## Residual scope

The remaining candidates have nonzero nonsquare determinant of degree 4,6,8; a positive-dimensional branch singular scheme containing Z(φ); and a rank-one MCM spectral module nonfree precisely over that zero locus. Normal candidates must fail local factoriality along that locus. No vanishing of the three open H⁰ groups, multiplication rank, source-image statement, secondary obstruction, socle, or smaller collision bound follows from these exclusions alone.
