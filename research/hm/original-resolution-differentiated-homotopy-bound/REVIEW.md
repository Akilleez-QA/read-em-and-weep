## 1. Exact operator derivative and graded signs

Use internal complex degree, with d of degree +1, h of degree -1, E of degree 0, and an even parameter connection D=nabla_V. Extend E by zero off the cohomology term. The accepted contraction implies

    dh+hd=1-E,    Ed=dE=0,
    H_r(F)=hF+(-1)^r EFh,
    partial_r(F)=dF-(-1)^r Fd,
    partial H+H partial=1-Pi,    Pi(F)=EFE.

Set a=D h, b=D E and c=D d, where these are induced covariant derivatives of maps. Then

    K_r(F)=aF+(-1)^r(bFh+EFa),
    Q_r(F)=cF-(-1)^r Fc,
    dotPi(F)=bFE+EFb.

Here K=[D,H] is an operator derivative. It is not the complete derivative D(HF), which also contains H(DF). Likewise Q=[D,partial] is the graded inner differential generated by c; replacing its right-hand sign with an ordinary commutator is incorrect for odd-degree F.

Differentiating the operator contraction with an even D gives

    partial K+K partial=-dotPi-QH-HQ.

The sign is fixed by differentiating all four operator factors; no total-form parity has been silently added. If instead D denotes a one-form-valued differential with a total grading, conventions must first be translated to this even directional statement.

There is also a useful completely formal certificate. Put

    T=dh+hd,
    S=da+ad+ch+hc,
    R_h=T-1+E,
    R_Dh=S+b,
    R_E=dE-Ed,
    R_DE=db+cE-bd-Ec.

Expansion in the free associative algebra gives, for s=(-1)^r,

    (partial K+K partial+QH+HQ+dotPi)(F)
      =R_Dh F+s R_DE Fh+s R_E Fa+bF R_h+EF R_Dh.

All four R expressions vanish by the contraction or its derivative. This certificate verifies the identity without any actual source or artificial numerical matrix evaluation. It also exhibits exactly why deleting QH or HQ is unjustified.

A closed input eliminates K partial(F), but it does not eliminate HQ(F). Projector vanishing on one degree does not eliminate compositions passing through adjacent degrees. Idempotence only implies E b E=0 and complementary off-diagonality of b; it does not imply b=0.

## 3. Frame derivative and factor-two normalization

On standard chart A let ell_A=tau_A^-1 ell_ref. The accepted product-root theorem identifies a degree-r strict endomorphism with square-line power r; the common root twist cancels. If lambda=tau_B/tau_A, the coefficient laws are

    F_B=lambda^r F_A,    h_B=lambda^-1 h_A,    E_B=E_A.

For the coefficient operator derivative K_A=[V,H_A], direct differentiation gives

    K_B(lambda^r F_A)
      =lambda^(r-1)(K_A(F_A)-(V log lambda)H_A(F_A)).

This is inhomogeneous. A list of all-chart 5/0 coefficient estimates alone would not turn these into a global degree-minus-one morphism.

A connection with square-line coefficient a_A acts on End-degree r by V+r a_A. Therefore its operator derivative is

    K^nabla_A=K_A-a_A H_A,
    a_B=a_A-V log lambda.

The two overlap errors cancel. The minus sign is output degree minus input degree, namely -1.

The original-reference connection has a_ref,A=-V log tau_A. Its derivative is consequently K_A+(V log tau_A)H_A. At an infinity coordinate for V_r, V log tau_A=-u_r/t_r. The reference frame itself is not regular at infinity. In an invented local degree-minus-one line example, a constant local coefficient 1 corresponds to reference coefficient t. Its transported reference derivative is -u/t, although its local coefficient derivative is zero. Since ord_O(u)=1 and ord_O(t)=2, this detects a genuine possible pole. It proves the correction cannot be omitted in general; it does not claim sharpness for every actual source entry.

For the retained old connection, the actual root coefficient in e_m is

    alpha_m=(1/2)V log h_m,
    e_m^2=h_m ell_m,
    c_m,A=e_m^2/ell_A=nu_m^2 tau_A.

The connection coefficient on the square line is

    a_m,A=2 alpha_m-V log c_m,A
         =-sum_i m_i V log x_i-V log tau_A.

The factor 2 is mandatory. H changes the root power by -2, equivalently the square-line power by -1. Using alpha_m itself as the square-line coefficient loses this factor. On one differentiated coordinate the full table is

    finite bit 0: 0;          finite bit 1: -y/x;
    infinity bit 0: u/t;      infinity bit 1: 0.

This independently checks both the infinity sign and the cancellation for old bit one. The source's actual nonzero alpha_m in the root frame has not been reset to zero.

## 4. All-chart bounds and ownership

The accepted homogeneous theorem supplies global carrier-square clearance for h and, through the whole-projector contraction identity, for E. This already includes the auxiliary v0 divisor and the product-root frame identification. On every regular standard chart,

    H_o,A^2 h_A and H_o,A^2 E_A are regular.

The genuine invariant V preserves the coordinate rings on both finite and infinity charts. Differentiating these cleared numerators therefore gives Vh_A<=3/0 and VE_A<=3/0. The three coefficient terms of K have carrier charges 3, 3+2, and 2+3. This proves 5/0 without pretending the bare derivatives glue homogeneously. Since E has degree zero, its derivatives do glue and have no scalar-connection correction.

For the original-reference derivative, multiplying by t_r clears its sole new infinity coefficient -u_r/t_r. Its H factor needs carrier four, so carrier five suffices after padding. This gives the separate standard-factor certificate. It cannot be spent from a standard bound belonging to an absent tag or relabeled as a legal factor without a domain/divisibility proof.

For the old connection, the legal sections have local representatives

    finite L_0=1, L_1=x(x^2-1);
    infinity L_0=t, L_1=1-t^2.

These are the accepted local representatives of sections of degrees one and three, rather than unweighted transports of finite functions. Each L_m a_m,A is regular. Combining a_m,A H_A with K_A gives 5/1, and the exact connection overlap law makes this an all-chart covariant statement. There is no extra standard factor: at infinity bit zero its required t is the actual connection owner's legal factor; at infinity bit one the coefficient is zero.

Every carrier charge is owned by the selected full homotopy occurrence. If the old connection has a different owner n, its legal charge remains at n: the two summands have respectively carrier5 at o, and carrier4 at o plus legal1 at n. Equal labels or equal divisor equations do not identify occurrences. At a common prime, multiplicities add. Derivatives of an already meromorphic input belong to H(DF), a separate summand; they are not covered by the isolated kernel increment five.

## 5. Complete contraction-parallel normalization

All three correction signs check. This is an additional mathematical rewriting, not a replacement of the frozen source connection.

Write b=nabla E, c=nabla d and P=1-E. Put

    A=[b,E].

Differentiating E^2=E gives E b E=0 and [A,E]=b. Thus nabla_1=nabla-A makes E parallel. Differentiating Ed=dE=0 gives E c E=0 and

    [A,d]=Ec+cE,
    q=nabla_1 d=P c P.

The induced derivative of d^2=0 gives dq+qd=0; also Eq=qE=0. With

    U=-h q,

one has [U,d]=(dh+hd)q=q and [U,E]=0. Hence nabla_2=nabla_1-U makes both d and E parallel.

Let k=nabla_2 h. Differentiating dh+hd=1-E, h^2=0 and Eh=hE=0 using nabla_2 gives

    dk+kd=0,    hk+kh=0,    Ek=kE=0.

Set C=-dk. Then [C,d]=[C,E]=0 and

    [C,h]=-dkh+hdk=(dh+hd)k=k.

Consequently S=A+U+C and nabla_3=nabla-S make d,E,h parallel. On strict endomorphisms the correction is ad_S, and it follows exactly that

    [nabla,H]=[ad_S,H].

There is no assertion that these new connections have zero curvature. Their preservation of d,E,h is the proved property. S contains the original derivatives and must remain in any transformed scalar expression. A multi-owner kernel may have different contractions and generally different S_o; the differences are not zero merely because all are called normalization corrections. For a pure word P=H_n ... H_1 differentiated with one common initial connection, the exact telescope is

    D(P)=L_n P-P L_1
      +sum_i H_n ... H_(i+1)(L_i-L_(i+1))H_i ... H_1,
    L_i=ad_(S_i).

The common-connection hypothesis matters. In this frozen source, identical carrier choices give identical h and E; with the same initial connection they consequently give identical S as rational maps. Their clearance occurrences nevertheless remain distinct. If different owner-specific old connections are used, their differences must first be retained in conversion to a common derivative. The reviewed producer wording distinguishes this functional equality from the separate occurrence bookkeeping.

For a scalar square-line connection a_A, a useful stronger cancellation is valid. Differentiated Eh=hE=0 yields [A,h]=E(nabla h)+(nabla h)E. Also [U,h]=-h(nabla d)h. Substitution, followed by hdh=h, gives

    k=Vh-E(Vh)-(Vh)E+h(Vd)h.

All scalar-connection terms cancel. Under standard frame change, the inhomogeneous term -(V log lambda)h from Vh is canceled by +(V log lambda)hdh from h(Vd)h. Thus k is genuinely a global degree-minus-one map, independent of the initial scalar line-connection coefficient. Its terms have carrier allowances 3,5,5,4 and no legal charge, so k<=5/0. The degree-zero C=-dk is also globally 5/0.

A<=5/0. For the old connection c<=0/1, and the equal expressions q=c-Ec-cE and U=-h c(1-E) give q<=2/1 and U<=4/1. Hence S<=5/1 for a common owner. The reference connection instead contributes its named standard factor. This does not improve the original full kernel's final owner bound by itself and does not make S a derivative-free expression.

## 6. Adjoint and normalized-derivative scope

The dual product rule correctly retains K*ell and the derivative of the dual row; a scalar weight contributes its own Vw term. Transposing or contracting the homotopy does not make it constant. For a finite Laurent coefficient functional, the stated adjoint of partial_x is

    [x^e](kappa partial_x f)
      =[x^e]((-partial_x kappa+(e+1)x^-1 kappa)f).

This follows by applying the product rule to the zero residue of a derivative. It requires the stated Laurent/residue domain and includes the exponent-weight term. It does not choose an expansion for an uncleared rational carrier denominator.

The genuine invariant V is regular at branch points and exchanges deck parity in its differentiated coordinate. Dividing it by y to obtain a normalized coordinate derivative is a different operation. No legal-free estimate for that division or unchanged full-sign membership follows from the present V estimates. A complete ordered, weighted, frame-correct kernel remains necessary before any scalar cancellation or terminal divergence can be asserted.
