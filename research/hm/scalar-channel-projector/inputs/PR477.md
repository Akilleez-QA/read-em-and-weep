# The adjoint module is a nonsplit primary–Rao extension

Work over C on P(V)=P^4. Let F be the normalized Horrocks–Mumford bundle, B=End_0(F), E=F(3), and U=H^0(E), of dimension four. Let C be its twenty-five disjoint transverse evaluation-degeneracy lines, and S=Sym(V*). Write

A_j=H^1(F(j)), K_j=H^1(B(j-1)), M_d=H^1(I_C(d)), P_j=M_(6-j)*.

Here A denotes the primary cohomology module, not a five-dimensional normalizer representation. All these spaces have their actual multiplication actions; P uses the dual action. Let N=SP_0 be the submodule of P generated in degree zero. We prove the exact graded module sequence

0 -> L=U tensor A(-3) -> K -> N -> 0.                         (1)

It is nonsplit in the category of graded S-modules. We then identify the exact Tor correction when passing to a fixed polynomial quotient. In particular the primary submodule does not generally pass to that quotient merely by dividing it by its own polynomial multiples.

## 1. The actual map, before counting dimensions

The evaluation Eagon–Northcott resolution twisted to degree d is

0 -> D tensor B(d-10) -> Lambda^3U tensor F(d-7)
  -> Lambda^2U tensor O(d-5) -> I_C(d) -> 0,

where D=det U is the trivial normalizer character. Fix compatible trivializations of D and the ambient determinant line det V; the normalizer lies in SL(V). The conclusion is independent of its nonzero scalar normalization. Let Z_d be the image in the free term, so this resolution splits into

0 -> B(d-10) -> Lambda^3U tensor F(d-7) -> Z_d -> 0,
0 -> Z_d -> Lambda^2U tensor O(d-5) -> I_C(d) -> 0.

Since H^1 and H^2 of every line bundle on P^4 vanish, the second sequence identifies M_d naturally with H^2(Z_d). The first then gives the exact segment

M_d -> H^3(B(d-10)) -> Lambda^3U tensor H^3(F(d-7)).

Dualizing this finite-dimensional segment, and using B*=B, F*=F(1), and Serre duality, gives

(Lambda^3U)* tensor H^1(F(3-d)) -> H^1(B(5-d)) -> M_d*.

Set j=6-d. Since (Lambda^3U)*=U tensor D*, this is

U tensor A_(j-3) -> K_j -> P_j,                              (2)

exact at K_j. These maps come from the fixed sheaf resolution, not from a choice of linear maps between equal-dimensional representations. Connecting maps commute with multiplication. Serre duality identifies the transpose of multiplication by a homogeneous polynomial with multiplication by that same polynomial. Consequently (2), for all integers j, is an exact-at-the-middle sequence of graded S-module maps L -> K -> P.

At j=0, or d=6, the two adjacent primary groups are H^2(F(-1)) and H^3(F(-1)), both zero. Thus K_0 -> P_0 is an isomorphism. PR476 proves that K is generated by K_0. It follows that the image of K -> P is exactly N=SP_0. This is an equality of actual submodules and uses generation, rather than an equality of Hilbert functions.

## 2. Injectivity and the full extension

The remaining question in (2) is injectivity of L -> K. The established cohomology dimensions of K, in degrees zero through six, are

5, 24, 55, 85, 100, 85, 32,

and K is zero in all other degrees. The low degrees and generation/vanishing are in PR476; the middle degrees follow from the evaluation sequences in PR463 and PR457, and the endpoint from PR456. In particular the same evaluation sequence at twist minus two gives h^1(B(3))=4 h^1(F(1))+(h^0(O_C(2))-dim S_2)=40+(75-15)=100; the needed primary H^2 and H^3 groups vanish, and C has no quadratic equation. These are cohomology dimensions, not ranks of a newly selected multiplication matrix.

The Hilbert function of S/I_C in degrees zero through six is

1, 5, 15, 35, 70, 120, 170.

Since C is a disjoint union of lines, h^0(O_C(d))=25(d+1) for d>=0. The restriction sequence therefore gives

(dim M_0,...,dim M_6)=(24,45,60,65,55,30,5).

There is no negative Rao part, and PR471 gives M_d=0 for d>=7. PR475 proves the actual module quotient P/N=R_6 tensor k(-1), where dim R_6=6. Hence the Hilbert function of N is

5, 24, 55, 65, 60, 45, 24.

Primary HM cohomology gives A the Hilbert function 5,10,10,2 in degrees zero through three, and zero in all other degrees. Thus L has Hilbert function 20,40,40,8 in degrees three through six. This is exactly dim K_j-dim N_j in every degree. Exactness of the already constructed maps says that the image of L_j is the entire kernel of K_j -> N_j. Its dimension equals dim L_j, so the map is injective in every degree. This proves (1). The dimension count proves injectivity only after both exactness and identification of the image N have been established.

The extension does not split as graded modules. If K were L direct-sum N with the maps of (1), projection onto L would annihilate K_0, since L_0=0. Generation by K_0 would force it to annihilate all of K, contradicting nonzero L. Thus the primary contribution is coupled to the degree-zero adjoint generators, not a separate summand of generators.

## 3. What this says about cubic relations

Use the isomorphism K_0=N_0=V to choose their common minimal free cover S tensor V. Let R_K and R_N be its respective kernels. The surjection K -> N gives

0 -> R_K -> R_N -> L -> 0.                                  (3)

Since L starts in degree three, R_K and R_N agree through degree two. Their subspaces of degree-three products of lower-degree relations consequently agree. Dividing degree three of (3) by this common product subspace yields an exact sequence of minimal relation spaces

0 -> (R_K/S_+R_K)_3 -> (R_N/S_+R_N)_3 -> U tensor A_0 -> 0.

Thus N has exactly twenty more minimal cubic relations than K. The quotient is the actual U tensor A_0, not an unspecified twenty-dimensional representation. This does not prove that K has no cubic relations: the possible cubic ghost pair in its resolution remains unresolved. Instead it locates precisely the twenty cubic relations that disappear when passing from the Rao-generated module N to its adjoint extension K. In degrees one and two both covers have the Euler relation and the same fifteen quadratic relations from PR476.

## 4. The correction for a fixed pair

For any homogeneous ideal J of S, tensoring (1) with S/J gives the exact sequence

Tor_1^S(S/J,N) --delta--> L/JL -> K/JK -> N/JN -> 0.

The kernel of L/JL -> K/JK is (L intersect JK)/JL. Therefore delta records the additional primary cancellations arising from lifts of classes in N; it is not legitimate to replace L intersect JK by JL without a proof. Equivalently,

0 -> (L/JL)/im(delta) -> K/JK -> N/JN -> 0.                  (4)

Take a quadratic u and cubic v, J=(u,v), without assuming any rank or genericity for their actual cohomological actions. With Q_5=(K/JK)_6, equation (4) becomes

0 -> [U tensor (A_3/(u A_1+v A_0))]/im(delta_6)
  -> Q_5 -> (N/(u,v)N)_6 -> 0.                             (5)

The analogous degree-five formula has primary term U tensor (A_2/u A_0), since A_-1=0. For J=(u^2,v), the degree-six primary term is U tensor (A_3/v A_0), since the quadratic-square contribution would come from A_-1. Each statement retains its own Tor image; none replaces a square by a first power.

If u,v form a regular sequence in S, delta has the following explicit, coefficient-free lift description. The Koszul complex uses differential (a,b) -> ua+vb and boundary c -> (-vc,uc). A degree-six cycle for N is a pair a in N_4, b in N_3 with ua+vb=0. Choose lifts a~ in K_4 and b~ in K_3. Then u a~+v b~ lies in L_6, and its class modulo u L_4+v L_3 is delta_6 of the cycle. Changing the lifts changes this by JL. A Koszul boundary lifts to a zero combination, so this depends only on the homology class. This proves the formula, with the displayed sign convention, and identifies the obstruction rather than merely naming Tor. For nonregular pairs the abstract exact sequence remains valid, but this two-term Koszul homology need not compute Tor.

Equation (5) gives a necessary and sufficient two-part criterion for Q_5=0: the quotient (N/(u,v)N)_6 must vanish, and delta_6 must cover the entire displayed primary quotient. Vanishing of that primary quotient is sufficient for the second condition, but is not necessary. No value for either condition at the original fixed pair is asserted.
