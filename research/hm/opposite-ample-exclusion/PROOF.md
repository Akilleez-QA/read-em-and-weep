## 2. Plane Koszul exactness forces complete negative coefficient surjectivity

The ideal of R is generated by the two negative linear forms. Its actual Koszul resolution, tensored by B(3), is

    0→(Λ²W)⊗B(1)→W⊗B(2)→I_R B(3)→0.              (5)

The determinant of W is trivial. The first vanishing in (1) makes the induced map

    W⊗H1(B(2))→H1(I_R B(3))

surjective. The restriction exact sequence and H1(B_R(3))=0 from (3) then make

    H1(I_R B(3))→H1(B(3))

surjective. Their composite is the actual multiplication by the two negative coordinate forms. Taking the positive inversion part, which is exact in characteristic zero, proves

    Ψ: W⊗U → P3 is onto.                            (6)

The positive target receives precisely the negative H1(B(2)) part because W is odd. This is simultaneous coefficient surjectivity, not surjectivity of a single hyperplane multiplication map.

## 3. The original pencil has no constant quotient

Dualizing (2) gives a left-exact sequence

    0→C*→P3* O→U* O(1).                             (7)

The global-section map in (7) is the transpose of (6). More explicitly, Γ is the universal multiplication by the negative defining linear form. The parameter-space coefficient convention may use W or its dual; the canonical determinant pairing W≅W* identifies these conventions. Equivalently, the annihilator identification of the negative direction line and negative equation line induces this invertible reparametrization. It only precomposes the coefficient map by an isomorphism. Thus the actual map in (7) is injective on global sections by (6).

It follows that H0(C*)=0. No constant subpencil has been discarded in this argument: it uses the original cohomological pencil and then PENCIL's canonical identification of its cokernel with the reduced C.

By FREE and Birkhoff–Grothendieck, C splits as a direct sum of line bundles of nonnegative degrees. H0(C*) counts exactly its degree-zero summands. Therefore every splitting degree is strictly positive. This is precisely ampleness of a bundle on P1, proving the first assertion in (4).

## 4. A small Leray bound controls degree-one summands

Put

    M=p*B(3)⊗π*O(-3)=p*B⊗O_X(3E).                 (8)

The p-direct images are p_*M=B, R1p_*M=K, and Rq p_*M=0 for q≥2. The exceptional filtration specializes to the actual sequence

    0→i_*B_R(2)→K→i_*(W⊗B_R(3))→0,                (9)

where i:R→P4. Indeed R1p_*O(E)=0, the j=2 layer is O_R(2), and the j=3 layer is W⊗O_R(3); tensoring with B gives (9). No splitting of this extension is assumed.

Both outer terms in (9) have H1 zero by (3), so H1(P4,K)=0. The p-Leray spectral sequence, with only its rows zero and one, therefore identifies

    H2(X,M)=coker[H0(P4,K)→H2(P4,B)].                (10)

In particular dim H2(X,M)≤2 by the full-space bound in (1). We neither set the displayed transgression to zero nor need to know its value.

On the π side, the base has dimension one. Its Leray spectral sequence therefore gives a canonical injection

    H1(T,(R1π_*p*B(3))⊗O(-3)) ↪ H2(X,M).           (11)

This injection does not require a higher-direct-image vanishing.

The actual C is the positive direct summand of R1π_*p*B(3). Hence H1(C(-3)) is a direct summand of the space in (11), not necessarily the entire space. Consequently h1(C(-3))≤2. Serre duality on T, with ω_T=O(-2) and det W=1, gives

    h0(C*(1))=h1(C(-3))≤2,

which proves the numerical bound.

To sharpen this to (4), retain the representation-level statement of TWIST, Section2. The functorial symmetric-square monad for B has acyclic terms in the relevant twist, and makes H2(B) a quotient of Λ²N⊗Hcoord. Here N denotes the CURRENT right monad target from MONAD. This avoids transferring the old W/W' labels in TWIST. The fixed character identities are

    Λ²(A⊕W')=1⊕A⊕T6,
    (1⊕A⊕T6)⊗(V⊕W)=2V⊕2P⊕2D⊕3W⊕2S⊕2T6.       (12)

The target contains neither the trivial representation nor W'. Semisimplicity and the full dimension bound dim H2(B)≤2 now imply H2(B) is either zero or W. Equation (10) and the injection (11) imply the same alternative for H1(C(-3)); equivariant Serre duality and W*=W prove (4).

Since C is ample, H0(C*(1)) is exactly the dual multiplicity representation of its degree-one part. Thus that part is either absent or W O(1). In particular W' O(1) is impossible. The odd twist O(-3) changes central parity: we used C(-3) as a direct summand in the FULL Leray group, not as its unchanged positive eigenspace.
