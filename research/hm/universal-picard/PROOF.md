# Universal Picard gluing: base change and the relative splitting obstruction

Status: authored structural proof, independently mathematically reviewed (PASS); not formally checked. All assertions are conditional on the exact geometric inputs below. Work over C. No selected actual HM/source coefficient, matrix, rank or numerical instance is evaluated.

## 1. Inputs and statement

Use PR582, commit **ec6e753518a59406b16e565b042621afad635cff**, `research/hm/twisted-conductor/PROOF.md`, and its pinned dependencies PR574, PR577, PR579 and PR581. Their notation is retained: ν:X→S is the normalization, π:X→B=P¹ is the elliptic fibration, h:B→Γ normalizes a six-nodal curve, and g:S→Γ satisfies gν=hπ. The thirty double points Z of S occur in six blocks Z_q of five over the nodes q of Γ. Their sixty normalization preimages W are reduced. Write N=O_B(−5). The actual normalization quotient factors through restriction to W, and Rπ_*O_X≅O_B⊕N[−1].

Choose orientations of the pairs of normalization preimages a_q,b_q of the six nodes. Let

    P=Spec A=(G_m)^30,   A=C[t_z^{±1}:z∈Z],
    D=Σ_q(a_q+b_q),      G=g×id_P,   H=h×id_P.

Let ℒ be the universal gluing line bundle on S×P: its normalized pullback is O_{X×P}, and its gluing ratio at z is t_z. This fixes a normalization of the universal family; tensoring it by a line bundle pulled back from P would give an equivalent local statement. The construction is the gluing construction in PR581, performed over A. In the surface-local fiber-product ring, the universal glued module {(f,g):f(0)=t_z g(0)} is freely generated by (t_z,1): multiplying the second branch by the unit t_z identifies it with the equal-residue module. Thus this is a line bundle, not merely a flat family of rank-one sheaves.

For u∈P(C), write L_u=ℒ|_{S×{u}} and let r(u) count the blocks in which the five ratios are not all equal. The new conclusions are:

1. G_*ℒ= h_*O_B(−D) ⊠ O_P. Its specialization injects into g_*L_u with cokernel of length 6−r(u).
2. The relative finite defect has a two-term free resolution over A. Its first Tor at u is one residue line for each uniform block, and no higher Tor occurs.
3. Derived base change holds by an explicit normalization complex whose terms are A-flat. Nevertheless, on any parameter neighborhood of a point with a uniform block, RG_*ℒ cannot split as the direct sum of its cohomology sheaves.
4. Conversely, on the open locus with no uniform block, such a splitting exists Zariski locally in the parameter. Thus the precise obstruction locus for parameter-local splitting is the union of the six uniform-block subtori, each of codimension four.

These are relative module-complex statements. They neither contradict the individual-fiber splitting of PR582 nor assert multiplicative formality or any selected source-instance conclusion.

## 2. Universal kernel and finite defect

Trivialize the quotient lines compatibly with the universal gluing construction and put V_P=⊕_{z∈Z}O_{{g(z)}×P}. Tensoring the normalization sequence with ℒ yields

    0 → ℒ → (ν×id_P)_*O_{X×P} → T_P → 0,

where G_*T_P=V_P and T_P has no higher direct images. The degree-zero residue map is

    d_P:H_*O_{B×P} → V_P.

At a fixed node q, it factors through the surjective residue map to A², followed by

    M_q:A² → A⁵,   (α,β) ↦ (α−t_zβ)_{z∈Z_q}.       (1)

This factorization can be read in completed node coordinates R=C[[s,t]]/(st), Rbar=C[[s]]⊕C[[t]], but the residue sequence itself is algebraic. If M_q(α,β)=0, subtraction of two rows gives (t_i−t_j)β=0. The Laurent polynomial ring A is a domain, and t_i−t_j is nonzero for distinct independent coordinates. Hence β=0 and then α=0. Thus M_q is injective, including after restriction to any nonempty open of the integral parameter space.

Consequently the kernel of d_P consists exactly of functions vanishing at both branch preimages of every node. Globally,

    ker(d_P)=H_*O_{B×P}(−D×P)
            =h_*O_B(−D) ⊠ O_P.                     (2)

The kernel equals G_*ℒ by left exactness of direct image. In particular this universal pushforward is flat over P, although it is not the family of all the individual pushforwards under ordinary specialization.

Let K_q=coker(M_q), regarded as a sheaf on q×P, and K_P=⊕_q K_q. Since the branch residue map is onto, coker(d_P)=K_P. Equation (1) gives the A-free resolution

    0 → A² → A⁵ → K_q → 0.                         (3)

Thus K_q has A-projective dimension at most one; globally it is exactly one since the fibers below show it is not flat. At parameter points where its fiber rank is constant and the map splits, its localization is free instead.

Tensor (3) with k(u)=C. The two columns have rank one when the five ratios are equal and rank two otherwise. Therefore

    Tor_1^A(K_q,k(u)) = ker M_q(u),

which has dimension one in the uniform case and zero otherwise; Tor_j=0 for j≥2. The dimension of K_q⊗_A k(u) is respectively four or three. As Γ-modules all these Tor modules are residue modules at q, since K_q is annihilated by the ideal of q×P.

## 3. Explicit derived base change

This section establishes base change directly rather than assuming that underived pushforward commutes with it. Set Π=π×id_P. Pulling a fixed splitting of Rπ_*O_X along the flat projection gives

    RΠ_*O_{X×P} ≅ O_{B×P} ⊕ (N⊠O_P)[−1].          (4)

For completeness, product base change here follows by taking a finite affine Čech cover over each affine open in B: the complex computing the proper direct image is tensored with the flat C-algebra A. This computes its product pullback and preserves the original quasi-isomorphisms. The same argument, or the displayed A-flat complexes below, applies to specialization.

The actual quotient map still factors through O_{W×P}. Write F=π_*O_W. It is supported on the twelve cusp points. The possible component of the restriction map from (N⊠O_P)[−1] to F⊠O_P lies in

    Ext¹_{B×P}(N⊠O_P,F⊠O_P)
      =H¹(B×P,(N∨⊗F)⊠O_P)=0.                     (5)

The equality uses that N is locally free. The vanishing uses that the support is a finite scheme over the affine scheme P, hence affine. Universal gluing weights are then applied after this restriction; they cannot restore the vanished component. Finite H_* is exact. Taking the cone of the normalization map consequently gives a noncanonical isomorphism

    RG_*ℒ ≅ C_P ⊕ (h_*N ⊠ O_P)[−1],
    C_P=[ H_*O_{B×P} --d_P--> V_P ],                (6)

with the displayed complex in degrees zero and one. Both its terms are flat over A: the first is an external product of a C-sheaf with A, and the second is a sum of copies of A. The elliptic summand is also flat over A. They are bounded flat complexes for the operation of derived specialization in the parameter direction.

Let i_u:Γ×{u}→Γ×P. The derived pullback of (6) is therefore calculated by ordinary termwise specialization, giving

    Li_u^*RG_*ℒ ≅ [h_*O_B --d_{L_u}--> V_{L_u}]
                    ⊕ h_*N[−1] ≅ Rg_*L_u.        (7)

The maps are precisely the specialized residue-difference maps, so (7) is the base-change identification supplied by the normalization triangles, not just an abstract coincidence of cohomology dimensions. This also proves

    H⁰(RG_*ℒ)=h_*O_B(−D)⊠O_P,
    H¹(RG_*ℒ)=K_P⊕(h_*N⊠O_P),                    (8)

and there is no other cohomology. Importantly, (6) and (8) do not split C_P into its own cohomology sheaves.

## 4. Failure of ordinary base change

The map from the specialization of (2) to g_*L_u is the inclusion of functions whose two branch residues vanish into the kernel of the specialized residue map. At a nonuniform block, that kernel is already the conductor m_R. At a uniform block of common ratio τ, the kernel is

    R_τ={(f,g)∈Rbar:f(0)=τg(0)} ≅ R.

Its conductor submodule is m_R, and R_τ/m_R is a one-dimensional residue line. Away from nodes the map is an isomorphism. Hence there is a canonical exact sequence, with the quotient described independently of choices as the Tor module,

    0 → (G_*ℒ)|_u → g_*L_u
      → Tor_1^A(K_P,k(u)) → 0.                    (9)

The quotient has length 6−r(u). This sequence can alternatively be obtained from the flat two-term complex in (6). At a uniform node it is the nonsplit sequence 0→m_R→R_τ→k(q)→0: a splitting would put a nonzero finite-length submodule inside the torsion-free module R_τ.

Thus ordinary base change is an isomorphism exactly on the all-nonuniform locus. The flat universal pushforward has degree −6 on each fiber, whereas the individual pushforward has degree −r(u), with the difference accounted for by (9).

## 5. Relative nonsplitting and its exact locus

Suppose u has a uniform block q. Assume, for contradiction, that on some open parameter neighborhood U of u there is an isomorphism

    RG_*ℒ|_{Γ×U} ≅ H⁰(RG_*ℒ)|_{Γ×U}
                     ⊕ H¹(RG_*ℒ)|_{Γ×U}[−1].     (10)

The same argument applies if such an isomorphism is asserted only near (q,u). By (8), the degree-zero sheaf and the elliptic part of the degree-one sheaf are parameter-flat. Derived specialization of the right side of (10) therefore has degree-zero cohomology

    h_*O_B(−D) ⊕ Tor_1^A(K_P,k(u)).                (11)

At q its completed stalk contains m_R⊕k(q). The residue summand is nonzero and is annihilated by the nonzerodivisor s+t of R. The actual degree-zero cohomology on the left, by (7), is R_τ≅R at q and has no such torsion. This is impossible. Completion is faithfully flat, so the contradiction also excludes an algebraic splitting. In particular, the individual-fiber formality in PR582 does not extend to a relative cohomology splitting across even one uniform block.

Conversely, suppose all six blocks are nonuniform at u. For each block choose two unequal ratios at u. On the intersection of the six principal opens where those differences are units, the corresponding two-by-two minors of M_q are units. Elementary projection onto those two rows gives a left inverse A⁵→A² and a complement to im M_q. Hence, on this parameter neighborhood,

    V_P = im(d_P) ⊕ K_P,
    C_P ≅ [H_*O_{B×P}→im(d_P)] ⊕ K_P[−1]
        ≃ (h_*O_B(−D)⊠O_P) ⊕ K_P[−1].            (12)

Combining (12) with (6) proves relative cohomology splitting there. This is only a parameter-local assertion; no global choice of complements on the whole open locus is needed.

For each q, its uniform locus is defined by the four independent equations t_z=t_{z_0} for z≠z_0 in the block. It is a smooth codimension-four subtorus of P. Equations (10)–(12) identify the union of these six subtori as the precise locus where a splitting cannot exist on any parameter neighborhood. At their common intersection, the six-dimensional descent torus, all six residue-line extensions in (9) are present.

## 6. Contribution and limits

The mechanism is not failure of derived base change. Derived base change holds by (6)–(7). The mechanism is nonflatness of the finite relative defect and a nontrivial relative extension that joins its first Tor to the conductor specialization, recovering the torsion-free fiber pushforward in (9). Fiberwise, the finite residue image is a vector subspace and has a complement; universally, that image has a nonflat quotient along the uniform locus and has no such complement there.

This resolves the family-level dependency deliberately left open by PR582 and adds the sharp locus where parameter-local splitting is possible. It concerns rank-one Picard gluing and coherent derived images, not a higher-order or algebraicity assertion about the original HM construction. It does not evaluate a selected actual HM/source instance or require large computation.
