# Scalar endomorphism rigidity and the full tensor bridge

The displayed normalizer tensor factors T⊗U′ and A*⊗J_5 are irreducible; this uses the Heisenberg factor and the quotient irreducible, not irreducibility of a tensor product for an arbitrary group.

## A relation-invariance exclusion

Write L=ker(S⊗W→H). We have L_2≠0 and I_2=0. For each d=0,1,2, it is impossible that every linear map W→H_d extends to an S-linear degree-d endomorphism of H.

Suppose otherwise. Fix r=Σ a_i⊗w_i in L_2, A∈End(W), and q∈S_d. The linear map w↦qA(w) would extend. Applying its extension to the relation r gives

q·[Σ a_i⊗A(w_i)]=0 in H_(2+d).

This holds for every q∈S_d. If z∈H_2 is nonzero, perfect pairing supplies k∈H_3 with b(z,k)≠0. Generation writes k as a sum of p w with p∈S_3 and w∈W, so some p gives b(pz,w)≠0. In particular S_3 z≠0. Since S_3=S_(3−d)S_d for d=0,1,2, annihilation by S_d is impossible. Thus the bracketed class above is zero, and (id⊗A)r belongs to L_2 for every A.

Choose a basis x,y of W and write r=a⊗x+b⊗y. The four matrix units in End(W) show that ax, ay, bx, by all vanish in H_2. Since H is generated by x,y, a and b annihilate H, hence a,b∈I_2=0. This forces r=0, contradicting L_2≠0. This is a universal module argument and uses no selected polynomial or HM-instance evaluation.

## Rigidity in degrees zero, one and two

Restriction to W injects E_d into Hom_C(W,H_d). These subspaces are normalizer-stable, and scalar multiplication embeds R_d in E_d. The three ambient representations and scalar subspaces are

- d=0: Hom(W,W)=1⊕U′, scalar subspace 1;
- d=1: Hom(W,T⊗W)=T⊕(T⊗U′), scalar subspace T;
- d=2: Hom(W,A*⊗Q_4)=(A*⊗U′)⊕(A*⊗J_5), scalar subspace A*⊗U′.

Each quotient by the displayed scalar subspace is irreducible. Therefore E_d is either exactly R_d or the entire ambient Hom-space. The latter is excluded by the preceding argument. Consequently

E_0=R_0=C, E_1=R_1=T, E_2=R_2=S_2.

No claim about an arbitrary endomorphism being equivariant is needed: the space of all endomorphisms is stable under normalizer conjugation, which is sufficient for this representation argument.

## Grading audit and global isomorphism

For every integer n, there is a natural identification

(H⊗_S H)_n* ≅ E_(5−n).

Indeed, a balanced functional f on total tensor degree n defines a unique map u:H_i→H_(i+5−n) by b(u(h),k)=f(h⊗k) for k∈H_(n−i). Perfectness provides existence and uniqueness. Balance f(sh⊗k)=f(h⊗sk), together with compatibility of b, gives u(sh)=s u(h), including degrees in which a graded component is zero. Conversely u gives such a balanced functional. Tensor swap corresponds to b-adjoint. Thus

(Sym²_S H)_n* ≅ E_(5−n)^+,
(Λ²_S H)_n* ≅ E_(5−n)^−.

Scalar multiplication is self-adjoint, because b(qh,k)=b(h,qk). The rigidity just proved gives dimensions of Sym²H in degrees 3,4,5 equal to dim R_2, dim R_1, dim R_0, respectively. Since Φ is onto N and N_n=R_(5−n)*, Φ is an isomorphism also in those degrees.

The tensor product is generated in degree zero. Its component of degree n>5 vanishes: every tensor can be written as a sum p(w⊗w′) with p∈S_n, and p acts by zero on the first H-factor. Negative components vanish as well. Therefore

Φ:Sym²_S H ≅ N

in every degree, and

H⊗_S H ≅ N⊕S/m².

The full tensor-pairing kernel is precisely S/m². Its symmetric part is zero. In particular the symmetric Hilbert function is (3,15,25,15,5,1), and the full tensor Hilbert function is (4,20,25,15,5,1).

## Next structural consequence: the endomorphism algebra

Under the preceding identification, dualizing Φ identifies the scalar embedding R_d→E_d^+ exactly: for q∈R_d and tensor degree 5−d, evaluation of Φ(hk) at q is b(qh,k). Hence the whole self-adjoint subspace of E is the scalar copy of R, not merely an abstract vector-space isomorphism.

The skew-adjoint space is M_d=(S/m²)_(5−d)*, with its dual multiplication action. Thus M is five-dimensional in degree 4, one-dimensional in degree 5, and zero otherwise. There are no endomorphisms in negative degrees (generation in degree zero) or above degree 5 (support). Products of two elements of M have degree at least 8 and vanish. Scalars are central. The splitting by adjoint therefore yields a canonical algebra isomorphism

End_S(H) ≅ R ⋉ M,

where the right side is the commutative square-zero extension with product (r,m)(r′,m′)=(rr′,rm′+r′m). The S_1 action M_4→M_5 is the dual evaluation action T⊗T*→C, up to the harmless normalization of det W; m²M=0. In particular End_S(H) has Hilbert function (1,5,15,25,20,4) and dimension 70. This commutativity concerns this graded S-linear endomorphism algebra, not all C-linear endomorphisms of H.

## Scope and review status

This is a structural derivation and independent mathematical audit of the endomorphism exclusion. It is conditional on the pinned representation, generation, and pairing inputs; it is not formal proof-assistant verification. It uses no actual HM matrices, numerical ranks, selected source-instance evaluation, coefficient search, or large computation. The full symmetric bridge is resolved, but H's Betti parameters, the original fixed-pair/source question, higher-order lifting, and algebraicity are not resolved here.
