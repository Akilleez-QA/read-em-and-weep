# Recovering the conductor and normalization from the primary resolution

Status: independently mathematically reviewed (PASS); see REVIEW.md. Not formally checked. Work over C. No selected HM/source coefficients, evaluation matrices, numerical rank test, numerical search, or large computation are used.

In particular A is a domain of dimension three and N is a normal domain finite and birational over A. Normality of this graded section ring also follows directly from normality of X: after choosing a Cartier divisor representing H and a variable t, it is the intersection of C(X)[t] with the Gauss valuation rings encoding div(f)+nH >= 0 for the homogeneous terms f t^n. Thus the finite ring N in these inputs is the integral closure of A, not merely a module with its Hilbert series.

## 2. The final cubic column is the actual node ideal

Write g=(g1,...,g10)^T. Each gi is cubic. Dualizing the actual resolution of A into omega_P shows

Ext^3_P(A,omega_P) = coker(P(2)^10 --g^T--> P(5))
                         = (P/(g1,...,g10))(5).                 (1)

Input 2 identifies this actual Ext module with R(5). An isomorphism of P-modules preserves its annihilator, so

                         (g1,...,g10)=I_Z.                      (2)

This is equality of homogeneous ideals, without saturation or passage to radicals. Since I_Z has ten minimal cubic generators, the ten entries form a basis of (I_Z)_3. A change of bases in the primary resolution only changes this basis and its common scalar, not the recovered ideal.

## 3. The cubic ideal is exactly the graded conductor

The conductor is

c = {a in A : aN is contained in A} = Ann_A(N/A).

We first check faithfulness, rather than assume it from support. Canonical biduality for the CM ring R gives

Ext^4_P(omega_R,omega_P) isomorphic to R.

An element of P annihilating omega_R acts as zero on this Ext module, so it belongs to I_Z. Conversely I_Z annihilates omega_R by its natural R-module structure. Therefore Ann_P(omega_R)=I_Z. Input 2 and (2) now give

                         c=I_Z/I_S=(g1,...,g10)A.               (3)

In particular c is a nonzero radical ideal of A, generated by ten cubics. It cuts out the reduced affine cone over Z, including its vertex; no extra vertex-supported correction is being discarded. It is also an N-ideal: if aN is contained in A and n is in N, then na is in A and (na)N is contained in A.

Thus the last primary differential recovers not only the set of nonnormal points but the full homogeneous conductor ideal. Its projective sheaf is the ideal of the thirty reduced nodes in S.

## 4. A finite rank-one endomorphism lemma

Let D be a Noetherian domain with fraction field K, and let E be its finite normalization. Suppose T is a finite torsionfree rank-one D-module equipped with an E-module structure extending its D-action. Then

                         underline End_D(T)=E.                 (4)

Here the assertion is ungraded if no grading is specified; in the graded case all identifications respect degrees. To prove it, embed T into the one-dimensional K-vector space T tensor_D K. Every D-linear endomorphism extends to a K-linear endomorphism, hence is multiplication by a unique element q of K. Torsionfreeness makes this scalar representation injective. Consequently End_D(T) is a commutative subring of K. It is finite as a D-module: Hom_D(T,T) embeds into a finite direct sum of copies of T by evaluating on finite generators. Every element of this finite D-algebra is integral over D, by the determinant trick, and so lies in E. Conversely multiplication by E preserves T, and its scalar action is faithful. These two inclusions prove (4).

This argument does not silently replace D-linear endomorphisms by E-linear ones: it proves that every D-linear endomorphism is multiplication by an element of E, and hence is E-linear. Nor does it assume T is reflexive over D.

## 5. Two intrinsic reconstructions of the normalization

Set omega_A=Ext^2_P(A,omega_P). Since N is a normal CM domain, its canonical module omega_N is torsionfree of rank one over N. It is also torsionfree of rank one over A, because the fraction fields agree. Input 2 identifies omega_A with this module, with its induced N-action. Apply (4), first to T=omega_A and then to the nonzero N-stable ideal T=c. We obtain canonical graded A-algebra identifications

             N = underline End_A(omega_A)
               = underline End_A(c).                            (5)

The displayed algebra structures are composition of endomorphisms; the A-algebra maps are multiplication by A. The scalar embedding into the common fraction field makes their equality with the actual normalization unambiguous. In particular it is not necessary to choose normalization generators or a multiplication table first.

Both modules in (5) are intrinsically available from the primary resolution:

c=(entries of g)/I_S,

omega_A=ker(g^T:P(2)^10 -> P(5)) /
                    im(L^T:P(1)^10 -> P(2)^10).                  (6)

Equations (2), (3), (5), and (6) therefore give an intrinsic reconstruction from the primary complex, rather than a new Betti-table guess. Only using degree-zero End would be wrong: by (5) its value is N_0=C, whereas the complete graded endomorphism algebra is N.

There is a compatible module-theoretic double-Ext formula:

             Ext^2_P(omega_A,omega_P) isomorphic to N.           (7)

Indeed the natural isomorphism omega_N -> omega_A induced by A -> N becomes, on applying Ext^2_P(-,omega_P), an isomorphism from the left side of (7) to Ext^2_P(omega_N,omega_P); CM canonical biduality identifies the latter with N. Formula (7) is an isomorphism of graded modules. We do not infer a ring multiplication from an unspecified double-Ext isomorphism: (5) provides the intrinsic ring structure.

The actual inclusion and conductor extension can also be recovered from this double dual. Put D=RHom_P(-,omega_P) and Q=Ext^2_P(omega_A,omega_P). Since D(A) has only H^2=omega_A and H^3=R(5), its canonical truncation triangle is

omega_A[-2] -> D(A) -> R(5)[-3] -> omega_A[-1].

Apply D and perfect biduality over P. CM duality for R and omega_A gives the triangle

omega_R(-5)[-1] -> A -> Q -> omega_R(-5),

and hence the natural exact sequence

0 -> A -> Q -> omega_R(-5) -> 0.                              (7a)

Naturality identifies (7a) with the actual normalization extension in Input 2. More explicitly, D(N)=omega_N[-2] -> D(A) factors through omega_A[-2], and the induced map there is Input 2's natural isomorphism. Applying D makes A -> Q -> N equal the original A -> N. Thus the edge map in (7a), not only the isomorphism class of Q, recovers the actual inclusion. This is distinct from claiming the dualizing complex D(A) splits into its two cohomology modules.

Dualizing Input 3 also gives the actual minimal resolution

0 -> P(-5) direct-sum P(-3)^5 -> P(-2)^10 -> P^4 -> omega_A -> 0. (8)

Exactness follows because N is CM of codimension two; minimality is preserved by dualization. In particular the intrinsic canonical module in (6) has precisely four generators in degree zero.

Sheafifying (5), using localization of Hom for finite presented modules, yields

End_(O_S)(c tilde)=nu_*O_X,
Spec_S End_(O_S)(c tilde)=X.                                  (9)

Equivalently Proj of either graded endomorphism algebra in (5) is the given finite normalization. These identities do not assert that A itself is normal or CM.

## 6. The next exact consequence: resolution of the conductor ideal

Let T_i(V)=Tor_i^P(V,C). In 0 -> c -> A -> R -> 0, the map T_0(A) -> T_0(R) is the identity on C. For i>0 every map T_i(A) -> T_i(R) is zero by disjoint degree supports in Input 1:

- i=1: degree 6 maps to degree 3;
- i=2: degree 7 maps to degrees 4 and 5;
- i=3: degree 10 maps to degree 7;
- i>=4: T_i(A)=0.

The actual long exact Tor sequence therefore gives T_0(c)=T_1(R), T_i(c)=0 for i>=4, and short exact sequences of graded vector spaces

0 -> T_(i+1)(R) -> T_i(c) -> T_i(A) -> 0,     1<=i<=3.

Reading their degrees, the actual minimal conductor resolution is

0 -> P(-10) direct-sum P(-8)^5
  -> P(-7)^20
  -> P(-6)^10 direct-sum P(-5)^4 direct-sum P(-4)^10
  -> P(-3)^10 -> c -> 0.                                     (10)

The splitting of the displayed Tor sequences as vector spaces is not asserted to be canonical. Their graded dimensions nevertheless determine the free terms of the existing minimal resolution, with no cancellation hypothesis. Thus depth_P(c)=2 and reg_P(c)=7. This is compatible with c being a nonzero ideal of the three-dimensional normalization, but not a CM module; (5) did not require it to be CM or reflexive.

## 7. Contribution and limits

The new step connects the actual cubic last syzygy with the exact homogeneous conductor and reconstructs the normalization as an intrinsic endomorphism algebra of either that ideal or the canonical module. It also supplies the conductor's complete minimal resolution. The inputs had already identified the normalization and computed the two ambient resolutions; the new claim is the reconstruction and exact conductor interface between them, not a fresh proof of those inputs.

No explicit entries of L, g, or f are produced. No full-adjoint vanishing, selected-source membership, higher-order lifting, or algebraicity conclusion follows. General canonical-module terminology is as in the Stacks Project, https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0DW3; the endomorphism lemma needed here is proved in Section 4 rather than invoked as an unverified normalization criterion.
