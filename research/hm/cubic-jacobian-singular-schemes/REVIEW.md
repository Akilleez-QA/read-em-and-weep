## 1. Input and scope

The input gives the two-branch fiber-product completed rings in dimensions four and two, sixty distinct reduced bad values, and smoothness away from those values through the normalization isomorphisms. The quotient presentation

    A_d = C[[u_1,...,u_d,v_1,...,v_d]]/(u_i v_j for all i,j)

is equivalent to that fiber product: a series splits uniquely into its common constant, its positive-degree pure u-part and its positive-degree pure v-part after all mixed monomials are killed. Thus the new calculation requires no additional statement about actual cubic coefficients or the geometry of the branches.

## 2. Completion of algebraic differentials

The distinction between algebraic and continuous differentials is handled correctly. Since R is essentially of finite type over C, Omega_(R/C) is finite. Its scalar extension E to the Noetherian complete local ring A is finite. Every finite A-module N is complete and separated in its maximal-ideal-adic topology.

For every C-derivation delta:R->N, the product rule gives delta(m_R^n) contained in m_R^(n-1)N. Consequently delta sends Cauchy sequences to Cauchy sequences, has a unique continuous extension to A, and its additivity and product rule survive passage to limits. The m_R-adic topology on N agrees with the m_A-adic topology because m_A=m_R A. Restriction and extension therefore give the required natural bijection between continuous derivations A->N and Hom_A(E,N).

For the power-series quotient A_d, images of the variables determine a continuous derivation by convergent termwise differentiation. The derivation kills the kernel precisely when it kills each u_i v_j: that kernel is generated as an ordinary ideal by these finitely many elements, so the Leibniz rule suffices for arbitrary kernel elements. This gives exactly the displayed finite presentation E_d. The use of finite target modules does not weaken uniqueness: both E and E_d are objects in that category, and evaluating the natural Hom identification on these two objects gives inverse maps. A local C-algebra isomorphism of the complete rings is automatically adically continuous.

In particular, the proof does not make the invalid substitution of unrestricted algebraic differentials of a formal power-series ring for the completed finite algebraic differential module of R.

## 3. Fitting index and minors

The presentation has 2d generators. Under the explicitly stated convention, Fitt_d is therefore generated by d-by-d minors. Transposing between the relation-row display and the map matrix changes no minor ideal. Every such determinant lies in m^d because every entry lies in m.

For the converse, the rows (i_j,j), one for each j, are distinct even if some i_j coincide. Restricting to the dv columns gives the diagonal matrix with entries u_(i_j), so every pure u monomial of degree d occurs as a minor. With the roles reversed, rows (i,j_i) give every pure v monomial of degree d. Every mixed monomial is zero in A_d. Hence these minors generate m^d, proving exact equality, not merely radical equality or equality of supports. This also checks the boundary case d=1, where the answer is m itself.

## 4. Descent and globalization

Fitting ideals commute with scalar extension, and Noetherian local completion is faithfully flat. Thus equality of the two extended ideals descends to

    Fitt_d(Omega_(R/C)) = m_R^d.

No formal coordinates need to be algebraized. At a bad point the ideal stalk of the reduced finite set is its maximal ideal; away from that set both its powers and the Jacobian ideal are the unit ideal, since the variety is smooth of dimension d there. Equality of stalks yields the asserted sheaf identities on V and T with ordinary ideal powers. There is no hidden symbolic-power or ambient-embedding convention.

For d=2 and d=4 these powers are strictly smaller than the maximal ideal and have maximal-ideal radical. Thus the contrast with the reduced conductor is valid: the schemes have the same support but different scheme structures.

## 5. Lengths

Modulo m^d, the common constant survives once, and each branch contributes independently its positive-degree monomials of degrees 1 through d-1. The number of monomials of degree at most d-1 in d variables is binom(2d-1,d), giving

    length(A_d/m^d) = 2 binom(2d-1,d) - 1.

For d=4 this is 69; for d=2 it is 5. Completion preserves these finite lengths, and summing over sixty distinct support points gives 4140 and 300. These are scheme lengths, not claims about reduced point counts.
