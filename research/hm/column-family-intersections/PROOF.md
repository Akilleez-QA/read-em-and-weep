# The projective column family, finite intersections, and lost origin data

## Inputs and scope

Work over the established characteristic-zero field k. Let S=Sym(T), dim(T)=5, with homogeneous maximal ideal m. The established quadratic presentation is

S(-2) tensor U -> S tensor W -> H -> 0,

with dim(W)=2 and dim(U)=10. Put I=Ann_S(H), D=Fitt_0(H), R=S/I, and Q=S/D.

We use two pinned structural inputs, not a selected source-instance evaluation:

1. The universal binary tensor description identifies U=E tensor W and S_2=E tensor Sym^2(W), where dim(E)=5. After choosing a basis x,y of W and the corresponding determinant trivialization, contraction by lambda=(alpha,beta) sends e tensor x to e tensor x(beta x-alpha y), and e tensor y to e tensor y(beta x-alpha y).
2. For every nonzero lambda over every field extension, the column ideal J_lambda generated by these ten quadratic entries satisfies HF(S/J_lambda)=(1,5,5). In particular m^3 is contained in J_lambda.

The auxiliary binary multiplication in Sym(W) is not multiplication in the five-variable ring S. We use it only to identify subspaces of S_2. All subsequent ideal calculations use the actual multiplication of S and the second input.

## 1. Exact two-column and three-column intersections

Write l_lambda=beta x-alpha y. The first input says

(J_lambda)_2 = E tensor l_lambda W.

If [lambda] and [mu] are distinct, l_lambda and l_mu are linearly independent. A binary quadratic divisible by both is a scalar multiple of their product. Hence

(l_lambda W) intersection (l_mu W) = k(l_lambda l_mu),
(l_lambda W) + (l_mu W) = Sym^2(W).

Tensoring with E preserves these intersections and sums. There are no relations in S degrees zero or one, and every column ideal contains all degrees at least three. Therefore, as homogeneous ideals,

J_lambda + J_mu = m^2,
J_lambda intersection J_mu = (E tensor k(l_lambda l_mu)) + m^3.

Here the parenthesized degree-two space denotes the ideal it generates; no assertion is made that these five quadrics alone generate all cubics. The intersection quotient has Hilbert function (1,5,10).

For three pairwise distinct projective parameters, a binary quadratic divisible by all three corresponding linear forms is zero. Consequently

J_lambda intersection J_mu intersection J_nu = m^3.

In particular, the intersection of all projective column ideals is already attained by any three distinct columns and equals m^3. This holds after any extension of the ground field. Repeated parameters do not change an intersection.

These statements are exact ideals, not merely dimension estimates.

## 2. Three columns reconstruct precisely the cubic truncation

Put A_i=S/J_i and C=S/m^2. Each A_i has its canonical quotient to C. For two distinct columns the standard difference-map sequence is

0 -> S/(J_1 intersection J_2) -> A_1 direct sum A_2 -> C -> 0.

Thus S/(J_1 intersection J_2) is the fiber product A_1 x_C A_2.

For three pairwise distinct columns, the natural map

S/m^3 -> A_1 x_C A_2 x_C A_3

is also an isomorphism of graded k-algebras. Injectivity follows from the intersection theorem. For surjectivity, in degrees zero and one a fiber-product element has the same component in all three factors. In degree two there is no compatibility condition because C_2=0. The degree-two map is

E tensor Sym^2(W) -> direct sum over i=1,2,3 of E tensor (Sym^2(W)/l_i W).

Its kernel is zero by three-factor divisibility, and both sides have dimension 15; hence it is an isomorphism. All degrees at least three vanish. This proves surjectivity degree by degree, and the map is induced from S, so it preserves products.

Thus three column quotients recover the entire length-21 algebra S/m^3, not merely its Hilbert function. For four or more columns, equality of constant and linear parts alone is not sufficient: their quadratic values must also lie in the image of the 15-dimensional simultaneous quadratic evaluation map. No unrestricted many-factor fiber-product claim is made.

## 3. A finite flat projective family

Let P=P(W*) parameterize one-dimensional subspaces of W*. With the convention O_P(-1) contained in W* tensor O_P, the universal contraction defines

U tensor O_P(-1) -> S_2 tensor O_P.

At [lambda] its image is E tensor l_lambda W. Every fiber has rank ten, so it is a subbundle. Evaluation of binary quadratics gives an exact sequence

0 -> U tensor O_P(-1) -> S_2 tensor O_P -> E tensor O_P(2) -> 0.

To verify exactness without a convention-dependent identification, the last map sends e tensor q to e tensor evaluation(q) on the tautological covector line. Its fiber kernel is E tensor l_lambda W, since lambda(l_lambda)=0. The first map is injective on each fiber by multiplication by the nonzero binary linear form l_lambda. Both maps are locally of constant rank, so the fiber calculation proves the asserted vector-bundle exactness. The chosen determinant trivialization is used only for the displayed coordinate formula for the first map.

Let A be the graded O_P-algebra obtained from S tensor O_P by the ideal generated by this quadratic subbundle. The degree-three multiplication map

T tensor U tensor O_P(-1) -> S_3 tensor O_P

is surjective on every geometric fiber by the uniform column theorem. Its cokernel is coherent and has zero fiber at every point, so it vanishes by Nakayama's lemma. Multiplication in S then makes every higher degree vanish as well. It follows that

A_0=O_P, A_1=T tensor O_P, A_2=E tensor O_P(2), A_d=0 for d>=3.

The multiplication A_1 tensor A_1 -> A_2 is the universal evaluation map S_2 tensor O_P -> E tensor O_P(2); products of positive-degree elements with A_2 vanish. This also directly specifies the family algebra.

As an ungraded O_P-module, A is locally free of rank 1+5+5=11. Therefore Spec_P(A) is finite flat of degree 11 over P, with fiber S/J_lambda at [lambda]. This is a structural family construction, not a numerical flatness test. No levelness assertion is needed here.

## 4. Exact information loss for the scalar and determinantal quotients

The pinned Hilbert functions are

HF(R)=(1,5,15,25,15,3),
HF(Q)=(1,5,15,35,35,3).

In particular I and D are contained in m^3. Both quotients map to every column quotient. For any three distinct columns, and likewise for all columns together, their simultaneous kernels are

ker(R -> product S/J_lambda) = m^3/I,
ker(Q -> product S/J_lambda) = m^3/D.

The first kernel has graded dimensions 25,15,3 in degrees 3,4,5 and length 43. The second has dimensions 35,35,3 in those degrees and length 73. Both simultaneous images are isomorphic to S/m^3 by Section 2, and have length 21.

In particular the projective column family, even using every member, cannot recover either I or D by taking the intersection of its defining ideals. Their intersection is the strictly larger ideal m^3. This is a root-cause exclusion for this recovery procedure, not an assertion that other constructions from the full presentation cannot recover I or D.

## 5. The missing information is retained at the binary origin

Put B=Sym(W), n=B_+, and M_d=direct sum over a>=0 of (Sym^a_S H)_d, with a denoting B degree. The pinned binary-module theorem shows that every M_d for d>=3 is supported at the binary origin. Such modules disappear upon specialization at any nonzero binary covector, explaining the common cubic kernel above.

There is a precise restoration statement requiring only the first infinitesimal neighborhood of that origin. The quotient M_d/n^2 M_d retains its degree-zero piece S_d, its degree-one piece H_d, and the multiplication map

W tensor S_d -> H_d.

Indeed n^2 M_d has no components in degrees zero or one. Since W generates H as an S-module,

I_d = { f in S_d : w f=0 in H_d for every w in W }.

Equivalently I_d is the common kernel of the two degree-one multiplication operators in M_d/n^2 M_d. This is not the entire kernel of W tensor S_d -> H_d, which is a different vector space. Thus the graded first binary infinitesimal neighborhood retains the information needed to recover the scalar annihilator, while every nonzero fiber in the projective family loses it in degrees at least three. This restores scalar-annihilator data only; it is not a formula for D and does not resolve the remaining determinantal connecting channels.
