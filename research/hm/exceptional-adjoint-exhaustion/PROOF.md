# Exceptional double-plane exhaustion and absence of opposite-pencil torsion

Status: authored structural proof independently mathematically reviewed in REVIEW.md; not formally checked. Work over C. All local computations below are universal ideal identities; no actual HM/source coefficient, point, parameter, matrix, derivative or instance rank is evaluated.

## 1. Statement and exact geometric inputs

Let F_HM be the actual normalized Horrocks--Mumford bundle, B=End0(F_HM), and H_t the negative hyperplane pencil through its positive fixed plane R. Then for EVERY parameter t, including the twelve exceptional ones,

    H0(H_t,B(3)) = Sym2 H0(H_t,F_HM(2)),  dimension three.    (1)

All these sections are even. Consequently the actual opposite-pencil cokernel C of PR934 is locally free: its torsion is zero. Its canonical reduced 26-to-31 pencil is fiberwise injective on the entire parameter line, not merely sheaf-injective.

## 2. The actual general section is a conic disjoint from a double conic

Write H=P3, E=F_HM|H(2), det E=O(3), and S=h h'^2 for its determinant cubic. The distinct linear forms h,h' are positive, because their planes are cones through the negative vertex v. Choose linear coordinates only to describe the involution abstractly: three positive directions and one negative direction z; R=(z=0), and v is the common positive-coordinate zero. No coefficients of the actual determinant or section are assigned values.

Every nonzero section of E is regular: a divisorial zero of degree d>=1 would give H0(E(-d))!=0, contrary to H0(E(-1))=0. Its zero scheme C0 is a locally complete-intersection curve of degree six, with omega_C0=O_C0(-1), and the regular-section sequence gives

    0 -> O -> E -> I_C0(3) -> 0,
    h0(I_C0(3))=1, generated by S.                 (2)

A general section may be chosen to avoid v, since the actual vertex evaluation is nonzero. No nonzero section has a curve component in R: its restriction to R is nonzero and a divisorial zero there would contradict H0(F_HM|R(k))=0 for k<=1.

On the open part of the simple plane (h=0) away from the doubled plane, the determinant has a simple zero, so evaluation of the two sections has rank one. Its kernel defines a nonconstant map to the section pencil: constancy would make one section vanish along that plane, contrary to regularity. Generic smoothness in characteristic zero therefore gives, for a general section, a multiplicity-one curve component meeting that plane's open part. This is the same rank-one argument used in the checked reduced-determinant proof; it needs reducedness only on this open component, not on the whole determinant.

The wholly Ferrand-doubled degree-three types and the triple-conic types in the classification have every support-component multiplicity at least two, respectively three, even for nonreduced bases. They are excluded by this multiplicity-one component. Three disjoint plane conics are also impossible: the product of their plane equations equals S by (2), so two would lie in the same plane, where two plane conics necessarily meet. The remaining classification type is

    C0 = Q disjoint-union Y,

where Q is a plane conic and Y is a Ferrand double of a plane conic Q'. If their plane equations are h_Q,h_Q', the cubic h_Q h_Q'^2 belongs to I_C0(3), hence equals S up to scalar. Thus Q lies in P=(h=0) and Q' in P'=(h'=0).

We now exclude double-line conics without assuming generic smoothness of these conics. Every global section of E is even, so C0 is invariant. The two connected components Q and Y have distinct degrees two and four, so inversion preserves each; their planes P,P' are also invariant. If Q or the intermediate conic Q' were a double line, its supporting line would be invariant (for Q' this is the support of the invariant Y, so no canonicity of the intermediate nonreduced structure is assumed). Such an invariant supporting line either passes through v or is contained in R: its two-dimensional invariant vector subspace either contains the negative eigendirection or lies in the positive eigenspace. The first is excluded by C0 avoiding v and the second by the absence of a curve component in R. Therefore Q and Q' are reduced plane conics, possibly pairs of meeting lines. They are disjoint; neither has a component in R or in the other conic's plane.

The last assertion follows from disjointness: a line component lying in the other plane would meet its plane conic. Thus every plane intersection used below is a genuine length-two scheme, even in tangent or reducible cases.

## 3. The Ferrand double and its actual parity

Write Q'=(h',q'), where q' is a quadratic lifting its plane equation. Since Q' is invariant and avoids v, this equation is even: an odd quadratic on the invariant plane vanishes at v. Choose an even lift q'. Similarly choose an even quadratic q for Q in P.

The Ferrand conormal I_Q'/I_Y is O_Q'. This is explicit in the primary classification; it also follows from omega_Y=O_Y(-1), omega_Q'=O_Q'(-1), and the Ferrand dualizing identity. The surjection defining Y therefore has the form

    I_Q'/I_Q'^2 = O_Q'(-1)+O_Q'(-2) -> O_Q',
    (h',q') |-> (a,b),                            (3)

where a and b have degrees one and two on Q' and no common zero. Every needed section lifts from P3, since Q' is a plane conic. The quotient line in (3) could initially have either involution sign. The odd sign is impossible: a would be proportional to z and b to z times a positive linear form, so both would vanish on the nonempty intersection Q' with R. Consequently the quotient sign is even. We may choose a positive linear lift a and an even quadratic lift b.

Put g=b h'-a q'. The actual ideal sheaf is

    I_Y=(h'^2,h'q',q'^2,g).                        (4)

This follows from the kernel of (3): the kernel modulo I_Q'^2 is generated by the Koszul relation (b,-a), since a,b generate O_Q'. Formula (4) is an equality of ideal sheaves, not an assumption about a chosen actual coefficient.

The linear section a does not vanish identically on a component of Q'. Otherwise b, a positive-degree section on that projective component, would have a zero there, contradicting surjectivity. In particular a and q' are a regular sequence on P'.

The space H0(I_Y(3)) has dimension six, with basis spaces

    h'^2 H0(O(1)),   C h'q',   C g.               (5)

To justify completeness rather than only exhibit six sections, twist the Ferrand sequence 0->I_Y->I_Q'->O_Q'->0 by three. The map on global sections is onto: the Koszul sequence for a,b on Q', twisted by three, has kernel O_Q', whose H1 vanishes for any reduced plane conic. Since h0(I_Q'(3))=13 and h0(O_Q'(3))=7, its kernel has dimension six. The six displayed sections are independent; any dependence of g on the first two would force a q' to vanish on P', impossible. Its even part has dimension five and its odd part dimension one.

## 4. The Ferrand linear coefficient cannot align with the simple plane

We claim

    a is not in span(h,h').                         (6)

Suppose otherwise. On P=(h=0), every even cubic in (5) is divisible by h': for g this follows from a|P being a multiple of h'. The restriction map from the five-dimensional even part H0(I_Y(3))_+ has S=h h'^2 in its kernel. If that kernel had dimension at least two, these cubics would all vanish on P and hence on Q, contradicting (2). Its image therefore has dimension four.

The even quadratics on P have dimension four: P has two positive coordinate directions and one negative direction. Thus this image equals h' times the entire space of even quadratics on P. In particular h' q is in its image. A lift is a cubic vanishing on Y and on Q, but its restriction to P is nonzero, so it is independent of S. This again contradicts (2). Assertion (6) follows.

This uses the actual inversion grading; dimension six without its even decomposition would not yield the assertion.

## 5. Ordinary-square sextics: the doubled plane divides twice

Let F6 be any global section of I_C0^2(6). The ideals and squares here are ordinary sheaf ideals throughout.

Restrict first to P'. Formula (4) gives the image ideal

    I_Y O_P' = (q'^2,a q')=q'(q',a),
    I_Y^2 O_P' = q'^2(q',a)^2.

Thus F6|P'=q'^2 r2, where r2 is a quadratic belonging to the square of the length-two complete-intersection ideal (a,q'). The plane length-two lemma gives

    F6|P'=c q'^2 a^2.                              (7)

For clarity, that lemma is valid for nonreduced length-two intersections: for an ideal (l,q) of degrees one and two on P2, its square resolution has generators l²,lq,q² and syzygies in degrees four and five, yielding H0(I_(l,q)^2(2))=C l² and H0(I_(l,q)^2(1))=0.

Let Z=Q intersect P', a length-two scheme lying on the line h=0 in P'. It is disjoint from Q', so q' is a unit near Z. The restriction of I_Q² to P' has image I_Z². Since F6 lies in I_Q², (7) implies, if c is nonzero, that a² belongs to I_Z². The same lemma then makes a|P' proportional to h|P', contrary to (6). Hence c=0 and

    F6=h' G5.

Because Q is a locally complete intersection and h' contains no component of it, h' is a nonzerodivisor on O/I_Q². Indeed a power of a complete-intersection ideal is Cohen--Macaulay and has no embedded associated primes. It follows that G5 belongs to I_Q² globally.

At the generic point of each component of Q', a is a unit and (4) becomes I_Y=(h'^2,g). Moreover (h',g) is a regular sequence there, since g modulo h' is -a q' and Q' is reduced at that generic point. The elementary colon identity

    ((h'^2,g)^2:h')=(h'^3,h'g,g²)

shows that G5|P' vanishes to order at least two along every component of Q'. Since Q' is reduced, this means globally

    G5|P'=q'^2 r1

for a linear form r1 on P'. Now G5 belongs to I_Q²; restricting near Z and dividing by the unit q'^2 gives r1 in I_Z². The degree-one part of that ideal square is zero by the lemma. Therefore G5|P'=0 and

    F6=h'^2 K4.                                    (8)

## 6. Cancel the determinant without replacing ordinary by symbolic squares

The same nonzerodivisor argument gives K4 in I_Q². At each generic component of Y, a is a unit and h'^2 is a generator of the regular-sequence ideal I_Y=(h'^2,g). Consequently

    (I_Y²:h'^2)=I_Y

there, so K4 belongs to I_Y generically. Since Y is locally complete intersection, O_Y is Cohen--Macaulay and has no embedded associated primes. A section of O_Y which vanishes after localization at every generic associated prime is zero. Thus K4 belongs to I_Y globally. This step concerns membership in the ordinary ideal I_Y; it does not assert that an ordinary square equals its symbolic saturation.

Restrict K4 to P=(h=0). Since K4 lies in I_Q², its restriction is c q² for some scalar c. The intersection Q' with P is nonempty and disjoint from Q. Because K4 also vanishes on Y, and hence on Q', its restriction at that intersection is zero, while q is nonzero there. It follows that c=0. Thus K4=h K3 and

    F6=S K3.

At the generic components of Q, h is an ideal generator, so K4 in I_Q² implies K3 in I_Q. At the generic components of Y, h is a unit, so K4 in I_Y implies K3 in I_Y. Both quotients are unmixed; these memberships therefore hold globally. Hence K3 lies in I_C0(3). By (2), K3 is a scalar multiple of S. Conversely S² plainly belongs to I_C0². We have proved the exact equality

    H0(I_C0²(6))=C S².                             (9)

This explicitly handles the nonreduced double conic rather than importing the reduced-curve cancellation from the earlier exhaustion theorem.

## 7. Actual exceptional exhaustion and base change

For the chosen regular section s of E, the exact ordinary-square sequence is

    0 -> E --multiply by s--> Sym2 E -> I_C0²(6) -> 0.

Choose a second global section t completing s. The section t² maps to (s wedge t)²=S², which is nonzero. Equation (9) therefore makes the map on global sections onto its one-dimensional right side. Since h0(E)=2, we obtain h0(Sym2 E)=3, with basis s²,st,t². This proves (1) at the arbitrary exceptional parameter. The good-parameter theorem supplies all remaining parameters, and all squares are even.

PR934's exact base-change sequence for the original opposite pencil is

    0 -> (A O(-6)) tensor k(t)
      -> H0(H_t,B(3))_+ -> Tor1(C,k(t)) -> 0.

The first map is the actual square-section map, injective everywhere, and both first and second spaces now have dimension three everywhere. Thus Tor1(C,k(t))=0 for every t. Over each local DVR, this forces the torsion of C to vanish. No surjectivity of cohomology base change was assumed in obtaining (1); the exact sequence is used only after its independent geometric proof.

Finally the reduced sheaf-injective presentation 0->E26 O(-1)->H31 O->C->0 has locally free quotient, so its fiber maps are injective at every point. The vertex-ideal extension 0->O(10)->D_vert->C->0 also has locally free middle term, but no splitting of that extension is asserted.

There is now also an exact fiberwise vertex interpretation which was not available before exceptional exhaustion. At every t, the full adjoint section space consists of squares of the actual two E-sections, and its vertex evaluation is the symmetric square of the nonzero E-vertex evaluation. Its image is exactly the one-dimensional O(-4) vertex channel, with no O(10) component. Hence

    H0(H_t,I_(v_t) B|H_t(3)) = (W O(-7)) tensor k(t),
    dimension two,

under PR934's actual vertex-square comparison. These sections are products of the E-section vanishing at the vertex with an arbitrary E-section. Thus all fiber-kernel vectors of the vertex-restricted 40-to-43 pencil Gamma0, including the twelve formerly exceptional fibers, are now genuinely vertex-vanishing square sections; no extra Tor classes remain. This follows from the independent exhaustion and actual evaluation map, not from an assumed base-change surjection.

## 8. Contribution and limits

The exceptional determinant's multiplicity alone did not determine the adjoint jump. The decisive additional ingredient is the inversion-even cubic count, which prevents the Ferrand coefficient from aligning with the simple determinant plane. Ordinary-square restriction and two controlled divisions then prove exceptional exhaustion and remove every nonzero torsion alternative from the actual C.

This determines the torsion question without selecting a maximal pattern, extension scalar, actual coefficient or evaluated rank. It does not determine the splitting type of the torsion-free rank-five degree-26 bundle C, solve remaining mixed multiplication constraints, exclude a catalogue source, or prove algebraicity of the original construction. No source/frontier decrement is claimed.
