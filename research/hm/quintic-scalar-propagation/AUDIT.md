## 2. The new Koszul step

The cochain complex Kᵖ=ΛᵖS₁*⊗B(p−4), for p=0,…,5, is exact as a sheaf complex. An invertible coordinate on each standard affine open gives a contracting homotopy. Its hypercohomology therefore vanishes.

With d_r of bidegree (r,1−r), E₂^(0,2) is the actual kernel

    ker[M₋₄ → S₁*⊗M₋₃] = Soc(M)₋₄.

There is no quotient by an earlier column. For r≥2 no incoming differential reaches this entry. Its only possible outgoing targets are (2,1), a subquotient of Λ²S₁*⊗H¹B(−2), and (3,0), a subquotient of Λ³S₁*⊗H⁰B(−1). Both vanish already at E₁. Later targets have negative second index. Consequently this entry survives unchanged to E∞ and must be zero.

The final Koszul term is B(1), but no cohomology vanishing of that term is needed. Nor is H¹B(−1) assumed zero. Higher cohomology rows cannot enter column zero from a nonexistent negative column. These checks exclude the most likely hidden assumptions in this step.

## 3. Outer propagation and the dual socles

Soc(M)₋₄=0 and M₋₄≠0 imply a₋₄≠0. Its image is N-stable, so irreducibility of M₋₃ makes it surjective. Since μ is surjective and multiplication is associative, the composite S₁⊗S₁⊗U→M₋₃ is surjective and factors through S₂⊗U. Hence S₂U=M₋₃.

Under Serre duality the transpose of a₋₄ is

    M₋₂ → S₁*⊗M₋₁,
    n ↦ [ℓ ↦ ℓn].

This map is injective because a₋₄ is surjective. Its kernel is Soc(M)₋₂, proving the claimed vanishing. It is also nonzero, so the associated multiplication a₋₂:S₁⊗M₋₂→M₋₁ is nonzero. Irreducibility of M₋₁ makes that multiplication surjective. The transpose is the adjoint map displayed above; it is not literally the 100-dimensional-domain multiplication map itself.

Likewise, the transpose of μ is M₋₁→S₁*⊗M₀. It is an isomorphism, so Soc(M)₋₁=0 and a₋₁ is nonzero. The irreducible M₀ is therefore its full image. This proves all outer-edge assertions and S₂M₋₂=M₀. The support theorem gives Soc(M)₀=M₀.

## 4. Equivalence, generation, and exact splitting

The image of ν is an N-submodule of irreducible M₋₂, while its common annihilator Soc(M)₋₃ is an N-submodule of irreducible M₋₃. Thus ν is either zero or surjective, and its common annihilator is respectively all of M₋₃ or zero. No injectivity of the map S₁⊗M₋₃→M₋₂ is asserted or needed.

If ν is surjective, the already established outer steps give S_jU=M_(j−5) for j=0,…,5. The support theorem then gives generation of all M by U, and S₅U=M₀≠0. The audited perfect-pairing criterion c=0 iff S₅U=0 proves c≠0.

If ν is zero, S₃U=0 because S₂U=M₋₃. Therefore S₅U=0 and c=0. Every homogeneous polynomial crossing the cut from degrees at most −3 to degrees at least −2 is a linear combination of monomials, and every such monomial crosses the zero linear edge. Consequently the lower and upper triples are each S-submodules, giving exactly the displayed direct sum. The lower summand is generated in degree −5; the upper one is generated in degree −2 by the two upper surjections. In particular M cannot then be generated by U, since its upper summand is nonzero. This checks the converse needed for all five conditions to be equivalent.

Precisely, the Serre pairing is zero within either triple and gives a perfect graded duality between them, with degree reflection t↦−5−t. That is the meaning of duality exchanging the summands. No splitting of B follows.

## 5. Symmetry of the middle detector

For A=M₋₃, the perfect pairing identifies M₋₂ with A*. Thus currying ν gives Θ:S₁→A*⊗A*. For a,b∈A,

    Θ(ℓ)(a,b)=⟨a,ℓb⟩=⟨b,ℓa⟩.

The last equality uses trace cyclicity and the sign (−1)^(2·2)=+1. It is valid even though the underlying endomorphism-valued cup product need not itself commute. Therefore Θ lands in Sym²A*, is N-equivariant, and is zero exactly when ν is zero. A nonzero Θ is injective since S₁ is irreducible. This says nothing by itself about the rank of a fixed symmetric form Θ(ℓ).

## 6. Audit of the added multiplicity-one calculation

I directly read [the primary Horrocks–Mumford paper](https://www.dam.brown.edu/people/mumford/alg_geom/papers/1973a--Rk2BdleP%5E4-NC.pdf), particularly the multiplicity criterion (2.2), Lemma 3.3(v), and the appendix. Lemma 3.3(v) gives Sym²V=V₁⊗χ₃′. The construction gives Λ²V=V₁⊗W. Galois conjugation ζ↦ζ² sends V→V₁→V₂=V*, interchanges W with W′, and interchanges χ₃ with χ₃′. It therefore gives exactly the two identities (3) in the proof. This uses a Galois operation on the historical representations, not a continuous group action on the bundle.

For Q=χ₄#, its trace row is (4,−4,0,1,−1,−1,−1,1,1). The trace row of g↦Q(g²) is

    (4,4,−4,1,1,−1,−1,−1,−1).

For example, the order-four class squares to the central involution; the order-six class squares into the order-three class; the last four classes square into the order-five classes, where Q has trace −1. Applying the symmetric/exterior square formulas gives exactly

    Sym²Q: (10,10,−2,1,1,0,0,0,0),
    Λ²Q:   (6,6,2,0,0,1,1,1,1).

Adding the specified appendix rows yields χ₃+χ₃′+χ₄ and 1+χ₅, respectively. In particular χ₄ here is the nonspinorial four-dimensional representation. No swap with χ₄# is permissible.

The tensor-square identities now give the two brackets in Section 6. Since V* is H₅-irreducible, Hom_H₅(V*,V*⊗R) identifies with R, with its stated quotient action; taking N-invariants gives Hom_N(V*,V*⊗R)=R^(N/H₅). Schur's lemma and self-duality give one invariant in χ₃⊗Sym²Q, none in W′⊗Λ²Q, and none in either term of the alternating bracket. Thus

    dim Hom_N(S₁,Sym²A*)=1,
    Hom_N(S₁,Λ²A*)=0.

Finally, tensor–Hom adjunction identifies Hom_N(S₁⊗A,A*) with Hom_N(S₁,A*⊗A*), the sum of those two spaces. Its dimension is therefore one, as claimed. This independently confirms that every equivariant middle map is symmetric, and that its scalar vanishes exactly when c does. This representation calculation alone does not prove nonvanishing; the next three sections do.

## 7. Identifying the middle socle with the H¹ Koszul kernel

At twist −3, E₁^(p,q)=ΛᵖV⊗H^qB(p−3). Hence E₂^(2,1) is exactly the displayed K: the incoming E₁^(1,1) vanishes by H¹B(−2)=0. The outgoing d₂ from E₂^(0,2)=Soc(M)₋₃ lands in K. If a socle class lies in its kernel, its only possible later outgoing differential has target E₃^(3,0), which is zero because E₁^(3,0)=Λ³V⊗H⁰B=0. No incoming differential exists. Zero hypercohomology therefore forces that kernel to be zero.

The outgoing d₂ from K has target E₂^(4,0), which is now zero because it is a subquotient of Λ⁴V⊗H⁰B(1). At (2,1), all later incoming differentials require a negative first index, and all later outgoing differentials have negative second index. Thus

    E₃^(2,1)=K/im[Soc(M)₋₃→K]=E∞^(2,1)=0.

Combined with the previously checked injectivity, this proves Soc(M)₋₃≅K. This is an isomorphism of actual N-modules, not a dimension comparison. No vanishing of H⁰B(2) is needed.

## 8. The actual H¹ multiplication

For K∨⊗F(−1), the four columns in the proof have exactly the stated twists and coefficient duals. F(−3) is acyclic: the ordinary HM table gives H⁰,H¹,H² zero, and duality reduces H³,H⁴ to H¹,H⁰ of the acyclic F(−1). F(−1) is itself acyclic. Hence the only E₁ entries are V⊗(1⊕χ₃) in bidegree (−1,2) and V₃⊗V₃ in (1,1). The only possible differential is d₂ between them. Total degree one is its kernel, with no extra filtration piece.

The known kernel dimension five and the irreducible decomposition V⊕(V⊗χ₃), of dimensions five and fifteen, force that kernel to be the V summand. Trace splitting does not affect H¹, giving T₋₁=H¹B(−1)≅V. There is no unstated evaluation of the spectral-sequence differential.

At twist zero, the earlier invariant spectral sequence has only the three entries listed in Section 8. The d₁ from χ₃ to W′ vanishes by irreducible inequivalence. The only possible map removing the χ₃ in total degree zero is d₂:1⊕χ₃→χ₃. Simplicity gives H⁰EndF=C, so its kernel must be exactly the trivial line and its χ₃ component is an isomorphism. Therefore its cokernel, the only possible invariant total-degree-one group, is zero. This proves T₀^H₅=0.

For the exact coordinate complex B(−1), E₂^(0,1) is the simultaneous multiplication kernel on T₋₁. Its sole possible higher outgoing differential has target a subquotient of Λ²V⊗H⁰B(1)=0. There are no incoming differentials or later nonnegative-row targets. Thus this socle is zero, proving multiplication b:S₁⊗T₋₁→T₀ is nonzero.

The actual source is V*⊗V=1⊕Z. The H₅-invariant line maps to zero since T₀^H₅=0. The irreducible Z has dimension 24, so the nonzero restriction b|Z is injective; h¹B=24 makes it an isomorphism. Consequently b is, after compatible N-equivariant identifications, a nonzero scalar times the trace quotient End(V)→End(V)/C·id. This step is essential: the dimension and absence of H₅-invariants alone would not have identified the target representation. The nonzero map out of the specified Z does identify it.

## 9. The universal wedge lemma and conclusion

Under those identifications, the differential defining K is the exact map D in Section 9, up to a nonzero scalar. Tensoring the quotient End(V)→End(V)/C·id with Λ³V is exact over C. Thus D(T)=0, for T=Σ_jT_j⊗e_j, means its unquotiented image lies in Λ³V⊗C·id. Comparing coefficients of x_i⊗e_j gives

    e_i∧T_j=δ_ijR

for one R∈Λ³V. Fix j. For every i≠j, the coefficient condition says T_j belongs to e_i∧V. Intersecting these conditions for any three distinct such indices forces T_j=0: a basis two-wedge cannot contain three distinct basis vectors. Since dim V=5 there are four available indices, and the conclusion applies to every j. Hence D is injective and K=0.

This is a universal tensor proof, not the rank computation of a selected HM instance. It proves Soc(M)₋₃=0, so the audited equivalence gives ν surjective, S₅U=M₀≠0, and c≠0. Since Φ=cΨ has irreducible target Sym²U* and Ψ is nonzero, Φ is surjective. Its invariant-quintic kernel is the χ₃ summand already identified in the quintic-pairing proof.

All socles except M₀ now vanish, and the whole module is generated in degree −5. The split-module alternative remains a correct conditional implication but is excluded for the actual bundle. The identity αμ=cA_Ψ therefore gives rank α=rank A_Ψ, without determining that common rank. No numerical normalization of c, nondegeneracy of a fixed Θ(ℓ), or source/Massey/obstruction value follows from this argument alone.
