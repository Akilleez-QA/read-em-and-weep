## Pencil and total length

I checked the two-column argument independently before reading the draft.
Surjectivity of T_1 permits a two-dimensional domain subspace on which it
is an isomorphism. Identifying that subspace with the target makes the
restricted pencil exactly tI+C. Its determinant is monic of degree two.

The constant-vector argument proves its cokernel has C-dimension exactly
two, rather than merely being generated by two elements annihilated by a
quadratic. Reduction of polynomial degree gives surjectivity from W;
the leading coefficient of (tI+C)p proves injectivity on constants.
Multiplication by t is -C, and the adjugate identity supplies the common
annihilator. The full primary cokernel is a quotient, so its length is
at most two.

Generalized eigenspace decomposition of -C identifies the localization
at a with a space of dimension equal to the multiplicity e_a of a in
the determinant. Its shifted operator has exponent at most e_a.
This proves both local bounds, including repeated roots. Roots of the
chosen determinant only contain the failure locus; the proof correctly
does not identify them with actual bad parameters.

## Naturality and exact fibers

PR440 supplies the actual compatible section-resolution surjections
15M_1+4M_2 onto H^1(B(4)) and 15M_3 onto H^1(B(6)).
The four extra summands multiply into M_4=0. Extending these maps
coefficientwise gives the asserted quotient from fifteen copies of R.
Quotienting further by the cubic image can only decrease length and
preserves annihilation. This establishes total length at most thirty,
local length at most 15e_a, and the same annihilator.

All omitted section-resolution relations remain imposed in the target
quotient. There is no unjustified equality with fifteen copies of R,
and no regular-sequence assertion on the finite-length primary module.

Specialization of the defining cokernel is right exact without
flatness of that cokernel. Thus the special fiber is canonically the
original multiplication quotient Q_0, not just a space of the same
dimension. Nakayama gives equality of nonzero-fiber and nonzero-support
conditions. The support-size bound, summed fiber-dimension inequality,
and equality for two simple roots follow. None implies Q_0=0.

The additional primary-fiber dichotomy is valid. A two-dimensional fiber
uses all the available total length, leaving no other support or
nonzero maximal-ideal multiple. A one-dimensional nonzero local fiber
gives a cyclic local module by Nakayama, of length one or two; its
fifteen-copy quotient has fiber dimension at most fifteen.

## Formal transport and the first parameter jet

For D=U_0^(-1)U_1, symmetry gives D^T U_0=U_0 D. Hence the formal
square root H=(I+tD)^(1/2) is U_0-self-adjoint and satisfies
H^T U_0 H=U_0+tU_1. The choice g=H^(-1) has the correct pullback
orientation: (g^(-1))^*u_0=u_0+tq_1. Its constant term is the identity.
The transformed cubic is v_0(Hx), with the required original constant
term. This construction does not assume any HM equivariance.

The formal endpoint quotient receives the same fifteen-copy primary
surjection. Its length and t-exponent bounds follow from the primary
summand at zero. The draft correctly distinguishes this quotient from
the completion of the fixed-cubic quotient: changing the cubic can
change the module, while leaving its controlling primary module and
special fiber unchanged.

Since t^2 kills the entire formal quotient, right-exact reduction
modulo t^2 reconstructs the entire module, including its parameter
action. The displayed first coefficient

    w_1=(1/2) sum_i (Dx)_i (partial v_0/partial x_i)

is the ordinary polynomial chain rule applied to v_0(Hx). Thus equation
(12a) follows. Its canonical character is relative to the specified
choices; it is not a claim that those choices are canonical. No higher
parameter coefficient is needed, and no source or collision jet is
being identified with this auxiliary parameter jet.

## Geometric interpretation and scope

The generic-fiber argument uses only a field extension of the fixed
cohomology groups, invertibility of the nonzero determinant over C((t)),
and the already pinned transported filtration and endpoint theorem.
The formal automorphism transports the regular sequence and retains
the original coefficient geometry at zero. Consequently generic
whole-coefficient order-six vanishing is justified separately from
the special-fiber identification with Q_0 plus Q_0.

The proof makes no identification with relative R^2 or its ordinary
dual, and does not infer nonflat cohomology base change from fiberwise
Serre duality. It correctly leaves the fixed endpoint, actual source
membership, pure conditions, module lifting, and algebraicity unresolved.
The length bound is not dim(Q_0)<=2 and does not automatically improve
the previously known uniform fixed-fiber bound.
