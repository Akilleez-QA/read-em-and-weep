# Twisted trace-free endomorphisms: structural exclusions

## Result and inputs

Over C, let F be the normalized Horrocks–Mumford bundle on P4, with c1(F)=−H, c2(F)=4H². Using the primary-source input H⁰(F(u))=0 for u<3, the argument below proves

    H⁰(P4, End₀(F)(1))=0.

The argument uses no HM matrix, selected section, multiplication-map evaluation, or computation of a particular rank. Its HM-specific inputs are exactly the Chern classes and the stated section vanishing. The section vanishing follows from the primary section-module resolution reproduced with its source in SOURCES.md. The proof is organized by the rank of a quadratic determinant and gives explicit reasons that the spectral module is locally free or maximal Cohen–Macaulay where needed. The mathematical review is recorded in AUDIT.md. No formal verification or novelty claim is made.

## 1. No generic-rank-one twisted homomorphism

A saturated rank-one subsheaf K of a locally free sheaf on a smooth variety is reflexive: in 0→K→E→Q→0, Q is torsion-free, and the depth lemma gives depth(K_x)≥min(2,dim O_x); K is torsion-free and satisfies S2. On P4 a rank-one reflexive sheaf is O(a).

Suppose ψ:F→F(1) has generic rank one. Its kernel is saturated, since the quotient is the image in a vector bundle. Write ker ψ=O(a). The saturated image in F(1) is O(b). Their inclusions give nonzero sections of F(−a) and F(1−b), hence

    a≤−3, b≤−2.

The torsion-free quotient has rank one and determinant O(−1−a), so it is I_Z(−1−a), with codim Z≥2. The induced nonzero map to O(b) belongs to

    Hom(I_Z(−1−a),O(b))=H⁰(O(a+b+1))=0,

a contradiction. Here Hom(I_Z,O)=O because a homomorphism is multiplication by a rational function regular off codimension two, and extends across that set on the normal smooth ambient space.

Let φ:F→F(1) be trace-free. Cayley–Hamilton gives φ(1)φ=q id, where q=−det φ∈H⁰(O(2)). If q=0 and φ≠0, φ has generic rank one, already impossible. If q=ℓ²≠0, then φ−ℓ id has generic rank one: over the function field its eigenvalues are 0 and −2ℓ, and φ cannot be scalar since its trace is zero. This too is impossible. Thus ranks zero and one of q are excluded.

## 2. Spectral construction and depth, including restrictions

For nonsquare q define

    π:X={z²=q(x)}⊂P5→P4.

This is a finite flat double cover with π_*O_X=O⊕O(−1). The homomorphism φ supplies F with an algebra-module structure: the O(−1) summand acts by F(−1)→F obtained from φ. Let M be the corresponding coherent sheaf on X. Then π_*M=F.

On each linear Pn in the base we construct its spectral sheaf directly from (F|Pn, φ|Pn); its finite pushforward is F|Pn by construction. This also agrees with restriction of the global construction. Whenever the restricted q remains nonsquare, the function-field extension has degree two and the two-dimensional base-field module F_generic has rank one over that extension. Thus the restricted spectral sheaf has rank one.

It is maximal Cohen–Macaulay. Indeed, at a closed base point y choose a regular system of n parameters in O_Pn,y. They form a regular sequence on the free module F_y. Under finite pushforward the same remains true after localization at any point x above y. The parameter ideal is primary to the maximal ideal there because the morphism is finite. Thus depth M_x=n=dim O_X,x at every closed point; the Cohen–Macaulay property localizes to the other points. Every spectral quadric used below is integral and has singular locus of codimension at least two: the rank-three quadric surface has only its vertex singularity, the rank-four quadric threefold likewise has a vertex, and the rank-five threefold slice is smooth. A quadric hypersurface is Cohen–Macaulay, hence S₂, and this singular-codimension check gives R₁; Serre’s criterion therefore supplies normality. On a normal spectral variety M is therefore rank-one reflexive. On its regular locus it is a line bundle. These assertions do not assume cyclicity at singular points.

## 3. Quadratic ranks four and five: smooth threefold slice

If rank(q)≥4, choose a base P3 on which q has rank four. Its spectral cover is a smooth quadric threefold Q3⊂P4. Its spectral sheaf is a line bundle.

For completeness Pic(Q3)=Z[O(1)] can be seen without a classification of MCM modules. Write its equation uv+x²+y²+z²=0. The complement of the hyperplane u=0 is A3. That hyperplane section is a reduced irreducible quadric cone. Localization for divisor class groups therefore says Cl(Q3) is generated by its hyperplane class; this class has infinite order, for example by intersecting with a line. Smoothness identifies Cl and Pic.

Consequently M=O_Q3(k), and the projection formula gives

    F|P3 = π_*M = O(k)⊕O(k−1).

Its first Chern class forces k=0, after which its second Chern class is zero, contradicting c2(F|P3)=4H².

## 4. Quadratic rank two: a normal quadric surface cone

If rank(q)=2, choose a base P2 on which q still has rank two. The spectral cover Q is a normal quadric surface cone. The spectral sheaf M is rank-one reflexive, including at the vertex.

Write Q={xz−y²=0}⊂P3 with remaining coordinate w, and let L={x=y=0}. Then Q\L={x≠0}≅A². The class-group localization sequence shows Cl(Q) is generated by [L]. The degree of L is one, whereas principal divisors have degree zero, so no nonzero multiple of [L] is zero. The hyperplane x=0 cuts the Weil divisor 2L. Hence every rank-one reflexive sheaf on Q is isomorphic to one of

    O_Q(k),  I_L(k),  k∈Z.

Here I_L represents −[L] and is reflexive. At the vertex this follows from 0→I_L→O_Q→O_L→0 and the depth lemma (Q is Cohen–Macaulay of dimension two, L is a smooth line of dimension one); elsewhere L is Cartier.

Riemann–Roch on the base P2 gives, using rank two, c1=−1 and c2=4,

    χ(F|P2(t)) = t²+2t−3.                         (RR)

Finite pushforward preserves Euler characteristics, and π*O_P2(1)=O_Q(1). The quadric hypersurface sequence and the ideal sequence of L give respectively

    χ(O_Q(k+t))=(t+k+1)²,
    χ(I_L(k+t))=(t+k)(t+k+1).

Neither polynomial equals (RR). In the first case the linear coefficient forces k=0, leaving constant 1 instead of −3. In the second case the linear coefficient is odd, whereas (RR) has linear coefficient 2. This excludes rank two without supposing that all rank-one MCM sheaves are line bundles.

## 5. Quadratic rank three: the cone depth obstruction

Choose a base P3 on which q has rank three. Its spectral cover X is a quadric threefold of rank four: the projective cone over a smooth quadric surface S≅P1×P1, with vertex v a point. The spectral sheaf M is rank-one MCM. Away from v it is a line bundle.

Choose a base P2 whose inverse image is a hyperplane S avoiding v and is smooth. Write

    M|S = O_S(a,b),   O_X(1)|S=O_S(1,1).

Comparing (RR) with χ(O_S(a+t,b+t))=(t+a+1)(t+b+1) yields

    a+b=0, ab=−4,

so (a,b)=(2,−2) or (−2,2). Importantly, this comparison alone is not a contradiction.

The complement X\{v} is the total space of O_S(1,1), with projection p and zero section S. Pullback induces Pic(S)≅Pic(X\{v}), inverse to restriction to S. One proof compactifies this line bundle to the P1-bundle P_S(O⊕O_S(−1,−1)); its Picard group is Pic(S)⊕Z by the projective-bundle formula, and removing its infinity section quotients out a class with relative degree one. Thus on X\{v}, M is p*O_S(a,b).

Take the affine vertex chart U⊂X, which is the affine cone over S in its O(1,1) embedding. Then U*=U\{v} is the associated G_m-bundle (the punctured tautological line bundle). The projection to S is affine, and

    H¹(U*,M) = ⊕_{n∈Z} H¹(S,O_S(a+n,b+n)).       (★)

On the overlap with the positive-line-bundle description of X\{v}, fiberwise inversion identifies the nonzero fibers with those of the dual tautological line bundle. This reverses the two-sided integer grading and does not change the direct sum. The sign chosen for the tautological bundle only reverses the index n. For (a,b)=(2,−2), the n=0 summand has dimension three by the P1 Künneth formula:

    H¹(S,O(2,−2)) = H⁰(P1,O(2))⊗H¹(P1,O(−2)) ≠ 0.

The reversed pair gives the same conclusion. On the other hand U is affine, so the local-cohomology exact sequence identifies H¹(U*,M) with H²_{v}(U,M). The MCM condition at the three-dimensional vertex says depth_v(M)=3, hence this local cohomology group vanishes. This contradicts (★), excluding rank three.

## 6. Conclusion and residual scope

All possible ranks of q have now been excluded for a nonzero trace-free φ. Therefore H⁰(P⁴,End₀(F)(1))=0.

This note proves only that vanishing. It does not claim a value of h¹, higher-twist vanishings, a multiplication-map rank, a source image, or an ambient deformation obstruction. Those statements require separate arguments. In particular no endpoint-vanishing theorem is silently imported to deduce h¹=55.
