## 1. Truncation and relation count

The full cubic relation space, rather than only the shadow of quadrics, is essential. For K=S R₂+S R₃ one has K₃=R₃ and K₄=XR₃ because XR₂⊂R₃. Since R has no degree-zero or degree-one terms, its minimal degree-four generators are R₄/(S₁R₃+S₂R₂)=R₄/XR₃. Thus b=130−dim(XR₃), and dim P₄=140−dim(XR₃)=10+b. Also dim P₃=70−50=20. These identities hold whether the cubic obstruction has dimension zero or fifteen.

## 2. Correct module theorem

The needed statement is not an unsupported application of the algebra-only Macaulay theorem. Roger Dellaca, *Hilbert polynomials and module generating degrees*, Theorem 2.6 on PDF page 3, explicitly records growth and persistence for graded free-module quotients, citing Gasharov's Theorem 4.2:

https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uci.edu/dist/7/2283/files/2015/09/Hilbert-polynomials-and-module-generating-degrees.pdf

Take maximal generator degree l=0, the theorem's auxiliary parameter p=0, and d=3. Its requirement d≥p+l+1 holds. The relation submodule is generated in degrees at most three, exactly as its persistence clause requires. The theorem can then be iterated, since the same relation submodule is generated in degrees at most every later d. No saturation, cyclicity, or constant Hilbert polynomial is assumed here.

The numeric calculation is correct: 20=binom(6,3), so 20^⟨3⟩=binom(7,4)=35. Equality would force h_P(d)=binom(d+3,3) for all d≥3. Consequently dim P=4 and e(P)=1.

Bibliographic attribution checked in the same PDF: Vesselin Gasharov, *Extremal properties of Hilbert functions*, Illinois Journal of Mathematics 41(4), 612–629 (1997). I verified the accessible Dellaca statement, not the original Gasharov proof. The theorem is a standard imported result, rather than something established from scratch in this audit.

## 3. Equality exclusion

The multiplicity argument is sound without equidimensionality or purity. By multiplicity associativity, the dimension-four support primes contribute positive integers length(P_q)e(S/q) with sum one. There is therefore exactly one such prime q. Each such prime has height one in the polynomial ring S, so it is generated by a homogeneous irreducible polynomial f. The Hilbert series of S/(f) shows e(S/(f))=deg f. Hence deg f=1.

An N-equivariant module has N-stable support. The unique maximal-dimensional support component is consequently N-stable. The degree-one part of its ideal is the invariant line C f in S₁=X, contradicting irreducibility of X. The proof correctly claims uniqueness only for the top-dimensional component; possible smaller-dimensional components do not invalidate the contradiction.

Thus equality b=25 is impossible. The proof does not mistakenly deduce freeness over S/(f), purity, or equality of the whole support with a hyperplane.

## 4. Divisibility and scope

The minimal relation quotient R₄/XR₃ inherits the degree-four central character of S₄⊗U. Under the imported assumption that the center acts primitively on X and trivially on U, this character is nontrivial. The nontrivial-central-character irreducibles of H₅ have dimension five, so b is divisible by five. Together with b<25 and b≥0, this gives exactly the stated necessary list. Without that representation-theoretic input, the argument would give only b≤24.

The comparison with the normalized shadow bound is numerically correct: dim Sym³X=35 and dim Sym⁴X=70, so dim(XR₃)≥70·50/35=100; the new argument raises this to 110. The existing implications c=d=0 when the cubic obstruction vanishes are imported from the previous audit, not newly proved here. No assertion that each surviving value of b is realizable is made.
