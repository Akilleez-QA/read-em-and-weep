# A structural upper bound on quartic relations of the HM middle module

## Result

Let S=Sym X over C, dim X=5, and let L be the audited HM middle module generated in degree zero by U=W′, with Hilbert function (2,10,20,20,10,2). Write R=ker(S⊗U→L) and b=dim Tor₁^S(L,C)₄, the number of minimal quartic relations. Then

    b ∈ {0,5,10,15,20}.

Equivalently, the full cubic relation space has polynomial shadow

    dim(XR₃) ≥ 110.

This is a new necessary constraint, independent of whether the fifteen-dimensional cubic obstruction vanishes. It excludes both b=25 and all larger values. It does not settle the surviving cubic scalar or prove quadratic generation.

## 1. Inputs and the correct truncation

The generation, Hilbert function, relation dimensions, normalizer action, and Betti conventions are those of ../middle-module-relations/PROOF.md and AUDIT.md. In particular,

    dim R₂=10, dim R₃=50, dim R₄=130,

and X is irreducible for the Heisenberg subgroup H₅, hence also for N. The center acts on X by a primitive fifth root and trivially on U. The perfect pairing and indecomposability results of ../apolar-middle-module/PROOF.md are compatible inputs but are not needed in this argument.

Define the cubic truncation of the presentation by

    K=S·R₂+S·R₃ ⊂ S⊗U,     P=(S⊗U)/K.

Both K and P are N-equivariant, and K is generated in degrees at most three. We use all cubic relations here, whether or not they are generated by quadrics. Since XR₂⊂R₃,

    K₃=R₃,     K₄=XR₃.

Thus

    h_P(3)=20,
    h_P(4)=140−dim(XR₃)=10+b.

The final identity follows from b=dim(R₄/XR₃). In particular this construction is valid in both surviving cubic cases a=0 and a=15.

## 2. Module growth and exclusion of the extremal value

We use the module forms of Macaulay growth and Gotzmann persistence: if F is a finite graded free S-module generated in degree zero and K⊂F is generated in degrees at most d≥1, then

    h_(F/K)(d+1) ≤ h_(F/K)(d)^{⟨d⟩};

if equality holds, equality persists in subsequent degrees. Here a^{⟨d⟩} is obtained from the usual d-binomial expansion by increasing every upper and lower binomial index by one. The precise module statement is recorded in Roger Dellaca, Hilbert polynomials and module generating degrees, Theorem 2.6, with maximal generator degree l=0 and parameter p=0; it cites Gasharov, Theorem 4.2:
https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uci.edu/dist/7/2283/files/2015/09/Hilbert-polynomials-and-module-generating-degrees.pdf

Since 20=binom(6,3), this theorem gives

    10+b=h_P(4) ≤ binom(7,4)=35,

so b≤25. Suppose b=25. Persistence applies because K is generated in degrees at most three. Iterating the theorem gives

    h_P(d)=binom(d+3,3) for every d≥3.

Therefore P has Krull dimension four and multiplicity one: its Hilbert polynomial has degree three and leading coefficient 1/6.

We now use the associativity formula for multiplicity of a graded module. The multiplicity equals the sum, over its dimension-four support primes p, of

    length_(S_p)(P_p) · degree(S/p).

All summands are positive integers. Consequently there is exactly one dimension-four support prime p, and degree(S/p)=1. Since S is a polynomial ring in five variables, p has height one and is principal, generated by an irreducible homogeneous polynomial f. Its degree is deg f, so f must be linear. Thus P has a unique top-dimensional support component, a hyperplane.

The N-equivariance of P preserves its support and permutes its top-dimensional components. Uniqueness therefore forces this hyperplane to be N-invariant. Its defining one-dimensional subspace C f⊂X is N-invariant, contradicting the irreducibility of X, whose dimension is five. Possible smaller-dimensional support components do not affect this argument.

It follows that b<25.

## 3. Heisenberg divisibility and interpretation

The quotient R₄/XR₃ is an H₅-representation on which its center has the same nontrivial character as S₄⊗U. Every irreducible H₅-representation with nontrivial central character has dimension five. Hence 5 divides b. Combined with b<25 this proves

    b∈{0,5,10,15,20},     dim(XR₃)=130−b≥110.

The normalized shadow inequality alone gives dim(XR₃)≥100, or b≤30. Macaulay growth improves this to b≤25, and normalizer symmetry excludes its equality case. This illustrates why equality rigidity supplies information that the raw Hilbert numerator and normalized growth cannot provide.

If the cubic obstruction vanishes, the previously audited minimality argument gives c=d=0 in the self-dual Betti notation of ../middle-module-relations/PROOF.md. The present result then additionally restricts its quartic parameter to the five values above; the middle ghost parameter e remains unrestricted by this argument. If the cubic obstruction is present, the same quartic bound still holds.

## Evidence boundary

This proof uses only audited module data, a general theorem for graded free-module quotients, finite-group representation divisibility, and the geometry of multiplicity-one support. No coordinates for an HM map, source map, cofactor map, quintic, or quadratic inclusion are selected. No multiplication matrix or rank instance is evaluated. The cubic fifteen-dimensional scalar remains unresolved, and no physical/source-obstruction conclusion is drawn.
