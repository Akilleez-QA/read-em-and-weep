# Actual section constraints on the double-conic obstruction

## Inputs and scope

Work over C with the actual rank-two plane bundle E and saturated inclusion

    0 → K=O_W(a) → E → I_Z(-2-a) → 0,       a∈{0,1}.

Here W=P², det(E)=O_W(-2), Z is zero-dimensional and locally complete intersection, and C is the fixed smooth conic. Set D=2C, R=Z∩C, δ=length R. Degrees on C=P¹ are P¹ degrees, not plane twists. The established restriction splitting is

    E_C=A_C⊕Q_C,
    deg A_C=2a+δ,   deg Q_C=-4-2a-δ,
    d=deg A_C-deg Q_C=4a+4+2δ.

The existing ribbon classification gives the maximal-line obstruction

    e∈H¹(C,L),       L=Hom(A_C,Q_C)⊗O_C(-4)=O_C(-d-4),

and identifies its Cartan boundary C_e with the binary catalecticant

    ⟨C_e(s),t⟩=±⟨e,st⟩,
    deg s=n=d-4,  deg t=6.

For d≥8 the entire boundary ε is C_e. For d=6 it can have one further diagonal-deformation row; for d=4 its domain has dimension one. In all d≥4, e=0 iff E_D splits; ε=0 always implies e=0, while the converse for ε requires care when d=4,6.

No actual Z, HM matrix, source, ambient map, or selected rank is evaluated below. The conclusions are conditional structural constraints on the actual extension, beyond arbitrary ribbon-local models.

## 1. Elementary split-ribbon exclusion

Suppose E_D=A⊕Q is split, labelled so A_C is the maximal summand. The line bundle Hom(K_D,Q) has reduced degree

    -4-4a-δ<0,

and its nilpotent layer has degree -8-4a-δ<0. The ribbon exact sequence therefore gives H⁰(D,Hom(K_D,Q))=0. Consequently the actual map K_D→E_D has zero Q component and factors through A.

The ideal generated by the coordinates of the section K_D→E_D is independent of frames. It is exactly the image ideal I_Z O_D, equivalently Fitt₁(coker[K_D→E_D]); it is not the tensor-product sheaf I_Z⊗O_D, which can have an additional kernel mapping to O_D. A section factoring through a line summand has principal coordinate ideal. Thus

    E_D split ⇒ I_Z O_D locally principal.                  (1)

At a reduced point p of Z lying on C, choose regular parameters q,t with C=(q). Then I_Z,p=(q,t), and its image (q,t) in S/(q²) is not principal. For example its minimal number of generators is two by Nakayama, since q and t remain independent modulo the square of the maximal ideal. Such a point excludes splitting, so e≠0 and ε≠0. This implication remains valid for d=4,6: it uses ε=0⇒splitting, never the false general converse splitting⇒ε=0 in these small gaps. In fact the present global formula makes d=4 incompatible with δ>0.

## 2. Exact local principal-ideal test, including nonreduced zeros

At p∈R let S=O_W,p, write q for the conic equation, I=I_Z,p, and set

    r_p=length S/(I,q),
    ℓ_p=length S/(I,q²).

Choose f∈I whose reduction generates I O_C,p=(t^{r_p}). Then f mod q² is a nonzerodivisor: in the completed ring C[[t,q]]/(q²), its reduction mod q is nonzero. Moreover

    length S/(f,q²)=2r_p.

Since (f,q²)⊂(I,q²), there is a surjection between the corresponding Artin quotients, proving ℓ_p≤2r_p. Equality holds iff the ideals are equal. This yields the exact equivalence

    I O_D,p principal ⇔ ℓ_p=2r_p.                         (2)

For the forward direction, if I O_D,p=(h), reduction shows h mod q has order r_p. Write f=uh in O_D,p; reduction makes u a unit, hence f generates the same ideal. This also verifies that principal means generated by a nonzerodivisor in this setting.

Always r_p≤ℓ_p≤2r_p, since the quotient by (I,q²) surjects onto the quotient by (I,q). A reduced Z point gives r_p=ℓ_p=1. Nonreduced points need not fail (2): the formal ideal (t,q²) passes it, while (t²,q) fails it. These are local algebra examples only, not constructions of actual global HM zero schemes. The local test alone asserts no existence of a global bundle or extension with prescribed Z.

## 3. The actual K section supports the maximal-line obstruction

The reduced K map has the form K_C→A_C, with zero divisor R. Denote this section by σ; it is a section of Hom(K_C,A_C)=O_C(R). The existence of the actual lifted map K_D→E_D implies

    σ e=0 in H¹(L(R)).                                   (3)

One can verify this in local graph coordinates. Choose local frames lifting the splitting A_C⊕Q_C. The lower component of the actual K section vanishes modulo q, while the upper component reduces to σ. On overlaps the lower components differ by the lower-left deformation cocycle times σ. They therefore provide a coboundary for σe. Neither diagonal deformations of the chosen reference factors nor the choice of K_D changes this lower-component identity.

Alternatively, away from R the section is primitive and supplies an actual lift of A_C as a line subbundle. Its meromorphic graph relative to local reference lifts has poles only at R. This gives the same obstruction class as a boundary of principal parts.

Since deg L(R)=-d-4+δ<0, the exact sequence

    0→L→L(R)→L(R)|R→0

has zero H⁰ in its first two terms. Thus (3) places e in the injective image of H⁰(L(R)|R). In particular e has unique principal parts supported on R and belongs to a δ-dimensional linear subspace of H¹(L).

### The precise pole divisor

Complete locally and choose a frame lifting A_C⊕Q_C. Up to units the actual section is

    (f,qb),       f mod q=t^{r_p}.

Its graph away from t=0 is q b/f; in the nilpotent layer this is represented by b₀/t^{r_p}, where b₀=b mod q. The principal part vanishes exactly when b₀ is divisible by t^{r_p}. Its exact pole order is

    m_p=max(0,r_p-ord_t b₀),

with m_p=0 if b₀=0. Let T=Σm_p p and τ=deg T. These numbers are independent of the local frame, as follows also from the intrinsic length formula below.

The ring O_D/(f) has length 2r_p, and its q-layer is C[[t]]/(t^{r_p}). Imposing qb kills a submodule of length m_p. Since the two section coordinates generate the image of I, this proves

    m_p=2r_p-ℓ_p,
    τ=2δ-ℓ,       ℓ=length(Z∩2C),
    0≤τ≤δ.                                               (4)

Here Z∩2C is defined by (I_Z,q²), not by the ideal-square quotient (I_Z²,q²) used in the separate full-section supplement. These two lengths must not be confused.

Every reduced Z point on C contributes m_p=1. Nonreduced points can contribute any value between 0 and r_p locally. The class e is supported on T with nonzero highest principal-part coefficient at every point of T.

For this actual global section, (2) and (4) also give a global equivalence, stronger than (1): e=0 iff τ=0 iff I_Z O_D is locally principal everywhere. This converse uses the actual K map and the injectivity of the principal-parts boundary; it is not an assertion that an arbitrary prescribed ideal determines a global bundle. Equivalently, when the coordinate ideal is locally principal, divide the section by its local regular generator. The resulting primitive vectors glue to a line subbundle lifting A_C, which yields splitting by the established ribbon theorem.

## 4. Exact Cartan rank from the pole divisor

The supported principal-parts description identifies e, by Serre duality, with a functional on O_T(n+6). Its nonzero highest pole coefficient at a point of multiplicity m means that, in a coordinate C[t]/(t^m), the functional is nonzero on t^{m-1}. Therefore its multiplication pairing on the local Artin algebra is perfect. Explicitly, if a nonzero element has initial order k, multiplication by t^{m-1-k} detects it. Summing over the points gives a perfect pairing

    H⁰(O_T(n)) × H⁰(O_T(6)) → C,

using the line-bundle weights and the functional supplied by e.

Since n=d-4=4a+2δ≥τ-1, restriction of H⁰(O_C(n)) to H⁰(O_T(n)) is surjective. Restriction of degree-six forms to T has rank min(7,τ): its kernel is H⁰(O_C(6)(-T)), of dimension max(7-τ,0). Factoring the catalecticant through these restriction maps and the perfect Artin pairing proves

    rank C_e=min(7,τ)=min(7,2δ-length(Z∩2C)).              (5)

In particular, if k distinct points of Z on C are reduced as points of Z, then

    rank C_e≥min(7,k).

This multiple-point bound is justified by the actual-section support and perfect-pairing argument. It is not inferred by treating arbitrary local nonsplitting conditions as automatically independent. There is also the upper bound rank C_e≤min(7,δ), which need not hold for arbitrary ribbon-local models without K.

## 5. Consequences for ε, the small gaps, and d≤20

For d≥8, the whole boundary is the Cartan catalecticant, so

    rank ε=min(7,τ).

When d=4, the global formula forces a=δ=0. Here K_D is a subbundle, and the extension between the plane lines K_D=O_D and Q_D=O_D(-2 plane) splits because H¹(D,O_D(2 plane))=0. Its positive-root line is the plane line O_D(2 plane), so the reference-boundary argument gives ε=0. In particular no reduced zero on C occurs in this case.

When d=6, necessarily a=0 and δ=1. Consequently τ∈{0,1}. The Cartan block has rank τ, and the full boundary is its stack with one diagonal-deformation row. Hence

    τ≤rank ε≤τ+1.

More precisely, the rank is τ when that row belongs to the Cartan row space, and τ+1 otherwise. If a reduced zero lies on C then τ=1, giving 1≤rank ε≤2. If τ=0 the bundle is split, but ε may still be nonzero from the diagonal direction; no choice of the actual diagonal row is evaluated here. These are constraints, not an assertion that every listed small-gap possibility occurs for a global HM bundle. The separately proved `SUPPLEMENT.md` sharpens this for the actual global section to the exact formula rank ε=τ+1: rank one when τ=0 and rank two when τ=1. Its proof uses the geometry of the plane double conic and does not evaluate the actual diagonal class.

If the existing gap bound d≤20 applies, then

    δ≤8-2a.

Thus a=0 gives 0≤δ≤8; for δ≥2, d≥8 and rank ε≤min(7,δ). The exceptional δ=0,1 cases are as above. For a=1, 0≤δ≤6 and d≥8 always, so

    rank ε=min(7,τ)=τ≤δ≤6.

In particular the actual a=1 branch cannot realize ribbon-local rank seven. This uses the global K section, not only the restricted splitting and determinant.

## Boundary of the result

These formulas do not determine δ, ℓ, τ, a, or the actual diagonal row. They do not determine the ambient detector map, a component-image quotient, or the value on any source. A nonzero ribbon boundary and its rank are not an evaluation of the ambient obstruction.
