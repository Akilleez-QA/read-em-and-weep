# Excluding the degree-one kernel without evaluating a matrix

## Result and inputs

Let E be the actual square-surface restriction on W=P² over C. Use the established inputs

- rank E=2, det E=O(-2), c₂(E)=16;
- E(8) is globally generated and H¹(E(8))=0;
- H¹(End E(t))=0 for t≥14.

Then every saturated rank-one subsheaf O(a)⊂E has a≤0. In particular the previously possible degree-one kernel branch is impossible. If B=End₀(E), then

    h⁰(B(-1)) ∈ {0,3},   h⁰(B(-2)) ∈ {0,1},
    H⁰(End E(t))=0 for every t≤-3.

The two nonzero dimensions occur together, exactly when E has a saturated O⊂E. Thus the square-component restriction has rank at most three. This does not select its actual rank or prove that its three potential functionals are independent.

## 1. Audited exclusion

A saturated rank-one subsheaf of a vector bundle on the smooth surface W is reflexive, hence a line bundle. Its torsion-free quotient has the form

    0 → O(a) → E → I_Z(-2-a) → 0,
    length Z = a²+2a+16.

Global generation of E(8) passes to I_Z(6-a). The previously established elementary bound a≤1 can be recovered as follows. Set d=6-a. A globally generated I_Z(d) cannot have d<0; d=0 would require Z empty, contrary to its positive length. Two sufficiently general sections have no common curve, so Bézout gives length Z≤d². Therefore 14a≤20 and a≤1.

Suppose a=1. Twisting the actual sequence by eight gives

    0 → O(9) → E(8) → I_Z(5) → 0,    length Z=19.

The quotient I_Z(5) is globally generated. Its cohomology sequence, using H¹(E(8))=0 and H²(O(9))=0, gives H¹(I_Z(5))=0. From the ideal sequence,

    h⁰(I_Z(5)) = h⁰(O(5)) - length Z = 21-19 = 2.

Choose a basis f,g of this two-dimensional space. Because it is the entire global-section space and the sheaf is globally generated, evaluation is a surjection O²→I_Z(5). Equivalently the sheaf ideal generated by f,g is exactly I_Z.

The two quintics have no common curve: otherwise their common ideal would vanish on that curve, whereas I_Z is the unit ideal away from finitely many points. They are therefore a homogeneous regular sequence. Their complete intersection has length 5·5=25. Its ideal sheaf equals I_Z by the evaluation surjection, so it also has length 19. This contradiction excludes a=1.

This argument does not presume Z reduced, general, or disjoint from the conic. It needs global generation as a sheaf, including at the points of Z. Merely having two quintics through nineteen points would not suffice; here those two sections generate the full ideal sheaf everywhere.

## 2. Entire negative endomorphism module

For a nonzero f:E→E(-k), k>0, trace and determinant vanish for degree reasons. Twisted Cayley–Hamilton gives f(-k)f=0. Its saturated kernel is O(a), its image lies in O(a-k), and it induces a nonzero map

    I_Z(-2-a) → O(a-k).

Since Hom(I_Z,O)=O on a smooth surface, this requires a nonzero section of O(2a+2-k). With a≤0, necessarily a=0 and k≤2.

There is at most one saturated nonnegative-degree line in E. For two such lines the composite of either with the other's quotient vanishes by negative degree, and saturation forces equality. If K≅O exists, write T=det(E)K⁻¹≅O(-2), and N=K T⁻¹≅O(2). Quotient and inclusion define the intrinsic nilpotent line j:N→B. Then

    H⁰(B(-1)) = j H⁰(O(1)),
    H⁰(B(-2)) = j H⁰(O),
    H⁰(B(-k)) = 0 for k≥3.

Without K all negative spaces are zero. Scalar summands have no negative sections, so these statements also give the asserted vanishings for End E. All compositions of negative-twist endomorphisms vanish. The identification with the displayed ordinary line bundles depends on trivializations; dimensions and the intrinsic map j do not.

A further vector-bundle consequence is H⁰(E(-1))=0: any such section would saturate to O(a) with a≥1. Since E*=E(2), Serre duality now gives

    H²(E(t))=H⁰(E(-t-1))*=0 for every t≥0.

Thus E(1) has no line subsheaf of degree ≥2. No assertion of semistability or stability follows: in the nonzero-detector branch it contains O(1), which destabilizes it. A nonzero section of E saturates to K≅O, and the quotient I_Z(-2) has no sections. Consequently h⁰(E) is exactly one in this branch and zero in the other branch.

## 3. Component detector and smaller extendability support

The existing duality identifies the component target dual with H⁰(B(-1)). Consequently

    dim H²(W,B(-2)) ∈ {0,3},
    rank r_W ≤ 3.

There is also an exact cohomology dichotomy. Riemann–Roch gives χ(B(-2))=-60. By the negative-module formula and Serre duality, its cohomology dimensions (h⁰,h¹,h²) are

    (1,64,3) if h⁰(E)=1,
    (0,60,0) if h⁰(E)=0.

This determines the component target dimension without selecting which branch the actual bundle occupies.

For X=8C, the existing full-section extension isomorphism remains valid and now gives

    h⁰(X,B(-1)|X) ∈ {0,3},
    h¹(X,B(-1)|X) ∈ {360,363}.

In the nonzero branch, length Z=16 and the ideal-square factorization specializes to

    0 → E → B(-2) → I_Z²(-4) → 0.

The doubled zero scheme defined by I_Z² has length 48. The ambient detector is still the actual Gysin pullback from this scalar ideal-square quotient; no actual ambient functional is evaluated.

There is a genuine reduced-conic refinement. Since H⁰(B(-3))=0, restriction H⁰(B(-1))→H⁰(C,B(-1)|C) is injective. In the nonzero branch the previously proved saturation of j on W implies that

    j_C: O_C(1) → B(-1)|C

is an injective sheaf map, even when Z meets C. Restriction H⁰(O(1))→H⁰(O_C(1)) is an isomorphism by the plane conic sequence. Therefore the image of the global detector-section space is exactly

    H⁰(C, j_C(O_C(1))).

A section of B(-1)|C extends globally if and only if it lies in this actual subsheaf on global sections; its extension is unique. Thus the earlier 2C line-membership realization improves to C. It requires the actual restricted nilpotent line, not its saturation on C and not only splitting degrees. If Z meets C, replacement by the saturation introduces spurious sections.

This does not assert that every section of B(-1)|C extends. The unconditional isomorphism for the entire section space is still established at 8C. The new negative H⁰ bound improves injectivity, not the required H¹ vanishing.

## 4. What does not improve automatically

For full endomorphism algebras, restriction to nC is injective for n≥2, since H⁰(End E(-2n))=0. At C its kernel can have dimension one in the K≅O branch. The supplied H¹ argument still guarantees surjectivity only at n≥9. Hence the universal nonsplitting bound n₀≤9 and the full 8C detector-extension bound are unchanged. In particular improved H²(E(t)) does not supply the still-missing H¹(E(7)) at the endpoint t=13 of the End-cohomology estimate.

If K exists and Z∩C is empty, the disjoint-zero residue criterion specializes to b=2 and t_n=2n-5. Its positive extension splits automatically through 2C, fails by 6C, and hence has first nonsplitting order 3≤n₀≤6. The formerly stated a=1 interval [4,6] is impossible. No new lower bound above three or universal upper bound below six follows from the present argument.

In that disjoint branch E|C≅O_C⊕O_C(-2), whose splitting gap on P¹ is four. If separate hypotheses impose a gap of at least eight, this disjoint branch is excluded under those hypotheses. No such lower bound is assumed here.
