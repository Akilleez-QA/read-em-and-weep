## 1. Numerator and the three-generator sequence

At `z=1`, `g = S - W^2` and `F = XY`. In `R = Q[X,Y,W]`:

- `W^6 ≠ 0` in a domain, so it is a nonzerodivisor.
- `(W^6) : (S-W^2) = (W^6)`.
- `(W^6, S-W^2) : (XY) = (W^6, S-W^2)`.

So `(W^6, S-W^2, XY)` is a regular sequence. The degree-3 piece of the ideal is spanned by `Xg, Yg, Wg, XF, YF, WF`. Those six polynomials are linearly independent in the 10-dimensional space of homogeneous cubics, and adjoining `a = X^3+Y^3` raises the rank from 6 to 7. Degree 3 cannot see `W^6`, so `a` is not in the ideal. A Gröbner normal form for the same membership is `W^2(X+Y) ≠ 0`.

The same colon test rejects a sequence with a zerodivisor: `(XY):(X)` properly contains `(XY)`, and `(X^2, X)` fails at the second step.

## 2. Čech cover and regularity at the chart

The representative is `z a / (W^6 (-g) F)` with `g = S - z W^2`.

It is a section of the triple overlap and of no pairwise overlap. `W` does not divide the numerator, neither factor of `F = XY` divides `a`, and `g` does not divide `z a` in `Q[z,X,Y,W]` (nor at `z=1`). Numerator and denominator are plane-homogeneous of degrees 3 and 10. On a cover with three opens every triple cochain is a cocycle; this one is not the restriction of a section from `D(W^6)∩D(-g)`, `D(W^6)∩D(F)`, or `D(-g)∩D(F)`.

Non-extension from each individual double overlap does not alone prove nonvanishing modulo the sum of the double-overlap boundaries. At stage 1, the class `N/(f1 f2 f3)` is the restriction of a section from one double overlap only when `N` lies in the principal ideal generated by the missing factor. The Čech coboundary is the sum of the three restrictions. A numerator can lie in none of `(f1)`, `(f2)`, `(f3)` and still lie in `(f1,f2,f3)`, in which case

`N/(f1 f2 f3) = A/(f2 f3) + B/(f1 f3) + C/(f1 f2)`

with `A,B,C` in `R`, and the class vanishes. The regular-sequence colon identity supplies that missing implication: stage-1 nonmembership in the sum ideal, together with injectivity of every later transition, is what puts the class in the direct limit.

**Must-fail control.** On the same sequence, `N = f1 + f2` is outside `(f1)`, outside `(f2)`, and outside `(f3)`, and `N = f1 + f2 + 0·f3` lies in the sum. Explicitly `N/(f1 f2 f3) = 1/(f2 f3) + 1/(f1 f3)`. Individual non-extension holds for `N`, and the class of `N` is a coboundary. The numerator `a` has the same three individual non-extensions and lies outside the sum ideal. The control separates those two numerators.

The nonvanishing argument is at the explicit chart `z=1`, in `R = Q[X,Y,W]`, for the ordered sequence `(f1,f2,f3) = (W^6, -g, F)` used by the cover `D(W^6), D(-g), D(F)`. Successive colons were computed by elimination, and each colon equals the ideal generated by the preceding elements. Every permutation of this sequence is a regular sequence in `R`; the script checks all six orders. The same ordered sequence is regular in `Q[z,X,Y,W]` before setting `z=1`. Specialization was not used as a substitute for the chart.

A Gröbner basis at `z=1` has leading terms including `X^2`, `Y^3`, and `W^6`, so the affine cone is supported only at the origin and the projective zero set is empty. That is the homogeneous-system-of-parameters input to the Cohen–Macaulay appeal in §5.1. The colon computation is the regularity proof used here; it does not stop at the generic point.

**All-n colon identity.** The standard fact is the Stacks Project, Lemma 10.68.9 (tag [07DV](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/07DV)): if `g1,...,gr` is a regular sequence and `e1,...,er` are positive integers, then `g1^{e1},...,gr^{er}` is a regular sequence in the same order. One elementary step uses that fact. If `h` is a nonzerodivisor on `R/J` and `a ≥ 1`, then `(J + (h^a)) : (h) = J + (h^{a-1})`: the condition `h x ∈ J + (h^a)` means `h(x - c h^{a-1}) ∈ J`.

Fix `n ≥ 1`. Apply the fact to three orders already checked in `R`.

1. `(f2, f3, f1)` regular implies `(f2^{n+1}, f3^{n+1}, f1)` regular, so `f1` is a nonzerodivisor modulo `(f2^{n+1}, f3^{n+1})` and `(f1^{n+1}, f2^{n+1}, f3^{n+1}) : f1 = (f1^n, f2^{n+1}, f3^{n+1})`.
2. `(f1, f3, f2)` regular implies `(f1^n, f3^{n+1}, f2)` regular, so `(f1^n, f2^{n+1}, f3^{n+1}) : f2 = (f1^n, f2^n, f3^{n+1})`.
3. `(f1, f2, f3)` regular implies `(f1^n, f2^n, f3)` regular, so `(f1^n, f2^n, f3^{n+1}) : f3 = (f1^n, f2^n, f3^n)`.

Each of those three colons is an equality. Colon ideals compose, `((I : f1) : f2) : f3 = I : (f1 f2 f3)`, so

`(f1^{n+1}, f2^{n+1}, f3^{n+1}) : (f1 f2 f3) = (f1^n, f2^n, f3^n)`

in `Q[X,Y,W]`, for every integer `n ≥ 1`. The inclusion of the power ideal `(f1^n, f2^n, f3^n)` in the colon is also the expansion `f1 f2 f3 · f_i^n = f_i^{n+1} · ∏_{j≠i} f_j`, which does not use regularity. The case `n=1` is the equality `(W^{12}, g^2, F^2) : (W^6 (-g) F) = (W^6, -g, F)` and is the first transition.

Each transition of the direct system

`R/(f1^n, f2^n, f3^n) → R/(f1^{n+1}, f2^{n+1}, f3^{n+1})`, `x ↦ (f1 f2 f3) x`

is therefore injective. Since `a` lies outside `(f1,f2,f3)`, the image of `a` under every composite of these maps is nonzero. The class of `a` is nonzero in the direct limit. That direct limit is the one named in §5.1, namely `H^3_{(X,Y,W)}(R)`. This is the all-n argument already stated there.

The script checks the displayed identity exactly over `Q` for `n=1,2,3,4`, both inclusions, and checks that `(f1 f2 f3)^{n-1} a` lies outside `(f1^n, f2^n, f3^n)` for those same four stages. Those are finite checks of this sequence. Injectivity for every `n ≥ 1` is the regular-sequence argument above, so stage-1 nonmembership survives in the direct limit.

The class is the fixed representative `z a / (W^6 (-g) F)` of `δ(t0)` for the unchanged pullback middle, with `V` and `β` fixed. The coupled formal deformation, and arbitrary changes of `α` or `β`, stay outside this step.

**Status.** The §2 direct-limit step is closed.

## 3. Injectivity into `H^2(O(3,-7))`

This is a proof, re-derived here. It is not a numerical sampling of the class.

`T_B` is upper-triangular in the ordered frame `(P, E0, E1, E2)` except for the extension entry `T_B[E0,E1] = z C`, which stays inside `⟨P, E0, E1⟩`. The flag

`⟨P⟩ ⊂ ⟨P, E0⟩ ⊂ ⟨P, E0, E1⟩ ⊂ B`

is `T_B`-invariant. Its quotients are the filtration lines `O(3,-3)`, `O(0,0)`, `O(4,-4)`, `O(3,-4)`, whose base degrees are the exponents `(3, 0, 4, 3)` on the diagonal of `T_B`. The off-diagonal entry was solved from `T_B r_∞ = z^3 r_0`; it equals `z C`.

Tensor the flag by `I = O(0,-1)`. The line bundle is flat, so

`0 → AI → BI → GI → 0`

stays exact, and the graded pieces of `BI` are

`O(3,-4)`, `O(0,-1)`, `O(4,-5)`, `O(3,-5)`.

On `Y = P^1 × P^2`, Künneth and the Bott vanishing `H^1(P^2, O(*)) = 0` give

`H^1(Y, O(α,β)) = H^1(P^1, O(α)) ⊗ H^0(P^2, O(β))`.

`H^1(P^1, O(α))` is zero for `α ≥ -1` and equals `-α-1` for `α ≤ -2`. Each line above has base degree at least `0`, so each `H^1` is zero. Vanishing passes through the three short exact sequences of the flag, so `H^1(BI) = 0`.

The long exact sequence of `0 → AI → BI → GI → 0` then says that

`H^1(GI) → H^2(AI)`

is injective. Here `AI = A ⊗ I = O(3,-6) ⊗ O(0,-1) = O(3,-7)`.

The script evaluates the dimension formula on the four bidegrees and checks the flag entries of `T_B`. It also evaluates the formula on `O(-3,0)`, where the dimension is positive, so the four zeros are not a constant return value.

## 4. Bidegree

`Hom(D, A) ⊗ DI = O(2,-7) ⊗ O(1,0) = O(3,-7)`, and `A ⊗ I` gives the same bidegree.

On the finite chart, `z` is the base coordinate. Every term of `z(X^3+Y^3)` has plane degree 3. Every term of `W^6 (-g) F`, including both `S` and `z W^2` inside `g`, has plane degree 10. The representative is plane-homogeneous of degree `3-6-2-2 = -7`.

The single factor `z` in the numerator is the finite frame of `t_0`, a section of `DI = O(1,0)`. Its exponent is 1. That exponent is not the base degree of `AI`. The base degree 3 is the `A`-frame: the `P`-slot of `T_B` is `z^3`, and `I` contributes base degree 0. The value line of the pairing is `L = P ⊗ D = O(4,-2)`, base degree `3+1 = 4`, which is the `z^4` in check 5.

## 5. The overlap identity

`τ` is built as follows.

1. `C` and `T` come from the two numerator divisions. `T_B` has diagonal `(z^3, 1, z^4, z^3)` from the base degrees, and its one off-diagonal entry is solved from the relation.
2. Each `b_ij` is the solution of `Ω_E b = ψ_j - ψ_i` supported on the third leg. The central scalars are the three partial-fraction terms of `-a^2/(r_1 r_2 r_3)`.
3. The unknown is the `B`-valued correction to `q_∞ ↦ z q_0`. The factor `z` is the transition of `D = O(1,1)` and is part of the map being tested. The mixed cocycle `H_i M^∞_{ij} - M^0_{ij} H_j` is required to be a multiple of the finite relation. The gauge is: charts 1 and 2 carry no `B`-correction, and the chart-0 correction has no component on the eliminated `E0` coordinate. That linear system has a unique solution. It simplifies to

   `z C /(W^6 S^3) · (T F, 0, 0, -a)`

   on chart 0 and to `0` on the other two charts. The pair-31 coefficient of the relation is `z C a / (F W^6 S^3)`.

4. `τ_i = R_{0,i} H_i J_{∞,i}` is the induced map of quotient frames. The quotient forms are `J^T Ω_5 J`, with `Ω(P, q) = 1`.

Entrywise,

`τ_i^T Ω_{finite,i} τ_i - z^4 Ω_{∞,i} = 0`

in `Q(z,X,Y,W)` for each of the three charts. The `(P, q)` entry on each chart is exactly `z^4`, which is `z^3` from the `P`-frame times the value-line factor `z`.

**Must-fail perturbation.** On the chart with frame `(P, E0, E2, q)`, the infinity pairing `Ω(E0, E2)` equals `g/z`. Right-multiplication by `diag(1, 1, 2, 1)` scales the `E2` leg by 2. If the unperturbed identity holds, the residual is

`z^4 (D^T Ω_∞ D - Ω_∞)`.

Its `(E0, E2)` entry equals `z^4 · (g/z) = z^3 g ≠ 0`, and its `(P, q)` entry is 0. The script checks that the directly expanded residual equals this matrix, and that `z^3 g` is not the zero element. Doubling one leg of a hyperbolic plane is not a symplectic shear: `D^T Ω D - Ω` is not zero.

**Controls that are not the counterexample.** The shear `q ↦ q + P` still satisfies the identity; an arbitrary matrix edit can be a shear and need not fail. Rescaling the `q` frame by 2 changes the `P–q` entry of `D^T Ω D - Ω` by `1`. That is a rescaling of the perfect `P–D` pairing, and it is not the control used here.
