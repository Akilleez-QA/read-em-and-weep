# An embedded primitive triple obstruction on P1 times P3

## Result and scope

Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, let

\[
X=\mathbf P^1_s\times\mathbf P^3,
\]

and identify the coordinates of $\mathbf P^3$ with the entries of a
$2\times2$ matrix. Embed

\[
Z=\mathbf P^1_s\times\mathbf P^1_t\hookrightarrow X,
\qquad
([s_0:s_1],[t_0:t_1])\longmapsto
([s_0:s_1],[s_0t_0:s_0t_1:s_1t_0:s_1t_1]).
\]

**Theorem.** No embedded primitive triple structure on this $Z$ in $X$
has associated line bundle $L=\mathcal O_Z(-2,1)$.

More precisely, every ribbon $Z_2\subset X$ with reduced scheme $Z$ and
nilpotent ideal $L=\mathcal O_Z(-2,1)$ has a nonzero obstruction to
extension to an embedded primitive triple. If the ribbon is specified by a
basepoint-free pair of binary quadratics $F_0,F_1$, the obstruction is,
up to the sign of a chosen Čech generator, the binary quintic

\[
\boxed{\quad
\mathcal R(F_0,F_1)
=\frac12(t_0F_1-t_1F_0)
\det\begin{pmatrix}
\partial_{t_0}F_0&\partial_{t_1}F_0\\
\partial_{t_0}F_1&\partial_{t_1}F_1
\end{pmatrix}.
\quad}
\]

It represents an element of
$H^1(Z,\mathcal O_Z(-2,5))$.

This is an obstruction to extending the specified embedding. It does not
exclude abstract primitive triples, other supports, or rank-two bundles
with the same Chern classes constructed by other methods. In fact, over
$\mathbb C$, every ribbon considered here has an abstract primitive
triple extension; see the final section on abstract extensions.

The proof below computes the obstruction directly. The references supply
the general primitive-multiple-scheme framework, not this particular
formula or nonexistence theorem.

## The conormal quotient and its kernel

Write the coordinates of $\mathbf P^3$ as
$[a_0:a_1:b_0:b_1]$. The ideal of $Z$ is generated by

\[
s_0b_0-s_1a_0,\qquad s_0b_1-s_1a_1.
\]

These equations form a complete intersection of two divisors of type
$(1,1)$. Since

\[
\mathcal O_X(a,b)|_Z=\mathcal O_Z(a+b,b),
\]

the conormal bundle is

\[
N^*_{Z/X}=\mathcal O_Z(-2,-1)^{\oplus2}.
\]

Let $I=\mathcal I_Z$ and $J=\mathcal I_{Z_2}$. A ribbon with associated
line bundle $L$ is specified by a surjection

\[
I/I^2\twoheadrightarrow I/J=L.
\]

For $L=\mathcal O_Z(-2,1)$, every such map is a pair of sections of
$\mathcal O_Z(0,2)$, hence a pair of binary quadratics $F_0,F_1$
depending only on $t$. Surjectivity means that they have no common zero
on $\mathbf P^1_t$. Its kernel is

\[
K=J/I^2=\mathcal O_Z(-2,-3).
\]

The corresponding quotient construction for double lines is explicit in
Nollet, Proposition 1.4. The local argument here works in codimension two
with any smooth support. See [Nollet, *The Hilbert schemes of degree three
curves*, §§1–2](https://www.numdam.org/article/ASENS_1997_4_30_3_367_0.pdf).

## Why a primitive triple forces a splitting

Set

\[
E=J/(IJ).
\]

Because $I$ annihilates this quotient, $E$ is a sheaf on the reduced
scheme $Z$. It fits into a canonical exact sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow I^2/(IJ)\longrightarrow E
\longrightarrow J/I^2\longrightarrow0.
\]

Multiplication identifies $I^2/(IJ)$ with $L^{\otimes2}$. To check
this and local freeness directly, choose local normal coordinates

\[
I=(x,y),\qquad J=(x,y^2).
\]

Then

\[
IJ=(x^2,xy,y^3),
\]

so $E$ is freely generated over $\mathcal O_Z$ by the classes of
$x$ and $y^2$; its subbundle $I^2/(IJ)$ is generated by $y^2$.
Consequently

\[
\tag{1}
0\longrightarrow L^2\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow0
\]

is an extension of line bundles.

Suppose now that an embedded primitive triple $Z_3\subset X$ has
canonical double $Z_2$, and write $H=\mathcal I_{Z_3}$. The standard
primitive local model gives, in compatible local coordinates,

\[
I=(x,y),\qquad J=(x,y^2),\qquad H=(x,y^3).
\]

One may check this model in completed local rings; completion is faithfully
flat. Primitivity gives a local hypersurface model with equation $y^3$
in a smooth threefold, and its embedding in the smooth fourfold adds one
regular normal equation $x$. The canonical filtration and local models
are reviewed in [Drézet, *Primitive multiple schemes*, §§4.1–4.2](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~jean-marc.drezet/papers/Var_M1.pdf).

The model shows $IJ\subset H$, and the quotient map

\[
E=J/(IJ)\longrightarrow J/H
\]

restricts to an isomorphism $I^2/(IJ)\to J/H$. Both are identified with
$L^2$ by multiplication. Composing with the inverse isomorphism gives
a retraction $E\to L^2$ of the first arrow in (1). Thus (1) must split.

This necessity does not assume a splitting. It derives one from the
primitive triple. Nor can one avoid it by placing the ribbon inside a
triple as a different double: every contained ribbon with the same smooth
support is the canonical double. Indeed $I^2\subset J$, so the
canonical double maps onto that ribbon. The induced surjection between
their invertible nilpotent ideals is an isomorphism.

The obstruction to this necessary splitting lies in

\[
\operatorname{Ext}^1_Z(K,L^2)
=H^1(Z,K^{-1}\otimes L^2)
=H^1(Z,\mathcal O_Z(-2,5)).
\]

## The actual coordinate transition

On the chart $s_0\ne0$, put $s=s_1/s_0$. On the chart $a_0\ne0$
of $\mathbf P^3$, normalize $a_0=1$ and put

\[
a_1=t,\qquad b_0=s+y_0,\qquad b_1=st+y_1.
\]

Then $Z=(y_0,y_1)$. Write

\[
f(t)=F_0(1,t),\qquad g(t)=F_1(1,t).
\]

The ribbon has ideal

\[
J=(I^2,e),\qquad e=f(t)y_1-g(t)y_0.
\]

If $\varepsilon$ is the local frame of $L=I/J$, then

\[
y_0=f\varepsilon,\qquad y_1=g\varepsilon.
\]

Because $f,g$ have no common zero, they generate the unit ideal in
$k[t]$, so such a frame can be chosen over this entire affine $t$-chart.
In $I^2/(IJ)=L^2$, multiplication gives

\[
y_0^2=f^2\varepsilon^2,\quad
y_0y_1=fg\varepsilon^2,\quad
y_1^2=g^2\varepsilon^2.
\]

On the other $s$-chart put $r=s_0/s_1=1/s$, use $b_0\ne0$, and
normalize the lower row instead. Its affine support and normal coordinates
are

\[
t'=\frac{st+y_1}{s+y_0},\qquad
z_i=-\frac{y_i}{s(s+y_0)}\quad(i=0,1).
\]

These are genuine ambient coordinate transitions, not a linearized normal
bundle approximation. The corresponding ribbon generator is

\[
e'=f(t')z_1-g(t')z_0.
\]

Modulo $I^3$, Taylor expansion gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
e'={}&-s^{-2}e+s^{-3}y_0e\\
&-s^{-3}(y_1-ty_0)(f'(t)y_1-g'(t)y_0).
\end{aligned}
\]

Here primes on $f,g$ denote differentiation with respect to $t$.
Since $I^3\subset IJ$ and $y_0e\in IJ$, the transition in $E=J/(IJ)$
is therefore

\[
\tag{2}
e'=-s^{-2}e+s^{-3}R(t)\varepsilon^2,
\qquad
R(t)=(g-tf)(fg'-gf').
\]

The square-line transition is

\[
\tag{3}
(\varepsilon')^2=s^{-4}\varepsilon^2,
\]

because $\varepsilon'=-s^{-2}\varepsilon$ in $I/J$.

## Why the off-diagonal term is nonzero in cohomology

It is enough to restrict (1) to $\mathbf P^1_s\times\mathbf A^1_t$.
Equations (2) and (3) give a Čech representative

\[
\pm s^{-1}R(t)
\]

in

\[
\frac{k[t][s,s^{-1}]}{k[t][s]+s^{-2}k[t][s^{-1}]}
\simeq H^1(\mathbf P^1_s,\mathcal O(-2))\otimes k[t].
\]

The class is nonzero whenever $R(t)\ne0$: $s^{-1}$ cannot occur in a
Čech coboundary in the displayed quotient.

Equivalently, a splitting would allow changes of lifts

\[
e\mapsto e+A(s,t)\varepsilon^2,\qquad
e'\mapsto e'+B(s^{-1},t)(\varepsilon')^2
\]

with $A\in k[t][s]$ and $B\in k[t][s^{-1}]$. Substitution into (2)
would require

\[
sR(t)+B(s^{-1},t)+s^2A(s,t)=0.
\]

The coefficient of $s^1$ is $R(t)$; neither of the last two terms has
such a coefficient. This proves non-splitting for any nonzero $R$,
without assuming a global Čech compatibility assertion.

Globally, Künneth gives

\[
H^1(Z,\mathcal O_Z(-2,5))
\simeq H^1(\mathbf P^1_s,\mathcal O(-2))
\otimes H^0(\mathbf P^1_t,\mathcal O(5)).
\]

The polynomial $R(t)$ has degree at most five and homogenizes to the
quintic $\mathcal R(F_0,F_1)$ stated above. Indeed, Euler's identity for
binary quadratics gives

\[
\det\begin{pmatrix}
\partial_{t_0}F_0&\partial_{t_1}F_0\\
\partial_{t_0}F_1&\partial_{t_1}F_1
\end{pmatrix}_{t_0=1,t_1=t}
=2(fg'-gf').
\]

## Nonvanishing for every valid quadratic pair

The polynomial ring $k[t_0,t_1]$ is a domain. If the product defining
$\mathcal R$ vanished, one of its two factors would vanish.

First, $t_0F_1-t_1F_0=0$ implies

\[
F_0=t_0h,\qquad F_1=t_1h
\]

for a homogeneous linear polynomial $h$. This produces a common zero,
contrary to basepoint-freeness.

Second, $fg'-gf'=0$ makes the rational function $g/f$ have zero
derivative. In characteristic zero it is constant. The two quadratics
are then proportional and have common zeros, again a contradiction.

Thus $\mathcal R\ne0$, sequence (1) cannot split, and an embedded
primitive triple cannot exist. This completes the theorem.

## Consequence for the proposed rank two construction

Let $u,v$ be the hyperplane classes from the two factors of $X$, and
put $H_X=u+v$. Then

\[
[Z]=(u+v)^2=H_X^2.
\]

If a primitive multiplicity-$n$ structure $Z_n$ were the regular zero
scheme of a section of $F(a,0)$, where $\det F=\mathcal O_X$, its class
would be $nH_X^2$. Since $u^2=0$, twisting by $\mathcal O_X(a,0)$
does not change $c_2$, so $c_2(F)=nH_X^2$.

Adjunction would require

\[
\omega_{Z_n}=\mathcal O_X(2a-2,-4)|_{Z_n}.
\]

For a primitive multiple structure,
$\omega_{Z_n}|_Z=\omega_Z\otimes L^{-(n-1)}$. Consequently

\[
\tag{4}
L^{n-1}=\mathcal O_Z(4-2a,2).
\]

For $n\ge2$, equation (4) permits only $n=2$ or $n=3$. For $n=3$,
it gives $L=\mathcal O_Z(2-a,1)$. The requisite quotient of
$\mathcal O_Z(-2,-1)^{\oplus2}$ would be generated by two everywhere
non-simultaneously-vanishing sections of $\mathcal O_Z(4-a,2)$.
There are no sections if $4-a<0$. If $4-a>0$, two such sections cannot
generate everywhere: their induced morphism to $\mathbf P^1$ would
make $\mathcal O_Z(4-a,2)$ a pullback of $\mathcal O(1)$, whereas
its self-intersection is $4(4-a)>0$. Thus $a=4$, and necessarily

\[
L=\mathcal O_Z(-2,1),\qquad
\omega_{Z_3}=\mathcal O_X(6,-4)|_{Z_3}.
\]

The theorem rules out the embedded triple before the subcanonical
condition can be lifted or a Serre bundle and its stability can be tested.
It blocks this particular proposed $c_2=3H_X^2$ construction; it is
not a nonexistence theorem for all bundles with that Chern class.

## Abstract extensions are a separate question

Over $\mathbb C$, Drézet §7.2 places the obstruction to extending a
primitive double $Z_2$ abstractly to multiplicity three in

\[
H^2\bigl(Z,(\Omega_{Z_2}|_Z)^*\otimes L^2\bigr).
\]

Dualizing the ribbon cotangent sequence and tensoring by $L^2$ gives

\[
0\longrightarrow T_Z\otimes L^2
\longrightarrow (\Omega_{Z_2}|_Z)^*\otimes L^2
\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow0.
\]

Here

\[
T_Z\otimes L^2
=\mathcal O_Z(-2,2)\oplus\mathcal O_Z(-4,4),
\qquad L=\mathcal O_Z(-2,1).
\]

Each outer term has zero $H^2$ by Künneth, so the abstract obstruction
group vanishes. The cited criterion therefore gives an abstract primitive
triple extension of every such complex ribbon. This directly emphasizes
what fails above: extension of its fixed embedding in $X$.

## Edge cases and exact verification

The assumptions are essential for the stated argument.

* A pair with a common factor need not give a ribbon with invertible
  quotient $L$. For example $(F_0,F_1)=(t_0^2,t_0t_1)$ has
  $\mathcal R=0$, but both sections vanish at $[0:1]$.
  The pair $(t_0^2,t_0^2)$ also has zero obstruction polynomial and a
  common zero. Conversely $(t_0^2,t_0^2+t_0t_1)$ has a common factor
  but affine $R=1$. These are tests outside the theorem's hypotheses.
* The affine transition formula is valid in characteristic two, but its
  nonvanishing proof is not. The basepoint-free inseparable pair
  $(t_0^2,t_1^2)$ has $R=0$ in characteristic two. The basepoint-free
  separable pair $(t_0^2,t_0t_1+t_1^2)$ instead has affine $R=t^2\ne0$.
  The homogeneous Jacobian is zero in this latter example too: the
  characteristic-zero division by (2) cannot be discarded. No
  characteristic-two existence or nonexistence classification is asserted.

The companion `check_obstruction.py` uses exact SymPy algebra. Run:

```
python check_obstruction.py
```

It starts from the ambient rational coordinate transition and verifies its
linear and quadratic terms, the projection to $L^2$, the square-line
transition, and the homogeneous quintic identity. It also checks the edge
cases just listed, and all 480 basepoint-free pairs among the 729 pairs
whose six quadratic coefficients lie in $\{-1,0,1\}$. The finite test
is a regression check; the factorization argument proves universal
nonvanishing. `check_output.txt` records a successful run with Python and
SymPy 1.14.0.
