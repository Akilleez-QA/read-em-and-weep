## 1. Arbitrary finite-colength ideals

Let S be the regular local ring of a smooth surface at a point of C, let q cut out C, and let I have finite colength. In the DVR S/(q), the image of I is generated by a nonzero element of order r. Such a generator has a preimage f in I. The pair (f,q) is a parameter sequence in S. Since S is Cohen–Macaulay, it is a regular sequence in either order. In particular q is regular in S/(f), and f is regular in S/(q^n) for every n.

Consequently the q-adic filtration of S/(f,q^n) has n layers isomorphic to S/(f,q), each of length r. The inclusion (f,q^n)⊂(I,q^n) proves

    length S/(I,q^n) ≤ nr.

Equality of these finite lengths is equivalent to equality of the ideals, hence to generation of the image of I by f. Conversely, if that image is (h), then f=uh modulo q^n; reduction in the DVR makes u a unit. Thus a principal image is necessarily generated by a regular element and is invertible as an ideal.

This argument requires neither reducedness nor the lci condition on I. It also explains why one must use the image ideal, rather than the tensor product I⊗O_X_n. Summing the nonnegative local deficits proves the asserted global equivalence with λ_n=nδ.

## 2. Passing between the ideal and the embedded line

Necessity uses the actual global section, not merely abstract local algebra. For any proposed lift A_n with quotient Q_n, the line Hom(K|X_n,Q_n) has successive reduced layer degrees

    −4−4a−δ−4j,   0≤j<n.

Every layer has zero H⁰, so the section K|X_n→E|X_n factors through A_n. Its coordinate ideal is therefore principal.

For sufficiency, division by a local regular generator h of the coordinate ideal produces a primitive vector: if the section coordinates are h v_i and generate (h), cancellation of h gives an identity expressing 1 in the ideal generated by the v_i. A primitive vector spans a direct-summand line locally. Cancellation also proves compatibility under changes of h and of the bundle frames, so these local lines glue. Their reduction is the saturated section line A_1.

Successive embedded-line lifts form a torsor under H⁰(O_C(−d−4m)), which is zero here, including d=4 and d=6. Thus uniqueness and compatibility of successful lifts are valid. This is not uniqueness of a complement or of arbitrary abstract Picard lifts.

## 3. Jordan partition and formal stopping

For B_p=O_Z,p, multiplication by q is nilpotent precisely at the support points on C. Its number of Jordan blocks is dim(B_p/qB_p)=r_p. On each block of size ℓ, quotienting by q^n contributes min(n,ℓ). This establishes all three displayed Jordan formulas in PROOF.md.

Replacing q by a local unit multiple does not affect the ideals q^nB_p, hence does not affect any quotient dimension or the partition recovered from them. If δ>0, λ_n=nδ holds exactly when every block has size at least n. Therefore h=min ℓ_i is the last successful thickening, and X_(h+1) is the first failure. No reappearance is possible. Since the total local algebra is finite-dimensional, h is finite.

If δ=0, I is the unit ideal near C, and the actual section is primitive at every order. The uniquely determined lines are compatible. Thus the all-order criterion is exactly absence of zero-scheme support on C; neither formal algebraization nor direct-sum splitting follows from that statement alone.

## 4. Higher principal parts and the exact pole divisor

Assume A_m exists. Lift local frames adapted to it to X_(m+1). The section then has coordinates (f,q^m b), with f modulo q a unit times t^r. The lower-left transition terms are divisible by q^m. Their reduced coefficients give the usual graph-lifting cocycle in

    L_m=Hom(A_1,Q_1)⊗O_C(−4m).

The transformation law for the actual lower section coordinate says that multiplying this cocycle by σ=f modulo q gives a coboundary. The twist is indeed L_m(R), because σ is a section of Hom(K_C,A_1)=O_C(R). Terms already carrying q^m use only reduced coefficients modulo q^(m+1); no ambient retraction is required.

Both H⁰(L_m) and H⁰(L_m(R)) vanish. Hence the boundary from principal parts along R is injective, and the obstruction has unique principal parts. The meromorphic graph supplies b_0/t^r, up to the harmless local units and common sign. Its pole order is s=max(0,r−ord_t b_0).

For the length calculation, q remains regular in S/(f). Thus the top q^m layer of S/(f,q^(m+1)) is canonically isomorphic to S/(f,q), a length-r module. The submodule generated by q^m b corresponds to the ideal generated by b_0 in the DVR quotient S/(f,q). Its length is exactly max(0,r−ord_t b_0)=s. Quotienting therefore gives

    length S/(I,q^(m+1))=(m+1)r−s.

Summation proves deg T_m=(m+1)δ−λ_(m+1). Under λ_m=mδ all Jordan blocks have length at least m, so this deficit is exactly the number of blocks of length m. Consequently the obstruction vanishes exactly when the next line lift exists. The principal-parts space for T_m has dimension deg T_m and injects into H¹(L_m), whose dimension is d+4m−1. The proof correctly does not interpret these dimensions as a realized moduli-family dimension.

The local deficit makes each pole multiplicity intrinsic. After the first failure there is no A_j and hence no successive embedded-line obstruction ob_j to name; later numerical deficits are correctly kept separate.

## 5. Finite bounds and numerical arithmetic

The inequality δh≤N_C≤N_a yields h≤floor(N_a/δ). The arrays for N_0=16 and N_1=19 are correct, as are the forced failures by X_17 and X_20 respectively whenever δ>0. A lift to those respective levels forces δ=0 and then all-order line lifting. The formula length Z=c₂(E)+2a+a² is also correct; using 16 and 19 requires the explicitly stated c₂(E)=16 input.

The symbolic complete-intersection example (t^r,q^h) indeed has r blocks of size h and λ_n=r min(n,h). It is correctly labelled a local model, without a global realization claim.

## 6. Full splitting and the δ≥4 consequence

For an existing A_n, splitting is equivalent to vanishing of the extension class in H¹(X_n,Hom(Q_n,A_n)). The reduced degree of this ratio is d. At step m its new layer has degree d−4m; the kernel for lifting an already zero extension class is the quotient by the connecting image from H⁰(M_m), as stated. Omitting that quotient would be incorrect, but PROOF.md includes it.

The new-layer H¹ vanishes exactly when d≥4m−1, and since d is even the uniform threshold is d≥4m. Thus line lifting through X_n forces splitting for n≤floor(d/4)+1=a+2+floor(δ/2). The proof properly treats this as a sufficient range rather than a necessary criterion for an individual split bundle.

For δ≥4, floor(N_a/δ)≤a+2+floor(δ/2): at δ=4 the two comparisons are 4≤4 and 4≤5, and thereafter the left side is nonincreasing while the right side is nondecreasing. Every surviving line order n≤h is therefore in the guaranteed splitting range. Conversely, any decomposition into two lines reduces to a decomposition with the prescribed unequal degrees on C; the larger summand reduces to the unique maximal embedded line A_1 and so provides a lift. Hence full splitting is equivalent to n≤h in these branches.
