# All invariant ideal contractions and eventual coefficient-cohomology vanishing

## Theorem

Retain the notation and hypotheses of PROOF.md. For n≥0 define the ideal sheaf

    K_n = (t₂^a t₃^b : a,b≥0, 2a+3b≥n),    K_0=O_Y.

These are weighted monomial ideals in the two complete-intersection equations of N; they are not ordinary powers of I_N. Then

    (q_*I_D^n)^S4 = K_n.

Consequently, for every k≥3,

    (q_*I_D^k)_sign = O_Y(-3) K_(k-3),
    (q_*(I_D^k EA))_sign = B0(-4) K_(k-3).

For k≤3 the sign pushforward is unrestricted. In particular K_1=K_2=(t₂,t₃), K_3=(t₃,t₂²). All equalities concern actual subsheaves under Vandermonde division, including quadruple collisions.

There exists an integer n0, not evaluated here, such that

    H²(Y,B0(-4)K_n)=0    for every n≥n0.

Thus sufficiently high triple-diagonal ordinary-ideal membership is a universal sufficient condition for vanishing of the first-ten class of a closed sign cochain. This asserts neither a numerical threshold nor membership of the actual physical source. Collision-ideal order is not deformation order.

## 1. Weighted associated graded downstairs

Fix a local frame of O_Y(1), and write u=t₂,v=t₃ in this frame. At a point y∈N let R=O_Y,y and let S=C[U,V]_(U,V) map to R by U↦u,V↦v. This is a local homomorphism of Noetherian local rings, essentially of finite type. The base S is regular of dimension two, R is regular and hence Cohen–Macaulay, and the regular sequence u,v gives

    dim R/(u,v) = dim R−2.

The local miracle-flatness criterion (Stacks Project, Lemma 10.128.1, Tag 00R4: https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00R4) therefore implies that R is flat over S. This applies at nonclosed points as well, with their local dimensions, and does not require R/(u,v) to be regular or normal.

On S take the decreasing filtration L_n generated by monomials of weights at least n, assigning weight2 to U and weight3 to V. Its associated graded algebra is

    gr_L S = C[U,V],  deg U=2, deg V=3.

Indeed L_n/L_(n+1) has as C-basis precisely the weight-n monomials. Localization at (U,V) does not change these quotients, since the action factors through S/(U,V)=C.

Flat base change identifies K_n R=L_n R and preserves the exact sequences defining all successive quotients. Consequently

    gr_K R = (R/I_N R)[U,V],  deg U=2, deg V=3.

No completion, infinite expansion, or eventual-convergence argument is involved. Frame changes multiply U and V by the appropriate line units, so globally the degree-n term is the direct sum of O_N(-2a-3b) indexed by 2a+3b=n.

## 2. Injectivity of the comparison of associated gradeds

Upstairs the regular-sequence ideal has

    gr_I O_Z = Sym_(O_D)(I/I²).

After the same descended frame, I/I² is freely generated by the two weight differences x,y. The pullbacks of u,v give the universal homogeneous polynomials

    q₂(X,Y)=(4/3)(X²-XY+Y²),
    q₃(X,Y)=2(s₁³+s₂³+s₃³),

where s₁=(X+Y)/3, s₂=(Y−2X)/3, s₃=(X−2Y)/3. Their degrees are 2,3.

The homomorphism C[U,V]→C[X,Y], U↦q₂,V↦q₃, is injective. For a proof without a selected numerical evaluation, each sᵢ satisfies

    z³−(q₂/4)z−q₃/6=0.

Thus C[X,Y]=C[s₁,s₂,s₃] is integral over C[q₂,q₃]. Equality of dimensions under integral extensions gives dim C[q₂,q₃]=2, so the two generators are algebraically independent. Tensoring this injection over C with any C-algebra preserves injectivity.

The reduced-image description gives an injection O_N→q_*O_D. Combining it with the preceding polynomial injection gives, on the local trivializing opens, an injection

    gr_K O_Y → q_*(gr_I O_Z).

Concretely it first embeds the coefficient ring O_N into q_*O_D and then substitutes q₂,q₃ for U,V. It is enough to work with all ordered points above a quotient open; no claim of injectivity into an arbitrarily selected local branch is needed. The local descriptions agree under frame change and therefore give the sheaf assertion. Reducedness of D was used for the established image ideal, not to cancel a polynomial over a domain.

## 3. Contraction equality without infinite expansions

By construction K_n pulls back into I^n, so K_n⊂(q_*I^n)^S4.

Conversely work on a stalk downstairs and suppose f∉K_n. Since K_0=O_Y and the filtration is decreasing, there is an integer r with 0≤r<n such that

    f∈K_r but f∉K_(r+1).

Its nonzero associated-graded class maps injectively by Section 2 into the degree-r I-adic quotient upstairs. Therefore q*f∉I^(r+1), and in particular q*f∉I^n. This proves the reverse inclusion. Away from N all the ideals are units, so there is no omitted locus.

The sign formulas follow from the already audited identity (I^k:v)=I^(k-3), alternant division, and projection formula. There is no induction involving infinitely many possible coefficient corrections.

## 4. Adjacent-generator resolution

For n≥1 choose the finitely many divisibility-minimal monomials generating K_n and order them as

    m_i=t₂^(a_i)t₃^(b_i),   i=0,...,r,
    a_0>a_1>...>a_r=0,    0=b_0<b_1<...<b_r.

The endpoint exponents are a_0=ceil(n/2), b_r=ceil(n/3). Put

    d_i=2a_i+3b_i,
    e_i=2a_i+3b_(i+1)  (i=0,...,r−1).

Then the exact adjacent-monomial resolution is

    0 → ⊕_(i=0)^(r−1) O_Y(-e_i)
      → ⊕_(i=0)^r O_Y(-d_i) → K_n → 0.

The ith syzygy has components t₃^(b_(i+1)−b_i) in position i and −t₂^(a_i−a_(i+1)) in position i+1. The last map is multiplication by the monomials m_i.

For completeness, over C[U,V] these adjacent syzygies generate all relations. A monomial relation can be separated by its common resulting monomial; the participating generators divide that monomial and form a consecutive interval in the ordered list. Differences between consecutive generators then span the coefficient relations whose sum is zero. General polynomial relations are sums of these monomial relations. The syzygies are independent: the zeroth component forces the first coefficient to vanish in the polynomial domain, and successive components force the rest. This proves the displayed free resolution over the polynomial base.

At points of N, flatness from Section 1 preserves this resolution. Off N, either t₂ or t₃ is a unit. The polynomial-base resolution localized where U or V is invertible resolves the unit ideal, and is split exact since its cokernel is free; tensoring with a target ring preserves its exactness. Hence the resolution is exact everywhere and its homogeneous maps glue with the stated line twists.

Each minimal generator has n≤d_i≤n+2: if a_i>0, removing one t₂ must drop below n, giving d_i<n+2; for a_i=0, removing one t₃ gives d_i<n+3. Every e_i is at least each adjoining d, hence e_i≥n. Thus all generator and syzygy twists tend to positive infinity uniformly as n grows.

## 5. Eventual vanishing is a Serre theorem, not a computed threshold

Tensor Section 4 by the locally free B0(-4), and write the result as 0→F_1→F_0→B0(-4)K_n→0. Its long exact sequence contains

    H²(F_0) → H²(B0(-4)K_n) → H³(F_1).

Self-duality B0≅B0∨ and Serre duality on P4 give

    H²(B0(-4-d))∨ = H²(B0(d−1)),
    H³(B0(-4-e))∨ = H¹(B0(e−1)).

Serre vanishing supplies an integer T such that H¹(B0(t))=H²(B0(t))=0 for every t≥T. Since d_i,e_i≥n, any n≥max(1,T+1) makes both outer groups zero. Thus H²(B0(-4)K_n)=0 for all sufficiently large n.

This establishes existence of a finite sufficient collision order for closed sign representatives, even though no such numerical order has been computed. Taking n0=max(1,T+1), specifically k≥max(4,T+4) guarantees that the H² of the kth-power sign coefficient is zero. It does not say that the physical source has this ordinary-ideal order, nor that every representative of a zero class has it. The small values of n require actual map information and do not inherit an unevaluated effective bound.
