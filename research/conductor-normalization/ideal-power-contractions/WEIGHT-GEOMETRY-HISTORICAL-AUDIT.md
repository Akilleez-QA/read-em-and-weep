# Independent audit: triple diagonals and the Koszul coefficient resolution

## Verdict

PASS for the coefficient-geometric claims in WEIGHT-DIFFERENCE-LOCUS.md and their use in section 5 of WEIGHTED-REMAINDER-INTAKE.md. The ideal is reduced, has precisely the four triple diagonals as components, and is a codimension-two complete intersection even along their common quadruple-root diagonal. The displayed Koszul twist and S4 determinant character are correct.

One useful scope clarification should accompany any cohomological continuation: once the combined remainder is a closed cochain for the genuine O-linear Čech differential, its ideal-valued lift is also closed. This follows from an injective coefficient-sheaf inclusion and does not require the individual coefficients in a two-generator expression to be closed. It still gives no regular primitive.

This audit independently checked the local equations against the cached text of PR1 comment 6276688181 and checked the arguments in both named documents. It does not independently establish the imported raw-source regularity, closure, source-to-canonical comparison, or actual geometric coefficient action. No source values or obstruction coordinates were evaluated.

## 1. Equations, support, and reducedness

Write a=x1-x4, b=x2-x4, c=x3-x4 on a common finite-root chart. The first two weight differences, divided by 3, are

    f=a(c-b),    g=b(c-a).

The third difference is g-f. Thus all three generate I=(f,g). Their common zero set has exactly the four prime components

    (a,b), (a,c), (b,c), (a-b,b-c).

These correspond, respectively, to Delta124, Delta134, Delta234, and Delta123. The x4 coordinate is free.

The linear factors a, c-b, b, c-a are pairwise nonassociate where required, so f and g have no common irreducible factor. In the polynomial ring C[a,b,c], this implies height(I)=2 and that f,g form a regular sequence. Hence its quotient is a one-dimensional complete-intersection Cohen–Macaulay ring. In particular it has no embedded associated primes.

The two Jacobian rows are

    (c-b,-a,a),    (-b,c-a,b).

They have rank two away from the origin on each of the three coordinate axes and on the diagonal line a=b=c. Thus every minimal component is generically reduced. A Noetherian ring satisfying S1 and reduced at all its minimal primes is reduced. This proves the scheme-theoretic equality

    (f,g)=(a,b) ∩ (a,c) ∩ (b,c) ∩ (a-b,b-c).

The proof excludes both nilpotent thickening and an embedded component at the origin. Adding x4 preserves the result. The quadruple diagonal is nevertheless a singular locus of the union; reducedness is not smoothness.

## 2. Globalization and coverage

Let a root have homogeneous coordinates [X_i:Y_i], and put [ij]=X_i Y_j-Y_i X_j. The three differences homogenize to

    3[14][32],    3[13][42],    3[12][43].

Each is a section of A=O(1,1,1,1). The relation between them is the homogenization of the affine relation, equivalently the two-dimensional bracket relation. Their span is the two-dimensional weight space U.

For any ordered configuration of four points of P1, including repeated points, an omitted point can be chosen outside its finite support. All four roots then belong to the corresponding common affine chart. These charts cover the whole product Z. Under a simultaneous fractional-linear coordinate change, every bracket product acquires the same determinant factor and the appropriate four coefficient-frame denominators; on overlaps these are units. Thus the local ideals glue to the image of

    U ⊗ A^-1 → O_Z.

The zero scheme is the reduced union D of the four triple diagonals on every chart, so this image is exactly I_D globally. No restriction to the usual chart omitting a preselected infinity point is being made.

The statement “complete intersection” is valid globally as the zero locus of a regular section of the rank-two bundle U* ⊗ A. It does not assert that the ideal sheaf is generated by two globally defined scalar functions on the projective variety.

## 3. Exact sequence and equivariance

For a local basis u,v of the constant vector space U whose evaluations are f,g in a frame of A, the untwisted ideal resolution is

    0 → det(U) ⊗ A^-2 → U ⊗ A^-1 → I_D → 0.

The first map sends u∧v to f v-g u in compatible line frames; the second is evaluation. This proves both the determinant representation and the line-bundle exponents directly. Tensoring by A gives exactly

    0 → det(U) ⊗ A^-1 → U ⊗ O_Z → I_D ⊗ A → 0.

The ungraded permutation linearization of A carries no extra S4 sign. S4 permutes the three pairing weights through S3; their only relation is their sum. Hence U is the standard two-dimensional S3 representation inflated to S4, namely [2,2]. Its determinant is the sign of that S3 permutation, which pulls back to sign on S4: an S4 transposition swaps exactly two pair partitions.

This identifies det(U)|S4, not every commuting equivariant character. With the convention H0(P(V),O(1))=V*, bracket products carry the simultaneous GL(V) character (det V*)^2, so det(U) additionally carries (det V*)^4. Keeping det(U) intrinsic avoids any convention-dependent omission. No S4 action on a fixed paired ambient has been assumed.

Tensoring the displayed sequence by a locally free coefficient bundle E preserves exactness. The local-freeness or suitable flatness condition is important and should not be silently dropped for arbitrary coefficient modules.

## 4. Ideal membership, small-diagonal order, and closure

Let F=A⊗E. For the transported regular cochain

    T=(1/9) Σ_(i<j) (sigma_i-sigma_j)(r_i-r_j),

each summand belongs to I_D⊗F, provided the coefficients have been transported to a common actual coefficient frame and the same Čech intersection. Therefore T restricts to zero on D scheme-theoretically.

At the small diagonal, J=(a,b,c), and f,g∈J². Consequently I_D⊂J² and T∈J² F. This proves order at least two for the combined remainder. It does not prove that its order is exactly two or that its zero scheme is precisely D. Along the generic point of a triple diagonal, the Jacobian calculation gives first-order transverse generators for I_D.

For a genuine O_Z-linear Čech differential, the inclusion I_D F→F induces a degreewise injective map of cochain complexes. Since E is locally free, the coefficient inclusion is injective. If the complete combined T is closed as an F-valued cochain, then its uniquely determined I_D F-valued cochain satisfies

    inclusion(delta T)=delta(inclusion(T))=0,

and injectivity gives delta T=0 in the ideal-valued complex. Thus a closed combined source does provide an ideal-valued cohomology class. This conclusion does not follow merely from the individual displayed weight factors if one has not established closure of the combined transported source.

There are two independent cautions:

- A lift of T to U⊗E under the Koszul surjection need not be closed. Its differential can be a nonzero Koszul syzygy. The coefficients multiplying the two scalar generators therefore need not be separately cocycles.
- A differential involving derivatives in root coordinates need not preserve I_D. The statement here concerns the O-linear Čech differential, not an arbitrary differential operator, covariant derivative, or total complex with additional differential terms.

For a cover not known to compute sheaf cohomology, one initially obtains a Čech class; identifying it with sheaf cohomology requires the usual acyclicity or resolution justification. Establishing a global regular primitive remains a separate cohomological or explicit cochain problem, and the source-to-canonical comparison boundary remains necessary.
