# 2. Completed exclusions and superseded routes

A negative result is useful only when its hypotheses and consequence remain visible. The following exclusions concern specific proposed routes, not the impossibility of the original objective.

## 2.1 The remaining odd rank-two alternative is excluded

The special order-five hyperplane analysis proves a particular D5 octic cannot extend to the ordinary square ideal of the section curve. Its actual boundary is nonzero on the required weight-zero line. With the preceding weight-one result and the target classification, this excludes the last scalar a = 12 branch. The residual odd map consequently has generic rank four; together with the projected rank-two map this yields injectivity of the full six-to-ten map. There is no remaining odd rank-two alternative in this chain. [Source][O].

## 2.2 First-order positive smoothing cannot supply the missing rank

An earlier sufficient criterion proposed that rank 33 at q₀ together with a nonzero scalar γ in the first smoothing channel would imply generic positive surjectivity. The implication remains logically valid, but its nonzero-scalar premise is false for the actual module. The cyclic identity aξ = q₀ζₐ forces all first Bocksteins to vanish. Thus γ = 0 exactly. Nonzero individual representation-theoretic legs do not establish a nonzero composite. The current paper must not call γ unknown or present its nonvanishing as an active goal. [Source][F].

## 2.3 Second order alone cannot finish the positive gate

The ordinary-square filtration forces the D5 input to vanish under the actual second Bockstein. Only A, of dimension three, contributes to a target of dimension four. Therefore even the subsequently proved nonzero actual scalar cannot make this obstruction surjective. Its generic rank is three. The final rank is genuinely a higher-order question. [Square-filtration theorem][S]; [actual nonvanishing][N].

## 2.4 A representation channel is not its actual coefficient

The existence and uniqueness up to scalar of a G-equivariant channel do not prove that the coefficient of that channel in the actual bundle is nonzero. The second-order argument therefore separates two steps: geometric identification and nonvanishing of the actual scalar; and generic rank of the corresponding universal symbol. Only their combination gives the rank increase. The first-order failure is a concrete warning against collapsing these steps.

## 2.5 Formal deformation universality is not arbitrary arc algebraicity

The separate V51 construction realizes universal marked formal germs within algebraic families. This does not mean every formal arc into a completed base is algebraic or convergent. Nor does a statement about marked isomorphism classes represent a deformation groupoid or construct unmarked global moduli. Section 5 preserves these distinctions.

## 2.6 Third order cannot supply the missing rank

The actual cubic third gate vanishes identically, not merely on squares or at ordinary polynomial values on the degeneracy scheme. The specialized third differential also kills the extra kernel line at each of the six axes. [Section 15](15-third-vanishing-and-fourth-frontier.md) proves these distinctions and advances the remaining generic finite Smith exponent to 4–31. The earlier conditional nonzero square curve is therefore not realized by the actual gate. Nonzero local residues and lower cohomology classes remain compatible with this global vanishing.
