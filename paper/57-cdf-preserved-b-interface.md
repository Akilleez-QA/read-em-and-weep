## 1. Preservation forces the whole kernel into B

The restricted kernel can only be A or A+D. If it were A alone, Q on the B block would be onto. The preserved negative block would then permit recursive corrections of every A cycle through all orders, contradicting the same actual second-symbol nonvanishing. Hence the restricted kernel is the entire actual A+D.

Character subtraction gives image V+2P+D, of dimension16, and cokernel D. The map from this cokernel to the original P cokernel vanishes by equivariance, so Y_B is contained in im Q. The quotient complex has H⁰=D and H¹=P, with connecting δ to the restricted D cokernel an isomorphism. This is an exact sequence of complexes, not a splitting of their differentials.

## 2. The actual original second determines both first symbols

Let α be the internal first symbol and γ the quotient first symbol. With the fixed connecting convention, legitimate first corrections give the canonical identity β₂=−γδ⁻¹α. The actual second on A is nonzero, forcing both α|A and γ nonzero. Their one-dimensional equivariant channels identify them with binary multiplication A⊗A→D and multiplication A⊗D→Sym⁶W followed by projection to P.

The remaining internal D channel is a scalar multiple of the first transvectant. Composing it with γ gives a nonzero universal polynomial family unless that scalar is zero. Since the actual original second vanishes on D, the scalar must vanish. Thus the internal first is zero on D and injective on A for every nonzero parameter.

On the parameter plane its quotient is the rank-two bundle E presented by 0→A O(-1)→D O→E→0, with c₁=3 and c₂=6. This identifies the forced first-page interface, not an unrelated ambient geometric bundle.

The one-sided preservation hypothesis must still be proved or refuted by actual geometry. A scalar commutant does not itself rule out a nonsplit preserved subcomplex: the direct-summand/projector branch is a different condition. Nothing here requires Q to preserve the complementary block.
