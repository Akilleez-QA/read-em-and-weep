# 7. Earlier primary and Rao reductions on the full space of quadrics

This section restores the earlier reductions that precede the inversion-even analysis. They concern the full projective space P¹⁴ of quadratic equations. The later four-or-five result concerns the inversion-even subfamily. A dense open in the full parameter space need not meet a given special subfamily, so the two scopes must not be conflated.

## 7.1 The actual extension rows

Let F be the normalized Horrocks-Mumford bundle and B = End₀(F). The actual multiplication-compatible primary/residual rows are

    0 → P₂ → H¹(B(2)) → N₂ → 0,
    0 → P₄ → H¹(B(4)) → N₄ → 0,

with dimensions (20, 85, 65) and (40, 85, 45). Here

    P₂ = H⁰(F(3)) ⊗ H¹(F),
    P₄ = H⁰(F(3)) ⊗ H¹(F(2)).

These are extension rows for the actual module, not freely chosen split vector-space models. The two flank problems are injectivity of q:P₂→P₄ and surjectivity of q:N₂→N₄. Even their simultaneous solution leaves an extension boundary. [Primary theorem][P]; [common-open theorem][C].

## 7.2 Generic primary injectivity without a chosen rank sample

Use the notation of the primary source: A₀ = H¹(F) has dimension five, W has dimension two, and U′ = sl(W) has dimension three. The quadratic representation is A₀ ⊗ U′ and the target H¹(F(2)) is identified with ∧²A₀. The actual nonzero multiplication has the exterior-action form

    (a ⊗ D)x = c (id ⊗ D)(a ∧ x),   c ≠ 0.

For each nonzero x, the subspace Lₓ = x ∧ A₀ has dimension four. The evaluation image generated by sl(W)Lₓ has dimension at least four. Its rank-four locus is a proper closed subset of P(A₀), of dimension at most three: otherwise every operator id ⊗ D would preserve every exterior star x ∧ A₀, forcing these operators to be scalar. Intersections of stars are the decomposable exterior lines; their decomposable sums make the scalar conclusion explicit.

The incidence of pairs ([x],[q]) with qx = 0 has dimension at most thirteen. Over the rank-four locus its fibers have dimension ten; elsewhere rank is at least five and fibers have dimension at most nine over P⁴. Its projective image in P¹⁴ is therefore proper. Outside that image, q:H¹(F)→H¹(F(2)) is injective. Tensoring with the four-dimensional H⁰(F(3)) gives actual primary injectivity. No evaluated multiplication matrix enters this proof. [Full argument][P].

## 7.3 Hyperplane section reductions: a = 0 and b ≤ 1

For this subsection only, C denotes the reduced union of the 25 disjoint Horrocks-Mumford lines. Let H = V(ℓ) be a general hyperplane and Z = C ∩ H its 25 reduced transverse points. Define

    a = h⁰(I_(Z,H)(2)),
    b = h⁰(I_(Z,H)(3)),
    M_d = H¹(I_C(d)).

These scalar a and b are not the negative quadratic direction a used in Section 4. The section-case chain summarized in the pinned square-jet theorem gives a = 0 and b ≤ 1. Its principal final exclusions are:

- The unique-quadric branch: differential variation of the actual section quadric forces an ambient quadric containing C, contradicting H⁰(I_C(4)) = 0. This uses reducible-curve incidence, not an integral-curve uniform-position theorem. [Quadric closure][Q].
- The b = 3 branch: after the finite-base and sextic exclusions in its dependencies, the remaining heptic carrier has a one-dimensional quartic defect. Exact Koszul compatibility kills its differential class; descent would give an invariant degree-seven surface, contrary to degree divisibility by five. [Heptic closure][H].
- The b = 2 branch: the carrier classification leaves heptic, octic and nonic alternatives. Small-defect compatibility eliminates the heptic; differentiated linkage syzygies eliminate the octic; canonical adjunction eliminates the last actual nonic differential symbol. [Classification][T]; [small defect][D]; [octic][O]; [nonic][N].

These references retain their earlier endpoint and carrier hypotheses. The summary does not replace that dependency chain with the bare numbers a and b. In particular the unique-cubic argument below resolves b = 1 without claiming b = 0.

## 7.4 The actual square Rao map is injective

The first-normal-jet criterion defines a canonical map

    j_H: H⁰(I_(Z,H)(3)) → H⁰(O_Z(2))/eval H⁰(O_H(2)).

To construct it, lift a section cubic to an ambient cubic G. On each component line L, divide G|L by ℓ|L and evaluate the resulting quadratic at L ∩ H. Changing the ambient lift adds evaluated quadrics, exactly the quotient ambiguity. Thus the map retains the actual first-order line directions, not just the reduced point configuration.

The double-hyperplane ideal sequence gives

    0 → H⁰(I_(Z,H)(2))
      → ker(ℓ²:M₁→M₃)
      → ker j_H → 0.

The global ideal vanishings required by this sequence hold in the actual setting. Consequently square Rao injectivity is equivalent to a = 0 and injectivity of j_H. [Exact jet construction][J].

If b = 0, the jet source is zero. If b = 1, suppose instead that its unique nonzero cubic had generically zero jet. Locally in hyperplane parameters t, choose algebraic ambient cubic lifts g_t vanishing to second order on the actual moving sections. Differentiating along the component curves and using uniqueness gives

    ∂ᵢg = cᵢ(t)g + ℓ_t qᵢ.

Commuting derivatives and applying the Koszul identity in the independent hyperplane coordinates forces the scalar one-form Σcᵢdtᵢ to be closed. On a simply connected analytic parameter open, a nowhere-zero scalar rescaling removes this connection. Now each parameter derivative is divisible by ℓ_t. Successive corrections divisible by ℓ_t² and ℓ_t³ increase that divisibility; at cubic degree the terminal scalar relations force every derivative to vanish. The corrected cubic is a fixed nonzero ambient polynomial vanishing on all sections in an analytic open, hence on every component of C. This contradicts H⁰(I_C(3)) = 0.

Thus the unique cubic has nonzero generic jet. A nonzero map from a one-dimensional space is injective, so ℓ²:M₁→M₃ is injective for general ℓ in both remaining cases. The pinned actual Rao duality identifies this with surjectivity of the corresponding residual quadratic map. The analytic rescaling is sufficient for the contradiction and does not assert an algebraic primitive of the closed one-form. [Square-jet proof][U].

## 7.5 The two good flanks share a dense smooth open

Primary injectivity is a nonempty open condition on P¹⁴. A general square proves that the residual-surjective open is also nonempty. Irreducibility of P¹⁴ makes their intersection nonempty and dense; intersecting with the smooth-quadric open remains nonempty.

This argument does not require the same square to have primary injectivity. Nor does it infer square behavior from a product of linear forms. It proves that a general smooth quadric in the full family has both actual good flanks. [Common-open theorem][C].

## 7.6 The remaining boundary is exactly 20 to 20

On this common open U, retain the tautological twists and set

    K = ker(N₂ ⊗ O_U(−1) → N₄ ⊗ O_U),
    E = coker(P₂ ⊗ O_U(−1) → P₄ ⊗ O_U).

Both are vector bundles of rank twenty. The snake lemma for the actual extension rows supplies a canonical boundary δ:K→E, with

    ker δ_q = ker(q:H¹(B(2))→H¹(B(4))),
    coker δ_q = coker(q:H¹(B(2))→H¹(B(4))).

Thus full invertibility is equivalent to invertibility of δ, not merely goodness of the flanks. In K-theory,

    [E] = 40[O] − 20[O(−1)],
    [K] = 65[O(−1)] − 45[O].

The determinant of δ is a section of O_U(85), up to fixed determinant lines, matching the degree-85 determinant of the full 85-by-85 multiplication map. The agreement of degrees does not prove nonvanishing. Chern classes on an open set likewise do not imply a contradiction without control of their restriction. The common-open theorem leaves δ's invertibility unresolved. [Source][C].

## 7.7 An exact structural failure family

The primary formula exhibits a Segre family

    Σ = P(A₀) × P(U′) ⊂ P¹⁴.

For every decomposable representation tensor q = a ⊗ D, one has qa = 0. Multiplication compatibility then forces the full adjoint kernel to have dimension at least four, and its equal-dimensional cokernel also has dimension at least four. If D is invertible, the primary kernel is exactly the line C a. Thus Σ is an unavoidable failure family for full quadratic invertibility. The full determinant, if nonzero, vanishes to local order at least four at its points. This is a local/symbolic-order assertion, not global membership in an ordinary fourth power of an ideal. [Source][P].

“Decomposable” here refers to A₀ ⊗ U′. It must not be silently interpreted as an ordinary polynomial product of two linear forms. This source alone does not prove that every reducible quadric fails. Nor does failure on Σ exclude generic invertibility elsewhere.

## 7.8 Relationship to the current frontier

These earlier results establish two good flanks and isolate δ on the full space of quadrics. The later parity and Bockstein analysis gives more precise information inside the inversion-even family. Neither result is a certificate for selected original coefficients, source membership, higher lifting, or the original Weil-class algebraicity conclusion. Keeping both reductions visible prevents the paper from losing established progress while concentrating on its newest obstruction.
